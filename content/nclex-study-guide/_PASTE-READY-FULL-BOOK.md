# Chapter 1 — Exam Strategy (Part I)

> **Study aid only.** Strategy framing for the 2026 Next Gen NCLEX. Confirm current rules in the official NCSBN test plan and Candidate Bulletin. Not affiliated with NCSBN.

![CAT and NGN overview diagram](visuals/cat-ngn-overview.jpg)

---

## Why this chapter matters

The NCLEX is not a trivia bowl. It asks: **Can you practice as a brand-new nurse safely?** If you understand *how* the exam thinks — CAT, NGN clinical judgment, Client Needs, and priority frameworks — every content chapter becomes easier to apply.

---

## How the NCLEX works (plain English)

### The big picture

| Fact | NCLEX-RN / NCLEX-PN (2026 framing) |
| --- | --- |
| Style | Computerized Adaptive Testing (**CAT**) |
| Length | **85–150** items |
| Time | Up to **5 hours** (breaks count toward the clock) |
| Scored focus | Content + **clinical judgment** (NGN) |
| Unscored | Some **pretest** items (you can’t tell which) |
| Result | Pass/fail vs. a set standard (not a “percent correct” you see) |

**Minimum-length mix (typical framing):** about **52** content-area items + **18** case-study clinical judgment items (3 cases × 6) + **15** pretest items. Longer exams add more scored content and stand-alone clinical judgment items as needed. Always verify current Candidate Bulletin details.

### CAT in one sentence

Do well → harder items. Struggle → easier items. The computer keeps going until it’s confident you are **above or below** the passing standard (or you hit the max items/time).

**What that means for you:**

- Don’t panic if questions feel hard — that can be a good sign.  
- Don’t assume an early stop = fail.  
- Keep using the **same safe decision process** on every item.

### Next Gen NCLEX (NGN) — still the real exam in 2026

NGN is not a side quest. Clinical judgment is built into the exam through:

- **Unfolding case studies** (usually 6 questions per case)  
- **Stand-alone** clinical judgment items (~10% depending on length)

#### Six clinical judgment steps (CJMM / NCJMM)

![Six clinical judgment steps](visuals/cjmm-six-steps.jpg)

![NGN bow-tie template](visuals/bow-tie-ngn-example.jpg)

![Maslow pyramid for NCLEX priority](visuals/maslow-pyramid-nclex.jpg)

1. **Recognize cues** — What data matters?  
2. **Analyze cues** — What does it mean?  
3. **Prioritize hypotheses** — What’s most likely / most urgent?  
4. **Generate solutions** — What could help?  
5. **Take action** — What do you do *now*?  
6. **Evaluate outcomes** — Did it work? What next?

#### Common NGN item flavors

- Extended multiple response / **SATA**  
- **Matrix / grid**  
- **Bow-tie** (condition ↔ actions ↔ monitors)  
- **Trend** (data over time)  
- Drag-and-drop / ordering  
- Highlight (hot spot)  
- Cloze / drop-down  

**Remember this:** NGN rewards *thinking like a nurse*, not trivia dump. Practice the *steps*, not just the labels.

### Stronger NGN clinical judgment drill (Enhanced edition)

Treat every unfolding case like a mini shift report:

1. **Scan for kill-me-now cues** (airway, SpO₂ trend, BP crash, suicidal means, Mag RR↓, silent chest).  
2. **Name the priority hypothesis** in one line (“flash pulmonary edema,” “PPH atony,” “stroke — NPO”).  
3. **Choose the first action that protects life/safety**, not the most educational or most complete workup.  
4. **Pick monitoring parameters that match the action** (after diuretic → UOP/K⁺/crackles/weight; after tPA → neuro checks/bleed).  
5. **Evaluate** whether the client improved; if not, escalate — NGN rewards reassessment.

**Matrix/SATA mindset:** each row/option is independently true/false — don’t use “process of elimination for the whole set” as if only one can be right.  
**Trend items:** ask what changed and what that change *means for urgency*.  
**Bow-tie:** condition ↔ actions ↔ monitors — practice even on multiple-choice stems.

**Honest caveat:** Item formats evolve; this book trains *judgment*, not a claim of official NCSBN item replicas.

## Core frameworks for answering questions

Write these on your scratch board if it helps. Use them on almost every item.

### 1) Safety first

Ask: **What keeps this client safest right now?**  
Urgent safety > nice-to-have teaching > routine tasks.

### 2) ABCs (+ “D” when relevant)

1. **Airway**  
2. **Breathing**  
3. **Circulation**  
4. Then disability / neuro, then exposure / environment when trauma/context fits  

**Exception awareness:** In some disaster/triage or specific protocols, priorities shift — but for classic bedside NCLEX stems, ABCs still win often.

### 3) Maslow (when ABCs don’t settle it)

Physiological needs → safety → love/belonging → esteem → self-actualization.

**Hungry, hypoxic, or bleeding beats anxiety** — unless the psych stem is specifically about therapeutic communication *and* no acute physiologic threat is present.

### 4) Nursing process

**Assess → Analyze → Plan → Implement → Evaluate**

- Don’t jump to a fancy intervention if **assessment data is missing** and the client isn’t crashing.  
- If the client *is* crashing, act (and get help) — don’t “assess forever.”

### 5) Acute before chronic / unstable before stable / unexpected before expected

- New confusion after surgery > chronic stable dementia baseline  
- Fresh post-op day 1 > day-of-discharge education (unless stem asks for teaching)

### 6) Elimination strategy

1. Read the **stem** and cover options if you can — predict the answer type.  
2. Spot the **true question** (first action? best response? who to see first?).  
3. Kill options that are **unsafe, out of scope, or ignore the priority**.  
4. For remaining options, pick the one that is **most complete / most immediate / most nursing**.

### Keyword cheat sheet

| Keyword | Often means… |
| --- | --- |
| **First / initial / priority** | Safety + assessment vs. action based on acuity |
| **Most appropriate / best** | Therapeutic + scope + priority |
| **Further teaching needed** | Find the **wrong** patient statement |
| **Assign / delegate** | Right task, person, circumstance, communication, supervision |
| **Early sign** | Subtle change (restlessness, slight SpO₂ drop, mild confusion) |
| **Late sign** | Obvious crisis (cyanosis, unresponsiveness) |

### Delegation (RN) — quick rules

**RN keeps:** unstable clients, new admissions/assessments, teaching that requires RN judgment, complex sterile procedures (as scoped), IV push meds (per policy/scope), care planning.

**Often LPN/LVN:** stable clients with predictable outcomes, reinforce teaching, many meds (per state/facility), wound care within scope, data collection.

**Often UAP/CNA:** ADLs, vitals on stable clients, I&O, positioning, transport of stable clients — **not** assessment, teaching, or clinical judgment.

**Remember this:** You can delegate a **task**, not the **accountability**. If the client is unstable or the outcome is unpredictable, keep it.

## Why it matters on NCLEX

Safety and Infection Prevention is a large Client Needs slice. Many “priority” and “first action” stems are really **infection control, falls, med safety, consent, or environment** questions in disguise. Master these and you protect points across the whole exam.

---

## Topic A — Infection prevention & isolation

### Pathophysiology / concept

Infection control interrupts the chain of infection (infectious agent → reservoir → portal of exit → mode of transmission → portal of entry → susceptible host). **Standard precautions** apply to **every** client. Transmission-based precautions add barriers when the pathogen’s spread mode demands it.

### Assessment cues

- Fever, local redness/drainage, rising WBC (context-dependent)  
- Clusters of similar illness on a unit (QI signal)  
- Client risk: neutropenia, open wounds, invasive lines, age extremes  

### Priority nursing actions

1. Hand hygiene before/after every contact (and after glove removal).  
2. Choose correct isolation category; post signage; stock PPE outside room.  
3. Teach visitors; cohort only per policy.  
4. For **C. difficile**: soap and water (spores); bleach-capable cleaning per policy — alcohol gel alone is not enough.  
5. Don/doff in correct order; never “clean” your phone with contaminated gloves.

### Medications / treatments (high-yield)

- Antibiotics: allergy history first; culture before first dose when ordered and client is stable enough.  
- Antipyretics treat comfort — they do not replace source control.

### Client teaching

- Cover cough; stay home when contagious (community teaching themes).  
- Don’t share personal items; complete antibiotic courses as prescribed.

### Safety / red flags

| Type | Classic organisms (high-level) | PPE / room highlight |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Standard | Everyone | Hand hygiene |
| Contact | C. diff, MRSA* | Gown + gloves |
| Droplet | Flu, N. meningitidis, many respiratory | Surgical mask **donned on room entry**; private room preferred; mask client for transport |
| Airborne | TB, measles | **N95** (fit-tested) + airborne infection isolation room (negative pressure, door closed) |
| Airborne **+ Contact** | Varicella, disseminated zoster | N95 + airborne room **+ gown and gloves** |

\*Follow current facility/CDC-aligned practice.

### Delegation notes

UAP can often gather supplies and remind about hygiene on stable clients; **RN** decides isolation type and teaches complex infection risk (e.g., neutropenia).

### Remember this

- Airborne = N95 + special room when indicated.  
- C. diff = soap and water.  
- Gloves do not replace hand hygiene.  
- Isolation is a **priority** intervention when exposure risk is live.

---

## Topic B — Falls & restraints

### Pathophysiology / concept

Falls = leading hospital injury theme. Risk rises with age, confusion, meds (sedatives, antihypertensives), mobility aids, unfamiliar environment, and urgency to toilet. **Restraints** are a last resort after least-restrictive alternatives fail — they can cause injury, humiliation, and worse outcomes if misused.

### Assessment cues (early vs late)

- **Early risk:** unsteady gait, “I need to get to the bathroom now,” new sedative, bed alarm ignored historically  
- **Late/harm:** found on floor, head injury signs, fracture pain, sudden change in LOC after unwitnessed fall  

### Priority nursing actions

1. Universal fall precautions + targeted interventions (non-skid footwear, call light in reach, bed low, adequate lighting, toileting schedule).  
2. Hourly rounding themes for high-risk clients.  
3. Post-fall: ABCs, neuro check if head injury possible, notify provider, incident process per policy.  
4. Restraints: least restrictive → order required (timing per policy) → assess circulation, skin, toileting, nutrition, release for ROM → document behavior that justified use and alternatives tried.

### Client teaching

- Use call light before standing; sit at edge of bed first.  
- Home: remove rugs, night lights, grab bars themes.

### Safety / red flags

- Restraint as “convenience” or punishment = never.  
- Soft wrist restraints still need frequent CMS checks.  
- Chemical restraint mindset: sedating to control behavior without clinical indication is a safety/ethics issue.

### Delegation notes

UAP can assist with ambulation of **stable** clients using proper technique and report near-falls; RN assesses fall risk and restraint necessity.

### Remember this

- Least restrictive first.  
- Restraints need orders, frequent checks, and a clear clinical reason.  
- Toileting schedules prevent many “I fell trying to get to the bathroom” stems.

## Topic C — Medication rights & high-alert safety

### Concept

Classic rights (person, med, dose, route, time — plus documentation, reason, response, education as taught in your program). High-alert meds (insulin, anticoagulants, opioids, chemo, concentrated electrolytes) need **independent double-checks** per policy.

### Priority actions

1. Two client identifiers.  
2. Allergy check — every time.  
3. Clarify unclear orders; never assume.  
4. Watch look-alike/sound-alike packaging.  
5. After giving: evaluate response (pain relief? BP drop? glucose change?).

### Safety / red flags

- Skipping ID because “I know this patient.”  
- Trailing zeros / missing leading zeros in doses.  
- IV push without checking compatibility/rate.

### Remember this

If an option skips **allergy check**, **ID check**, or **compatible route/time**, it’s usually wrong.

---

## Topic D — Sterile technique & invasive device basics

### Concept

Sterile field protects sterile body cavities and devices. Clean technique reduces bioburden but is not sterile. Central lines and urinary catheters are major CLABSI/CAUTI prevention themes.

### Priority actions

- Sterile: don’t turn back on field; 1-inch border contaminated; wet field = contaminated; above waist.  
- Foley: sterile insertion; keep bag below bladder; closed system; peri care; remove ASAP when ordered/criteria met.  
- Central line: aseptic hub scrub; dressing integrity; report fever/redness at site.

### Remember this

Break in sterile field → stop and restart. Don’t “kinda” salvage it.

---

## Topic E — Informed consent & incident reports

### Consent roles

- **Provider** obtains informed consent (explain procedure, risks, benefits, alternatives).  
- **Nurse** witnesses signature, verifies client understanding at a basic level, advocates if client is confused/coerced/has unanswered questions — **does not** replace the provider as primary educator of risks/benefits.  
- Consent requires capacity; emergency exceptions exist per law/policy.

### Incident reports

- Factual, objective, timely.  
- Do **not** write “incident report filed” as a punitive narrative in the medical record progress note (follow facility charting rules).  
- Purpose = safety/QI, not blame theater.

### Remember this

Nurse witnesses/clarifies; provider informs. Incident reports feed QI.

---

## Topic F — Fire & disaster basics

### Fire

- **RACE:** Rescue → Alarm → Contain → Extinguish/Evacuate  
- **PASS:** Pull → Aim → Squeeze → Sweep  

### Disaster / triage themes (high-level)

Mass casualty may shift from “sickest first” to **greatest good for greatest number** (expectant category exists in true disaster triage). Know your facility’s role; NCLEX usually tests recognition that priorities can differ from bedside ABC norms in declared disaster protocols.

### Remember this

RACE/PASS are classic. Disaster triage ≠ everyday ED prioritization.

---



---

## Topic G — Pressure injury prevention & skin

### Concept

Pressure + shear + moisture → tissue ischemia. Staging 1–4 (+ unstageable, deep tissue pressure injury). Prevention is cheaper than treatment — and it’s a core safety quality metric.

### Priority actions

- Turn q2h themes (individualize); offload heels; moisture management.  
- Nutrition/protein; never massage reddened bony areas.  
- Document size/stage carefully; wound photos per policy.  
- Braden/risk tool on admission and regularly.

### Remember this

Red non-blanching over bone = stage 1 at minimum — intervene. Don’t stage what you can’t see (slough/eschar obscuring the base = unstageable). Do **not** remove stable, dry, adherent eschar on a heel or ischemic limb — it is protective; debridement only when ordered for unstable or infected wounds.

---

## Topic H — Pain vs oversedation

### Concept

Pain is real and deserves treatment; opioids still require RR/sedation monitoring. Pasero/sedation scales themes. Multimodal analgesia reduces opioid need.

### Priority actions

- Assess pain with appropriate scale (numeric, faces, FLACC, PAINAD).  
- Reassess after intervention.  
- Hold opioids for oversedation/low RR per parameters; naloxone for overdose.  
- Nonpharm: positioning, ice/heat as ordered, distraction.

### Remember this

Treat pain and protect the airway. Sedation precedes respiratory arrest — watch the trend.

## Clinical judgment vignette 1 — Wrong isolation

**Stem:** Client coughing blood-tinged sputum, night sweats, weight loss; parked in double room; nurse about to use surgical mask only.

**What to do first:** Move toward airborne precautions pathway (N95 + airborne/negative-pressure room as indicated), mask client for transport, notify infection prevention/provider — don’t wait for “confirmed culture” if suspicion is high per protocol.

**Why:** Airborne exposure risk starts now. NCLEX rewards early correct isolation.

---

## Clinical judgment vignette 2 — Fall + toileting

**Stem:** Confused elderly client on antihypertensives and zolpidem; repeatedly trying to climb out “to pee”; bed alarm sounding; UAP asks for soft restraints “so we can finish med pass.”

**What to do first:** Toilet now, bed low, non-skid footwear, closer observation/rounding, consider sitter or room near desk per policy — **restraints last**, not convenience during med pass.

**Why:** Toileting urgency is a top fall driver. Least restrictive alternatives first.

## Topic I — Neutropenic & protective environment themes

### Concept

Low ANC → infection risk from the environment and the client’s own flora. Fever in neutropenia is an emergency pathway (cultures + prompt antibiotics as ordered).

### Priority actions

- Hand hygiene meticulously; limit visitors; no fresh flowers/plants per policy; safe food practices (follow facility neutropenic diet rules — they vary).  
- Avoid rectal temps, IM injections when possible if platelets also low.  
- Private room themes; HEPA/protective environments when indicated for profound neutropenia.  
- Teach client to report fever, sore throat, new cough immediately.

### Red flag

> **Red flag:** Neutropenic client with T 38.3°C (100.9°F) — don’t “recheck in 4 hours and hope.” Escalate per febrile neutropenia pathway.

---

## Topic J — Blood transfusion safety (fundamentals lens)

### Priority actions

1. Informed consent / type & crossmatch as ordered.  
2. Two-nurse ID check at bedside per policy — unit to client.  
3. Baseline VS; stay for first 15 minutes (or per policy); compatible NS only for priming/flush themes.  
4. Reaction: **stop transfusion**, maintain IV with NS per protocol, notify provider/blood bank, save tubing/bag, VS, treat per reaction type (allergic, febrile, hemolytic, TACO/TRALI themes).

### Red flag

> **Red flag:** Flank pain, fever, hypotension early in transfusion → stop; possible acute hemolytic reaction (ABO mismatch energy).

---

## Topic K — Body mechanics, transfers, and device safety

- Wide base, lift with legs, don’t twist; use lift equipment — nurses’ backs matter and client falls during transfers are common stems.  
- Bed to chair: non-skid footwear, lock brakes, gait belt as policy, stand on weak side themes as taught.  
- Wheelchair: footrests, brakes when transferring.  
- SCDs/TEDs: correct size; not on limbs with active DVT per typical practice; skin checks.

---

## Topic L — Sensory / seizure / aspiration environmental safety

- Seizure pads, suction, O₂ ready when indicated.  
- Aspiration: HOB↑ for meals, thicken only as ordered, chin tuck as taught by SLP.  
- Sensory deficits: call light in reach of functional side; explain before touch; night lights.  
- Suicide precautions environmental search when ordered (see psych chapter).

# Chapter 3 — Management of Care

> **Study aid only.** Scope and assignment rules vary by state and facility. Verify with your nursing regulatory body. PN candidates: think Coordinated Care.

![Delegation rights framework](visuals/delegation-five-rights.jpg)

---

## Why it matters on NCLEX

Management of Care is often a large slice of RN items (commonly cited in the teens–low twenties percent range on published test plans — verify current NCSBN percentages). These stems test whether you protect clients through **prioritization, delegation, advocacy, privacy, continuity, and escalation** — not whether you sound impressive.

---

## Topic A — Multi-client prioritization

### Concept

When several clients need you, rank by **ABCs, acute vs chronic, unstable vs stable, unexpected vs expected, actual problem vs risk** (actual usually wins if equal acuity framing). Maslow helps when ABCs are equal: physiologic and safety before psychosocial teaching.

### Assessment cues that scream “see first”

- New stridor, anaphylaxis signs, SpO₂ plunging  
- Active hemorrhage, chest pain with diaphoresis  
- Sudden change in LOC  
- Post-op fresh bleeds or airway risk  
- Suicidal client with means and plan (safety)

### Priority actions

1. Who is dying/crashing or about to?  
2. Who needs a **nursing assessment** only you can do?  
3. What can be delegated so you stay with the unstable client?

### Worked prioritization vignettes (practice these)

**1.** Day-2 hip replacement requesting oral pain med vs new post-op thyroidectomy with neck swelling and stridor → **thyroidectomy airway first.**  
**2.** Chronic stable HF with baseline crackles requesting education vs client with sudden crushing chest pain and diaphoresis → **chest pain first.**  
**3.** Client ready for routine discharge teaching in 1 hour vs client pulling at ET tube and desatting → **airway client first**; assign teaching later or to another RN when safe.  
**4.** UAP reports “confused” diabetic → **RN assesses** glucose/neuro before assuming dementia baseline.  
**5.** Three “stable” requests: snack, linen change, scheduled PO vitamin — none beat a fresh OR arrival with hypotensive trend.  
**6.** Psych client with escalating agitation and clenched fists vs client needing reinforcement of already-taught incentive spirometry → **safety/de-escalation first**, then IS.

### Remember this

Stable chronic pain teaching loses to new hypoxia. Expected low-grade post-op fever on day 1 often loses to unexpected chest pain.

---

## Topic B — Assignment vs delegation vs supervision

### Definitions (NCLEX lens)

- **Assignment:** distributing responsibility for client care among staff who are authorized and competent (e.g., RN-to-RN caseload).  
- **Delegation:** transferring authority to perform a **specific nursing task** to a competent person in a selected situation while **retaining accountability**.  
- **Supervision:** monitoring, follow-up, evaluation of outcomes — you still own the result.

### Five rights of delegation

Right **task**, **circumstance**, **person**, **direction/communication**, **supervision/evaluation**.

| Role | Typically can | Typically cannot |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **RN** | Assess, teach (initial), care plan, unstable, IVP per policy | “Dump” accountability |
| **LPN/LVN** | Stable predictable care, reinforce teaching, many meds per scope, data collection | Full initial admission assessment / complex unstable judgment (state-dependent) |
| **UAP** | ADLs, vitals on stable, I&O, positioning | Assess, teach, medicate, clinical judgment |

### Float nurse safety

Float staff get orientation to unit essentials (codes, equipment, documentation). Do **not** accept an assignment outside competency without support — negotiate: lighter acuity, buddy RN, or charge reassignment. “I am not oriented to this pump/population” is advocacy, not laziness. Document concerns if you must refuse an unsafe assignment per policy.

### Safety / red flags

- Delegating assessment of a fresh post-op or newly admitted client to UAP  
- Asking UAP to “evaluate if the pain med worked” as the only follow-up  
- Unclear handoff (“watch him”)  
- Floating without stating competency limits

### Remember this

You can delegate a **task**, not the **accountability**. Float = speak up early about skill gaps.

---

## Topic C — Advocacy, HIPAA, advance directives & ethics

### Advocacy

Speak up for consent, pain control, unsafe staffing patterns (use chain of command), cultural/language needs (qualified interpreter — not family minors as primary medical interpreters).

### HIPAA

Share minimum necessary with care team. No hallway gossip, no posting PHI on social media, no leaving screens open. Exceptions exist for legally required reporting (abuse, certain threats) — know the **concept**.

### Advance directives

Living will / durable power of attorney for healthcare. Ask on admission; honor DNR/AND orders per policy; if family conflicts with documented wishes, escalate to provider/ethics — don’t unilaterally override valid directives.

### Ethical principles (NCLEX style)

| Principle | Plain English | Exam vibe |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Autonomy** | Client chooses | Informed consent/refusal |
| **Beneficence** | Do good | Pain relief, advocacy |
| **Nonmaleficence** | Do no harm | Stop unsafe med/act |
| **Justice** | Fairness | Equitable resource use |
| **Fidelity** | Keep promises | Follow through on care plan |
| **Veracity** | Truth-telling | Honest answers about prognosis per role |

When principles clash (e.g., autonomy vs beneficence), use ethics consult / chain of command — don’t invent solo solutions.

### Remember this

Privacy + client wishes + chain of command when safety is threatened. Name the ethical principle when the stem asks.

---

## Topic D — Informed consent nuances

### Concept

Provider explains procedure, risks, benefits, alternatives; nurse **witnesses signature** and verifies client understanding / voluntary consent — nurse does **not** obtain consent for surgeries they don’t perform. Client must have capacity; interpreter if language barrier; no coercion.

### Priority actions

- If client has last-minute questions about risk/benefit → **call provider back** before signing.  
- Pre-medicated / heavily sedated clients generally should not newly sign — capacity concern.  
- Emergency exceptions when client can’t consent and delay threatens life — follow law/policy.  
- Minors / guardianship: know who may legally consent in your jurisdiction.

### Remember this

Questions about the procedure → provider. Questions about timing/prep → nurse can teach. Witness ≠ explain the surgery.

---

## Topic E — Continuity, discharge readiness & referrals

### Concept

Continuity = clean handoffs (SBAR), medication reconciliation themes, follow-up appointments, home safety, teach-back.

### Discharge readiness criteria (high-yield)

Ready when: vitals reasonably stable for destination; client/caregiver can teach-back critical meds and warning signs; prescriptions/DME arranged; transportation/safe destination; follow-up scheduled; barriers (language, literacy, cognition) addressed.  
**Not ready:** unstable VS, new confusion unexplained, can’t state when to call 911 for their condition, no caregiver when one is required, unfinished teaching on high-risk meds (warfarin, insulin).

### Referrals (examples)

PT/OT, dietitian, social work/case management, home health, wound care, spiritual care — RN recognizes need and initiates per policy.

### Remember this

Teach-back beats “any questions?” Discharge unsafe → hold and escalate, don’t shrug.

---

## Topic F — Quality improvement & unsafe practice

### Concept

QI uses data (falls, CLABSI rates) to improve systems. Report near-misses. If a colleague is impaired or unsafe: **remove risk to clients first**, then follow policy for reporting — protect clients over friendships.

### Chain of command when provider orders unsafe

1. Clarify with provider (read-back; state concern with data).  
2. If still unsafe → charge nurse / nursing supervisor.  
3. Continue up (house supervisor, medical director, ethics) per policy until resolved.  
4. Do **not** give a med/perform act you know is unsafe solely because “the doctor said.” Document facts objectively.

### Remember this

Client safety > loyalty to a coworker or fear of a provider. Use policy pathways.

---

## Topic G — SBAR handoff & assignment making

### Concept

Handoff failures cause real harm. **SBAR** (Situation, Background, Assessment, Recommendation) structures urgency without novels. Assignment making matches **acuity + continuity + geography + skill mix**.

### Priority actions

- Lead with what the oncoming nurse must do in the next hour.  
- Include pending labs, new anticoagulants, fall/suicide precautions, isolation.  
- When floating: don’t take an assignment outside your competency without orientation/support — advocate for safe assignment via charge/supervisor.

### NGN angle

Trend items often hide in handoff data: “BP drifting down since 1400” beats a single normal value.

### Remember this

Clear handoff is a safety intervention. Unsafe assignment → speak up using chain of command.

---

## Topic H — Informed refusal, AMA, and capacity (high-level)

### Concept

Competent adults can refuse treatment after being informed of risks. AMA (against medical advice) departure still requires education, documentation, and offering partial alternatives when possible. Capacity is decision-specific and can fluctuate with hypoxia, intoxication, psych crisis.

### Priority actions

- Notify provider; ensure risks/benefits explained.  
- Document quotes, witnesses, what was offered.  
- If lack of capacity + emergent threat to life, emergency exceptions may apply — follow law/policy; don’t invent.

### Remember this

Respect autonomy when capacity present; protect when capacity absent and danger is imminent — escalate.

---

## Topic I — Disaster triage (START high-level)

### Concept

Mass casualty: do the **greatest good for the greatest number**. START-style categories (know the idea, not a memorized script as absolute law):

- **Red (immediate):** life-threatening but salvageable now (e.g., airway issues you can open, controlled bleeding urgency).  
- **Yellow (delayed):** serious but can wait briefly.  
- **Green (minor):** walking wounded.  
- **Black (expectant/deceased):** unlikely to survive given resources — still treat with dignity; roles vary by protocol.

Walking wounded are often tagged green first to clear the scene. This is **not** everyday ED triage — don’t apply black tags to routine single-client care.

### Remember this

Disaster = utilitarian sorting. Everyday care = sickest salvageable individual first. Know which frame the stem is using.

# Chapter 4 — Cardiac

> **Study aid only.** Cardiac protocols (ACS pathways, ACLS) follow facility/AHA-aligned standards — verify current practice. Labs/ranges vary.

![Heart failure left vs right](visuals/heart-failure-left-right.jpg)

![Shock types overview](visuals/shock-types-overview.jpg)

---

## Why it matters on NCLEX

Cardiac items test **perfusion, oxygen supply/demand, fluid overload, and lethal rhythm urgency**. NGN cases love trending vitals, troponin, ECG changes, and response to nitro or diuretics. When stems feel “busy,” sort cues into **congestion vs hypoperfusion** and **stable vs unstable**.

---

## Topic A — CAD / ACS / MI

### Pathophysiology / concept

Coronary arteries supply myocardium. Atherosclerotic plaque → ischemia (stable angina with exertion) → plaque rupture/thrombus → **acute coronary syndrome** (unstable angina / NSTEMI / STEMI). Time is muscle: ischemic but still-viable myocardium in the border zone (area at risk) can be salvaged with timely reperfusion; delayed care enlarges infarct and raises arrhythmia, HF, and shock risk.

Supply–demand mismatch also drives demand ischemia (severe anemia, tachyarrhythmia, hypotension). NCLEX usually focuses on atherosclerotic ACS, but the same ABC and monitoring mindset applies.

### Assessment cues

**Early / typical:** chest pressure/pain ± radiation to arm/jaw/back, dyspnea, diaphoresis, nausea, sense of doom; women/diabetics/elderly may have atypical cues (fatigue, epigastric discomfort, dyspnea alone, “indigestion”).

**Concerning progression:** ongoing pain despite rest/nitro, hypotension, new crackles (LV failure), lethal arrhythmias, cardiogenic shock, new murmur (papillary muscle rupture / VSD themes post-MI).

**Labs/diagnostics themes:** 12-lead ECG urgently; serial troponin; CXR if HF suspected; prepare for cath lab when indicated.

### Priority nursing actions

1. ABCs; sit upright if dyspneic; O₂ **if hypoxic** per current protocol mindset (not automatic high-flow for every chest-pain client).  
2. Continuous cardiac monitoring; **12-lead ECG urgently** — do not delay for lengthy history.  
3. Notify provider / activate ACS pathway.  
4. IV access; aspirin as ordered (chewable themes); nitroglycerin as ordered (watch BP; **hold themes if recent PDE-5 inhibitors** — sildenafil/tadalafil windows).  
5. Morphine only as ordered for refractory pain — not a substitute for reperfusion strategy.  
6. NPO if cath/possible intervention; prepare for reperfusion themes (PCI/thrombolysis eligibility concepts).  
7. Comfort, anxiety reduction, and clear explanations reduce catecholamine surge.

### Medications / treatments (high-yield)

| Med theme | Nursing hooks |
| --- | --- |
| Antiplatelets (ASA, P2Y12) | Bleeding precautions; hold peri-procedure per orders |
| Anticoagulants in ACS | Protocol-driven; HIT/bleeding watch |
| Nitroglycerin | Vasodilation; HA/hypotension; no PDE-5 combo |
| Beta blockers | ↓ myocardial O₂ demand; watch HR/BP/bronchospasm |
| ACEI/ARB (post-MI/HF) | K⁺/Cr; cough/angioedema for ACEI |
| Statins | Long-term plaque stabilization; muscle sx reporting |
| Thrombolytics (selected STEMI) | Bleed risk; neuro checks for ICH |

### Complications to anticipate

- Arrhythmias (PVCs → VT/VF; bradycardia; heart blocks)  
- Left ventricular failure / pulmonary edema  
- Cardiogenic shock  
- Mechanical complications (new murmur, sudden pulmonary edema) days after large MI  
- Bleeding at PCI access site; retroperitoneal bleed (back/flank pain, hypotension)

### Client teaching

- Call emergency services for unrelieved chest pain (don’t drive yourself).  
- Risk factor modification: smoking, lipids, HTN, diabetes, activity as cleared, cardiac rehab themes.  
- Nitro storage/use teaching when prescribed (sit/lie; call EMS if pain persists after prescribed doses per teaching).  
- Dual antiplatelet adherence after stent — premature stop = stent thrombosis risk.

### Safety / red flags

> **Red flag:** Sudden hypotension + tachycardia after nitro — stay with client, support ABCs, notify.  
> **Red flag:** New loud murmur / flash pulmonary edema post-MI — mechanical complication until proven otherwise.  
> **Red flag:** Expanding hematoma or unexplained hypotension after femoral access — escalate immediately.

### Delegation notes

UAP can report chest pain complaints immediately and obtain VS on stable clients; **RN stays with unstable ACS** clients, interprets ECG urgency, and titrates vasoactive/antianginal therapies per orders/policy.

### Remember this

- ECG + don’t leave unstable chest-pain clients alone.  
- Aspirin/nitro per orders; watch BP and PDE-5 history.  
- Atypical presentations still count — especially women, elderly, diabetics.

---

## Topic B — Heart failure (left vs right, congestion vs perfusion)

### Pathophysiology / concept

Pump failure → backup (**pulmonary congestion** in left-sided; **systemic edema**/JVD/hepatomegaly in right-sided) and/or poor forward flow (fatigue, oliguria, cool extremities, confusion). Compensatory SNS/RAAS worsen remodeling over time. Many clients have mixed pictures; chronic right-sided often follows left-sided disease.

| Feature | Left-sided HF | Right-sided HF |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Backup | Lungs | Systemic veins |
| Cues | Dyspnea, orthopnea, PND, crackles, pink frothy sputum (acute) | JVD, edema, weight gain, ascites, liver congestion |
| Forward fail | Fatigue, oliguria, cool skin, confusion | Same perfusion themes if severe |

### Assessment cues

**Congestion:** dyspnea, orthopnea, crackles, weight gain, edema, JVD, S3 themes.  
**Hypoperfusion:** cool clammy skin, hypotension, confusion, falling UOP, narrow pulse pressure themes.  
**Acute decompensation:** pink frothy sputum = pulmonary edema emergency energy — ABC first.

### Priority nursing actions

1. ABCs; high Fowler’s; O₂ as indicated; consider NIV themes as ordered for flash edema.  
2. Telemetry; **daily weights**; strict I&O; compare to dry-weight goals.  
3. Diuretics as ordered; monitor K⁺ and BP; slow IV loop pushes per policy (ototoxicity themes).  
4. Low-sodium diet teaching; fluid restriction if ordered.  
5. Watch digoxin toxicity if applicable (GI, visual changes, bradycardia — confirm with levels/protocol).  
6. Cluster care; reduce anxiety; elevate legs for edema when perfusion allows (not if severe pulmonary edema needing upright posture).

### Medications (high-yield)

- Loop diuretics (furosemide): K⁺ wasting; ototoxicity if pushed too fast IV  
- ACEI/ARB/ARNI themes: cough/hyperkalemia/angioedema awareness for ACEI; ARNI washout after ACEI per protocol  
- Evidence-based beta blockers in stable chronic HFrEF themes  
- Aldosterone antagonists: K⁺ sparing — watch hyperkalemia with ACEI/ARB  
- SGLT2 themes increasingly common in HF — genital infection / volume depletion counseling as ordered

### Client teaching

- Weigh daily — report gain per provider parameters (commonly ~2–3 lb in a day or ~5 lb in a week themes — follow specific teaching).  
- Sodium awareness; med adherence; vaccines as advised.  
- Know when to seek care (worsening dyspnea, orthopnea, swelling, sudden weight gain).  
- Activity pacing; elevation of legs; skin care over edematous areas.

### Safety / red flags

> **Red flag:** Rapid weight gain + new orthopnea = decompensation — escalate, don’t “watch overnight” without reassessment.  
> **Red flag:** Hypokalemia on loops → dysrhythmia / dig toxicity risk.  
> **Red flag:** Confusion + cool clammy + falling BP = hypoperfusion — this is not “just anxiety.”

### Remember this

Daily weights are a vital sign in HF. Treat congestion and protect perfusion — they are not the same problem.

## Clinical judgment vignette 1 — Flash pulmonary edema

**Stem:** 72-year-old with HFrEF becomes acutely dyspneic; SpO₂ 86% on room air; pink frothy sputum; BP 168/98; HR 118; crackles throughout.

**What to do first:** Sit upright / high Fowler’s, apply O₂ per protocol, call for help/rapid response, continuous monitoring, prepare for IV diuretic and possible NIV as ordered — **do not** leave to “finish charting.”

**Why:** Airway/breathing and pulmonary edema are ABC emergencies. Congestion with hypertension often responds to upright posture, O₂, and diuresis/afterload reduction per orders; hypoperfusion shock would look different (low BP, cool clammy).

## Topic C — Hypertension & hypertensive crisis themes

### Concept

Sustained elevated BP damages vessels/organs (brain, heart, kidney, eye). **Hypertensive urgency** vs **emergency** (acute end-organ damage: encephalopathy, ACS, acute HF, aortic dissection themes, papilledema) drives how aggressively and how carefully BP is lowered.

### Assessment

Often silent; headache/vision changes/chest pain/neuro deficits/severe back pain (dissection vibe) = concerning. Accurate cuff size and technique matter — false highs from wrong cuff create noise.

### Priority actions

- Accurate BP technique; trend readings; both arms if dissection suspected.  
- Meds as ordered; **slow controlled reduction** in emergencies per protocol (avoid crashing BP and watershed stroke).  
- Lifestyle teaching: DASH/sodium, weight, alcohol, activity, smoking, adherence.

### Med themes

Diuretics, ACEI/ARB, CCB, beta blockers — hypotension/dizziness teaching; orthostatics; don’t stop clonidine abruptly (rebound HTN).

### Remember this

Lower BP safely; teach lifelong management; watch acute end-organ clues.

---

## Topic D — Shock overview (cardiac lens)

### Concept

Shock = inadequate tissue perfusion. Types: hypovolemic, cardiogenic, distributive (septic/anaphylactic/neurogenic), obstructive (PE/tamponade themes).

| Type | Sketch | Early nursing priorities |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hypovolemic | Volume loss | Stop bleed/fluid replace per orders |
| Cardiogenic | Pump fail | Don’t drown with fluids; support BP/O₂; treat cause |
| Septic | Vasodilation/infection | Cultures, antibiotics, fluids per sepsis bundles |
| Anaphylactic | Allergy | Epinephrine IM themes, airway |
| Obstructive | Block | Relieve cause (e.g., tension PTX, tamponade) |

### Assessment cues

Tachycardia, hypotension, cool clammy (or warm in early distributive), oliguria, rising lactate themes, altered LOC, delayed CRT.

### Priority nursing actions

1. Recognize shock early — trending BP/HR/UOP/LOC beats one snapshot.  
2. ABCs; O₂; IV access; notify provider/rapid response.  
3. **Match fluids to type** — cardiogenic and some obstructive states are not “wide-open fluids.”  
4. Treat cause (stop bleed, antibiotics, epinephrine, reperfusion, needle decompression themes as scoped).

### Remember this

Treat cause + restore perfusion. Cardiogenic ≠ “just give lots of fluid.”

---

## Clinical judgment vignette 2 — Cardiogenic vs hypovolemic

**Stem:** Post-MI client: BP 82/50, cool clammy, crackles, JVD. UAP asks if they should hang a liter bolus “like last shock patient.”

**What to do first:** RN reassessment — this pattern fits **cardiogenic** shock (pump failure + congestion). Support ABCs, notify provider, prepare ordered inotropes/vasopressors/reperfusion support — **do not** automatically bolus large fluids.

**Why:** Wrong fluid strategy worsens pulmonary edema. Type of shock drives intervention.

---

## Topic E — Dysrhythmia nursing priorities

### Concept

Electrical problems alter rate/output. Focus on **pulse presence, stability (BP, LOC, chest pain, dyspnea), and lethal rhythms**. Always treat the patient, not only the monitor strip.

### Priority actions

- Assess **patient**, not only the monitor (pulse check).  
- Unstable tachycardia/bradycardia → rapid response / ACLS-aligned pathway.  
- VF/pVT → CPR + defibrillation themes.  
- Asystole/PEA → high-quality CPR + reversible causes (Hs/Ts mindset) — not defibrillation for asystole.  
- Afib: rate control/anticoagulation stroke-risk themes as ordered.  
- PVCs: treat causes (hypoxia, K⁺/Mg²⁺).  

### Med themes

Amiodarone, adenosine, atropine, epinephrine — recognition of emergency use classes, not cookbook dosing from memory alone. Verify ACLS/facility. Warn clients that adenosine causes brief asystole sensation.

### Remember this

No pulse + shockable lethal rhythm = CPR/defib energy. Stable vs unstable changes the pathway.

---

## Topic F — VTE / DVT / anticoagulation nursing

### Pathophysiology / concept

Virchow’s triad: stasis, endothelial injury, hypercoagulability. DVT → PE risk. Orthopedic surgery, cancer, pregnancy/postpartum, estrogen, prior VTE, and immobility raise risk.

### Assessment cues

Unilateral leg swelling, pain, warmth, redness; Homans’ is unreliable — don’t rely on it. Sudden dyspnea/chest pain after DVT risk = PE until ruled out.

### Priority nursing actions

- Prevention: early ambulation, SCDs on correctly (not on active DVT limb per typical practice), anticoag prophylaxis as ordered.  
- Suspected DVT: don’t massage limb; elevate as ordered; prepare for duplex; anticoagulate as ordered.  
- Bleeding precautions once anticoagulated; teach fall prevention.

### Medication monitoring hooks

| Agent | Monitor vibe |
| --- | --- |
| Heparin UFH | aPTT / anti-Xa; HIT risk |
| LMWH | Renal dosing; bleeding |
| Warfarin | INR; Vit K consistency; interactions |
| DOACs | Renal function; bleed precautions |

### Remember this

Prevention beats treatment. Unilateral swelling after surgery → take seriously.

---

## Topic G — Pacemaker / ICD teaching themes

- Immobilize shoulder briefly as ordered post-implant; don’t raise arm above head initially.  
- ID card; avoid strong magnets/MRI unless device-compatible.  
- Report dizziness, persistent hiccups, or shocks (ICD).  
- Microwaves usually fine; follow manufacturer teaching.  
- At airport/security, inform agents; don’t linger in anti-theft gates.

### Remember this

Arm precautions early post-op; know when to report shocks.

---

## Clinical judgment vignette 3 — Unstable ACS

**Stem:** Client with crushing pain, diaphoresis, BP 88/54, HR 40s, pale. Monitor shows slow wide complex themes.

**What to do first:** Stay with client, support ABCs, activate emergency/ACS/rapid response, ensure defibrillator/pacing readiness per protocol, do **not** send ambulatory to XR alone, do **not** give nitro if profoundly hypotensive.

**Why:** Hypotension + bradycardia = unstable. Nitro would worsen BP. This is resuscitation + pathway activation, not routine med-surg choreography.

# Chapter 5 — Respiratory

![Oxygen delivery devices ladder](visuals/o2-delivery-devices.jpg)

![ABG interpretation ladder](visuals/abg-interpretation-ladder.svg)

## Why it matters on NCLEX

Respiratory stems are classic **ABC** killers. Silent chest, PE, falling SpO₂ trends, and post-op sudden dyspnea appear constantly in priority and NGN items. Train yourself to spot **work of breathing**, **ability to speak**, and **mental status** as much as the monitor number.

---

## Topic A — Asthma

### Pathophysiology / concept

Reversible airway obstruction: bronchoconstriction + inflammation + mucus. Triggers: allergens, infection, exercise, irritants, NSAIDs/aspirin in sensitive clients, cold air. Status asthmaticus = severe attack not responding to usual therapy — impending respiratory failure.

### Assessment cues

**Early:** wheeze, cough, prolonged expiration, tachypnea, anxiety, accessory muscle use.  
**Late/ominous:** **silent chest**, inability to speak full sentences, cyanosis, exhaustion, SpO₂ drop, rising PaCO₂ (fatigue) — impending respiratory failure.

### Priority nursing actions

1. Position upright; O₂ as indicated.  
2. Short-acting beta agonist (albuterol) as ordered; anticholinergic add-ons per protocol.  
3. Systemic corticosteroids for inflammation as ordered (onset slower — still give).  
4. Monitor response; prepare for escalation if silent chest / fatigue.  
5. Keep client calm; avoid unnecessary procedures that increase distress.

### Meds (high-yield)

| Med | Hook |
| --- | --- |
| SABA (albuterol) | Rescue; tachy/tremor; overuse = poor control |
| Ipratropium | Often added in ED severe exacerbations |
| Systemic steroids | Treat inflammation — not instant bronchodilation |
| ICS controllers | Rinse mouth; thrush prevention; not solo rescue |
| Mag sulfate IV | Selected severe exacerbations per protocol |

### Client teaching

- Spacer use; rinse mouth after ICS.  
- Asthma action plan; when to seek emergency care (rescue not lasting, can’t speak, blue lips).  
- Avoid triggers; peak flow themes if used.  
- Get vaccines as advised; treat infections early.

### Safety / red flags

> **Red flag:** Decreasing wheeze **with** increasing lethargy and falling SpO₂ = silent chest — escalate, do not chart as “improved.”  
> **Red flag:** Speaks only 1–2 words between breaths.

### Remember this

Silent chest ≠ improvement. Speaks of dangerous airflow collapse.

---

## Topic B — COPD

### Pathophysiology / concept

Chronic bronchitis / emphysema themes → chronic airflow limitation, air trapping, CO₂ retention in some clients, hypoxic drive nuances (still give O₂ carefully per orders — **don’t withhold** in emergency hypoxia). Exacerbations are often infection-triggered.

### Assessment cues

Dyspnea, barrel chest themes, pursed-lip breathing, chronic cough/sputum, clubbing sometimes; exacerbation = more dyspnea, sputum change (color/volume), hypoxia/hypercapnia, new confusion/somnolence.

### Priority actions

- Tripod/upright; controlled O₂ titration per target SpO₂ orders.  
- Bronchodilators, steroids, antibiotics if indicated for exacerbation.  
- Pulmonary hygiene; nutrition (work of breathing burns calories).  
- Smoking cessation support — the highest-yield long-term intervention.  
- Watch for NIV failure: rising CO₂ + declining LOC → escalate airway plan.

### Teaching

- Pursed-lip / diaphragmatic breathing.  
- Vaccinations themes; pacing activities; energy conservation.  
- Report sputum/color change and fever.  
- Home O₂ safety: no smoking, secure tanks, fire risk teaching.

### Safety / red flags

> **Red flag:** Rising CO₂ + somnolence after “just a little more O₂ without orders.” Titrate to targets.  
> **Red flag:** Home O₂ + smoking = fire/explosion risk counseling mandatory.

### Remember this

Titrate O₂ to ordered targets; treat exacerbations early; stop smoking messaging.

---

## Topic C — Pneumonia

### Concept

Infection of lung parenchyma → consolidation, impaired gas exchange. Aspiration risk in dysphagia/altered LOC/tube feeds/alcohol intoxication. Community vs hospital-onset changes organism risk and empiric themes — nursing focus remains oxygenation, cultures, antibiotics, and prevention of complications.

### Assessment

Fever, productive cough, pleuritic pain, crackles, tachypnea, **confusion in elderly** (may be primary cue), hypoxia, rusty/green sputum themes by organism folklore (don’t over-rely).

### Priority actions

- O₂; cultures then antibiotics as ordered when stable enough — **don’t delay sepsis care** inappropriately.  
- Hydration if not contraindicated; incentive spirometry / turn-cough-deep breathe.  
- Aspiration precautions when indicated; elevate HOB; swallow screen if neuro event.  
- Vaccination teaching (pneumococcal/influenza themes as advised).

### Complications

Empyema, bacteremia/sepsis, respiratory failure, ARDS in severe cases.

### Remember this

Elderly: confusion may be pneumonia. Antibiotics after cultures when possible; don’t delay sepsis care inappropriately.

---

## Topic D — Pulmonary embolism

### Pathophysiology / concept

Thrombus (often DVT) → pulmonary artery obstruction → V/Q mismatch, strain on right heart, possible sudden death. Fat/air/amniotic emboli are less common but tested as pattern recognition.

### Assessment cues

**Sudden** dyspnea, pleuritic chest pain, tachycardia, anxiety, hemoptysis sometimes, hypoxia; post-op/immobile/OCPs/cancer/pregnancy = risk themes. Massive PE: hypotension, syncope, right-heart strain.

### Priority nursing actions

1. Stay with client; call rapid response/provider.  
2. O₂; high Fowler’s; monitor VS/ECG.  
3. Prepare for diagnostics/anticoagulation/thrombolysis per orders.  
4. Prevention elsewhere: early ambulation, SCDs, anticoag prophylaxis as ordered.

### Medication / treatment hooks

Anticoagulation promptly as ordered; thrombolysis/embolectomy for selected massive PE; IVC filter themes in selected cases — verify current indications.

### Safety / red flags

> **Red flag:** Sudden dyspnea + risk factors → think PE until proven otherwise. Don’t leave alone.  
> **Red flag:** Syncope + hypoxia post-op day 2 — escalate.

### Remember this

Sudden dyspnea + risk factors → PE until proven otherwise. Don’t leave alone.

## Clinical judgment vignette 1 — Silent chest

**Stem:** Teen with known asthma: after two nebulizers, wheezes are quieter; parent says “thank goodness.” Client is drowsy, SpO₂ 88%, can only whisper.

**What to do first:** Treat as respiratory failure trajectory — upright, high-flow O₂ as ordered, activate rapid response, prepare for escalation (ABGs, continuous nebs, mag, possible airway), **do not** discharge or “observe quietly without help.”

**Why:** Quiet chest + fatigue = less air movement, not cure. NGN loves this cue flip.

## Topic E — Oxygen delivery & chest tubes

### O₂ devices (conceptual ladder)

Nasal cannula → simple mask → Venturi (precise FiO₂ — useful in COPD titration) → non-rebreather (high FiO₂; bag must inflate) → high-flow nasal cannula / NIV / advanced airways. Humidify high flows as indicated; skin care on ears; fire safety with O₂.

### Chest tubes

- System **below** chest level; keep upright; no dependent loops full of drainage blocking flow.  
- Tidaling in water seal often expected with respiration; **continuous bubbling in water seal** may indicate air leak.  
- Continuous gentle bubbling in suction chamber may be expected when suction applied (device-dependent — know your system).  
- Don’t routinely clamp without direction; dislodgement → occlusive dressing themes per protocol (often three-sided for sucking chest wound concepts); call for help.  
- Output: sudden stop may mean kink/clot; sudden large bloody output → hemorrhage concern — notify.

![Chest tube drainage system zones](visuals/chest-tube-system.jpg)

### Remember this

Below chest; know bubbling vs tidaling meaning; protect airway if tube issues.

## Topic F — ABGs intro

### Concept

| Look at | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| pH | Acidemia vs alkalemia |
| PaCO₂ | Respiratory component |
| HCO₃⁻ | Metabolic component |
| PaO₂ / SaO₂ | Oxygenation |

**Shortcut:** Match the component that explains the pH = primary process. The other may compensate.

| Pattern | Common NCLEX contexts |
| --- | --- |
| Respiratory acidosis | Hypoventilation, COPD retention, opioid OD |
| Respiratory alkalosis | Hyperventilation, anxiety, PE early, pain |
| Metabolic acidosis | DKA, lactic shock, diarrhea, renal failure |
| Metabolic alkalosis | Vomiting, NG suction, diuretic themes |

**Remember this:** Treat the patient, not only the number — but know which patterns fit the stem.

## Topic G — Acute respiratory failure & ARDS themes

### Pathophysiology / concept

Respiratory failure: inadequate gas exchange — hypoxemic (low PaO₂) and/or hypercapnic (high PaCO₂). **ARDS**: inflammatory alveolar injury → shunting, stiff lungs, refractory hypoxemia; often after sepsis/trauma/aspiration/pancreatitis.

### Assessment cues

Worsening dyspnea, tachypnea, accessory muscles, falling SpO₂ despite O₂, agitation → lethargy (CO₂ rising), crackles, pink frothy sputum in cardiogenic overlap (differentiate by history/BNP/echo themes).

### Priority nursing actions

1. Escalate early — don’t wait for perfect ABG if client crashing.  
2. High-flow O₂ / NIV / intubation as ordered and scoped.  
3. Positioning (prone therapy themes in ARDS as ordered).  
4. Treat cause (infection, fluid overload, PE).  
5. Conserve energy; cluster care; anxiety support without oversedating.

### Remember this

Refractory hypoxia + bilateral infiltrates context → ARDS pathway thinking. Lethargy after fighting for air is ominous.

## Topic H — Tracheostomy & suctioning safety

### Priority actions

- Spare trach at bedside for new trachs; obturator; suction equipment ready.  
- Suction only as needed; preoxygenate per protocol; sterile technique for open suction; limit pass time.  
- If dislodged newly: emergency — cover stoma/ventilate per training, call for help.  
- Cuff pressures and aspiration precautions as ordered; oral care to reduce VAP risk themes.

### Safety / red flags

> **Red flag:** New trach dislodgement in first days = emergency airway — don’t casually retie and walk away.  
> **Red flag:** Continuous bloody suction returns or sudden desat during suction — stop, oxygenate, notify.

### Remember this

New trach dislodgement = emergency. Don’t yank ties casually in the first days.

## Clinical judgment vignette 2 — Post-op PE

**Stem:** POD2 total knee: sudden dyspnea, HR 128, SpO₂ 90%, anxiety. Pain controlled earlier.

**What to do first:** Stay, high Fowler’s, O₂, VS/ECG, call rapid response/provider for possible PE — do **not** ambulate alone “to help lungs” without assessment.

**Why:** Sudden dyspnea + tachy after ortho surgery is classic PE pattern.

## Clinical judgment vignette 3 — Chest tube air leak

**Stem:** Client with chest tube: continuous bubbling in water-seal chamber; SpO₂ stable; dressing dry.

**What to do first:** Assess client breath sounds/VS, check tubing connections from chest to system for loose links, ensure system integrity — notify provider of new/ongoing air leak per protocol. Don’t clamp routinely without order.

**Why:** Continuous water-seal bubbling suggests air leak (client or system). Assessment before blind clamping.

## Topic I — Tuberculosis nursing themes (high-yield)

### Concept

Airborne isolation for suspected/confirmed pulmonary TB until cleared per protocol. Medication adherence for months prevents resistance.

### Priority actions

- Airborne room + N95 for staff when indicated; door closed.  
- Collect sputum specimens correctly; mask client for transport.  
- RIPE therapy themes: hepatotoxicity monitoring; rifampin orange body fluids teaching; INH + B6 for neuropathy prevention themes; ethambutol vision changes reporting; pyrazinamide uric acid/joint themes.  
- Directly observed therapy (DOT) concepts for adherence.

### Red flag

> **Red flag:** New night sweats, hemoptysis, weight loss + abnormal CXR — isolate pending workup; don’t park in hallway waiting room without masking.

### Remember this

Airborne + adherence. Orange urine on rifampin is expected teaching — not “bleeding.”

## Topic J — Aspiration precautions & prevention

### Who is at risk

Stroke, Parkinson, altered LOC, sedation, enteral feeds, poor dentition, GERD, emergency intubation themes.

### Nursing actions

- HOB elevated for feeds; residual checks per protocol; pause feeds when flat for care.  
- Swallow screen after stroke before PO.  
- Oral care — reduces pneumonia risk even in NPO/intubated clients.  
- Suction available; thicken liquids only as ordered by speech therapy.

### Clinical judgment note

“Client hungry” never outranks an unsafe swallow. Aspiration pneumonia is a preventable NGN outcome evaluation item.

## Topic A — Stroke (CVA)

### Pathophysiology / concept

Brain tissue dies when blood flow stops. **Ischemic** (thrombotic/embolic) is most common; **hemorrhagic** (ICH/SAH) bleeds into/around brain. “Time is brain.” Penumbra can be saved with timely reperfusion in eligible ischemic strokes — hemorrhagic paths differ (**no tPA**). Embolic strokes often sudden (afib); thrombotic may stutter with TIAs.

### Assessment cues

**FAST:** Face droop, Arm weakness, Speech difficulty, Time to call emergency care.  
Other: sudden severe headache (esp. hemorrhagic/SAH “worst headache”), visual field loss, ataxia, neglect, altered LOC, aphasia vs dysarthria, unilateral numbness.

**NIHSS themes** (high-level): structured deficit scoring — know that serial neuro checks matter more than memorizing every item.

### Priority nursing actions

1. ABCs; **NPO until swallow screen** passed — aspiration is a killer complication.  
2. Exact **last known well** time — drives eligibility windows.  
3. Rapid stroke protocol / CT to differentiate ischemic vs hemorrhagic.  
4. Glucose check (hypoglycemia mimics stroke).  
5. Elevate HOB per protocol if not contraindicated; head midline; avoid fever if possible.  
6. After ischemic confirmation and eligibility: thrombolysis/thrombectomy pathways as ordered — BP parameters matter (too high or too low both dangerous in context).  
7. Hemorrhagic: BP control per orders; reverse anticoagulation if ordered; neurosurg consult themes.  
8. Frequent neuro checks — report worsening (extension, bleed, edema).

### Medications / treatments (high-yield)

| Theme | Hooks |
| --- | --- |
| Alteplase (tPA) | Strict inclusion/exclusion; bleed risk; ICU monitoring; no anticoags/antiplatelets for a period after per protocol |
| Antiplatelets | Secondary prevention ischemic as ordered |
| Anticoagulation | Afib-related prevention when appropriate (timing after acute stroke varies) |
| Statins / BP meds | Secondary prevention |
| Nimodipine | SAH vasospasm themes — BP/hypotension watch |

### Complications

Aspiration pneumonia, DVT/PE, increased ICP/herniation, hemorrhagic transformation after tPA, shoulder subluxation on flaccid side, depression, neglect injuries.

### Client teaching

- Call emergency services at FAST signs — don’t “wait and see.”  
- Med adherence; smoking cessation; diabetes/HTN/lipid control; afib anticoagulation importance.  
- Aspiration precautions / diet texture as prescribed.  
- Unilateral neglect safety (scan environment); transfer toward strong side teaching with PT/OT.

### Safety / red flags

> **Red flag:** Sudden neuro decline after tPA → possible hemorrhage — stop infusions per protocol, notify, prepare CT.  
> **Red flag:** Wet voice / coughing with sips before swallow screen cleared.  
> **Red flag:** Pulling on flaccid arm during transfer.

### Delegation notes

UAP can assist with safe positioning and report changes; **RN** does swallow screen and neuro assessment.

### Remember this

- NPO until swallow cleared.  
- Last known well time is critical data.  
- Ischemic ≠ hemorrhagic treatment path.

## Topic B — Increased ICP & head injury themes

### Pathophysiology / concept

Skull is closed box (Monro-Kellie): brain + blood + CSF. Increase in one → ICP rises unless compensation. High ICP → ischemia → herniation risk. Cerebral perfusion pressure ≈ MAP − ICP — both extremes of BP matter.

### Assessment cues

**Early:** change in **LOC** (irritability, confusion, restlessness), headache, vomiting (sometimes projectile), pupil changes beginning.  
**Late:** Cushing’s triad themes (**bradycardia, irregular respirations, widening pulse pressure/hypertension**), fixed dilated pupils, posturing, coma.

### Priority nursing actions

1. Maintain airway; avoid hypoxia/hypercarbia (both raise ICP).  
2. HOB elevation ~30° often; head midline; avoid tight collar ties.  
3. Minimize clustering of painful care; stool softeners to avoid Valsalva; no hip flexion extremes if they raise ICP.  
4. Osmotic therapy / hypertonic saline as ordered; monitor labs.  
5. Seizure precautions when indicated.  
6. No lumbar puncture if mass lesion/herniation risk without clearance.  
7. Quiet environment; control fever; treat pain without oversedating (need neuro exams).

### Meds (high-yield)

- Mannitol / hypertonic saline themes: monitor osmolality, electrolytes, volume status, IV site (hypertonic vesicant risk themes)  
- Antiepileptics if ordered  
- Avoid hypotonic free water overload themes  
- Steroids more for tumor edema than routine TBI — follow orders/guidelines

### Safety / red flags

> **Red flag:** Sudden LOC drop + Cushing’s signs = emergency herniation pathway.  
> **Red flag:** CSF leak (halo sign / clear drainage) — report; don’t pack nose aggressively.  
> **Red flag:** Dilating pupil on one side after head injury.

### Remember this

Early ICP = LOC change. Late = Cushing’s. Protect airway and cerebral perfusion.

## Topic C — Seizures

### Pathophysiology / concept

Abnormal synchronized electrical discharge. Status epilepticus = prolonged/repeated seizures without recovery — airway and brain injury risk. Causes: epilepsy, missed meds, fever (peds), hyponatremia, hypoglycemia, alcohol withdrawal, brain injury, infection.

### Assessment cues

Aura possible; tonic-clonic motor activity; incontinence; postictal confusion; focal deficits after (Todd’s paralysis themes); automatisms in focal seizures.

### Priority nursing actions

1. Protect from injury — lower to floor if needed, pad, turn side when able.  
2. **Nothing in the mouth.**  
3. Time the seizure; loosen clothing; suction ready for **after**.  
4. Oxygen if ordered/available; don’t restrain forcibly.  
5. Status: call emergency response; benzos per protocol.  
6. After: neuro check, glucose if indicated, document phases, meds as ordered; side-lying recovery.

### Client teaching

- Med adherence (breakthrough seizures often = missed doses).  
- Safety: driving laws vary; swim with buddy; medical ID; cook with care.  
- Triggers: sleep deprivation, alcohol, flashing lights in photosensitive types, illness.  
- Never swim alone; shower > bath if possible for safety.

### Medication hooks

Phenytoin (levels, gum hyperplasia, IV compatibility/cardiac monitoring themes), carbamazepine (levels, blood dyscrasia themes), valproate (liver, platelets), levetiracetam (mood), lamotrigine (rash/SJS teaching), benzos for acute status.

### Remember this

Airway/safety; time it; nothing in mouth; status = emergency meds/airway.

**What to do first:** Protect airway/position, call help, O₂, prepare benzo per protocol, suction for after — **refuse tongue blade**, don’t leave for med room without calling help first.

## Topic D — Meningitis basics

### Concept

Infection/inflammation of meninges. Bacterial is a **medical emergency** and often **droplet** precautions (e.g., N. meningitidis) until adequately treated per protocol. Viral often less fulminant but still serious. Fungal more in immunocompromised.

### Assessment cues

Fever, severe headache, nuchal rigidity, photophobia, altered LOC; Kernig/Brudzinski themes; petechial/purpuric rash with meningococcus (purpura fulminans concern). Infants: bulging fontanel, poor feeding, high-pitched cry — less classic stiff neck.

### Priority actions

- Droplet precautions early when suspected.  
- Blood cultures / LP as ordered when safe; antibiotics promptly — don’t delay for LP if unstable and Abx ordered.  
- Dark quiet room; seizure precautions; monitor ICP signs.  
- Close-contact prophylaxis themes as directed by public health/provider.  
- Fluids carefully; SIADH risk themes in some meningitis.

### Remember this

Droplet + don’t delay antibiotics for bacterial meningitis suspicion. Rash + meningitis signs = scary fast.

## Topic E — Spinal precautions & SCI

### Concept

Protect spinal cord when trauma mechanism suggests injury (falls, MVC, diving). Movement can convert incomplete injury to complete.

### Priority actions

- Manual in-line stabilization / C-collar as trained; log roll with team.  
- ABC first — airway with jaw thrust themes if trauma.  
- Motor/sensory baseline; report loss of function.  
- **Autonomic dysreflexia** in chronic SCI with a lesion **at or above T6** (severe HTN + pounding headache; flushing/sweating above the lesion, pallor/gooseflesh below): sit up with legs lowered, loosen constricting clothing, find the noxious stimulus **below the level of injury** (full or kinked catheter, fecal impaction, pressure injury) — this is hypertensive emergency energy; notify provider and anticipate a rapid-acting antihypertensive if BP does not fall.

### Safety / red flags

> **Red flag:** SCI client with sudden severe headache + BP 210/110 — sit up and find stimulus; don’t lay flat and “observe.”  
> **Red flag:** Neurogenic shock (hypotension + bradycardia after cervical/high thoracic injury) ≠ hypovolemia automatically.

### Remember this

Spine precautions until cleared. Autonomic dysreflexia = sit up + find stimulus.

## Topic F — Myasthenia gravis & Guillain-Barré

### Myasthenia gravis

Autoimmune attack on ACh receptors → fatigable weakness, ptosis, risk of **myasthenic crisis** (airway/breathing failure). Tensilon test historical; now antibody/EMG. Crisis vs cholinergic crisis (overdose of cholinesterase inhibitors) — both can cause weakness; differentiate carefully under protocol (often ICU).

**Nurse focus:** med timing before meals; aspiration risk; respiratory monitoring; infection can trigger crisis; avoid certain meds that worsen MG (aminoglycosides, some beta blockers — verify lists).

### Guillain-Barré

Post-infectious ascending paralysis; autonomic instability; **airway** may fail as paralysis climbs. Often needs ICU monitoring; plasmapheresis/IVIG themes.

**Nurse focus:** serial VC/NIF themes as ordered; prepare for intubation; DVT prophylaxis; fear/communication support (client may be locked-in aware); pain management for paresthesias.

### Remember this

GBS rising weakness → watch breathing capacity. MG crisis = ventilatory emergency potential.

---

## Topic G — Parkinson’s & Alzheimer’s care themes

### Parkinson’s

Dopamine loss → tremor, rigidity, bradykinesia, postural instability. Fall risk; aspiration; med timing (levodopa/carbidopa) critical for “on” periods. Deep brain stimulator teaching if applicable.

### Alzheimer’s / dementia

Safety, redirection, routine, caregiver support; don’t argue delusions aggressively; wandering precautions; cholinesterase inhibitors SE (GI, bradycardia themes); memantine themes.

### Remember this

Fall + aspiration + dignity. Schedule care when Parkinson meds are working.

### MS

Demyelination with relapses/remissions or progressive courses. Fatigue, vision changes (optic neuritis), weakness, ataxia, bladder issues, heat sensitivity (Uhthoff). ## Topic A — Diabetes mellitus & acute crises

### Pathophysiology / concept

Insulin deficiency/resistance → hyperglycemia. Chronic damage to vessels/nerves. Acute crises:

| | **Hypoglycemia** | **DKA** | **HHS** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Who | Any on insulin/secretagogues | Often T1; can be T2 | Often T2 older adults |
| Key lab vibe | Low glucose | High glucose + ketones + anion-gap acidosis | Very high glucose, profound dehydration, usually minimal ketones |
| Mental status | Shaky → confused → seizure/coma | Fruity breath, Kussmaul, abd pain | Profound lethargy/coma risk |

### Assessment cues

**Hypo (adrenergic → neuroglycopenic):** sweat, tremor, hunger, tachycardia → confusion, seizure, coma.  
**Hyper:** polyuria/polydipsia/polyphagia, blurry vision, dehydration.  
**DKA:** Kussmaul respirations, fruity breath, abdominal pain, vomiting.  
**HHS:** extreme dehydration, neuro changes, often precipitating illness.

### Somogyi vs dawn phenomenon (concepts)

Both cause morning hyperglycemia — different “why”:  
- **Dawn:** natural early-morning hormone surge → rising glucose; often needs evening dose adjustment per provider.  
- **Somogyi (rebound) theme:** nocturnal hypo → counter-regulatory rebound hyper; check **3 a.m. glucose** when taught — if low at 3 a.m. then high at breakfast, discuss with provider (may need less evening insulin, not more).  
Don’t memorize contested debates — know that **nighttime checks** guide the plan.

### Priority nursing actions

**Hypoglycemia (conscious, can swallow):** 15-15 rule concept — ~15 g fast carb, recheck ~15 min, repeat as needed; then protein/complex carb per protocol.  
**Unconscious / NPO:** glucagon IM/SQ or IV dextrose per orders — protect airway.  
**DKA/HHS:** ABCs; IV fluids first themes; **check K⁺ before starting insulin — hold insulin and replace potassium first if K⁺ < 3.3 mEq/L** (insulin drives K⁺ intracellularly); insulin infusion per protocol once K⁺ is adequate; continue replacement as ordered once urine output is established (total body K⁺ depleted even if serum looks normal/high initially); add dextrose to fluids as glucose approaches ~200–250 mg/dL so the drip can keep closing the gap; treat precipitating cause; NPO; frequent labs/neuro checks.

### Medications (high-yield)

- Insulins by kinetics (rapid/short/intermediate/long) — **peak = hypo risk window**  
- Never share pens; site rotation; needle safety  
- Oral agents themes: metformin (contrast/lactic caution concepts), sulfonylurea hypo risk, SGLT2 infection/DKA awareness themes — verify current teaching  

### Client teaching — foot care & sick-day rules (fuller)

**Foot care:** inspect tops/soles/between toes daily (mirror/caregiver if needed); wash/dry carefully; moisturize (not between toes); closed leather shoes; seamless socks; never go barefoot; no bathroom surgery on calluses/ingrown nails — podiatry; report non-healing ulcers, redness, drainage.  
**Sick-day:** never stop insulin blindly when ill (stress raises glucose); check glucose more often; check ketones when taught (esp. T1); sip fluids; soft carbs if not eating; call for persistent vomiting, inability to keep fluids, moderate/large ketones, or glucose staying very high per personal parameters; know when to seek ED.  
MedicAlert themes; carry fast sugar.

### Safety / red flags

- Beta blockers may mask hypo tachycardia.  
- Drawing insulin: clear before cloudy when mixing (if mixing still taught); never mix long-acting analogs that shouldn’t be mixed.  
- Hypo unawareness in longstanding diabetes.

### Delegation notes

UAP can bring juice if RN directs after confirming hypo plan; RN interprets glucose patterns and teaches.

### Remember this

- Treat hypo now — brain needs glucose.  
- DKA/HHS: fluids + insulin protocol + potassium vigilance.  
- Peak of insulin = watch for hypo.  
- Sick days ≠ stop insulin; feet are sacred.

## Topic B — Thyroid disorders (including storm vs myxedema)

### Pathophysiology / concept

Thyroid hormones (T3/T4) set metabolic rate. **Hypo** = underactive (Hashimoto common). **Hyper** = overactive (Graves common). Extremes: **myxedema coma** (hypo crisis) and **thyroid storm** (hyper crisis).

### Assessment cues

| Hypothyroid | Hyperthyroid |
| --- | --- |
| Fatigue, cold intolerance, weight gain, bradycardia, constipation, dry skin | Anxiety, heat intolerance, weight loss, tachycardia, diarrhea, tremor |
| Myxedema coma: hypothermia, hypoventilation, brady, altered LOC | Storm: high fever, severe tachy/arrhythmia, delirium — emergency |

### Thyroid storm vs myxedema (contrast)

| | **Thyroid storm** | **Myxedema coma** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Temp | High fever | Hypothermia |
| HR | Severe tachy / arrhythmia | Bradycardia |
| Neuro | Agitation → delirium | Stupor → coma |
| Trigger themes | Infection, surgery, abrupt antithyroid stop | Cold, infection, sedatives, missed levothyroxine |
| Nurse energy | Cooling, O₂, meds per protocol, ICU | Airway, warming, IV hormone per orders, cautious sedation |

### Priority nursing actions

- Hypo: levothyroxine teaching (morning empty stomach themes; don’t stop abruptly); watch for over-replacement. Myxedema: ABCs, warming, IV hormone per orders, cautious sedation.  
- Hyper: cool quiet environment; antithyroid meds/beta blockers as ordered; iodine/surgery/RAI themes. Storm: escalate care, cooling, meds per protocol.  
- Post-thyroidectomy: airway (hematoma/edema), **hypocalcemia** if parathyroids affected (tetany, Chvostek/Trousseau), laryngeal nerve injury (voice/stridor).

### Meds

- Levothyroxine: take consistently; separate from some binders/iron/calcium timing.  
- Methimazole/PTU themes: agranulocytosis teaching (fever/sore throat → report).  
- Beta blockers for symptom control in hyper.

### Client teaching

- Lifelong replacement often needed in hypo.  
- RAI: radiation precautions per instructions (sleep apart, fluid intake, avoid close contact with pregnant/infants for instructed period).  
- Report storm/myxedema warning signs.

### Remember this

Post-op thyroid = airway + calcium. Storm and myxedema are ICU-level emergencies — opposite temperature/HR patterns.

## Topic C — Parathyroid (hyper / hypo)

### Pathophysiology / concept

PTH raises serum calcium (bone, kidney, gut activation themes).  
**Hypoparathyroid** (often post-thyroidectomy): **low Ca²⁺**, high phosphate themes → tetany, laryngospasm risk.  
**Hyperparathyroid:** **high Ca²⁺** → stones, bones, groans, psychic moans; osteoporosis/fracture risk; dehydration.

### Priority nursing actions / teaching

- HypoCa: calcium + vitamin D as ordered; seizure/airway watch; teach early tetany signs.  
- HyperCa: hydration, mobility as tolerated, strain urine if stones; caution digoxin toxicity themes with high Ca²⁺; prepare for surgery if adenoma.

### Remember this

Post-thyroidectomy tingling/spasms = think calcium. High Ca²⁺ = stones and bones.

## Topic D — Adrenal: Addison vs Cushing & steroid teaching

### Pathophysiology / concept

Adrenal cortex: cortisol + aldosterone (and androgens).  
**Addison (insufficiency):** low cortisol/aldosterone → weakness, hypotension, hyperpigmentation (primary), salt craving, **hyperkalemia/hyponatremia** themes; crisis = shock.  
**Cushing (excess cortisol):** central obesity, moon face, buffalo hump, hyperglycemia, infection risk, thin skin, hypertension, mood changes — exogenous steroids common cause.

### Assessment cues

Addison crisis: profound hypotension, fever, hypoglycemia, hyperkalemia themes after stress/stopped steroids.  
Cushing: infection, poor wound healing, fractures risk, hyperglycemia.

### Priority nursing actions

**Addison / crisis:** IV fluids + glucocorticoid replacement per orders; treat hyperkalemia; never abruptly stop chronic steroids.  
**Cushing:** infection precautions; monitor glucose/BP; careful skin care; if surgical cause, post-op adrenal insufficiency risk.

### Steroid teaching (high-yield — expand)

- **Taper** — never stop chronic glucocorticoids cold turkey (adrenal crisis).  
- Take with food; morning dosing often mimics circadian rhythm as taught.  
- **Infection** risk — report fever early; may mask signs.  
- **Glucose** rises — monitor if diabetic.  
- Weight/BP/mood changes; calcium/vit D and fall precautions for bone loss themes.  
- Stress dosing concepts as taught by provider (illness/surgery may need higher doses).  
- Medical ID for adrenal insufficiency.

### Safety / red flags

Abrupt steroid cessation → adrenal crisis. Infection in Cushing can be subtle then severe.

### Remember this

Addison = need hormones + salt/volume in crisis. Cushing = protect from infection/injury. Never stop steroids cold turkey — taper, infection, glucose.

## Topic E — Pituitary basics & SIADH vs DI (endocrine angle)

### Concept

Posterior pituitary releases **ADH** (and oxytocin). Anterior hormones include ACTH, TSH, GH — tumors/surgery can disrupt. After pituitary surgery: watch DI (high UOP), CSF leak themes (clear drainage, halo), neuro status. (See also renal chapter for fluid/Na⁺ table — same physiology, different exam angle.)

### SIADH vs DI (endocrine view)

| | **SIADH** | **DI** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ADH | Too much | Too little / kidneys don’t respond |
| Water | Retained | Lost as dilute urine |
| Na⁺ | Dilutional hyponatremia | Hypernatremia risk |
| Nurse | Fluid restriction as ordered; seizure precautions; slow Na⁺ correction | Replace fluids; desmopressin (central DI) as ordered; strict I&O/weights |

Triggers for SIADH themes: CNS injury, small-cell lung cancer, some meds. DI: head trauma, pituitary surgery, nephrogenic causes.

### Remember this

SIADH = soaked (low Na⁺). DI = high UOP dilute urine. Pituitary post-op → watch UOP and CSF leak cues.

# Chapter 8 — Renal & Fluids/Electrolytes

> **Study aid only.** Exact lab reference ranges vary by laboratory. Know direction and urgency; verify local ranges. Not a dialysis prescription guide.

![Electrolyte imbalance cue map](visuals/electrolyte-imbalance-map.jpg)

![AV fistula arm rules](visuals/av-fistula-arm-rules.jpg)

![Acid-base common causes](visuals/acid-base-common-causes.jpg)

---

## Why it matters on NCLEX

Fluids/electrolytes and renal items sit at the intersection of **cardiac rhythm safety, neuro status, and medication effects**. Hyperkalemia, volume overload, transfusion reactions, and dialysis access problems are frequent “who do you see first?” drivers.

---

## Topic A — Fluids & electrolytes

### Pathophysiology / concept

Water follows sodium; potassium drives cardiac conduction; calcium drives neuromuscular stability; magnesium often rides with K⁺ issues.

### Assessment cues (high-level)

| Imbalance | Classic cues |
| --- | --- |
| **Hyponatremia** | Confusion, seizures (severe), headache |
| **Hypernatremia** | Thirst, dry mucosa, irritability/neuro changes |
| **Hypokalemia** | Weakness, dysrhythmias, U-wave themes, dig toxicity risk ↑ |
| **Hyperkalemia** | Peaked T waves, weakness, bradydysrhythmias — **emergency potential** |
| **Hypocalcemia** | Tetany, Chvostek/Trousseau, laryngospasm risk |
| **Hypercalcemia** | “Bones, stones, groans, psychic moans,” weakness |
| **Hypomagnesemia** | Torsades risk themes, reflexes ↑, accompanies low K⁺/Ca²⁺ |

### Fluid volume excess vs deficit (comparison)

| | **Fluid volume deficit** | **Fluid volume excess** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| VS vibe | Tachycardia, hypotension, weak pulses | HTN (often), bounding pulses |
| Respiratory | Clear unless weak/aspiration | Crackles, dyspnea, ↑ RR |
| Other | Dry mucosa, oliguria, poor turgor, weight loss | Edema, JVD, weight gain, S3 themes |
| Causes themes | Bleed, vomit/diarrhea, burns, DI, under-resuscitation | HF, renal failure, SIADH, iatrogenic IV fluids |
| Nurse focus | Replace per orders; treat cause; orthostatics | Restrict fluids/Na⁺ as ordered; diuretics; elevate HOB; daily weights |

Daily weights = best chronic volume tracker (same scale, same time, same clothes themes).

### Priority nursing actions

1. Telemetry when K⁺/Mg²⁺ deranged.  
2. Replace or remove electrolytes **per orders** — IV K⁺ never IV push; controlled infusion only.  
3. Seizure precautions for severe Na⁺ shifts; correct Na⁺ carefully (over-rapid correction risks demyelination themes).  
4. Hyperkalemia emergency themes: protect heart (calcium as ordered), shift K⁺ (insulin+glucose, beta agonists as ordered), remove K⁺ (binders, dialysis).

### Client teaching

- Diuretic diet: K⁺ rich vs avoid depending on drug.  
- Fluid restriction adherence when ordered (HF/SIADH/renal).  
- Report weight gains (~2–3 lb in a day or ~5 lb in a week themes — follow personal parameters).

### Safety / red flags

- IV potassium push = never.  
- Peaked T waves → escalate.  
- Rapid Na⁺ swings.

### Remember this

Treat patient + trend. K⁺ emergencies are cardiac emergencies. Weights win for volume.

## Topic B — Acid-base (integrated)

### Shortcut

1. Look at **pH**  
2. Look at **PaCO₂** (respiratory) and **HCO₃⁻** (metabolic)  
3. Match the one that explains pH → primary  
4. Other may compensate  

| Pattern | Example drivers |
| --- | --- |
| Resp acidosis | Hypoventilation, COPD, oversedation |
| Resp alkalosis | Hyperventilation, anxiety, PE early |
| Met acidosis | DKA, lactic shock, diarrhea |
| Met alkalosis | Vomiting, NG suction, diuretic themes |

### Remember this

Fix the cause (ventilate, perfuse, insulin for DKA, etc.).

---

## Topic C — AKI & CKD; nephrotic vs nephritic

### Pathophysiology / concept

**AKI:** sudden drop in GFR — prerenal (perfusion), intrinsic (ATN/nephritis), postrenal (obstruction).  
**CKD:** progressive irreversible loss; stages by GFR; complications = fluid overload, hyperkalemia, anemia, mineral bone disease, uremia, acidosis.

### Nephrotic vs nephritic (themes)

| | **Nephrotic** | **Nephritic** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hallmark vibe | Massive proteinuria → hypoalbuminemia → edema | Inflammation → hematuria (“cola” urine), HTN, oliguria |
| Other | Hyperlipidemia themes; infection/clot risk | RBC casts themes; rising Cr |
| Nurse | Skin care, infection watch, nutrition as ordered, anticoag awareness if ordered | BP control, fluid/Na⁺ management, monitor renal function |

### Assessment cues (AKI/CKD)

Oliguria/anuria, rising creatinine/BUN, hyperkalemia, metabolic acidosis, edema/HTN, uremic frost/encephalopathy late, pericarditis themes late.

### Priority nursing actions

- Map cause: fluids for prerenal if ordered; relieve obstruction; avoid nephrotoxins (NSAIDs, aminoglycosides themes).  
- Monitor I&O, daily weight, K⁺, ECG.  
- Renal diet themes: often protein/K⁺/phos/Na⁺ modifications as ordered.  
- Prepare for dialysis if refractory overload/K⁺/acidosis/uremia.  
- CKD: EPO/iron as ordered; phosphate binders **with meals**; HTN/diabetes control.

### Meds caution

- Adjust renally cleared drugs.  
- ACEI/ARB: renal protective long-term but monitor K⁺/Cr.  
- Contrast precautions (hydration as ordered).

### Client teaching

- No NSAIDs casually; med list review.  
- Fistula care if dialysis-bound.  
- Fluid/diet adherence.

### Remember this

Protect kidneys from hypoperfusion and toxins. Nephrotic = protein/edema; nephritic = blood/HTN. Hyperkalemia and volume overload drive urgency.

---

## Topic D — UTI, pyelonephritis & kidney stones

### UTI / pyelo

Lower UTI: dysuria, frequency, urgency, suprapubic discomfort.  
**Pyelonephritis:** flank pain, fever, chills, N/V — systemic; can seed sepsis.  
**Priorities:** cultures before antibiotics when possible; fluids as ordered; analgésics/antipyretics; teach wipe front-to-back, void after sex, don’t hold urine, finish antibiotics; catheter prevention (sterile insert, early removal).

### Kidney stones (urolithiasis)

Severe colicky flank pain radiating to groin, hematuria, N/V, can’t sit still.  
**Nurse:** pain control, strain all urine, hydration as ordered, ambulation, filter for stone analysis; infection + obstruction = emergency energy (fever + unrelieved pain). Teaching: fluid intake goals as ordered; diet depends on stone type (don’t invent one diet for all).

### Remember this

Fever + flank pain = pyelo/sepsis watch. Stones = pain + strain + fluids; infection with obstruction escalates.

---

## Topic E — BPH & TURP irrigation / clots

### Concept

**BPH:** prostate enlargement → urinary retention, weak stream, nocturia, overflow incontinence themes. Meds: alpha-blockers (orthostatic hypotension teaching), 5-ARI themes (sexual SE, pregnant partner handling precautions for crushed tabs).

### TURP continuous bladder irrigation (CBI)

Goal: keep catheter patent and urine pink-to-clear; prevent clot retention.  
**Nurse:** hang irrigant (usually isotonic sterile) as ordered; adjust rate so output stays without bright red clots/obstruction; measure true urine = output minus irrigant in; watch for **hyponatremia/TURP syndrome** themes (confusion, N/V, HTN or bradycardia patterns) with absorption of irrigant — report neuro changes.  
If outflow blocked (bladder spasms, no drip out): check kinks; hand-irrigate per protocol/orders only; notify if large clots persist. Secure catheter; traction as ordered; analgesics/antispasmodics as ordered.

### Remember this

CBI: pink and flowing. Bright red + clots + spasms = patency emergency. Confusion post-TURP → think TURP syndrome / Na⁺.

---

## Topic F — Dialysis nursing priorities

### Hemodialysis concepts

Blood cleaned via dialyzer; access via AV fistula/graft or catheter. Complications: hypotension, disequilibrium themes, bleed from heparin, infection, cramps.

### Fistula care rules (classic NCLEX)

- **No BP, no sticks, no tight clothes** on access arm.  
- Feel **thrill**, hear **bruit** — report absence.  
- Post-dialysis: watch bleeding, VS, hold certain meds until after session per orders (many antihypertensives held pre-dialysis).

### Peritoneal dialysis & peritonitis signs

Catheter in abdomen; dwell/drain. **Peritonitis:** cloudy effluent, abdominal pain, fever, rebound — report ASAP; save bag for culture; antibiotics as ordered (often intraperitoneal themes); sterile technique with exchanges; teach client asepsis at home. Other issues: incomplete drain (position change), protein loss, hyperglycemia from dialysate dextrose themes.

### Remember this

No BP/sticks on fistula arm. Cloudy PD fluid = infection until proven otherwise.

## Topic G — SIADH vs diabetes insipidus (high-yield contrast)

| | **SIADH** | **DI** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Problem | Too much ADH → water retention | Too little ADH effect → water loss |
| Na⁺ vibe | Dilutional **hyponatremia** | **Hypernatremia** risk |
| Urine | Concentrated / low output | Large volumes dilute urine |
| Nurse focus | Fluid restriction as ordered; seizure precautions; careful Na⁺ correction | Replace fluids; desmopressin as ordered; monitor UOP/weight |

(Endocrine chapter covers pituitary/surgical angle — same ADH story.)

### Remember this

SIADH = soaked inside (low Na⁺). DI = dry pipes (high UOP, high Na⁺ risk).

## Topic H — IV fluid types, IV complications & transfusions

### IV fluid types (conceptual)

- **Isotonic** (NS, LR): volume expansion — watch overload.  
- **Hypotonic** (0.45% NaCl): pushes water into cells — avoid in ICP risk.  
- **Hypertonic** (3% NaCl): pulls water from cells — ICU-level monitoring for severe hyponatremia.  
- Colloids/blood: follow transfusion rights and reactions protocol.

### IV complications

| Problem | Cues | Nurse actions (themes) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Infiltration** | Cool, pale, swollen site; damp dressing | Stop IV; remove per policy; elevate; warm/cold per fluid type/policy; restart elsewhere |
| **Phlebitis** | Red, warm, streak, tender cord | Stop/remove; warm compress themes; restart other arm; culture if ordered |
| **Extravasation** | Vesicant leak — burning, blistering, necrosis risk | **Stop**; don’t flush; aspirate residual if policy; antidote/protocol; notify; mark area |
| **CLABSI risk** | Fever, chills with central line | Sterile care; scrub hub; report |

### Blood transfusion reactions — deeper algorithm

1. **Stop transfusion immediately** — keep IV line open with **NS** (new tubing themes per policy).  
2. Stay with client; VS; notify provider **and** blood bank.  
3. Recheck identifiers; save blood bag/tubing for lab per policy — don’t casually discard.  
4. Support ABCs; meds (antihistamine, epinephrine, diuretics) as ordered by reaction type.  
5. Document; collect urine if hemolysis suspected (hemoglobinuria).

| Reaction vibe | Cues | Energy |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Acute hemolytic | Fever, flank/chest pain, hypotension, hemoglobinuria (ABO mismatch) | Emergency — stop, support, blood bank |
| Febrile non-hemolytic | Fever, chills, headache | Stop, antipyretic as ordered |
| Allergic | Urticaria, itching ± anaphylaxis | Stop; antihistamine/epi as ordered |
| TACO | Dyspnea, crackles, HTN during/after volume | Stop/slow; sit up; diuretic as ordered |
| TRALI themes | Acute dyspnea/hypoxia, non-cardiogenic pulmonary edema vibe | Stop; O₂/ventilatory support; report |

Two-nurse check, baseline VS, stay first 15 minutes themes — follow facility.

### Remember this

Match fluid tonicity to the problem. Infiltration ≠ extravasation (vesicants escalate). Transfusion reaction → **stop first**, NS, notify, save bag.

# Chapter 9 — GI & Hepatic

> **Study aid only.** GI bleed and liver failure management follow facility protocols — verify. Not endoscopic procedure guidance.

![GI bleed ABC priority flow](visuals/gi-bleed-abc-flow.jpg)

---

## Why it matters on NCLEX

GI/hepatic stems mix **ABCs (bleed, airway in hematemesis), neuro (encephalopathy), and fluid/electrolyte** judgment. Pancreatitis, obstruction, gallbladder disease, and feeding-tube safety add pain + shock + aspiration risk. Expect priority, SATA, and “what do you teach?” items.

---

## Topic A — GI bleed

### Pathophysiology / concept

Upper (PUD, varices, Mallory-Weiss, erosive gastritis) vs lower (diverticular, angiodysplasia, colitis, hemorrhoids). Blood loss → hypovolemic shock. **Esophageal varices** in portal hypertension can bleed torrentially — treat as ABC emergency until proven otherwise. Melena often points upper; bright hematochezia may be lower *or* brisk upper.

### Assessment cues

**Early:** restlessness, thirst, orthostatic dizziness, mild tachycardia.  
**Progressing:** hematemesis, coffee-ground emesis, melena, hematochezia; cool clammy skin; oliguria; syncope.  
**Late / shock:** hypotension, altered LOC, metabolic acidosis themes. Hgb drop **lags** acute bleed — don’t wait for labs if the client is crashing.

### Priority nursing actions

1. ABCs — airway if vomiting blood; suction ready; NPO.  
2. Two large-bore IVs; fluid/blood products as ordered; type & cross.  
3. Continuous VS; stay with unstable client.  
4. Notify provider; prepare for endoscopy / balloon tamponade themes as ordered.  
5. Proton pump inhibitor infusion themes as ordered; **octreotide** for varices as ordered.  
6. Avoid NSAIDs; reverse anticoags if ordered; bleeding precautions.

### Esophageal varices bleeding priorities

- Volume + airway first; don’t leave client alone.  
- Avoid straining, vomiting when possible (antiemetics as ordered); soft foods only when cleared.  
- No blind NG placement in known varices unless ordered by experienced provider — facility rules apply.  
- After bleed: lactulose if encephalopathy risk; alcohol cessation teaching; beta-blocker prophylaxis themes as ordered.

### Safety / red flags

- Bright red hematemesis + hypotension  
- Known cirrhosis + bleed = varices until proven otherwise  
- Rising HR before BP falls — early hypovolemia cue

### Remember this

Volume and airway first. Don’t feed an unstable bleeder. Varices = torrential bleed potential.

---

## Topic B — GERD, PUD & H. pylori

### Pathophysiology / concept

**GERD:** LES relaxation → acid reflux → esophagitis, Barrett risk long-term, aspiration risk if severe.  
**PUD:** mucosal break in stomach/duodenum — *H. pylori* and NSAIDs are classic drivers; complications = bleed, perforation, obstruction (pyloric).

### Assessment cues

GERD: heartburn, regurgitation, worse after meals/lying flat, chronic cough/hoarseness themes.  
PUD: epigastric pain — duodenal often relieved by food; gastric may worsen with food; melena/hematemesis if bleeding; rigid abdomen if perforation.

### Priority nursing actions / teaching

- Elevate HOB; small meals; avoid late-night eating, alcohol, caffeine, peppermint, chocolate, fatty foods as individually trigger-linked.  
- Sit upright after meals; smoking cessation.  
- PPIs/H2 blockers as ordered — take PPI before meals per teaching.  
- **H. pylori:** complete full antibiotic + PPI regimen (adherence prevents resistance/relapse).  
- NSAID avoidance or gastroprotection per orders; report black stools / severe sudden pain.

### Safety / red flags

Sudden severe abdominal pain + board-like abdomen after PUD history = perforation → surgical emergency energy.

### Remember this

GERD = lifestyle + acid suppression. PUD bleed/perforation = ABCs and escalate. Finish *H. pylori* therapy.

---

## Topic C — Pancreatitis

### Pathophysiology / concept

Autodigestion of pancreas (gallstones/alcohol common). Cytokine storm → third-spacing, ARDS, hypocalcemia, hyperglycemia risk. Severity ranges from mild edematous to necrotizing.

### Assessment cues

**Early:** severe epigastric pain radiating to back, N/V, worse lying flat.  
**Progressing:** fever, tachycardia, volume depletion.  
**Concerning:** Cullen/Grey-Turner rare late; ↑ lipase/amylase themes; hypocalcemia tetany; dyspnea (ARDS).

### Priority nursing actions

- NPO; aggressive IV fluids as ordered; pain control (often opioids).  
- Antiemetics; glucose monitor; electrolyte replace.  
- Position for comfort (fetal/side-lying).  
- Watch ARDS/shock; ERCP themes if gallstone etiology.  
- Advance diet slowly when pain/enzymes improve per orders; jejunal feeding themes sometimes used.

### Teaching

Alcohol cessation; low-fat diet when restarted; gallstone follow-up; report severe recurrent pain.

### Remember this

Rest the pancreas (NPO), fluids, pain control, watch calcium and lungs.

---

## Topic D — Cholecystitis & biliary colic

### Pathophysiology / concept

Gallstones (cholelithiasis) obstruct cystic duct → inflammation (**cholecystitis**). Fat intolerance common. Risk themes: “fair, fat, fertile, forty” is a mnemonic — not destiny. Choledocholithiasis / ascending cholangitis = fever, jaundice, RUQ pain (Charcot themes) — escalate.

### Assessment cues

RUQ / epigastric pain radiating to right shoulder/scapula, especially after fatty meal; N/V; fever; positive Murphy-type pain themes; dark urine/clay stools if CBD obstructed.

### Priority nursing actions

- NPO if acute; IV fluids; analgesia; antiemetics; antibiotics as ordered.  
- Prepare for cholecystectomy (lap themes); post-op: early ambulation, shoulder gas pain teaching, low-fat diet progression.  
- T-tube care if present: drainage character/amount; keep bag below insertion; report sudden cessation or bilious peritonitis signs.

### Remember this

RUQ after fatty meal = biliary until proven otherwise. Fever + jaundice + pain = cholangitis concern — escalate.

---

## Topic E — Appendicitis vs peritonitis

### Pathophysiology / concept

Appendicitis: lumen obstruction → inflammation → possible rupture → **peritonitis**. Peritonitis = peritoneal inflammation from perforation, trauma, or spontaneous (ascites) — fluid shifts and sepsis risk.

### Assessment cues

**Appendicitis early:** periumbilical pain → migrates to RLQ (McBurney); anorexia, low-grade fever, rebound/guarding.  
**Rupture / peritonitis:** pain may briefly ease then diffuse; rigid board-like abdomen, rebound, fever, tachycardia, absent bowel sounds, shock.

### Priority nursing actions

- NPO; IV access; no heating pads to abdomen (may ↑ perforation risk conceptually).  
- Avoid laxatives/enemas if appendicitis suspected.  
- Pain control after assessment baseline; prep for surgery.  
- Post-rupture: antibiotics, drains, sepsis vigilance.

### Safety / red flags

Sudden relief of appendicitis pain can mean rupture — not “cured.” Rigid abdomen + fever = escalate now.

### Remember this

RLQ migratory pain = appendicitis pattern. Rigid abdomen = peritonitis emergency vibe.

---

## Topic F — Liver failure, ascites & hepatic encephalopathy

### Pathophysiology / concept

Liver fails to synthesize clotting factors/albumin, detoxify ammonia, handle bilirubin → coagulopathy, ascites/edema, jaundice, encephalopathy. Portal hypertension → varices/ascites. **Ascites** = fluid in peritoneum; large-volume **paracentesis** can drop BP — albumin as ordered.

### Assessment cues

Jaundice, ascites, spider angiomas, bruising/bleeding, asterixis, confusion/coma, fetor hepaticus themes; ↑ INR, ↑ bili, ↓ albumin, ↑ ammonia themes.

### Priority nursing actions

1. Neuro checks; safety for confused clients.  
2. Lactulose as ordered — goal is improved mentation / stooling per parameters.  
3. Low-protein only if specifically ordered in acute encephalopathy (nutrition has nuance — follow orders).  
4. Bleeding precautions; soft toothbrush; fall risk.  
5. Ascites: sodium restriction, diuretics (spironolactone ± loop), measure abdominal girth/daily weight.  
6. Paracentesis assist: void first if ordered; baseline VS; watch post-tap hypotension; sterile field; send fluid if infection suspected.  
7. Avoid hepatotoxic meds/alcohol; dose-adjust drugs.

### Meds (high-yield)

- Lactulose / rifaximin themes for encephalopathy  
- Vitamin K / FFP/blood products as ordered for bleed  
- Spironolactone ± loop for ascites  

### Client teaching

- Why lactulose matters (trap ammonia via stooling).  
- No alcohol; med review; infection reporting (**SBP** risk with ascites — fever + abd pain).  
- Skin care with jaundice/pruritus.

### Safety / red flags

- Sudden confusion in cirrhotic = encephalopathy until proven otherwise  
- GI bleed in cirrhotic  
- Fever + ascites = possible spontaneous bacterial peritonitis  

### Remember this

Encephalopathy → lactulose + safety. Cirrhosis = bleed risk. Protect airway if obtunded. Post-paracentesis = watch BP.

---

## Topic G — Bowel obstruction

### Pathophysiology / concept

Mechanical (adhesions, hernia, tumor, volvulus) or paralytic ileus. Proximal dilation, vomiting, fluid shifts, ischemia/perforation risk → peritonitis.

### Assessment cues

Crampy pain, distention, vomiting (feculent if distal), high-pitched → absent bowel sounds progression, obstipation; XR/CT air-fluid levels themes.

### Priority nursing actions

- NPO; NG decompression as ordered.  
- IV fluids/electrolytes; pain control; antiemetics.  
- Monitor for peritonitis (rigid abdomen, rebound, fever) → surgical emergency energy.  
- Ambulation for ileus prevention post-op when appropriate.

### Remember this

NPO + decompress + fluids; peritonitis signs = escalate now.

---

## Topic H — Inflammatory bowel, diverticulitis, dumping & ostomy

### Ulcerative colitis vs Crohn’s (pattern)

UC: continuous colon, bloody diarrhea. Crohn’s: skip lesions anywhere, fistulas possible. Both: dehydration, anemia, infection risk with immunosuppressants/biologics.

### Diverticulitis

LLQ pain, fever; NPO/clear liquids as ordered; antibiotics; avoid barium/colonoscopy in acute flare per orders; watch perforation/abscess. Later: high-fiber teaching when stable (acute phase often low-fiber/NPO first).

### Dumping syndrome (post-gastrectomy high-yield)

Rapid gastric emptying → hyperosmolar load → fluid shift + reactive hypo themes.  
**Cues:** diaphoresis, tachycardia, dizziness, diarrhea soon after meals; late hypoglycemia possible.  
**Teaching:** small frequent meals; separate liquids from solids; high protein/fat, lower simple carbs; lie down after meals as taught; avoid extreme sweets.

### Ostomy care basics

- Stoma should be **pink/red and moist** — pale/dusky/black = perfusion emergency.  
- Empty pouch when ~1/3–1/2 full; skin barrier integrity; measure wafer to fit closely.  
- Ileostomy: liquid output, dehydration/electrolyte risk; colostomy: more formed distally.  
- Psychosocial support; diet trial-and-error; gas/odor management teaching.

### Priority nursing actions (IBD flare)

## Topic J — TPN & enteral feeding safety

### Enteral (tube feeding)

- Confirm placement per policy before each use (x-ray initial; mark tube; residual/pH per protocol — follow facility).

### TPN (parenteral)

## Topic A — Fractures, compartment syndrome, osteoporosis & hip fracture

### Pathophysiology / concept

Bone break → bleeding, soft-tissue swelling. Casts/edema in closed fascial compartments → rising pressure → ischemia of muscle/nerve (**compartment syndrome**) — time-critical limb threat. Fat embolism risk after long-bone/pelvic fractures (esp. young adults).  
**Osteoporosis:** low bone mass → fragility fractures (hip, spine, wrist). **Hip fracture** in older adults: fall risk, delirium, DVT/pneumonia/skin breakdown post-op.

### Assessment cues

Fracture: pain, deformity, loss of function, crepitus, swelling.  
**Compartment (6 Ps themes):** Pain out of proportion / pain on passive stretch (early), Pressure, Paresthesia, Pallor, Paralysis (late), Pulselessness (**late** — don’t wait for it).  
Fat embolism: respiratory distress, neuro changes, petechiae themes after fracture.  
Hip fracture: shortened externally rotated leg themes; groin/hip pain; can’t bear weight.

### Priority nursing actions

1. Immobilize; neurovascular checks distal to injury **frequently**.  
2. Elevate per orders (but if compartment suspected, elevation may be limited — follow protocol; notify provider **immediately**).  
3. Ice as ordered; pain control; tetanus status.  
4. Open fracture: cover with sterile dressing; antibiotics as ordered.  
5. Traction care: weights hang free; alignment; skin/pin care.  
6. Fat embolism: O₂, support ABCs, notify provider.  
7. Hip fracture: ABCs, pain, immobilize as ordered; post-op: abduction pillow themes per prosthesis type, NV checks, DVT prophylaxis, early PT as ordered, delirium watch, skin care; don’t flex hip acutely beyond ordered limits for some approaches.

### Osteoporosis teaching

Weight-bearing exercise as cleared; calcium/vit D per plan; fall prevention (home hazards, vision, meds); bisphosphonate teaching themes (upright after dose, water, esophageal irritation); smoking/alcohol reduction.

### Client teaching (casts)

Keep dry; don’t stick objects inside; report numbness/color change/increased pain; mobility/weight-bearing as ordered; DVT prevention.

### Safety / red flags

Pain out of proportion after cast/fracture = compartment until proven otherwise. Pulseless is late. Sudden dyspnea after long-bone fracture = fat embolism concern.

### Delegation notes

UAP can report client complaints of numbness; RN performs NV assessment.

### Remember this

Compartment = pain out of proportion + passive stretch pain → call now. Hip fracture = NV + DVT + delirium + positioning rules.

| --- | --- | --- |

### Remember this

OA = use-related; RA = autoimmune systemic + infection risk on immunosuppressants. Gout = hydrate + med adherence + flare vs prevention teaching.

## Topic C — Immuno / oncology, SLE, chemo/radiation safety

### Pathophysiology / concept

Chemo/radiation suppress marrow → **neutropenia** (infection risk), thrombocytopenia (bleed), anemia (fatigue/O₂ carry). Oncologic emergencies: febrile neutropenia, tumor lysis, SVC syndrome, spinal cord compression (high-level recognition).  
**SLE (lupus):** autoimmune multisystem — butterfly rash themes, photosensitivity, arthritis, renal/CNS involvement possible; flares with sun/stress/infection; immunosuppressant infection risk.

### Assessment cues

Fever in neutropenic client = emergency energy even if they “look okay.” Mucositis, cough, dysuria may be muted. ANC low. SLE: new edema/HTN (renal), chest pain, neuro changes — escalate.

### Priority nursing actions

**Neutropenic precautions themes:** private room when indicated; hand hygiene; avoid sick visitors/fresh flowers per policy; no rectal temps; meticulous oral care; cultures + prompt antibiotics for fever as ordered.  
Soft toothbrush; avoid invasive procedures when possible; bleed precautions if platelets low.  
Tumor lysis: hydration, allopurinol/rasburicase themes, monitor K⁺/phos/uric acid/Ca²⁺.

### Chemo / radiation safety for nurses

- Chemo: PPE per policy for mixing/admin/disposal; don’t crush chemo casually; spill kits; pregnant staff often restricted from handling — follow policy.  
- Extravasation: know vesicant protocols — stop infusion, don’t just “flush harder,” follow antidote/protocol.  
- Radiation: time, distance, shielding; cluster care; dosimeter when required; sealed source — tongs/lead container themes; body fluids may have restrictions after some therapies — follow radiation safety officer rules.  
- Skin: don’t wash off markings; gentle skin care; report moist desquamation.

### Skin cancer ABCDE teaching

**A**symmetry, **B**order irregularity, **C**olor variation, **D**iameter (concerning if growing / often taught >6 mm themes), **E**volving — report changing moles; sun protection (SPF, shade, no tanning beds).

### Client teaching

- Report fever immediately (exact threshold per oncology team).  
- Food safety; avoid crowds when counts nadir.  
- Electric razor; avoid contact sports if platelets low.  
- SLE: sunscreen, med adherence, report infection/flare cues, contraception counseling if teratogenic meds.

### Safety / red flags

Febrile neutropenia. Uncontrolled chemo nausea → dehydration. New back pain + leg weakness in cancer client (cord compression). Facial/arm swelling + cancer (SVC).

### Remember this

Fever + neutropenia = urgent antibiotics pathway. Chemo = PPE + extravasation know-how. ABCDE for skin lesions. SLE = sun + infection + organ flare watch.

## Topic D — Integument, pressure injuries, wound healing & burns

### Pressure injury staging (review)

- **Stage 1:** non-blanchable erythema, intact skin.  
- **Stage 2:** partial-thickness loss with exposed viable pink/red dermis; intact or ruptured serum-filled blister. Does **not** include skin tears, burns, abrasions, or moisture-associated skin damage.  
- **Stage 3:** full-thickness skin loss; fat may show; no exposed bone/tendon/muscle.  
- **Stage 4:** exposed bone/tendon/muscle.  
- **Unstageable:** base obscured by eschar/slough.  
- **DTPI:** deep maroon/purple discoloration themes.  
Never reverse-stage (“Stage 4 healing” not “Stage 2”). Turn q2h themes; offload heels; nutrition (protein/calories); moisture management; support surfaces.

### Wound healing factors

Favor: perfusion, nutrition, glycemic control, moist wound healing as ordered, clean technique. Impair: smoking, steroids, infection, ischemia, malnutrition, edema, radiation.

### Burns — pathophysiology / concept

Burns destroy skin barrier → fluid loss, infection, heat loss. Depth: superficial → partial → full thickness. Rule of nines themes for TBSA adult. Capillary leak early → hypovolemia; then fluid remobilization later.

### Assessment cues (burns)

Airway: facial burns, singed nasal hair, hoarseness, carbonaceous sputum → **inhalation injury**. Circumferential chest burns → ventilation restriction. Pain severe in partial thickness; full thickness may be insensate.

### Priority nursing actions (burns)

1. **Airway** first — early intubation themes if inhalation suspected.  
2. Stop burning process; remove constricting jewelry/clothing if not stuck.  
3. Fluid resuscitation per protocol (Parkland-style concepts — large burns need aggressive LR themes as ordered).  
4. Warm environment; sterile/clean dressing per protocol; tetanus.  
5. Pain control; monitor UOP as resuscitation endpoint theme (~0.5 mL/kg/hr adults commonly cited — confirm orders).  
6. Infection prevention; nutrition (hypermetabolic).  
7. Circumferential limb: watch compartment; escharotomy as provider procedure.

### Client teaching / rehab themes

Positioning to prevent contractures; PT/OT; sun protection of healed skin; psychosocial support.

### Safety / red flags

Hoarse voice after smoke exposure. Drooling/stridor. Circumferential torso burn with rising ventilatory pressures.

### Remember this

Burns: airway > fluids > infection/pain/contractures. Pressure injuries: stage accurately, offload, nutrition. Don’t reverse-stage.

## Topic E — Hematology: anemia, sickle cell, HIT, DIC

### Anemia concept

↓ RBC/Hgb → fatigue, pallor, dyspnea on exertion, tachycardia. Causes: blood loss, hemolysis, production failure (iron/B12/folate, marrow).

### Sickle cell crisis priorities

Vaso-occlusive pain crises, infection risk (functional asplenia themes), acute chest syndrome (chest pain, fever, hypoxia — emergency energy), stroke risk.  
**Priorities:** fluids, opioids (believe the pain), O₂ if hypoxic, treat infection triggers, incentive spirometry themes, warm environment, hydroxyurea teaching themes as ordered; avoid high altitude/dehydration extremes as taught.

### HIT (heparin-induced thrombocytopenia) high-level

Immune reaction to heparin → **platelet drop** with **paradoxical thrombosis** risk. Stop all heparin (including flushes) as ordered; use alternative anticoagulation per protocol; avoid platelet transfusions routinely in HIT (can worsen thrombosis — follow specialist orders). Timing often 5–10 days after heparin, or sooner if re-exposed.

### DIC overview

Widespread clotting → consumption of platelets/factors → **bleeding + clotting together**. Triggers: sepsis, trauma, abruptio, malignancy. Cues: oozing from sites, petechiae, ↑ PT/PTT themes, ↓ platelets/fibrinogen, ↑ D-dimer themes.  
**Nurse:** treat cause; support ABCs; blood products as ordered; avoid unnecessary sticks; gentle care — don’t invent heparin vs antifibrinolytic battles without orders.

### Priority actions (anemia)

O₂ if hypoxic; transfusion as ordered; conserve energy; treat cause.

### Remember this

HIT = stop heparin, watch clots. Sickle = hydration + pain + O₂ + infection vigilance + acute chest. DIC = bleed + clot; treat cause. Anemia = cause + symptoms + transfusion per orders.

## HIV opportunistic infection themes (not encyclopedia)

Focus on **immunity down → opportunistic risk up**: pneumonia themes (including PCP when CD4 low — know “report fever/dyspnea early”), oral thrush, TB exposure caution, live vaccine caution when immunocompromised. ART adherence prevents resistance; standard precautions (not isolation solely for HIV status). Teach: safer sex, no sharing needles, infection reporting, medication timing.

### Remember this

HIV nursing = adherence + infection vigilance + dignity + standard precautions; CD4/viral load trends matter.

## Topic A — Antepartum essentials

### Pathophysiology / concept

Pregnancy is a high-flow, high-volume state. Screening catches preeclampsia, GDM, anemia, infections. **Placenta previa** (painless bleeding, placenta over os) vs **abruption** (painful bleeding, board-like uterus) are classic contrasts. Ectopic pregnancy (first trimester pain + bleeding + shoulder pain themes) is a rupture risk.

### Assessment cues — prenatal danger signs (teach these)

- Vaginal bleeding  
- Severe headache, visual changes, epigastric/RUQ pain (preeclampsia)  
- Decreased fetal movement  
- Signs of preterm labor (regular contractions, pelvic pressure, fluid leak)  
- Dysuria / fever  
- Sudden swelling of face/hands  
- Persistent vomiting with dehydration (hyperemesis complications)

### Priority nursing actions

- Accurate BP, urine protein themes, weight trends.  
- Rh-negative clients: Rho(D) immune globulin timing concepts (28 weeks / after sensitizing events / postpartum if baby Rh+).  
- GDM teaching: glucose monitoring, diet, insulin if ordered; fetal surveillance themes.  
- Bleeding: assess amount, pain, VS; NPO; IV access; **no vaginal exams in suspected previa** until localization known.  
- Kick counts teaching as ordered; when to call.

### Medication / treatment hooks

Prenatal vitamins / folate (NTD prevention early); iron for anemia (take with vitamin C themes; constipation teaching); avoid ACEI/ARB/teratogens — verify every drug with current pregnancy labeling.

### Remember this

Painless bleeding → think previa (don’t do vaginal exam). Painful rigid uterus → abruption energy.

## Topic B — Labor & fetal monitoring

### Stages (nursing priorities)

| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Dilation | Comfort, hydration, monitor fetus/contractions, progress |
| 2 | Pushing → birth | Coach pushing, prepare for delivery, perineal support themes |
| 3 | Placenta | Watch separation signs, oxytocin as ordered, blood loss |
| 4 | Recovery (1–2 h) | Fundus, lochia, VS, bonding, hemorrhage vigilance |

### Fetal heart patterns

| Pattern | Meaning vibe | First moves |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Accelerations | Generally reassuring | Continue surveillance |
| Early decelerations | Head compression — often benign mirror | Observe; document |
| Variable decelerations | Cord compression | Reposition; amnioinfusion as ordered |
| Late decelerations | Uteroplacental insufficiency | **Intervene now** |

### Priority actions for nonreassuring patterns (intrauterine resuscitation)

Left lateral (or other position off vena cava), O₂ per protocol, stop oxytocin per protocol, IV bolus as ordered, notify provider, prepare for expedited delivery if unresolved. Check for tachysystole.

### Meds

- Oxytocin: titration for labor; watch tachysystole and late decels — **stop per protocol** when fetus in trouble.  
- Epidural: prehydrate as ordered; watch hypotension (left lateral, fluids, ephedrine/phenylephrine as ordered); bladder catheterization themes.  
- Opioids in labor: watch for neonatal respiratory depression at birth — priority is airway positioning and **positive-pressure ventilation per NRP**. Naloxone is **not** part of initial neonatal resuscitation and is avoided in infants of opioid-dependent mothers (seizure risk).

### Remember this

Late decels → act, don’t just document. Stop Pitocin per protocol when fetus is in trouble.

## Topic C — Postpartum hemorrhage (PPH)

### Pathophysiology / concept

**4 Ts:** Tone (atony — most common), Trauma (lacerations), Tissue (retained placenta), Thrombin (coagulopathy). Atony = boggy fundus. Risk: multiparity, macrosomia, twins, prolonged labor, magnesium, chorio, prior PPH.

### Assessment cues

Saturating pads rapidly, boggy uterus, clots, hypotension/tachycardia, anxiety; may underestimate with slow steady loss — **quantify** (QBL themes). Fundus displaced up/right → full bladder.

### Priority nursing actions

1. Call for help / stay with client.  
2. **Fundal massage** for boggy fundus.  
3. Express clots; empty bladder (catheter if needed).  
4. Oxytocin and other uterotonics as ordered (misoprostol, methylergonovine — **caution HTN**, carboprost — **caution asthma**).  
5. IV access/fluids/blood products; O₂; second IV; labs/coags.  
6. Prepare for balloon tamponade / OR / IR if refractory.  
7. After stabilization: warm, emotional support, explain events.

### Safety / red flags

> **Red flag:** Pad soaked in <15 minutes or large clots — massage and escalate.  
> **Red flag:** Firm fundus + bright bleeding → think laceration/trauma, not more massage alone.  
> **Red flag:** Giving methergine to client with BP 170/110.

### Remember this

Boggy fundus → massage. Know uterotonic contraindications at a high level.

## Topic D — Preeclampsia / eclampsia / HELLP

### Pathophysiology / concept

Hypertensive disorder of pregnancy with proteinuria/end-organ themes after 20 weeks (or postpartum). Severe features: high BP, thrombocytopenia, LFTs, creatinine, pulmonary edema, visual/neuro symptoms. Eclampsia = seizures. HELLP = hemolysis, elevated liver enzymes, low platelets — RUQ pain, nausea, DIC risk.

### Assessment cues

Headache, visual changes, RUQ/epigastric pain, hyperreflexia/clonus, edema, ↓ UOP, fetal growth concerns, proteinuria, rising BP from baseline.

### Priority nursing actions

- Quiet room; seizure precautions; bed rest themes as ordered.  
- **Magnesium sulfate** as ordered for seizure prophylaxis: monitor RR, DTRs, UOP, Mag levels; calcium gluconate antidote available.  
- Antihypertensives as ordered (labetalol/hydralazine/nifedipine themes — verify).  
- Delivery is definitive cure themes — timing per OB.  
- Postpartum still at risk for days — continue vigilance; don’t assume “delivery fixed it” instantly.  
- Strict I&O; lung sounds (pulmonary edema).

### Mag toxicity cues

Loss of reflexes, ↓ RR (<12 themes), ↓ UOP, extreme flushing, cardiac issues — **stop Mag**, give calcium gluconate per protocol, support airway.

### Remember this

Mag: reflexes, respirations, urine output. Antidote = calcium gluconate. Headache/visual changes = take seriously.

---

## Clinical judgment vignette 2 — Mag toxicity

**Stem:** Severe preeclampsia on mag; RR 10; DTRs absent; client drowsy; infusion still running.

**What to do first:** Stop mag, support airway/breathing, oxygen, calcium gluconate per protocol, notify provider — not “encourage ambulation to wake up.”

**Why:** Mag toxicity progresses to respiratory arrest.

## Topic E — Newborn care

### Priority concepts

1. **Airway** — clear secretions as needed; skin-to-skin when stable.  
2. **Thermoregulation** — dry thoroughly, hat, warmer/skin-to-skin; cold stress → hypoxia/hypoglycemia/acidosis cascade.  
3. APGAR at 1 and 5 minutes themes (appearance, pulse, grimace, activity, respirations).  
4. ID bands; security; delayed bathing themes as policy.  
5. Vitamin K, eye prophylaxis as ordered.  
6. Hypoglycemia risk: IDM, LGA/SGA, preterm, cold stressed — feed/protocols; heel-stick glucose.  
7. Jaundice: physiologic after 24h vs pathologic earlier/rapid rise; phototherapy eye mask/hydration/temp.  
8. Newborn screening / hearing / CHD pulse ox themes as applicable.  
9. Cord care; circumcision care if done; feeding cues (rooting, hands to mouth).

### Assessment red flags

Grunting, flaring, retractions; central cyanosis; temp instability; lethargy; sugar low; bilious vomiting; ambiguous genitalia with crisis risk (CAH themes high-level); no void/stool in expected windows per policy.

### Teaching

Back to sleep (ABC: alone, back, crib); car seat rear-facing; feeding cues; cord care; when to call (fever, poor feeding, jaundice spreading, fewer wet diapers). Never shake. Smoke-free environment.

### Remember this

Airway + warm + sugar. Pathologic jaundice early is concerning. Back to sleep.

## Topic F — Preterm labor & PROM / cord prolapse

### Concept

Contractions + cervical change before 37 weeks. PROM = membrane rupture before labor; PPROM if preterm. Infection (chorio) and cord prolapse risks. GBS status drives intrapartum antibiotic themes.

### Priority actions

- Lateral position; tocolytics as ordered; antenatal corticosteroids for fetal lungs if indicated.  
- Mag for neuroprotection in early preterm as ordered (different goal than preeclampsia dosing — follow OB orders).  
- PROM: note time/color/odor of fluid; avoid unnecessary digital exams; monitor fetal tachy/maternal fever.  
- **Cord prolapse:** knee-chest or Trendelenburg, lift presenting part off cord with gloved hand, O₂, emergency delivery prep — **do not** push cord back; keep hand in place until OR relief.

### Remember this

Cord prolapse = relieve pressure, call for help, to OR. Steroids for fetal lung maturity when indicated.

## Topic G — Mastitis, endometritis, postpartum mood

### Mastitis

Unilateral red painful breast + fever; continue breastfeeding/pumping as advised; antibiotics; hydration; complete emptying.

### Endometritis

Postpartum uterine infection: fever, foul lochia, uterine tenderness — antibiotics, Fowler’s, fluids; more common after cesarean.

### Mood

| | Baby blues | Postpartum depression | Postpartum psychosis |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Timing/vibe | Early days, mild, self-limited | Persistent, impaired function | Sudden, delusions/hallucinations |
| Action | Support, sleep, normalize | Screen, refer, treat, safety | **Emergency** — safety of mother/infant |

### Remember this

Fever + uterine tenderness after birth ≠ ignore. Psychosis = safety first.

**What to do first:** Fundal massage, call for help, empty bladder, uterotonics as ordered, IV access/fluids — don’t send to shower.

**Why:** Atony + full bladder classic; early PPH hypovolemia.

## Topic H — Gestational diabetes & hyperemesis (teaching depth)

### GDM

Insulin resistance of pregnancy → maternal hyperglycemia → fetal hyperinsulinemia → macrosomia, birth trauma, neonatal hypoglycemia. # Chapter 12 — Pediatrics

> **Study aid only.** Pediatric dosing is weight-based and error-prone — always verify with current references and double-checks. Vaccine schedules change; confirm CDC schedule. Not clinical protocol.

![Pediatric respiratory distress cues](visuals/peds-respiratory-distress.jpg)

![Croup vs epiglottitis](visuals/croup-vs-epiglottitis.jpg)

![Tet spell knees to chest](visuals/tet-spell-knees-chest.jpg)

---

## Why it matters on NCLEX

Kids **compensate then crash**. Peds stems test developmental safety, dehydration, airway differences (epiglottitis vs croup), congenital heart “spells,” and mandated reporting. Weight-based calc safety is non-negotiable. NGN loves trending tachypnea + feeding fatigue before the sudden desaturation.

---

## Topic A — Growth, development & safety teaching

### Concept

Milestones are ranges; **red flags** matter more than memorizing every week. Anticipatory guidance prevents injury (choking, drowning, falls, burns, guns, car seats). Piaget/Erikson themes appear as communication and teaching strategy questions.

### High-yield red flags (examples — verify with developmental refs)

- Not tracking/smiling by expected windows  
- No babbling / no single words by expected ages  
- Loss of previously acquired skills  
- Extreme tone abnormalities  
- No walking by late toddler windows — evaluate

### Age-themed safety

| Age vibe | Safety themes |
| --- | --- |
| Infant | Back to sleep, no loose bedding, rear-facing car seat, water heater temp, never shake, crib safety |
| Toddler | Choking hazards, cabinet locks, water safety, rear-facing as long as possible then forward per guidelines |
| Preschool | Stranger safety, helmet, poison control number, firearm locked |
| School-age | Bike/helmet, sports, bullying, internet beginnings |
| Adolescent | Driving, substances, mental health, sexual health, consent, suicide screening themes |

### Communication tips

- Infant: caregiver presence; swaddle/comfort.  
- Toddler: simple words; choices of two; procedures on stuffed animal first.  
- Preschool: concrete; may fear body injury; bandage after shots.  
- School-age: want explanations; involve in care.  
- Adolescent: privacy; speak to them directly; confidential care limits (safety).

### Remember this

Injury prevention teaching is Health Promotion gold. Regression with stress is common — assess for red flags vs temporary regression.

---

## Topic B — Dehydration & fluid safety

### Pathophysiology / concept

Higher % body water + higher metabolic rate → kids dehydrate faster (GE, fever, burns). Mild → severe: from sticky mucosa to sunken fontanel/eyes, no tears, oliguria, lethargy, delayed CRT, shock. Fontanel assessment in infants is high-yield.

### Priority nursing actions

- Oral rehydration therapy (ORT) for mild-moderate if tolerating PO — small frequent sips.  
- IV fluids for severe / shock / intractable vomiting — boluses per PALS-aligned orders.  
- Daily weights; strict I&O; avoid diluted homemade “guess” recipes when commercial ORS available.  
- Electrolyte caution with IV hypotonic fluids (iatrogenic hyponatremia themes).  
- Replace ongoing losses; treat fever; skin care for diarrhea.

### Teaching

ORT small frequent sips; signs to return (lethargy, no urine, blood in stool, persistent vomiting, dry mucous membranes worsening). Hand hygiene to prevent spread.

### Safety / red flags

> **Red flag:** Lethargy + CRT >3–4 sec + sunken fontanel = IV pathway, not “try juice at home.”  
> **Red flag:** Mixing formula incorrectly (too dilute/concentrated) → electrolyte disasters.

### Remember this

Lethargy + poor perfusion = IV pathway, not “just try juice.”

---

## Topic C — Pediatric respiratory: RSV, croup, epiglottitis

### RSV / bronchiolitis

Viral lower airway in infants; wheeze, tachypnea, feeding difficulty. Supportive care primary; isolation contact/droplet per policy; suction nose before feeds; watch apnea in young infants; hydration carefully.

### Croup (laryngotracheobronchitis)

Viral; barky cough, stridor, hoarseness; steeple sign themes. Keep child calm; steroids; nebulized epinephrine for moderate-severe as ordered. Cool mist evidence mixed — follow orders.

### Epiglottitis (less common with Hib vaccine but still tested)

**Bacterial** emergency: drooling, dysphagia, distress, tripod, high fever, **no cough** often. **Do not** throat-exam with tongue blade. Keep with parent, NPO, airway team / OR for controlled airway, antibiotics after airway secured as ordered.

### Comparison memory hook

| | Croup | Epiglottitis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Vibe | Barky cough, stridor | Drooling, no cough, toxic |
| Action | Calm, steroids ± racemic epi | Airway first, don’t agitate throat |

### Other airway notes

Foreign body: unilateral wheeze/sudden cough — don’t blind finger sweep. Asthma in kids: same silent-chest rules as adults.

### Remember this

Epiglottitis = don’t look in the throat. Kids with retractions/flaring/grunting need escalation.

## Clinical judgment vignette 1 — Epiglottitis

**Stem:** 4-year-old tripoding, drooling, muffled voice, SpO₂ 92%, parent asks you to “look at the throat with a light.”

**What to do first:** Keep child calm in parent’s lap, NPO, call for emergency airway support/provider, prepare for controlled airway — **do not** examine throat or agitate with IV sticks until airway plan ready (per team).

**Why:** Agitation can complete obstruction.

## Topic D — Congenital heart disease (high-level)

### Concept

Left-to-right shunts (VSD/ASD/PDA) → pulmonary overcirculation, failure to thrive, CHF themes. Right-to-left / cyanotic lesions (TOF, transposition) → cyanosis, polycythemia, spells. Obstructive (coarctation) → BP/pulse discrepancies (arms vs legs).

### Assessment cues

Poor feeding, sweating with feeds, tachypnea, murmur, cyanosis, differential cyanosis, delayed CRT, hepatomegaly in CHF, clubbing in chronic cyanosis.

### Priority nursing actions

- Cluster care; optimize feeding (fortify calories as ordered); digoxin/diuretic themes as ordered (dig toxicity same cues — bradycardia, vomiting).  
- **Tet spell:** knees-to-chest, calm, O₂, morphine/fluids as ordered.  
- Pre/post ductal sat comparisons when relevant.  
- Infective endocarditis prophylaxis only when currently indicated — guidelines change; don’t invent.  
- Post-op cardiac: chest tube, rhythm, perfusion, pain, infection.

### Remember this

Feeding fatigue + sweat = cardiac red flag in infants. Tet spell → knees to chest.

---

## Clinical judgment vignette 2 — Tet spell

**Stem:** Toddler with TOF becomes deeply cyanotic and dyspneic after crying in clinic hallway.

**What to do first:** Knees-to-chest (or squat position), calm, O₂, call for help, prepare ordered meds — don’t chase for pulse ox alone while child remains knee-chest capable.

**Why:** Increases systemic vascular resistance, reduces right-to-left shunt.

## Topic E — Vaccines, febrile seizures, abuse reporting

### Vaccines

Know that schedules update — teach importance, common mild reactions vs emergencies (anaphylaxis). Injection site by age (vastus lateralis infants; deltoid when muscle adequate). Document VIS themes. Live vaccines caution in severe immunocompromise — verify.

### Febrile seizures

Usually brief, age-limited; protect airway/safety; antipyretics for comfort don’t always prevent recurrence; teach when to seek care (first seizure, prolonged, focal, status).

### Abuse / mandated reporting

Nurses are **mandated reporters**. Inconsistent history, patterned injuries, spiral fractures themes in non-ambulatory infants, STIs in young children, retinal hemorrhages — private interview, treat injuries, report per law. Don’t confront aggressively alone in a way that blocks reporting or safety.

### Remember this

Mandated reporter = report suspicion per law. Weight-based dosing double-check. Epiglottitis ≠ croup management.

### Red flag

> **Red flag:** Bilious vomiting in newborn = emergency until proven otherwise (malrotation/volvulus themes).

### Remember this

Bilious vomiting in newborn = emergency until proven otherwise.

## Topic G — Otitis, meningitis peds, Kawasaki

- Otitis media: pain, fever; finish Abx; feed upright; hearing follow-up if recurrent.  
- Pediatric meningitis: bulging fontanel, high-pitched cry, photophobia — droplet as indicated, Abx after cultures when safe.  
- Kawasaki: prolonged fever (≥5 days themes), strawberry tongue, rash, conjunctivitis, extremity changes → **coronary aneurysm** risk; IVIG + aspirin as ordered (special aspirin indication); delay live vaccines after IVIG per timing rules — verify.

### Remember this

Kawasaki = fever + mucocutaneous signs → cardiac follow-up. Incomplete Kawasaki still dangerous.

## Topic H — Pain, med safety, and sickle cell hooks

### Pain

Use age-appropriate scales (FLACC, Faces, numeric). Believe the child; combine pharm + nonpharm (distraction, sucrose for infants per policy, positions).

### Med safety

mg/kg; never exceed adult max casually; independent double-checks for high-alert; use kg not pounds in calc; leading zeros.

### Sickle cell

Pain crisis = opioids + fluids + O₂ as ordered + heat; fever = emergency infection risk (functional asplenia); acute chest syndrome = respiratory emergency; stroke risk; hydroxyurea themes; avoid deferral of pain as “drug seeking” stereotype.

# Chapter 13 — Psychosocial Integrity

> **Study aid only.** Crisis and suicide protocols follow facility and local law. Duty-to-warn and reporting rules vary by jurisdiction — know concepts and verify locally. Not psychotherapy.

![Therapeutic communication keep-kill](visuals/therapeutic-communication.jpg)

---

## Why it matters on NCLEX

Psych items are less about DSM trivia and more about **safety, therapeutic communication, and legal/ethical boundaries**. Alcohol/benzo withdrawal can kill — treat them as medical as well as psychiatric. Med toxicity (lithium, MAOIs, serotonin syndrome, NMS) is classic priority material.

---

## Topic A — Therapeutic communication

### Concept

Goal: help client express feelings, clarify, and participate in care. Presence > clever advice.

### Keep / kill phrases

**Keep:** “Tell me more.” “This sounds difficult.” “What concerns you most?” silence, reflection, clarification, open-ended questions.  
**Kill:** “Don’t worry.” “Why did you…” “You should…” “Everyone feels that way.” changing subject, false reassurance, probing curiosity, judgment.

### Priority nursing actions

- Private setting when possible.  
- Cultural humility; qualified interpreters.  
- Match acuity: if client is psychotic and unsafe, safety first, then communication.

### Remember this

Empathetic + reality-based + client-centered. Avoid “why” and false reassurance.

---

## Topic B — Suicide risk

### Concept

Ask directly about ideation, plan, means, intent, history. Asking does not “plant” the idea. Highest risk periods include after starting antidepressants (energy returns before mood — conceptual teaching) and after sudden “improvement” with resolved planning.

### Assessment cues

Talk of being a burden, giving away possessions, goodbye messages, accessing means, severe hopelessness, command hallucinations to harm self.

### Priority nursing actions

1. Ensure immediate safety — remove means; 1:1 observation when indicated.  
2. Ask directly; do not leave high-risk client alone.  
3. Contract for safety is **not** a substitute for observation when high risk.  
4. Collaborate on safety plan; involve team; follow involuntary hold laws as applicable conceptually.  
5. Document quotes and actions factually.

### Remember this

Direct questions + means restriction + observation level matches risk.

---

## Topic C — Schizophrenia

### Concept

Positive symptoms (hallucinations, delusions, disorganized speech) vs negative (flat affect, avolition). Reality testing impaired. Antipsychotics: EPS, NMS, metabolic syndrome with atypicals.

### Priority nursing actions

- Safety from command hallucinations (content assessment).  
- Calm, short, concrete communication; don’t argue delusions — acknowledge feelings, present reality gently.  
- Med adherence support; monitor EPS (dystonia, akathisia, TD).  
- NMS red flags: rigidity, high fever, autonomic instability — emergency.

### Remember this

Don’t argue delusions. Assess hallucination commands for safety. NMS = emergency.

---

## Topic D — Bipolar disorder & mania milieu safety

### Concept

Mania: elevated/irritable mood, decreased sleep need, grandiosity, pressured speech, risky behavior, poor intake. Depression poles as MDD. Lithium common mood stabilizer; anticonvulsant mood stabilizers also used.

### Priority nursing actions

- Mania: reduce stimulation, private room near nurses’ station themes, high-calorie finger foods, sleep promotion, set firm consistent limits on unsafe behavior, redirect rather than argue, remove hazardous items, supervised activities.  
- Protect other clients from intrusive/aggressive behavior — milieu safety.  
- Lithium: monitor levels; toxicity cues (see meds section); hydration/Na⁺ consistency; teratogenicity awareness.

### Remember this

Mania = stimulation down + nutrition + sleep + limits + milieu safety. Lithium toxicity is serious.

---

## Topic E — Depression, anxiety & PTSD / trauma-informed care

### Depression

SIG-E-CAPS themes; priority = suicide risk + ADLs + med teaching (SSRIs: time to effect, serotonin syndrome awareness with interactions, sexual SE). Don’t stop abruptly.

### Anxiety / panic

Stay with client during panic; short sentences; breathing coaching; quiet environment. Benzos: sedation, dependence, withdrawal risk. Buspirone slower onset themes.

### PTSD / trauma-informed basics

Intrusions (flashbacks/nightmares), avoidance, negative mood/cognition, hyperarousal. Triggers can be sensory.  
**Trauma-informed nursing:** safety, trust, choice, collaboration, empowerment; ask permission before touch; avoid re-traumatizing restraint when alternatives exist; grounded presence during flashbacks (“You are safe here; you are in the hospital”). Refer to therapy modalities as ordered (exposure, EMDR themes — know they exist, don’t practice them solo).

### Remember this

Panic: stay with them. Depression: always screen suicide. PTSD: safety + choice + don’t force storytelling. SSRI + MAOI / certain combos = serotonin syndrome risk.

---

## Topic F — Personality disorders (boundaries)

### Borderline personality themes

Intense relationships, fear of abandonment, impulsivity, self-harm risk, **splitting** (staff as all-good/all-bad).  
**Nurse:** consistent limits across shifts; team communication so splitting fails; validate feelings without reinforcing unsafe behavior; collaborative **safety planning** (not no-harm contracts) + observation level matched to self-harm risk; avoid over-involvement or punitive coldness.

### Antisocial personality themes

Disregard for rights of others, deceit, lack of remorse themes.  
**Nurse:** firm limits; safety of staff/peers; don’t moralize endlessly; document objectively; don’t bargain privileges around intimidation.

### Remember this

Consistency + boundaries + safety. Don’t take splitting personally — communicate as a team.

---

## Topic G — Substance: alcohol CIWA, opioids/naloxone, stimulants

### Alcohol / sedative-hypnotic withdrawal

Can cause seizures, DTs (autonomic hyperactivity, hallucinations, confusion) — **medically dangerous**. **CIWA**-guided benzodiazepines as ordered (score drives dosing themes); thiamine before glucose in clients with alcohol use disorder; seizure precautions; quiet safe environment; fall risk; fluids/electrolytes; aspiration precautions if vomiting.

### Opioid withdrawal vs overdose

**Withdrawal:** miserable (myalgias, diarrhea, yawning, piloerection) — usually not directly lethal like alcohol/benzo withdrawal — support hydration and MAT protocols (buprenorphine/methadone themes as ordered).  
**Overdose:** pinpoint pupils, respiratory depression, unresponsiveness — **naloxone** as ordered/protocol; support airway/breathing; may need repeat doses (naloxone may wear off before opioid); stay with client; watch for withdrawal precipitation after naloxone.

### Stimulant intoxication/crash

Cardiac/psych agitation; crash depression/suicide risk.

### Remember this

Alcohol/benzo withdrawal can kill — CIWA + benzos + thiamine concepts. Opioid OD = naloxone + airway. Don’t confuse withdrawal misery with OD apnea.

---

## Topic H — High-yield psych meds: lithium, MAOI, serotonin syndrome vs NMS

### Lithium toxicity cues

Therapeutic **0.6–1.2 mEq/L** (maintenance); **> 1.5 mEq/L = toxic**, > 2.0 mEq/L = emergency. Draw a trough ~12 h after the last dose. Early: nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, fine tremor worsening, polyuria/polydipsia themes. Progressing: coarse tremor, ataxia, confusion, seizures.  
**Actions:** hold lithium, notify provider, levels/labs as ordered; hydrate; teach steady salt/fluid intake (sudden low Na⁺/dehydration raises toxicity risk); NSAID/diuretic interaction awareness themes; avoid dehydration in heat/illness.

### MAOI diet / tyramine

MAOIs + **tyramine-rich foods** (aged cheeses, cured meats, fermented soy, draft beer themes — follow current diet list) → hypertensive crisis. Teach diet + all med interactions (cold meds, SSRIs — washout periods). Headache + severe HTN = emergency energy.

### Serotonin syndrome vs NMS

| | **Serotonin syndrome** | **NMS** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Trigger vibe | Serotonergic stacking (SSRI+MAOI, etc.) | Antipsychotics (dopamine block) |
| Onset | Often faster (hours) | Often slower (days) |
| Neuromuscular | Hyperreflexia, clonus, myoclonus | **Lead-pipe rigidity**, bradyreflexia |
| Autonomic | Diaphoresis, tachy, unstable VS | Fever, tachy, labile BP |
| Nurse | Stop offending agents, support ABCs, notify — cooling/benzos/cyproheptadine themes as ordered | Stop antipsychotic, ABCs, cooling, ICU — dantrolene/bromocriptine themes as ordered |

### Remember this

Lithium: levels + hydration + salt consistency. MAOI: tyramine diet. Clonus → think serotonin; lead-pipe rigidity → think NMS.

## Topic I — ECT nursing care

### Concept

Electroconvulsive therapy for severe depression / catatonia / some mania — brief controlled seizure under anesthesia. Informed consent; NPO; hold certain meds (e.g., anticonvulsants) per orders; remove dentures; void.

### Post-ECT priorities

Airway/VS; reorient (temporary confusion/memory gaps common); fall risk; assess return of gag before PO; document seizure length per protocol; offer reassurance that memory issues often improve.

### Remember this

ECT = consent + NPO + post-op style airway/reorientation — not “barbaric punishment.”

---

## Topic J — Restraints, seclusion & least-restrictive

Least restrictive first: verbal de-escalation → offer PRN → quiet room → seclusion → physical restraint as last resort when imminent harm. Time-limited orders; face-to-face provider evaluation per rules; continuous or frequent monitoring; offer food/toileting/ROM; never as punishment or for staffing convenience; debrief after. Chemical restraint ethics apply. Document behaviors that justified escalation.

### Remember this

Least-restrictive ladder + time limits + monitoring + dignity. Same principle as medical restraints — plus trauma sensitivity.

---

## Topic K — Abuse / IPV screening & safety planning

### Concept

Intimate partner violence, child/elder abuse. Interview **privately**. Nonjudgmental. Provide resources. **Report** per law for mandated categories (children/elders/vulnerable adults). For competent adult IPV, reporting rules vary — safety planning + resources still core; know your jurisdiction.

### IPV screening & safety planning (high-yield)

- Screen when alone; normalize (“We ask everyone”).  
- If disclosure: believe, validate, assess immediate danger, weapons in home, strangulation history (high danger).  
- Safety plan themes: packed bag, code word with trusted person, documents/keys/meds ready, shelter/hotline numbers, exit strategies — client sets pace (leaving can increase short-term danger).  
- Do not confront abuser or hand materials that increase risk if partner searches belongings — follow advocacy best practices.  
- Photograph injuries per policy/consent; treat injuries; document objectively (quotes, body map).

### Priority actions

- Separate from suspected abuser for interview.  
- Report mandated cases; document objectively.  
- Safety plan; shelters/hotlines.

### Remember this

Private interview. Mandated reporting for kids/elders/vulnerable. Adult IPV: safety planning over lectures. Safety over secrecy.

---

## Topic L — Eating disorders (NCLEX lens)

Anorexia: restriction, low weight, bradycardia, refeeding syndrome risk when nutrition restarts (monitor phos/K⁺/Mg²⁺). Bulimia: binge-purge, enamel erosion, parotid swelling, electrolyte swings from vomiting/laxatives.  
**Priorities:** medical stabilization first; supervised meals as ordered; therapeutic alliance; cardiac monitoring when indicated.

### Remember this

Refeeding can be medically dangerous — electrolytes. Eating disorders are medical + psych.

## Why it matters on NCLEX

Pharmacological and Parenteral Therapies is a large Client Needs band (~13–19% RN). The exam tests **class patterns, safety checks, monitoring labs, and teaching** — not obscure brand trivia. NGN cases may ask you to choose which labs to trend after a new med or which teaching statement shows risk for harm.

## How to study pharm for NCLEX

For every class, lock four hooks:

1. **Use** (why give it?)  
2. **Key side effects / toxicities**  
3. **Labs / monitoring**  
4. **Teaching / hold parameters**

Drill **high-alert** and **look-alike/sound-alike** separately. Build a one-page “antidote and hold parameter” sheet in week −2.

## Topic A — High-alert medications

### Concept

High-alert = elevated risk of significant harm if used in error. Independent double-checks per policy. Smart pumps help but do not replace thinking.

### Classic high-alert groups

- Insulins (including U-500 / concentrated)  
- Anticoagulants / thrombolytics  
- Opioids (PCA included)  
- Chemotherapeutic agents  
- Concentrated electrolytes (KCl, Mg, hypertonic NaCl)  
- Neuromuscular blockers (OR/ICU contexts)  
- Epidural/intrathecal analgesics  

### Priority nursing actions

- Two-nurse check when required.  
- Smart-pump libraries; no workarounds or “basic mode” bypass without policy.  
- Clarify unusual doses; read back verbal orders.  
- Label lines; trace tubing from bag to site.  
- Separate look-alike packaging; tall-man lettering awareness.

### Safety / red flags

> **Red flag:** “We’ve always given KCl IV push here” — never.  
> **Red flag:** PCA family dosing by proxy when not ordered.  
> **Red flag:** Insulin syringe used for non-insulin — or tuberculin syringe for U-100 confusion.

### Remember this

High-alert mistakes are rarely “minor.” Slow down.

## Topic D — Endocrine: insulin deep dive

### Kinetics mindset (examples — verify products)

| Type | Role | Hypo risk window vibe |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Rapid (lispro/aspart/glulisine) | Meal coverage | ~1 hour peak themes |
| Regular | Short / IV in DKA protocols | Later than rapid |
| NPH | Intermediate | Peak mid-window — snack planning |
| Long (glargine/detemir/degludec) | Basal | Usually less peak — still can cause hypo |

### Safety rules

- Match insulin to meal timing (rapid with food arrival).  
- Site rotation; never share pens.  
- Clear before cloudy when mixing **only** if that pair is mixable (never mix long-acting glargine etc.).  
- U-500 and concentrated products need extreme caution and dedicated processes.  
- Pump users: know DKA risk if infusion interrupts.  
- Sick-day rules: don’t stop insulin blindly; check ketones as taught; call for guidance.

### Oral / injectable non-insulin agents (pattern)

- Metformin: GI SE; lactic acidosis risk themes with hypoxia/renal failure/contrast timing per protocol.  
- Sulfonylureas: hypo risk — especially elderly.  
- SGLT2: genital infections; euglycemic DKA awareness; volume depletion.  
- GLP-1 RAs: GI SE; aspiration/peri-op themes evolving — verify current.  
- TZDs: edema/HF caution themes.

### Remember this

Peak = snack plan. Unconscious hypo → glucagon/IV dextrose per orders. 15-15 rule for conscious hypo with intact swallow.

## Topic G — Opioids, psych, maternity pharm themes

### Opioids

### Psych meds

## Topic H — Calculation safety

### Habits

1. Dimensional analysis.  
2. Leading zeros yes (0.5); trailing zeros no (5.0 forbidden).  
3. Pediatric: mg/kg → check max/day; weigh in kg.  
4. Never round intermediate steps into oblivion; round at appropriate final step per protocol.  
5. IV: mL/hr and gtt/min; know drop factor.  
6. Independent double-check for high-alert.  
7. If answer is 10× off typical clinical sense — recalculate.

### Mini sense-checks

- Adult acetaminophen single dose is usually 325–1000 mg; the safety limit is the **daily** maximum (3–4 g/day depending on source and liver status). Recheck any order that pushes past the daily ceiling, especially with combination opioid products.  
- Heparin infusions are protocol-driven — don’t freestyle.  
- Insulin doses in hundreds of units should scream U-500 / error check.

### Remember this

If an option skips allergy, ID, or compatible route/time — wrong. Calc errors are preventable with process.

## Clinical judgment vignette 3 — Vancomycin flushing reaction

**Stem:** Vanc started 20 minutes ago; client flushed, itchy, BP stable, no wheeze; infusion was running “wide open.”

**What to do first:** Stop/slow infusion per protocol, assess airway (rule out anaphylaxis), notify provider, anticipate antihistamine and slower restart — don’t automatically give IM epinephrine without assessment if this is a classic rate-related flushing reaction (formerly called “red man syndrome”) with a stable airway.

**Why:** Differentiate the flushing reaction (rate-related) from true anaphylaxis (airway/hypotension/wheeze) — both need respect; anaphylaxis needs epi.

5. Safety (falls, suicide means, infection exposure)  
6. Pain, education, psychosocial (unless that’s the stem’s true focus and client is stable)

**Tie-breakers:** acute > chronic · unstable > stable · unexpected > expected · actual > risk (when acuity otherwise similar)

## FAQ

### What is CAT on the NCLEX?

**CAT** (Computerized Adaptive Testing) means the exam adapts to your performance. Do well → harder items; struggle → easier ones. The computer continues until it’s confident you’re above or below the passing standard — or you hit the maximum items or time.

### What is Next Gen NCLEX (NGN)?

NGN is the current NCLEX format. It emphasizes **clinical judgment** through unfolding case studies and stand-alone items (bow-tie, matrix, trend, and more). It’s not optional extra content — it’s how the exam works in 2026.

### How long should I study?

It depends on how fresh your content is. Many grads do well with **4–8 weeks** of focused practice after school. Use the **2-week** plan if knowledge is hot and the test is soon; use **8 weeks** if you need a rebuild or you’re balancing work and family.

### What’s the difference between NCLEX-RN and NCLEX-PN?

Both use CAT, NGN clinical judgment, and a similar length/time window (85–150 items, up to 5 hours). RN organizes content under categories like **Management of Care** and **Pharmacological and Parenteral Therapies**; PN uses **Coordinated Care** and **Pharmacological Therapies**, with different percentage bands. Download the official test plan for the exam you’re taking.

### How many questions is the NCLEX?

You’ll see between **85 and 150** items. Some are unscored pretest items — you won’t know which. The exam may stop early once the computer is confident about pass/fail.

### Does this book replace the official test plan?

No. Third-party books prepare you — they do **not** replace [NCSBN](https://www.ncsbn.org) test plans or the Candidate Bulletin.

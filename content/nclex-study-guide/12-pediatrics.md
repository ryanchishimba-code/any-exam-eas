## Why it matters on NCLEX

Kids **compensate then crash**. Peds stems test developmental safety, dehydration, airway differences (epiglottitis vs croup), and mandated reporting. Weight-based calc safety is non-negotiable.

## Topic A — Growth, development & safety teaching

### Concept

Milestones are ranges; **red flags** matter more than memorizing every week. Anticipatory guidance prevents injury (choking, drowning, falls, burns, guns, car seats).

### High-yield red flags (examples — verify with developmental refs)

- Not tracking/smiling by expected windows  
- No babbling / no single words by expected ages  
- Loss of previously acquired skills  
- Extreme tone abnormalities  

### Age-themed safety

| Age vibe | Safety themes |
| --- | --- |
| Infant | Back to sleep, no loose bedding, rear-facing car seat, water heater temp, never shake |
| Toddler | Choking hazards, cabinet locks, water safety, rear-facing as long as possible then forward per guidelines |
| Preschool | Stranger safety, helmet, poison control number |
| School-age | Bike/helmet, sports, internet beginnings |
| Adolescent | Driving, substances, mental health, sexual health, consent |

### Remember this

Injury prevention teaching is Health Promotion gold. Regression with stress is common — assess for red flags vs temporary regression.

## Topic B — Dehydration

### Pathophysiology / concept

Higher % body water + higher metabolic rate → kids dehydrate faster (GE, fever, burns). Mild → severe: from sticky mucosa to sunken fontanel/eyes, no tears, oliguria, lethargy, delayed CRT, shock.

### Priority nursing actions

- Oral rehydration therapy (ORT) for mild-moderate if tolerating PO.  
- IV fluids for severe / shock / intractable vomiting — boluses per PALS-aligned orders.  
- Daily weights; strict I&O; avoid diluted homemade “guess” recipes when commercial ORS available.  
- Electrolyte caution with IV hypotonic fluids (iatrogenic hyponatremia themes).

### Teaching

ORT small frequent sips; signs to return (lethargy, no urine, blood in stool, persistent vomiting).

### Remember this

Lethargy + poor perfusion = IV pathway, not “just try juice.”

## Topic C — Pediatric respiratory: RSV, croup, epiglottitis

### RSV / bronchiolitis

Viral lower airway in infants; wheeze, tachypnea, feeding difficulty. Supportive care primary; isolation contact/droplet per policy; suction nose before feeds; watch apnea in young infants.

### Croup (laryngotracheobronchitis)

Viral; barky cough, stridor, hoarseness; steeple sign themes. Cool mist / night air folklore vs evidence — follow orders; steroids; nebulized epinephrine for moderate-severe as ordered. Keep child calm.

### Epiglottitis (less common with Hib vaccine but still tested)

**Bacterial** emergency: drooling, dysphagia, distress, tripod, high fever, **no cough** often. **Do not** throat-exam with tongue blade. Keep with parent, NPO, airway team / OR for controlled airway, antibiotics after airway secured as ordered.

### Comparison memory hook

| | Croup | Epiglottitis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Vibe | Barky cough, stridor | Drooling, no cough, toxic |
| Action | Calm, steroids ± racemic epi | Airway first, don’t agitate throat |

### Remember this

Epiglottitis = don’t look in the throat. Kids with retractions/flaring/grunting need escalation.

## Topic D — Congenital heart disease (high-level)

### Concept

Left-to-right shunts (VSD/ASD/PDA) → pulmonary overcirculation, failure to thrive, CHF themes. Right-to-left / cyanotic lesions (TOF, transposition) → cyanosis, polycythemia, spells. Obstructive (coarctation) → BP/ pulse discrepancies.

### Assessment cues

Poor feeding, sweating with feeds, tachypnea, murmur, cyanosis, differential cyanosis, delayed CRT, hepatomegaly in CHF.

### Priority nursing actions

- Cluster care; optimize feeding; digoxin/diuretic themes as ordered (dig toxicity same cues).  
- Tet spell: knees-to-chest, calm, O₂, morphine/fluids as ordered.  
- Pre/post ductal sat comparisons when relevant.  
- Infective endocarditis prophylaxis only when currently indicated — guidelines change; don’t invent.

### Remember this

Feeding fatigue + sweat = cardiac red flag in infants. Tet spell → knees to chest.

## Topic E — Vaccines, febrile seizures, abuse reporting

### Vaccines

Know that schedules update — teach importance, common mild reactions vs emergencies (anaphylaxis). Injection site by age (vastus lateralis infants; deltoid when muscle adequate). Document VIS themes.

### Febrile seizures

Usually brief, age-limited; protect airway/safety; antipyretics for comfort don’t always prevent; teach when to seek care (first seizure, prolonged, focal, status).

### Abuse / mandated reporting

Nurses are **mandated reporters**. Inconsistent history, patterned injuries, spiral fractures themes in non-ambulatory infants, STIs in young children — private interview, treat injuries, report per law. Don’t confront aggressively alone in a way that blocks reporting.

### Remember this

Mandated reporter = report suspicion per law. Weight-based dosing double-check. Epiglottitis ≠ croup management.

## Topic F — Pyloric stenosis, intussusception, Hirschsprung (high-yield)

| Condition | Classic cue | Nurse priorities |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pyloric stenosis | Projectile nonbilious vomit, olive mass, hungry, dehydrated/alkaline | NPO, IV fluids/electrolytes, prep surgery |
| Intussusception | Currant jelly stools, intermittent severe pain, sausage mass | Air/contrast enema themes may reduce; NPO; watch perforation |
| Hirschsprung | Failure to pass meconium, ribbon stools, obstruction | Prep for pull-through; measure abd girth; avoid rectal temps if ordered |

### Remember this

Bilious vomiting in newborn = emergency until proven otherwise (malrotation/volvulus themes).

## Topic G — Otitis, meningitis peds, Kawasaki (pattern)

- Otitis media: pain, fever; finish Abx; feed upright.  
- Pediatric meningitis: bulging fontanel, high-pitched cry, photophobia — droplet as indicated, Abx after cultures when safe.  
- Kawasaki: prolonged fever, strawberry tongue, rash, conjunctivitis → coronary aneurysm risk; IVIG + aspirin as ordered (special aspirin indication).

### Remember this

Kawasaki = fever + mucocutaneous signs → cardiac follow-up. Incomplete Kawasaki still dangerous.

## Concept check

**Q1.** Toddler with drooling, tripod position, high fever, no cough. Priority?  
A. Examine throat with tongue blade at bedside alone  
B. Keep child calm with parent, NPO, prepare for emergency airway management, notify provider  
C. Force flat supine for XR immediately without airway plan  
D. Give orange juice  

**Q2.** Infant with diarrhea, sunken fontanel, CRT 4 seconds, lethargic. Best theme?  
A. Home observation only  
B. Emergent evaluation and IV fluid resuscitation per orders  
C. Tap water boluses by parents  
D. Restrict all fluids  

**Q3.** Nurse suspects child abuse. Correct action?  
A. Ignore if parents are “nice”  
B. Report per mandated reporter law/policy after ensuring immediate safety  
C. Post photos on social media  
D. Wait until absolute courtroom proof  

#### Answers

**Q1: B.** Epiglottitis airway emergency — don’t blade the throat.  
**Q2: B.** Severe dehydration/shock pathway.  
**Q3: B.** Mandated reporting — safety first.

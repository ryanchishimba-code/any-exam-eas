## Why it matters on NCLEX

Maternity items reward **pattern recognition under time pressure**: late decelerations, boggy fundus, magnesium toxicity, newborn thermoregulation. One correct priority action saves mother and baby in the stem — and in real life. NGN cases often unfold from subtle BP/proteinuria to seizure prophylaxis decisions.

## Topic A — Antepartum essentials

### Pathophysiology / concept

Pregnancy is a high-flow, high-volume state. Screening catches preeclampsia, GDM, anemia, infections. **Placenta previa** (painless bleeding, placenta over os) vs **abruption** (painful bleeding, board-like uterus) are classic contrasts. Ectopic pregnancy (first trimester pain + bleeding + shoulder pain themes) is a rupture risk.

### Assessment cues — prenatal danger signs (teach these)

- Vaginal bleeding  
- Severe headache, visual changes, epigastric/RUQ pain (preeclampsia)  
- Decreased fetal movement  
- Signs of preterm labor (regular contractions, pelvic pressure, fluid leak)  
- Dysuria / fever  
- Sudden swelling of face/hands  
- Persistent vomiting with dehydration (hyperemesis complications)

### Priority nursing actions

- Accurate BP, urine protein themes, weight trends.  
- Rh-negative clients: Rho(D) immune globulin timing concepts (28 weeks / after sensitizing events / postpartum if baby Rh+).  
- GDM teaching: glucose monitoring, diet, insulin if ordered; fetal surveillance themes.  
- Bleeding: assess amount, pain, VS; NPO; IV access; **no vaginal exams in suspected previa** until localization known.  
- Kick counts teaching as ordered; when to call.

### Medication / treatment hooks

Prenatal vitamins / folate (NTD prevention early); iron for anemia (take with vitamin C themes; constipation teaching); avoid ACEI/ARB/teratogens — verify every drug with current pregnancy labeling.

### Remember this

Painless bleeding → think previa (don’t do vaginal exam). Painful rigid uterus → abruption energy.

## Topic B — Labor & fetal monitoring

### Stages (nursing priorities)

| Stage | What’s happening | Nurse focus |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Dilation | Comfort, hydration, monitor fetus/contractions, progress |
| 2 | Pushing → birth | Coach pushing, prepare for delivery, perineal support themes |
| 3 | Placenta | Watch separation signs, oxytocin as ordered, blood loss |
| 4 | Recovery (1–2 h) | Fundus, lochia, VS, bonding, hemorrhage vigilance |

### Fetal heart patterns

| Pattern | Meaning vibe | First moves |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Accelerations | Generally reassuring | Continue surveillance |
| Early decelerations | Head compression — often benign mirror | Observe; document |
| Variable decelerations | Cord compression | Reposition; amnioinfusion as ordered |
| Late decelerations | Uteroplacental insufficiency | **Intervene now** |

### Priority actions for nonreassuring patterns (intrauterine resuscitation)

Left lateral (or other position off vena cava), O₂ per protocol, stop oxytocin per protocol, IV bolus as ordered, notify provider, prepare for expedited delivery if unresolved. Check for tachysystole.

### Meds

- Oxytocin: titration for labor; watch tachysystole and late decels — **stop per protocol** when fetus in trouble.  
- Epidural: prehydrate as ordered; watch hypotension (left lateral, fluids, ephedrine/phenylephrine as ordered); bladder catheterization themes.  
- Opioids in labor: watch for neonatal respiratory depression at birth — priority is airway positioning and **positive-pressure ventilation per NRP**. Naloxone is **not** part of initial neonatal resuscitation and is avoided in infants of opioid-dependent mothers (seizure risk).

### Remember this

Late decels → act, don’t just document. Stop Pitocin per protocol when fetus is in trouble.

## Clinical judgment vignette 1 — Late decelerations

**Stem:** Multipara on oxytocin; recurrent late decelerations; contractions every 1.5 minutes; client dizzy supine.

**What to do first:** Stop oxytocin per protocol, left lateral, O₂ as ordered, fluid bolus as ordered, notify provider — don’t increase Pitocin “to finish faster.”

**Why:** Tachysystole + lates = uteroplacental insufficiency pathway.

## Topic C — Postpartum hemorrhage (PPH)

### Pathophysiology / concept

**4 Ts:** Tone (atony — most common), Trauma (lacerations), Tissue (retained placenta), Thrombin (coagulopathy). Atony = boggy fundus. Risk: multiparity, macrosomia, twins, prolonged labor, magnesium, chorio, prior PPH.

### Assessment cues

Saturating pads rapidly, boggy uterus, clots, hypotension/tachycardia, anxiety; may underestimate with slow steady loss — **quantify** (QBL themes). Fundus displaced up/right → full bladder.

### Priority nursing actions

1. Call for help / stay with client.  
2. **Fundal massage** for boggy fundus.  
3. Express clots; empty bladder (catheter if needed).  
4. Oxytocin and other uterotonics as ordered (misoprostol, methylergonovine — **caution HTN**, carboprost — **caution asthma**).  
5. IV access/fluids/blood products; O₂; second IV; labs/coags.  
6. Prepare for balloon tamponade / OR / IR if refractory.  
7. After stabilization: warm, emotional support, explain events.

### Safety / red flags

> **Red flag:** Pad soaked in <15 minutes or large clots — massage and escalate.  
> **Red flag:** Firm fundus + bright bleeding → think laceration/trauma, not more massage alone.  
> **Red flag:** Giving methergine to client with BP 170/110.

### Remember this

Boggy fundus → massage. Know uterotonic contraindications at a high level.

## Topic D — Preeclampsia / eclampsia / HELLP

### Pathophysiology / concept

Hypertensive disorder of pregnancy with proteinuria/end-organ themes after 20 weeks (or postpartum). Severe features: high BP, thrombocytopenia, LFTs, creatinine, pulmonary edema, visual/neuro symptoms. Eclampsia = seizures. HELLP = hemolysis, elevated liver enzymes, low platelets — RUQ pain, nausea, DIC risk.

### Assessment cues

Headache, visual changes, RUQ/epigastric pain, hyperreflexia/clonus, edema, ↓ UOP, fetal growth concerns, proteinuria, rising BP from baseline.

### Priority nursing actions

- Quiet room; seizure precautions; bed rest themes as ordered.  
- **Magnesium sulfate** as ordered for seizure prophylaxis: monitor RR, DTRs, UOP, Mag levels; calcium gluconate antidote available.  
- Antihypertensives as ordered (labetalol/hydralazine/nifedipine themes — verify).  
- Delivery is definitive cure themes — timing per OB.  
- Postpartum still at risk for days — continue vigilance; don’t assume “delivery fixed it” instantly.  
- Strict I&O; lung sounds (pulmonary edema).

### Mag toxicity cues

Loss of reflexes, ↓ RR (<12 themes), ↓ UOP, extreme flushing, cardiac issues — **stop Mag**, give calcium gluconate per protocol, support airway.

### Remember this

Mag: reflexes, respirations, urine output. Antidote = calcium gluconate. Headache/visual changes = take seriously.

## Clinical judgment vignette 2 — Mag toxicity

**Stem:** Severe preeclampsia on mag; RR 10; DTRs absent; client drowsy; infusion still running.

**What to do first:** Stop mag, support airway/breathing, oxygen, calcium gluconate per protocol, notify provider — not “encourage ambulation to wake up.”

**Why:** Mag toxicity progresses to respiratory arrest.

## Topic E — Newborn care

### Priority concepts

1. **Airway** — clear secretions as needed; skin-to-skin when stable.  
2. **Thermoregulation** — dry thoroughly, hat, warmer/skin-to-skin; cold stress → hypoxia/hypoglycemia/acidosis cascade.  
3. APGAR at 1 and 5 minutes themes (appearance, pulse, grimace, activity, respirations).  
4. ID bands; security; delayed bathing themes as policy.  
5. Vitamin K, eye prophylaxis as ordered.  
6. Hypoglycemia risk: IDM, LGA/SGA, preterm, cold stressed — feed/protocols; heel-stick glucose.  
7. Jaundice: physiologic after 24h vs pathologic earlier/rapid rise; phototherapy eye mask/hydration/temp.  
8. Newborn screening / hearing / CHD pulse ox themes as applicable.  
9. Cord care; circumcision care if done; feeding cues (rooting, hands to mouth).

### Assessment red flags

Grunting, flaring, retractions; central cyanosis; temp instability; lethargy; sugar low; bilious vomiting; ambiguous genitalia with crisis risk (CAH themes high-level); no void/stool in expected windows per policy.

### Teaching

Back to sleep (ABC: alone, back, crib); car seat rear-facing; feeding cues; cord care; when to call (fever, poor feeding, jaundice spreading, fewer wet diapers). Never shake. Smoke-free environment.

### Remember this

Airway + warm + sugar. Pathologic jaundice early is concerning. Back to sleep.

## Topic F — Preterm labor & PROM / cord prolapse

### Concept

Contractions + cervical change before 37 weeks. PROM = membrane rupture before labor; PPROM if preterm. Infection (chorio) and cord prolapse risks. GBS status drives intrapartum antibiotic themes.

### Priority actions

- Lateral position; tocolytics as ordered; antenatal corticosteroids for fetal lungs if indicated.  
- Mag for neuroprotection in early preterm as ordered (different goal than preeclampsia dosing — follow OB orders).  
- PROM: note time/color/odor of fluid; avoid unnecessary digital exams; monitor fetal tachy/maternal fever.  
- **Cord prolapse:** knee-chest or Trendelenburg, lift presenting part off cord with gloved hand, O₂, emergency delivery prep — **do not** push cord back; keep hand in place until OR relief.

### Remember this

Cord prolapse = relieve pressure, call for help, to OR. Steroids for fetal lung maturity when indicated.

## Topic G — Mastitis, endometritis, postpartum mood

### Mastitis

Unilateral red painful breast + fever; continue breastfeeding/pumping as advised; antibiotics; hydration; complete emptying.

### Endometritis

Postpartum uterine infection: fever, foul lochia, uterine tenderness — antibiotics, Fowler’s, fluids; more common after cesarean.

### Mood

| | Baby blues | Postpartum depression | Postpartum psychosis |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Timing/vibe | Early days, mild, self-limited | Persistent, impaired function | Sudden, delusions/hallucinations |
| Action | Support, sleep, normalize | Screen, refer, treat, safety | **Emergency** — safety of mother/infant |

### Remember this

Fever + uterine tenderness after birth ≠ ignore. Psychosis = safety first.

## Clinical judgment vignette 3 — Boggy fundus

**Stem:** 45 minutes after vaginal birth; fundus boggy above umbilicus and displaced right; lochia soaking pad; BP 100/60 from 118/72.

**What to do first:** Fundal massage, call for help, empty bladder, uterotonics as ordered, IV access/fluids — don’t send to shower.

**Why:** Atony + full bladder classic; early PPH hypovolemia.

## Topic H — Gestational diabetes & hyperemesis (teaching depth)

### GDM

Insulin resistance of pregnancy → maternal hyperglycemia → fetal hyperinsulinemia → macrosomia, birth trauma, neonatal hypoglycemia. Teaching: carb awareness, glucose log, after-meal targets per OB, exercise as cleared, fetal kick counts, when insulin is added. Postpartum: screen for ongoing diabetes; breastfeeding usually encouraged.

### Hyperemesis gravidarum

Persistent vomiting with weight loss/electrolyte imbalance/dehydration — not “normal morning sickness.” Priorities: IV fluids, electrolytes, antiemetics as ordered, thiamine before glucose if prolonged starvation risk (Wernicke themes), small frequent bland intake when able, emotional support (often dismissed unfairly).

## Topic I — Cesarean & postpartum infection prevention

- Incentive spirometry, early ambulation, SCDs, incision assessment (REEDA themes).  
- Lochia progression: rubra → serosa → alba; foul odor/fever = report.  
- Rhogam if indicated; rubella vaccine postpartum if nonimmune (avoid pregnancy per teaching window).  
- Contraception counseling before discharge when appropriate; pelvic rest teaching per OB.

## Clinical judgment teaching box — NGN cue sorting

When a maternity/pediatric stem feels crowded, sort cues into:

1. **Airway/breathing threats** (maternal Mag toxicity RR↓; newborn grunting; epiglottitis drooling; RSV apnea risk)  
2. **Circulation / bleeding / shock** (PPH pad count; pediatric CRT/fontanel; cyanotic spell)  
3. **Neurologic emergencies** (eclampsia; febrile status; rising ICP cues in meningitis)  
4. **Infection / isolation** (endometritis; varicella exposure; neutropenic fever)  
5. **Teaching & safety** (back to sleep; car seat; mandated reporting; med adherence)

Then ask: *What kills first if I do nothing for 5 minutes?* That answer is usually your first action.

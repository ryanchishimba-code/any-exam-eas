## Why it matters on NCLEX

Neuro items test **airway protection, time-sensitive perfusion (stroke), rising ICP recognition, and seizure safety**. Early vs late signs are classic NCLEX bait. NGN cases often trend serial neuro checks — a “soft” LOC change beats a single normal pupil exam from two hours ago.

## Topic A — Stroke (CVA)

### Pathophysiology / concept

Brain tissue dies when blood flow stops. **Ischemic** (thrombotic/embolic) is most common; **hemorrhagic** (ICH/SAH) bleeds into/around brain. “Time is brain.” Penumbra can be saved with timely reperfusion in eligible ischemic strokes — hemorrhagic paths differ (**no tPA**). Embolic strokes often sudden (afib); thrombotic may stutter with TIAs.

### Assessment cues

**FAST:** Face droop, Arm weakness, Speech difficulty, Time to call emergency care.  
Other: sudden severe headache (esp. hemorrhagic/SAH “worst headache”), visual field loss, ataxia, neglect, altered LOC, aphasia vs dysarthria, unilateral numbness.

**NIHSS themes** (high-level): structured deficit scoring — know that serial neuro checks matter more than memorizing every item.

### Priority nursing actions

1. ABCs; **NPO until swallow screen** passed — aspiration is a killer complication.  
2. Exact **last known well** time — drives eligibility windows.  
3. Rapid stroke protocol / CT to differentiate ischemic vs hemorrhagic.  
4. Glucose check (hypoglycemia mimics stroke).  
5. Elevate HOB per protocol if not contraindicated; head midline; avoid fever if possible.  
6. After ischemic confirmation and eligibility: thrombolysis/thrombectomy pathways as ordered — BP parameters matter (too high or too low both dangerous in context).  
7. Hemorrhagic: BP control per orders; reverse anticoagulation if ordered; neurosurg consult themes.  
8. Frequent neuro checks — report worsening (extension, bleed, edema).

### Medications / treatments (high-yield)

| Theme | Hooks |
| --- | --- |
| Alteplase (tPA) | Strict inclusion/exclusion; bleed risk; ICU monitoring; no anticoags/antiplatelets for a period after per protocol |
| Antiplatelets | Secondary prevention ischemic as ordered |
| Anticoagulation | Afib-related prevention when appropriate (timing after acute stroke varies) |
| Statins / BP meds | Secondary prevention |
| Nimodipine | SAH vasospasm themes — BP/hypotension watch |

### Complications

Aspiration pneumonia, DVT/PE, increased ICP/herniation, hemorrhagic transformation after tPA, shoulder subluxation on flaccid side, depression, neglect injuries.

### Client teaching

- Call emergency services at FAST signs — don’t “wait and see.”  
- Med adherence; smoking cessation; diabetes/HTN/lipid control; afib anticoagulation importance.  
- Aspiration precautions / diet texture as prescribed.  
- Unilateral neglect safety (scan environment); transfer toward strong side teaching with PT/OT.

### Safety / red flags

> **Red flag:** Sudden neuro decline after tPA → possible hemorrhage — stop infusions per protocol, notify, prepare CT.  
> **Red flag:** Wet voice / coughing with sips before swallow screen cleared.  
> **Red flag:** Pulling on flaccid arm during transfer.

### Delegation notes

UAP can assist with safe positioning and report changes; **RN** does swallow screen and neuro assessment.

### Remember this

- NPO until swallow cleared.  
- Last known well time is critical data.  
- Ischemic ≠ hemorrhagic treatment path.

## Clinical judgment vignette 1 — Stroke arrival

**Stem:** Client with right face droop and aphasia; last known well 90 minutes ago; family offers coffee “to calm nerves.”

**What to do first:** NPO, ABCs, activate stroke pathway, glucose, prepare for CT — politely hold PO fluids until swallow cleared and pathway allows.

**Why:** Aspiration risk + time window. Coffee is not comfort if lungs fill.

## Topic B — Increased ICP & head injury themes

### Pathophysiology / concept

Skull is closed box (Monro-Kellie): brain + blood + CSF. Increase in one → ICP rises unless compensation. High ICP → ischemia → herniation risk. Cerebral perfusion pressure ≈ MAP − ICP — both extremes of BP matter.

### Assessment cues

**Early:** change in **LOC** (irritability, confusion, restlessness), headache, vomiting (sometimes projectile), pupil changes beginning.  
**Late:** Cushing’s triad themes (**bradycardia, irregular respirations, widening pulse pressure/hypertension**), fixed dilated pupils, posturing, coma.

### Priority nursing actions

1. Maintain airway; avoid hypoxia/hypercarbia (both raise ICP).  
2. HOB elevation ~30° often; head midline; avoid tight collar ties.  
3. Minimize clustering of painful care; stool softeners to avoid Valsalva; no hip flexion extremes if they raise ICP.  
4. Osmotic therapy / hypertonic saline as ordered; monitor labs.  
5. Seizure precautions when indicated.  
6. No lumbar puncture if mass lesion/herniation risk without clearance.  
7. Quiet environment; control fever; treat pain without oversedating (need neuro exams).

### Meds (high-yield)

- Mannitol / hypertonic saline themes: monitor osmolality, electrolytes, volume status, IV site (hypertonic vesicant risk themes)  
- Antiepileptics if ordered  
- Avoid hypotonic free water overload themes  
- Steroids more for tumor edema than routine TBI — follow orders/guidelines

### Safety / red flags

> **Red flag:** Sudden LOC drop + Cushing’s signs = emergency herniation pathway.  
> **Red flag:** CSF leak (halo sign / clear drainage) — report; don’t pack nose aggressively.  
> **Red flag:** Dilating pupil on one side after head injury.

### Remember this

Early ICP = LOC change. Late = Cushing’s. Protect airway and cerebral perfusion.

## Topic C — Seizures

### Pathophysiology / concept

Abnormal synchronized electrical discharge. Status epilepticus = prolonged/repeated seizures without recovery — airway and brain injury risk. Causes: epilepsy, missed meds, fever (peds), hyponatremia, hypoglycemia, alcohol withdrawal, brain injury, infection.

### Assessment cues

Aura possible; tonic-clonic motor activity; incontinence; postictal confusion; focal deficits after (Todd’s paralysis themes); automatisms in focal seizures.

### Priority nursing actions

1. Protect from injury — lower to floor if needed, pad, turn side when able.  
2. **Nothing in the mouth.**  
3. Time the seizure; loosen clothing; suction ready for **after**.  
4. Oxygen if ordered/available; don’t restrain forcibly.  
5. Status: call emergency response; benzos per protocol.  
6. After: neuro check, glucose if indicated, document phases, meds as ordered; side-lying recovery.

### Client teaching

- Med adherence (breakthrough seizures often = missed doses).  
- Safety: driving laws vary; swim with buddy; medical ID; cook with care.  
- Triggers: sleep deprivation, alcohol, flashing lights in photosensitive types, illness.  
- Never swim alone; shower > bath if possible for safety.

### Medication hooks

Phenytoin (levels, gum hyperplasia, IV compatibility/cardiac monitoring themes), carbamazepine (levels, blood dyscrasia themes), valproate (liver, platelets), levetiracetam (mood), lamotrigine (rash/SJS teaching), benzos for acute status.

### Remember this

Airway/safety; time it; nothing in mouth; status = emergency meds/airway.

## Clinical judgment vignette 2 — Status epilepticus

**Stem:** Client seizing continuously for 8 minutes; SpO₂ dropping; family screaming for tongue blade.

**What to do first:** Protect airway/position, call help, O₂, prepare benzo per protocol, suction for after — **refuse tongue blade**, don’t leave for med room without calling help first.

**Why:** Status = airway + abort seizure. Objects in mouth cause dental/aspiration injury.

## Topic D — Meningitis basics

### Concept

Infection/inflammation of meninges. Bacterial is a **medical emergency** and often **droplet** precautions (e.g., N. meningitidis) until adequately treated per protocol. Viral often less fulminant but still serious. Fungal more in immunocompromised.

### Assessment cues

Fever, severe headache, nuchal rigidity, photophobia, altered LOC; Kernig/Brudzinski themes; petechial/purpuric rash with meningococcus (purpura fulminans concern). Infants: bulging fontanel, poor feeding, high-pitched cry — less classic stiff neck.

### Priority actions

- Droplet precautions early when suspected.  
- Blood cultures / LP as ordered when safe; antibiotics promptly — don’t delay for LP if unstable and Abx ordered.  
- Dark quiet room; seizure precautions; monitor ICP signs.  
- Close-contact prophylaxis themes as directed by public health/provider.  
- Fluids carefully; SIADH risk themes in some meningitis.

### Remember this

Droplet + don’t delay antibiotics for bacterial meningitis suspicion. Rash + meningitis signs = scary fast.

## Topic E — Spinal precautions & SCI

### Concept

Protect spinal cord when trauma mechanism suggests injury (falls, MVC, diving). Movement can convert incomplete injury to complete.

### Priority actions

- Manual in-line stabilization / C-collar as trained; log roll with team.  
- ABC first — airway with jaw thrust themes if trauma.  
- Motor/sensory baseline; report loss of function.  
- **Autonomic dysreflexia** in chronic SCI with a lesion **at or above T6** (severe HTN + pounding headache; flushing/sweating above the lesion, pallor/gooseflesh below): sit up with legs lowered, loosen constricting clothing, find the noxious stimulus **below the level of injury** (full or kinked catheter, fecal impaction, pressure injury) — this is hypertensive emergency energy; notify provider and anticipate a rapid-acting antihypertensive if BP does not fall.

### Safety / red flags

> **Red flag:** SCI client with sudden severe headache + BP 210/110 — sit up and find stimulus; don’t lay flat and “observe.”  
> **Red flag:** Neurogenic shock (hypotension + bradycardia after cervical/high thoracic injury) ≠ hypovolemia automatically.

### Remember this

Spine precautions until cleared. Autonomic dysreflexia = sit up + find stimulus.

## Topic F — Myasthenia gravis & Guillain-Barré

### Myasthenia gravis

Autoimmune attack on ACh receptors → fatigable weakness, ptosis, risk of **myasthenic crisis** (airway/breathing failure). Tensilon test historical; now antibody/EMG. Crisis vs cholinergic crisis (overdose of cholinesterase inhibitors) — both can cause weakness; differentiate carefully under protocol (often ICU).

**Nurse focus:** med timing before meals; aspiration risk; respiratory monitoring; infection can trigger crisis; avoid certain meds that worsen MG (aminoglycosides, some beta blockers — verify lists).

### Guillain-Barré

Post-infectious ascending paralysis; autonomic instability; **airway** may fail as paralysis climbs. Often needs ICU monitoring; plasmapheresis/IVIG themes.

**Nurse focus:** serial VC/NIF themes as ordered; prepare for intubation; DVT prophylaxis; fear/communication support (client may be locked-in aware); pain management for paresthesias.

### Remember this

GBS rising weakness → watch breathing capacity. MG crisis = ventilatory emergency potential.

## Topic G — Parkinson’s & Alzheimer’s care themes

### Parkinson’s

Dopamine loss → tremor, rigidity, bradykinesia, postural instability. Fall risk; aspiration; med timing (levodopa/carbidopa) critical for “on” periods. Deep brain stimulator teaching if applicable.

### Alzheimer’s / dementia

Safety, redirection, routine, caregiver support; don’t argue delusions aggressively; wandering precautions; cholinesterase inhibitors SE (GI, bradycardia themes); memantine themes.

### Remember this

Fall + aspiration + dignity. Schedule care when Parkinson meds are working.

## Clinical judgment vignette 3 — Rising ICP

**Stem:** Post-op craniotomy night: client was oriented; now restless, BP 188/70, HR 58, one pupil sluggish.

**What to do first:** Stay, support airway/oxygenation, HOB/midline as ordered, notify provider/neurosurg urgently, prepare osmotic therapy as ordered — this is late ICP trajectory, not “give PRN sleep aid.”

**Why:** LOC change + Cushingoid vitals + pupil change = herniation risk.

## Topic H — Bell’s palsy vs stroke (quick contrast)

| | Bell’s palsy | Stroke |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Onset | Facial nerve inflammation; often overnight | Sudden; may include arm/leg/speech/vision |
| Forehead | Often involved (whole side of face) | UMN patterns may spare forehead — still don’t DIY diagnose |
| Nurse action | Eye protection if can’t close lid; antivirals/steroids as ordered | Stroke pathway if any doubt |

**Rule:** If any doubt about central cause or other deficits — treat as stroke until cleared.

## Topic I — Multiple sclerosis & migraine nursing hooks

### MS

Demyelination with relapses/remissions or progressive courses. Fatigue, vision changes (optic neuritis), weakness, ataxia, bladder issues, heat sensitivity (Uhthoff). Nursing: energy conservation, fall safety, bowel/bladder program, infection prevention (can trigger flares), med adherence (injectables/infusions/orals — infection risk with some DMTs).

### Migraine

Severe headache ± aura, photophobia, nausea. Red flags for secondary headache: sudden “thunderclap,” neuro deficit, fever/neck stiffness, new after trauma, cancer/HIV context — escalate. Teaching: triggers, abortive timing, med overuse headache awareness.


## Concept check

**Q1.** Suspected stroke client arrives. Highest early nursing priority among these?  
A. Hot breakfast tray immediately  
B. Maintain NPO until swallow screen; support ABCs; activate stroke pathway  
C. Ambulate to CT alone  
D. Give aspirin before CT always without orders  

**Q2.** Earliest sign of rising ICP among typical options?  
A. Fixed dilated pupils only  
B. Change in level of consciousness  
C. Cushing’s triad complete  
D. Decerebrate posturing  

**Q3.** During a tonic-clonic seizure, the nurse should:  
A. Insert a tongue blade  
B. Protect from injury, time seizure, nothing in mouth  
C. Force oral meds mid-seizure  
D. Leave to get a full chart  

**Q4.** Autonomic dysreflexia first actions?  
A. Lay flat and increase fluids blindly  
B. Sit upright, loosen constricting items, find noxious stimulus, notify  
C. Give PRN opioid and leave  
D. Ignore headache in SCI clients  

**Q5.** After tPA, client’s NIHSS worsens and BP spikes. Concern?  
A. Expected improvement only  
B. Possible intracranial hemorrhage — emergent evaluation pathway  
C. Ambulate to reduce stiffness  
D. Give IM ketorolac for headache  

**Q6.** GBS client with rising weakness — which monitoring theme matters most?  
A. Ability to whistle only  
B. Respiratory capacity (VC/NIF themes) and airway readiness  
C. Hair growth rate  
D. Preference for TV channel  

#### Answers

**Q1: B.** Airway/aspiration prevention + pathway. Aspirin only when ordered and hemorrhage excluded as applicable.  
**Q2: B.** LOC change is early; Cushing’s/posturing are late.  
**Q3: B.** Safety and timing; never force objects into mouth.  
**Q4: B.** Sit up + find stimulus + notify.  
**Q5: B.** Decline after tPA → bleed until proven otherwise.  
**Q6: B.** Ascending paralysis threatens ventilation.

## Chapter Remember this

- Stroke: FAST, last known well, NPO until swallow screen.  
- ICP: early LOC → late Cushing’s.  
- Seizure: protect, time, nothing in mouth; status = emergency.  
- Meningitis: droplet + rapid treatment themes.  
- Spinal: immobilize until cleared; know autonomic dysreflexia cue.  
- MG/GBS: watch the airway.  
- Parkinson/dementia: safety, timing, dignity.

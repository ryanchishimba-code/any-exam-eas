# Chapter 2 — Cardiovascular

> **Study aid only.** Next-best-step cardiology for Step 2 CK / Step 3. Verify current ACS and HF guidance before clinical use.

## Why this chapter matters

Cardiovascular items dominate Step 2 CK: ACS pathways, HFrEF GDMT, valvular timing, tamponade, and arrhythmia decisions. Boards test the **clock and the trap**, not an encyclopedic consult note.

Highest-yield misses: withholding a mortality-reducing beta-blocker in stable HFrEF, waiting on troponin for obvious STEMI, missing aVR + diffuse ST depression as left-main equivalent, and calling papillary-muscle rupture “just CHF.”

## Core concepts

- **STEMI:** ST elevation in ≥2 contiguous leads or a new LBBB equivalent → activate PCI (door-to-balloon goal ≤90 min). Fibrinolytics only if PCI cannot happen in time. Dual antiplatelet therapy + anticoagulation per protocol. Troponin confirms; it does not gate the cath lab.
- **NSTEMI / UA:** Anticoagulate + DAPT. Early invasive strategy when TIMI/GRACE is high, ongoing pain, or instability.
- **HFrEF four pillars:** ACEi/ARB or ARNI + evidence-based beta-blocker + MRA + SGLT2i — all reduce mortality. Do not withhold the beta-blocker in a euvolemic outpatient because “they have heart failure.”
- **Acute decompensated HF:** IV loop diuretic first. Noninvasive ventilation for respiratory distress. Avoid routine inotropes.
- **Severe AS:** Angina, syncope, or HF = valve replacement discussion now (AVR/TAVR), even if the patient calls symptoms “mild.”
- **Papillary muscle rupture:** Inferior MI, new holosystolic murmur, flash pulmonary edema, shock — surgical emergency, not “more Lasix.”
- **Tamponade:** Beck triad + pulsus paradoxus → echo, then pericardiocentesis. Do not start with a fluid challenge as the definitive plan.
- **AF:** Unstable → synchronized cardioversion. Stable → rate vs rhythm. Anticoagulate by CHA₂DS₂-VASc.
- **aVR STE + diffuse STD:** Left main or proximal LAD — emergent angiography.

## Clinical applications

- 55-year-old, crushing pain 2 hours, ST elevation V1–V4 — activate the cath lab. Do not wait for troponin.
- Known HFrEF, 10 lb gain, orthopnea — IV furosemide, not an echo-first delay.
- Post-inferior MI, new murmur, shock — papillary rupture until proven otherwise.
- Trauma, hypotension, JVD, muffled sounds — tamponade pathway.
- Cocaine chest pain — benzodiazepines first; avoid unopposed beta-blockade.

## Comparison table — ACS

| Type | ECG | Next step | Trap |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| STEMI | ST elevation ≥2 leads | Cath lab + DAPT | Waiting for troponin |
| High-risk NSTEMI | ST depression / T inversion | Early invasive + anticoagulation | Conservative strategy in high TIMI |
| Low-risk UA | Nonspecific | Medical therapy, later stress | Immediate cath without risk stratification |

## HFrEF pillars

| Class | Examples | Mortality benefit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ACEi/ARB/ARNI | Sacubitril-valsartan | Yes — foundational |
| Beta-blocker | Carvedilol, metoprolol succinate, bisoprolol | Yes — start when euvolemic |
| MRA | Spironolactone, eplerenone | Yes — watch K⁺ / Cr |
| SGLT2i | Dapagliflozin, empagliflozin | Yes — even without diabetes |

## Misconceptions

- Withholding beta-blockers in stable HFrEF.
- Treating aVR elevation + diffuse depression as “nonspecific.”
- Intubating before a BiPAP trial in hypercapnic ADHF who can still protect the airway.
- Assuming every systolic murmur needs same-day surgery.

## Pearls

- STEMI is a clinical + ECG diagnosis.
- Syncope in severe AS is an intervention indication.
- Inferior MI + new MR murmur + shock = OR, not a longer observation.

Practice this chapter with mixed ACS / HF items, then read the rationale for every wrong option.

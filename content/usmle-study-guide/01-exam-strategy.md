## Why this chapter matters

USMLE items are less “what is the diagnosis?” and more **what do you do next under time pressure**. Step 1 still tests mechanisms. Step 2 CK tests the next best diagnostic or therapeutic step. Step 3 adds CCS-style sequencing: stabilize, diagnose, treat, and monitor.

This chapter locks the sitting differences, the decision habit, and the traps that waste a block.

## The next-best-step habit

Ask four questions on every stem, in this order:

1. **Unstable?** Airway, hypotension, ST elevation, status epilepticus, anaphylaxis — act before the extra test.
2. **What is the discriminating finding?** One number, one time window, one contraindication.
3. **What changes management now?** If the answer does not change the next order, it is not the answer.
4. **What is the trap?** Waiting for troponin in STEMI. Daptomycin for MRSA pneumonia. CT-negative thunderclap without LP.

Write those four lines in your error log after every miss.

## How to study this bank

- **Mixed blocks over topic marathons** once you know the framework. Boards mix systems inside a block.
- **Rationale first on misses.** If you cannot teach the wrong answers, you did not learn the item.
- **Time the last 3–4 weeks.** A timed 40-item block teaches pacing better than another passive video.
- **Step 3 CCS:** practice the order — IV access and monitors, then targeted labs/imaging, then therapy, then interval follow-up.

## Traps that feel smart

- **More data before the obvious action.** STEMI does not wait for troponin. Bacterial meningitis does not wait for LP if imaging delays antibiotics.
- **Treating a surrogate as a hard outcome.** LDL down is not the same as fewer MIs.
- **One-size BP targets.** Permissive hypertension in ischemic stroke unless you are lysing.
- **Memorizing drugs without the contraindication.** Daptomycin + pneumonia. Beta-blocker monotherapy in cocaine chest pain.

## Exam-day checklist

- Sleep and food beat a last-hour cram of rare pathways.
- Flag and move. One item cannot own an 8-minute average.
- For CCS-style thinking: if the patient is crashing, treat first, then order the confirmatory test.

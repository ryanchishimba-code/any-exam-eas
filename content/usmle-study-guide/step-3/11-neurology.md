# Neurology in practice

## Framework

The first hour of stroke, meningitis, and status is the Step 2 chapter. Step 3 adds the drug you send home, the driving conversation, and the prognosis you do not fake.

| After the emergency | The order |
| --- | --- |
| Ischemic stroke, cause not yet named | Antithrombotic as the etiology allows, a statin, blood pressure that is not being dropped into the basement in the first day unless a specific reason exists, swallowing screen, and a search for atrial fibrillation. Confirm current AHA secondary-prevention drugs. |
| Seizure, back to baseline, first event | Glucose was done. Imaging and labs as indicated. Do not promise a lifetime drug in every first unprovoked seizure. Counsel about not driving until the local rule is met. You do not know every state's interval. Say "do not drive until cleared." |
| Migraine, recurrent | A triptan or another acute therapy if there is no vascular contraindication, and a preventive when the frequency earns it. Do not use an opioid as the plan. |
| Dementia, clinic | Reversible screens (depression, B12, thyroid, drugs that are anticholinergic). Safety at home. A cholinesterase inhibitor is a discussion, not a cure. Do not promise independence. |
| Headache after 50, jaw claudication | Steroids if vision is threatened. Do not wait on the biopsy. |

Prognosis items: a patient who herniates, who cannot protect the airway, or who has a massive hemorrhage with worsening exam is an intensive-care and goals-of-care conversation. Do not offer a clinic follow-up as the answer.

## Checklist

- [ ] Secondary prevention after a stroke is a list, not one drug.
- [ ] Driving counsel after a seizure.
- [ ] No opioid as the migraine plan.
- [ ] Look for reversible dementia contributors.
- [ ] Goals of care when the exam is deteriorating.

## Common traps

- Dropping blood pressure aggressively in the first hours after an ischemic stroke without a reason.
- Phenytoin for every tingling spell.
- A clinic appointment as the disposition for a worsening intracranial hemorrhage.

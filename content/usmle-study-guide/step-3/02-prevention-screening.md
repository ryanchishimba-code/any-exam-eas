# Prevention, screening, and immunizations

> **Study aid only.** USPSTF grades and the CDC schedule change. Offer grade A and B services. Do not invent a screening test the task force has not endorsed. This chapter is a map to the primary source, not a replacement for it.

## What this chapter covers

The continuing-care frame of Step 3 is full of “this person is here for a physical.” That visit is a list of services matched to age, sex, and a few risk factors. Health maintenance is an explicit Day 2 competency.

## The framework

Say the grade before the test. A and B: offer. C: selective. D: recommend against. I: not enough evidence to make the call. Then match the person.

| Person in the stem | Service to know cold, then confirm |
| --- | --- |
| Woman 40 to 74 | Biennial mammography (USPSTF 2024, grade B) |
| Woman 21 to 65 | Cervical screening. Start at 21, not at sexual debut. Interval depends on the test and the age. |
| Adult 45 to 75 | Colorectal screening. Strategy is chosen with the patient. A glove test is not a strategy. |
| Adult 50 to 80 with 20 pack-years, smoking now or quit within 15 years | Annual low-dose CT |
| Man 65 to 75 who has ever smoked | One-time abdominal aortic ultrasound |
| Woman 65 or older | Osteoporosis screening |
| All adults | Depression screening, tobacco, unhealthy alcohol |
| Sexually active woman 24 or younger | Chlamydia and gonorrhea |
| Pregnant | The prenatal infectious screen, folate already in progress, and the vaccines ACOG and CDC agree on (inactivated influenza, COVID as currently directed, Tdap each pregnancy) |
| Adult with diabetes | The ADA bundle: A1C, kidney (eGFR and albumin), blood pressure, lipids, foot, eye. Not a random chemistry panel for its own sake. |
| Asplenic patient | The vaccines that cover encapsulated organisms, on the CDC schedule, plus what to do about fever |
| Adult vaccines | Influenza yearly, COVID as the current CDC page says, Td or Tdap on schedule, zoster, pneumococcal, RSV for the ages CDC names. Open the adult schedule. Do not trust a card with no date. |

Counseling that has evidence: tobacco cessation with medication plus support, alcohol brief intervention, obesity and activity, folic acid before conception, breastfeeding support, skin cancer counseling for young fair-skinned people as the USPSTF frames it. A lecture about “lifestyle” with no offer of treatment is not the answer when a treatment exists.

Pre-exposure HIV prevention and post-exposure prophylaxis are CDC protocols with a clock and a lab set. Step 3 can ask who qualifies. Open the current criteria rather than a slogan.

## High-yield atlas

A prevention visit is a grade, an age, and a risk factor. Offer A and B. Do not sell an I statement as a requirement. A symptom converts screening into diagnosis.

| Presentation and findings | Next best step | First-line management and the source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A woman 40 to 74 with average risk, here for a physical, no lump. | Offer biennial mammography. Do not add ultrasound or MRI for density alone as a USPSTF mandate. | USPSTF 2024, grade B: biennial mammography ages 40 to 74. Evidence is insufficient at 75 and older. A palpable mass is not screening. It is a diagnostic pathway even if the last mammogram was normal. |
| An adult 45 to 75 with no alarm symptoms. | Offer a screening strategy and match it to what the patient will finish. Do not call a single office fecal smear a program. | USPSTF: grade B for 45 to 49, grade A for 50 to 75. Symptoms (bleeding, iron deficiency, a change in bowel habit) are diagnostic colonoscopy, not the next screening anniversary. |
| Age 50 to 80, at least 20 pack-years, smokes now or quit within 15 years. | Annual low-dose CT if they meet the frame. Not a chest radiograph. Not a CT for a cough that is a diagnostic problem. | USPSTF. Outside the frame, do not screen because they cough or because a relative had cancer. |
| A man 65 to 75 who has ever smoked. | One-time ultrasound. | USPSTF grade B for that group. Do not screen a woman or a man who never smoked as a routine grade A/B offer. Confirm the current grade before you expand the population. |
| A woman 21 to 65. Under 21 even if sexually active. Over 65 with adequate prior normal screens. | Cytology every 3 years from 21 to 29. From 30 to 65, cytology every 3 years, primary HPV every 5, or co-testing every 5. | USPSTF. Do not start before 21. Do not screen yearly. A visible cervical lesion is a biopsy, not a Pap interval. |
| A woman 65 or older, or a younger postmenopausal woman with a risk you can name. | DEXA. | USPSTF for women 65 and older. Treatment decisions (bisphosphonate and the like) depend on the score and the fracture risk, not on a vitamin alone if the T-score and the history say treat. Confirm the current treatment threshold. |
| Any adult visit, including pregnancy and the postpartum visit. | Ask. If positive, ask about suicide directly and arrange treatment or a referral the same day if the risk is high. | USPSTF grade B for adults, including pregnant and postpartum people. A contract for safety is not a discharge plan. |
| A sexually active woman 24 or younger, or an older woman at increased risk. | Chlamydia and gonorrhea nucleic acid amplification. Offer HIV and syphilis testing when the visit is about sexual health. | USPSTF. Treat partners as the CDC STI guidelines say. Do not wait for a test to treat a syndrome that is already mucopurulent if follow-up is unsure, and do not skip the test either. |
| Any person who smokes, at any visit. | Advise stopping and offer medication plus support. A pamphlet alone is not the whole answer. | Varenicline or combination nicotine replacement, plus behavioral support. USPSTF and CDC. Pregnancy changes the medication choice. Confirm before you prescribe. |
| A positive screen for unhealthy use, no withdrawal. | A brief intervention and an offer of naltrexone or acamprosate when the diagnosis is a use disorder and the liver and the opioid situation allow. | USPSTF screening. SAMHSA and psychiatric guidelines for medication. Withdrawal is a different visit and may need a benzodiazepine protocol in a monitored setting. |
| A pregnant woman whose record lacks influenza, COVID as currently directed, and Tdap. | Give the inactivated vaccines the CDC and ACOG agree on this season. Tdap in every pregnancy. | CDC schedule and ACOG. Live vaccines are not the routine list. Confirm the current COVID product. Do not give MMR in pregnancy. |
| An older adult, or a person with a missing spleen, asking what shots they still need. | Open the current CDC adult schedule. Do not trust a card with no date. | CDC. Zoster recombinant vaccine for the ages on the schedule. Pneumococcal conjugate is the backbone, especially after splenectomy, with the polysaccharide timing the schedule spells out. |
| A person with known diabetes here for a planned visit, not for ketoacidosis. | A1C, blood pressure, eGFR and urine albumin, feet, eyes, and a statin decision. Hold the lecture and do the bundle. | ADA Standards of Care in Diabetes—2026. A1C under 7% for many nonpregnant adults if hypoglycemia is not severe. Pregnancy and frailty change the goal. Drug class follows ASCVD, heart failure, and CKD, not a reflex metformin-only plan. |
| Abdominal pain, hypotension, and a pulsatile mass. | The operating room or a bedside ultrasound if it will not delay surgery. Not a screening appointment. | A ruptured aneurysm is an emergency. The USPSTF one-time screen is for a well man. Do not confuse them. |
| A prior infarct, a stent, or symptomatic peripheral artery disease, and an LDL you have not yet treated. | A high-intensity statin unless it cannot be used. Add-on therapy is the current cholesterol guideline when LDL stays high. Confirm the threshold. Do not invent one. | ACC/AHA cholesterol guideline. Lifestyle is additive, not a substitute, after an infarct. |

| Pair | How you tell them apart |
| --- | --- |
| Breast cancer screening versus the neighbor | A new lump is diagnostic imaging and a possible biopsy, not a routine screening interval. |
| Colorectal cancer screening versus the neighbor | Iron deficiency in a man or a postmenopausal woman is a diagnostic colonoscopy even if they are inside a screening window. |
| Lung cancer screening versus the neighbor | A current 40 pack-year smoker at 62 meets the frame even if he feels well. |
| Abdominal aortic aneurysm screening versus the neighbor | A pulsatile mass and abdominal pain is not screening. It is a possible rupture and an emergency. |
| Cervical cancer screening versus the neighbor | Postmenopausal bleeding is endometrial, not a Pap question. |
| Osteoporosis screening versus the neighbor | A man is not in that automatic USPSTF bucket. Do not invent a universal male age. |
| Depression screening versus the neighbor | Suicidal ideation with a plan and intent is an emergency evaluation, not a screening code. |
| Sexually transmitted infection screening versus the neighbor | A man is not in that exact USPSTF sentence. Do not invent a universal male age. Risk still matters and the CDC has separate guidance. |
| Tobacco cessation versus the neighbor | A single sentence of advice is better than silence and worse than advice plus a drug he will take. |
| Alcohol use versus the neighbor | Withdrawal with tremor and autonomic excess is a monitored benzodiazepine protocol. |
| Vaccines in pregnancy versus the neighbor | MMR is postpartum if she is not immune, not during pregnancy. |
| Adult zoster and pneumococcal vaccines versus the neighbor | Asplenia adds encapsulated-organism coverage and a fever plan, not just the routine age rule. |
| Diabetes visit bundle versus the neighbor | A sick day is a different chapter: hold the SGLT2 inhibitor, hold metformin if the kidney is failing, give basal insulin. |
| AAA symptoms are not screening versus the neighbor | A known 4 cm aneurysm and no pain is surveillance, on a vascular schedule, not an emergency. |
| Statin in clinical atherosclerotic disease versus the neighbor | Primary prevention in a low-risk person is a shared decision, not an automatic high-intensity statin. |

### Breast cancer screening

Grade B means offer it. An I statement is not a mandate.

**Vignette.** A 52-year-old woman asks if she still starts mammograms at 50. She has no family history and no lump.
**Reasoning.** The 2024 recommendation offers screening from 40, every two years, through 74. Starting at 50 is the old habit. The diagnosis is Breast cancer screening.
**NBME trap.** A feed card that still says start at 50 for average risk.
**Hook.** 40 to 74, every two years, offer it.

### Colorectal cancer screening

Screening is for a person who feels well. Diagnosis is for a person with a clue.

**Vignette.** A 47-year-old has no symptoms and has never been screened. He wants to know if 50 is soon enough.
**Reasoning.** The offer starts at 45. Waiting until 50 misses the grade B years. The diagnosis is Colorectal cancer screening.
**NBME trap.** A glove test in the office called screening.
**Hook.** 45, not 50. Symptoms skip the anniversary.

### Lung cancer screening

Pack-years and recency, not symptoms.

**Vignette.** A 60-year-old quit 20 years ago after 15 pack-years and wants a scan because he coughs in the winter.
**Reasoning.** He does not meet pack-years or the 15-year quit window. A winter cough is not a screening indication. The trap is scanning him to be kind. The diagnosis is Lung cancer screening.
**NBME trap.** Chest radiograph as the screening test.
**Hook.** 20 pack-years, 50 to 80, quit under 15 years, yearly low-dose CT.

### Abdominal aortic aneurysm screening

One look, not a yearly ritual.

**Vignette.** A 70-year-old man who smoked in his twenties asks for the same aneurysm ultrasound his wife was offered at a health fair.
**Reasoning.** He meets the indication. She, as a woman, is not in the grade B population. The trap is screening both because the fair did. The diagnosis is Abdominal aortic aneurysm screening.
**NBME trap.** Yearly ultrasounds after a normal one-time screen.
**Hook.** Men 65 to 75 who have smoked, once.

### Cervical cancer screening

The start age is 21, not sexual debut.

**Vignette.** An 18-year-old who is sexually active is told she needs a Pap smear today.
**Reasoning.** USPSTF starts at 21. The trap is screening at coitarche. The diagnosis is Cervical cancer screening.
**NBME trap.** A normal Pap used to clear postmenopausal bleeding.
**Hook.** 21, not the first time they had sex.

### Osteoporosis screening

Quantity of bone. Osteomalacia is a mineralization problem and a different lab story.

**Vignette.** A 67-year-old woman has never had a bone-density test. She has not broken a bone.
**Reasoning.** Age alone puts her in the screening group. The trap is waiting for the first fracture. The diagnosis is Osteoporosis screening.
**NBME trap.** Calcium tablets as a substitute for measuring the bone.
**Hook.** Women 65 and older get the scan.

### Depression screening

Screening only helps if a positive screen changes what you do.

**Vignette.** A postpartum woman scores high on a depression screen and says she has thought about not waking up, without a plan.
**Reasoning.** You ask the next question about plan, means, and intent, and you do not send her out for three months. The trap is a pamphlet. The diagnosis is Depression screening.
**NBME trap.** Not asking because it might plant the idea.
**Hook.** Ask, including after birth.

### Sexually transmitted infection screening

Age and risk, not a moral impression.

**Vignette.** A 22-year-old woman has no symptoms and one partner. She is due for a visit.
**Reasoning.** 24 and younger is the screen even without symptoms. The trap is waiting for discharge. The diagnosis is Sexually transmitted infection screening.
**NBME trap.** Skipping the test because she looks well.
**Hook.** Women 24 and under, even if they feel fine.

### Tobacco cessation

Tobacco is the disease-modifying exposure in COPD and in vascular disease.

**Vignette.** A man with claudication says he is not ready for a pill but he smokes a pack a day.
**Reasoning.** You still advise and offer. The trap is waiting until he asks. The medication is an offer, not a lecture. The diagnosis is Tobacco cessation.
**NBME trap.** Saving the conversation for the pulmonary clinic.
**Hook.** Offer the drug. Do not only hand a pamphlet.

### Alcohol use

Unhealthy use is not the same as withdrawal.

**Vignette.** A man drinks six beers most nights, has never seized, and wants to cut back. He is not on opioids.
**Reasoning.** This is a clinic conversation and a medication offer, not a detox admission. The trap is a benzodiazepine prescription for a person who is not withdrawing. The diagnosis is Alcohol use.
**NBME trap.** Naltrexone in a person who needs opioids for pain or who has acute hepatitis. Check the liver and the medication list.
**Hook.** Screen, then offer a drug that works.

### Vaccines in pregnancy

Pertussis vaccine in pregnancy protects the newborn who is too young to be vaccinated.

**Vignette.** A woman at 28 weeks has never had a pertussis vaccine as an adult. She wants to wait until after delivery.
**Reasoning.** The antibody has to cross the placenta before birth. Waiting until postpartum misses the newborn. The trap is one Tdap per lifetime only. The diagnosis is Vaccines in pregnancy.
**NBME trap.** Live influenza vaccine in pregnancy.
**Hook.** Tdap every pregnancy.

### Adult zoster and pneumococcal vaccines

A conjugate recruits T cells. A polysaccharide alone is a weaker teacher.

**Vignette.** A 66-year-old had the old polysaccharide pneumococcal shot years ago and shingles last year. He thinks he is done.
**Reasoning.** The schedule has moved. Prior disease is not the recombinant zoster series. The trap is a memory of one shot. The diagnosis is Adult zoster and pneumococcal vaccines.
**NBME trap.** A feed card from five years ago used as the schedule.
**Hook.** Open the CDC page. The card may be old.

### Diabetes visit bundle

The visit is a set of measurements that change treatment.

**Vignette.** A 55-year-old with type 2 diabetes and an eGFR of 48 has an A1C of 8.2 and no urine albumin on the chart for two years.
**Reasoning.** The missing albumin and the low eGFR change the drug and the blood-pressure agent. The trap is only raising the metformin. The diagnosis is Diabetes visit bundle.
**NBME trap.** A chemistry panel with no albumin and no feet.
**Hook.** A1C, kidney, pressure, feet, eyes.

### AAA symptoms are not screening

Screening finds a silent dilation. Rupture is shock.

**Vignette.** A 72-year-old man who smoked collapses with back pain and a blood pressure of 80. The belly is tender.
**Reasoning.** This is rupture until proven otherwise. A scheduled ultrasound tomorrow is the trap. The diagnosis is AAA symptoms are not screening.
**NBME trap.** Sending a hypotensive patient to the CT scanner unmonitored.
**Hook.** Pain and shock are not a screening visit.

### Statin in clinical atherosclerotic disease

Secondary prevention is not the same conversation as a low-risk primary-prevention number.

**Vignette.** A 60-year-old a month after a stent is on aspirin only. LDL was 140 before the infarct and has not been repeated.
**Reasoning.** He already has disease. The statin is indicated. The trap is a diet trial for three months as the only plan. The diagnosis is Statin in clinical atherosclerotic disease.
**NBME trap.** Waiting for a second event.
**Hook.** Disease already happened: statin, unless it cannot be used.

## Checklist

- [ ] Grade first. Then the test.
- [ ] Lung cancer screening is a pack-year history, not a cough.
- [ ] AAA screening is men 65 to 75 who have smoked, one time.
- [ ] Cervical screening does not start before 21.
- [ ] Vaccines in pregnancy are a specific list, not “all of them” and not “none of them.”

## Common traps

- Screening an 80-year-old man who never smoked for an aneurysm because it feels thorough. That is not the USPSTF population.
- PSA as a must-do. Know the current grade before you champion it. Shared decision-making is not the same as a mandate.
- A feed card that still starts mammograms at 50 for average risk. The 2024 recommendation starts the offer at 40.
- Live vaccines in pregnancy. The routine list above is inactivated or subunit. Confirm any exception on the CDC page.

## Practice this in the Qbank

Prevention items are “who is this person.” Highlight age, smoking, and pregnancy before you look at the options. The feed is useful for grades you swap. It is dangerous for ages. If the card and USPSTF disagree, USPSTF wins, and you retire the card.

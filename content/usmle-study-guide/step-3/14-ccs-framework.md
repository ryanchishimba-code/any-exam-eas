# CCS: a framework for the cases

> **Study aid only.** A way to spend the 10 or 20 minutes USMLE allows. Not a scoring key. The Bulletin says CCS affects the Step 3 result and that its weight is no greater than its share of time. This chapter does not invent a point value.

## What this chapter covers

The order of operations on a computer-based case, and the habits that waste the clock. Use the official CCS materials to learn the buttons. This page is the medicine.

## The framework

![CCS: do the safe thing early](visuals/ccs-order.svg)

A case is a place, a clock, and a patient who changes. You are graded, in the only sense this book will claim, as part of the day. Play it as good generalist care.

| Minute | What you do |
| --- | --- |
| First | Choose the site. Office, emergency department, or inpatient. Move an unstable patient before you finish the history. |
| Next | A focused exam. Continuous monitoring, oxygen, glucose, and access when the person is sick. Do not order a full review of systems as a substitute for treatment. |
| Then | The treatment that is dangerous to delay: fluids for shock, a benzodiazepine for a seizure that is happening, antibiotics for suspected meningitis or neutropenic fever, decompression of a tension pneumothorax, aspirin and an ECG pathway for a possible coronary syndrome. |
| Then | Focused tests. Advance the clock to when the result would return. Read it. Change the plan. |
| Before the end | Counsel. Stop tobacco. Name the follow-up. Pregnancy test when it would change a drug or an x-ray. Vaccines only when this visit is the right visit, not as decoration on a crashing patient. |

Orders you would be ashamed to place on a real person are not “CCS strategy.” A daily chest radiograph, a thyroid panel for a sprained ankle, and antibiotics for a virus are still wrong. The case is not a scavenger hunt.

Advance the clock with a reason. “Reexamine in 30 minutes” after a treatment. “See the morning labs” after an admission. Ending the case while the person is hypotensive, because you want to move on, is not finishing.

Location changes are care. A person who needs a surgeon, a delivery, or a ventilator does not stay in the office because you started there. Call the consultant when you would call one. Do not call one to avoid a decision you can make.

## Checklist

- [ ] Site of care matches how sick they are.
- [ ] Treatment that cannot wait comes before the long differential’s labs.
- [ ] You read results after you advance the clock. You do not order the same test again out of nerves.
- [ ] Counseling and follow-up happen when the person is stable.
- [ ] You are not spraying orders you would not sign.

## Common traps

- A complete physical exam while the oxygen saturation is 78%.
- Forgetting naloxone, glucose, or a finger-stick because you were thinking about a rare toxin.
- Discharging a chest-pain patient from the office.
- Treating the 10-minute case and the 20-minute case with the same depth. The short case is the emergency. Do the emergency and move.

## Practice this in the Qbank

The question bank’s multiple-choice items teach the decision. CCS teaches the sequence. Do cases on the official practice software. After each case, write the first three orders you should have placed and the one order you placed that was noise. The feed is irrelevant during a case. It is a study tool for the fact you blanked, later.

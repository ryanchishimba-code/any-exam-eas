# CCS: a framework for the cases

> **Study aid only.** A way to spend the 10 or 20 minutes USMLE allows. Not a scoring key. The Bulletin says CCS affects the Step 3 result and that its weight is no greater than its share of time. This chapter does not invent a point value.

## What this chapter covers

The order of operations on a computer-based case, and the habits that waste the clock. Use the official CCS materials to learn the buttons. This page is the medicine.

## The framework

![CCS: do the safe thing early](visuals/ccs-order.svg)

A case is a place, a clock, and a patient who changes. You are graded, in the only sense this book will claim, as part of the day. Play it as good generalist care.

| Minute | What you do |
| --- | --- |
| First | Choose the site. Office, emergency department, or inpatient. Move an unstable patient before you finish the history. |
| Next | A focused exam. Continuous monitoring, oxygen, glucose, and access when the person is sick. Do not order a full review of systems as a substitute for treatment. |
| Then | The treatment that is dangerous to delay: fluids for shock, a benzodiazepine for a seizure that is happening, antibiotics for suspected meningitis or neutropenic fever, decompression of a tension pneumothorax, aspirin and an ECG pathway for a possible coronary syndrome. |
| Then | Focused tests. Advance the clock to when the result would return. Read it. Change the plan. |
| Before the end | Counsel. Stop tobacco. Name the follow-up. Pregnancy test when it would change a drug or an x-ray. Vaccines only when this visit is the right visit, not as decoration on a crashing patient. |

Orders you would be ashamed to place on a real person are not “CCS strategy.” A daily chest radiograph, a thyroid panel for a sprained ankle, and antibiotics for a virus are still wrong. The case is not a scavenger hunt.

Advance the clock with a reason. “Reexamine in 30 minutes” after a treatment. “See the morning labs” after an admission. Ending the case while the person is hypotensive, because you want to move on, is not finishing.

Location changes are care. A person who needs a surgeon, a delivery, or a ventilator does not stay in the office because you started there. Call the consultant when you would call one. Do not call one to avoid a decision you can make.

## High-yield atlas

Each case is a place, a clock, and a patient who changes. Play it as the care you would sign.

The vignette withholds the name. The reasoning earns it.

**Vignette 1.** The case opens in the office. The patient is sweaty, gray, and clutching the chest. The nurse asks if you want the full history first.
**Reasoning.** Move the patient to the emergency department before the social history. Monitors, access, oxygen if hypoxic, aspirin if there is no contraindication, and an ECG. A complete review of systems while the oxygen saturation is unread is the trap. Advance the clock only after the treatment that cannot wait has been ordered.

**Vignette 2.** You are already in the emergency department. Blood pressure is 70 and the neck veins are flat. The lungs are clear. You order a full panel and wait.
**Reasoning.** This is a tank problem until proven otherwise. Access, a fluid bolus, and a finger-stick glucose come before a thyroid panel. The trap is a beautiful laboratory list and no treatment. Reassess after the bolus. If the lungs get wet, you stop.

**Vignette 3.** A 10-minute case. The patient is seizing.
**Reasoning.** A short case is the emergency. Benzodiazepine, glucose, airway, and a longer-acting antiseizure drug. Do not spend the ten minutes on a family history. The trap is repeating the benzodiazepine as the only plan.

**Vignette 4.** The patient improves after oxygen and a nebulizer. You leave the continuous oxygen and the nothing-by-mouth order in place overnight because you are afraid to change anything.
**Reasoning.** De-escalate. Stop oxygen that is no longer needed. Convert intravenous medicine to oral when they can swallow. The trap is treating day two like minute two. Advance the clock and read the new saturation before you renew the emergency orders.

**Vignette 5.** You are unsure of the site. The patient has a fever of 39 and a blood pressure of 88.
**Reasoning.** That patient does not stay in the office. Emergency department or a monitored bed, cultures, an antibiotic that covers the likely source, and fluid with reassessment. A consultant does not replace the antibiotic. The trap is a clinic draw and a ride home.

**Vignette 6.** The case is stable and the diagnosis is already treated. You have not counseled and you have not named a follow-up day.
**Reasoning.** Counseling and a real follow-up belong on a stable case. Tobacco, the medicine they must not stop, and when to return. A dietary lecture during a resuscitation does not. The trap is ending the case the moment the pain stops.

**Vignette 7.** You order a pregnancy test after the CT because you remembered at the end.
**Reasoning.** The test comes before the radiation and before a drug that would change if they were pregnant. The trap is a thorough scan that should have waited five minutes for a urine test.

**Vignette 8.** Altered mental status. You order a head CT first. Glucose is not back. Naloxone has not been considered.
**Reasoning.** Glucose, oxygen, and naloxone if an opioid is possible are the first minutes. The CT is for the focal sign or the trauma, not a substitute for a finger-stick. The trap is imaging a hypoglycemic patient.

**Vignette 9.** Chest pain in the office. You schedule a treadmill for next week and send them home.
**Reasoning.** Pain that could be ischemic, dissecting, or embolic leaves the office for the emergency department. A treadmill is for a stable, resolved, low-risk story after an ECG. The trap is the outpatient test as a reflex.

**Vignette 10.** You placed twenty orders including a TSH and a cortisol for an ankle sprain, because a forum said CCS rewards completeness.
**Reasoning.** Orders you would not sign are not strategy. Shotgun orders are the wrong medicine. The trap is confusing a scavenger hunt with care. Order what this complaint needs, advance the clock, and read the result.

## Checklist

- [ ] Site of care matches how sick they are.
- [ ] Treatment that cannot wait comes before the long differential’s labs.
- [ ] You read results after you advance the clock. You do not order the same test again out of nerves.
- [ ] Counseling and follow-up happen when the person is stable.
- [ ] You are not spraying orders you would not sign.

## Common traps

- A complete physical exam while the oxygen saturation is 78%.
- Forgetting naloxone, glucose, or a finger-stick because you were thinking about a rare toxin.
- Discharging a chest-pain patient from the office.
- Treating the 10-minute case and the 20-minute case with the same depth. The short case is the emergency. Do the emergency and move.

## Practice this in the Qbank

The question bank’s multiple-choice items teach the decision. CCS teaches the sequence. Do cases on the official practice software. After each case, write the first three orders you should have placed and the one order you placed that was noise. The feed is irrelevant during a case. It is a study tool for the fact you blanked, later.

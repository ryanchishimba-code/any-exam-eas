# Musculoskeletal and blood problems in clinic

## Framework

These systems are mid-weight on Step 3. The exam wants the red flag and the drug you do not start blindly.

**Bone and joint**

A hot joint is still a tap. Gout flares are treated as flares. Urate-lowering therapy is for recurrent disease, started with a plan for the flare, not as the only new drug in the peak of pain (ACR). Back pain without red flags is movement. Cauda equina and fever plus a spine in a patient who injects drugs are emergencies. Osteoporosis: screen women 65 and older (USPSTF). Do not stop denosumab without a follow-on agent.

**Blood**

| Clinic finding | Move |
| --- | --- |
| Iron deficiency in an adult man or postmenopausal woman | Find the bleeding, including the colon. Do not refill iron forever. |
| B12 deficiency with neurologic symptoms | Replace B12. Folate alone is the wrong entire plan. |
| A rising creatinine and a falling platelet count and a clot on heparin | Stop heparin. HIT. |
| Fatigue, a very high white count, and blasts on the smear | This is not a clinic iron trial. Urgent hematology. |
| Anticoagulant and a major bleed | The reversal depends on the drug. Know that warfarin, a DOAC, and heparin do not share one antidote. Look it up rather than invent a dose. This book will not invent one. |

Perioperative items show up here too. Hold a drug that causes bleeding when the procedure requires it, and do not hold a beta-blocker the morning of surgery in a patient who needs it for the heart without a reason. Continue a statin. The exact bridge for a mechanical valve is a protocol, not a guess. Say "protocol" if the stem does not give you the valve and the procedure.

## Checklist

- [ ] Tap the hot joint even if the patient has gout.
- [ ] Iron deficiency has a source.
- [ ] HIT is stop heparin, not transfuse platelets for the number.
- [ ] Denosumab is not stopped casually.
- [ ] Do not invent a reversal dose.

## Common traps

- Allopurinol alone during a severe flare.
- Iron without a colon evaluation in the right adult.
- Platelets for HIT.

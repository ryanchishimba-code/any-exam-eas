# Ambulatory pediatrics

## Framework

Step 3 pediatrics is the well visit, the fever you do not send home, and the parent who refuses a vaccine. The surgical emergencies are in the Step 2 chapter. Use them if the case turns acute. Then return to follow-up.

| Visit | Do not skip |
| --- | --- |
| Newborn | Feeding, jaundice, the screen results, a car seat conversation, and when to come back. Bilious vomiting is not a feeding talk. |
| Infant fever under 28 days | The AAP pathway. Do not export an older child's "watch at home" plan. Confirm the current age bands. |
| Developmental concern or regression | Evaluate. Do not schedule "another year of watching" as the only plan. |
| Asthma or croup that is going home | The drug and the return precautions. A barking cough that is comfortable can go home after dexamethasone. Stridor at rest is not a waiting room. |
| Vaccine refusal | Hear the concern. Recommend the CDC schedule. Do not discharge the child from the practice as your first move on an exam item. Document. A mild illness is not a contraindication. Anaphylaxis to a component is. |

Dehydration: oral rehydration if the child can drink. Intravenous fluid if not. Recheck the heart rate and the mouth. Do not end a CCS case on "gastroenteritis" without a route of fluid.

## Checklist

- [ ] Age in days before you disposition a fever.
- [ ] Bilious vomiting is still an emergency at follow-up.
- [ ] Oral rehydration when the child can drink.
- [ ] Recommend the schedule. Do not invent a delayed schedule as the answer.
- [ ] Return precautions are an order.

## Common traps

- A honey pacifier for an infant under one year. Botulism.
- Aspirin for a viral child. Reye syndrome is the exam link.
- Watching bilious emesis overnight.

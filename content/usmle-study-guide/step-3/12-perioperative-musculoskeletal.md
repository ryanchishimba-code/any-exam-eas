# Perioperative care, musculoskeletal problems, and skin

> **Study aid only.** Elective risk is an ACC/AHA perioperative question. The hot joint and the dangerous rash are emergencies. Musculoskeletal is about 5–7% of Step 3 MCQs. Skin is about 4–6%. They are in one chapter so the “clinic versus operating room” fork stays visible.

## What this chapter covers

How you clear or do not clear someone for surgery, the orthopedic emergencies a generalist must recognize, and the skin findings that are either a biopsy or a hospital.

## The framework

Emergency surgery does not wait for a stress test. Elective surgery asks whether the person can climb a flight of stairs, whether there is an active cardiac condition, and whether a test would change anything. Revascularizing a heart only to get through a hernia repair is not the ACC/AHA approach. Hold antiplatelet therapy around a recent stent only with the cardiologist who placed it. Continue most beta blockers and statins. Manage diabetes with basal insulin, not with a sliding scale alone, on the morning of surgery.

| Problem | Fork |
| --- | --- |
| Hot native joint | Tap before antibiotics if minutes are safe. |
| Open fracture, compartment syndrome, cauda equina, necrotizing infection | Operating room. A palpable pulse does not clear a compartment. |
| Hip fracture | Prompt surgery, pain control, clot prevention, and a later bone-health plan. |
| Testicular torsion | Do not wait on imaging if the exam is classic. |
| Chronic knee osteoarthritis | Weight loss, exercise, topical or oral anti-inflammatory if the stomach and kidney allow, injection as a bridge. Replacement when function is gone. Imaging that matches the pain does not mandate surgery. |
| Gout flare | Anti-inflammatory the kidney can tolerate. Do not start or stop urate-lowering carelessly. See the Step 2 musculoskeletal chapter and the ACR guideline. |
| Melanoma suspect | Excisional biopsy. A shave is the wrong operation when melanoma is likely. |
| Cellulitis versus necrotizing infection | Pain out of proportion and toxicity are the operating room. Mark simple cellulitis and use an oral drug. |
| Zoster | Antiviral timing matters for the new rash. Vaccinate later as the CDC schedule says, including older adults. |
| Wound infection on day one | Often streptococcal or clostridial. Open the wound. Do not just change the antibiotic. |

Back pain is musculoskeletal until it has a red flag: cancer history, fever, injection drug use, deficit, urinary retention, trauma, or anticoagulation with a new severe pain. Those get imaged. The rest get movement, not a week of bed rest and an early MRI.

## Checklist

- [ ] No stress test to “clear” an emergency.
- [ ] Do not revascularize only for a low-risk elective case.
- [ ] Tap a hot joint. Operate a compartment, a cauda equina, a torsion, and a necrotizing infection.
- [ ] Excise, do not shave, a likely melanoma.
- [ ] Red flags before the MRI of ordinary back pain.

## Common traps

- Stopping a beta blocker on the morning of surgery out of habit.
- Antibiotics without drainage.
- Bed rest for nonspecific back pain.
- A feed card that says every back pain needs an MRI. It does not.

## Practice this in the Qbank

Write “tonight’s operating room or not” before you choose a medicine. Perioperative items: which test changes management. The feed can hold a red flag. It should not hold a clearance algorithm that contradicts ACC/AHA.

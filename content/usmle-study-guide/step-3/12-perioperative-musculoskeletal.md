# Perioperative care, musculoskeletal problems, and skin

> **Study aid only.** Elective risk is an ACC/AHA perioperative question. The hot joint and the dangerous rash are emergencies. Musculoskeletal is about 5–7% of Step 3 MCQs. Skin is about 4–6%. They are in one chapter so the “clinic versus operating room” fork stays visible.

## What this chapter covers

How you clear or do not clear someone for surgery, the orthopedic emergencies a generalist must recognize, and the skin findings that are either a biopsy or a hospital.

## The framework

Emergency surgery does not wait for a stress test. Elective surgery asks whether the person can climb a flight of stairs, whether there is an active cardiac condition, and whether a test would change anything. Revascularizing a heart only to get through a hernia repair is not the ACC/AHA approach. Hold antiplatelet therapy around a recent stent only with the cardiologist who placed it. Continue most beta blockers and statins. Manage diabetes with basal insulin, not with a sliding scale alone, on the morning of surgery.

| Problem | Fork |
| --- | --- |
| Hot native joint | Tap before antibiotics if minutes are safe. |
| Open fracture, compartment syndrome, cauda equina, necrotizing infection | Operating room. A palpable pulse does not clear a compartment. |
| Hip fracture | Prompt surgery, pain control, clot prevention, and a later bone-health plan. |
| Testicular torsion | Do not wait on imaging if the exam is classic. |
| Chronic knee osteoarthritis | Weight loss, exercise, topical or oral anti-inflammatory if the stomach and kidney allow, injection as a bridge. Replacement when function is gone. Imaging that matches the pain does not mandate surgery. |
| Gout flare | Anti-inflammatory the kidney can tolerate. Do not start or stop urate-lowering carelessly. See the Step 2 musculoskeletal chapter and the ACR guideline. |
| Melanoma suspect | Excisional biopsy. A shave is the wrong operation when melanoma is likely. |
| Cellulitis versus necrotizing infection | Pain out of proportion and toxicity are the operating room. Mark simple cellulitis and use an oral drug. |
| Zoster | Antiviral timing matters for the new rash. Vaccinate later as the CDC schedule says, including older adults. |
| Wound infection on day one | Often streptococcal or clostridial. Open the wound. Do not just change the antibiotic. |

Back pain is musculoskeletal until it has a red flag: cancer history, fever, injection drug use, deficit, urinary retention, trauma, or anticoagulation with a new severe pain. Those get imaged. The rest get movement, not a week of bed rest and an early MRI.

## High-yield atlas

Emergency surgery does not wait for a stress test. Elective surgery asks whether the person can climb a flight of stairs or the equivalent, about 4 METs, whether an active cardiac condition is unstable, and whether any test would change the operation. Revascularizing a coronary artery only to “clear” a hernia is not the ACC/AHA approach. The hot joint, the compartment, and the necrotizing infection are operating rooms. A film that matches knee pain does not mandate a replacement.

### The cardiac clearance that is not a clearance

Active unstable coronary disease, decompensated heart failure, a dangerous arrhythmia, or a severe symptomatic valve disease is the reason to stop an elective case. Stable disease and a person who can climb a flight of stairs do not need a stress test to get a cataract or a hernia done. Emergency surgery proceeds. A stent that is recent is a reason to talk to the cardiologist before anyone stops the antiplatelet drug. The 2024 AHA/ACC perioperative guideline is the source for how long dual therapy is protected. This chapter will not invent a day count beyond pointing you at that document. Do not stop a P2Y12 inhibitor because a preoperative form says “hold blood thinners.” Continue most beta blockers the person already takes. Do not start a beta blocker on the morning of surgery in a person who has never taken one. Statins are continued. ACE inhibitors and ARBs are often held the morning of surgery because of hypotension under anesthesia. SGLT2 inhibitors are held for a few days because of ketoacidosis. Confirm the current hold interval rather than guessing it on the day.

| What you see | The fork | The source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ruptured viscus, ischemia, hemorrhage | Operate. No stress test | ACC/AHA perioperative guideline |
| Elective hernia, climbs stairs, stable heart | No revascularization “to get through it” | Same |
| Recent stent and a form that says hold all blood thinners | Call the cardiologist. Do not stop the antiplatelet drug on a form | 2024 AHA/ACC perioperative guideline |
| Beta blocker already taken every day | Continue it | Do not start one the morning of surgery in a naive patient |
| SGLT2 inhibitor | Hold before surgery | Ketoacidosis risk. Confirm the interval |

**Vignette.** He can walk up two flights. The hernia is elective. A stress test last year was fine. Someone orders a catheterization “so the anesthesiologist is comfortable.”
**Reasoning.** The test would not change a plan he does not need. Proceed when the rest of the evaluation is done. The diagnosis is an elevated-looking preoperative request that the guideline does not support. The trap is a stent he did not need.
**NBME trap.** They delay a perforated ulcer for a stress test. Emergency surgery does not wait.
**Hook.** Unstable or emergency: the operating room. Stable and active: no extra revascularization.
**CCS.** The functional history, an ECG if the guideline would want one, continue the beta blocker and the statin, hold the ACE inhibitor and the SGLT2 on the schedule, do not order a cath to feel better.

### The joint, the compartment, and the hip

A hot native joint is a tap before antibiotics if minutes are safe. Cell count, Gram stain, culture, and crystals. Septic arthritis and gout can share a joint. A prosthetic joint is a call to orthopedics early. Do not copy the native-joint script and send him home. Compartment syndrome is pain out of proportion and pain on passive stretch. A pulse can be present. Fasciotomy. An open fracture is antibiotics and the operating room now. A hip fracture in an older adult is prompt surgery, pain control, clot prevention, and a later bone-health plan. Do not invent an hour cutoff. Delay costs function and clots.

**Vignette.** One hot knee. He has gout on the problem list. You can tap it in the next few minutes. Someone wants to start vancomycin and skip the needle because “he always has gout.”
**Reasoning.** Tap first. The diagnosis is a hot native joint, septic until the fluid says otherwise, and gout does not exclude infection. The trap is a steroid for presumed gout with no needle.
**NBME trap.** They are reassured by a palpable pulse in a leg with pain on stretch after a tibial fracture. The pulse is the last thing to go. Fasciotomy.
**Hook.** Native and hot: tap. Pulse present: the compartment can still be dying. Hip fracture: prompt surgery, not a week of “medical optimization” for a stable patient.
**CCS.** Tap, cultures, antibiotics after the tap if minutes were safe, orthopedics, fasciotomy for a compartment, fixation for the hip, clot prophylaxis, pain control.

### Back pain, torsion, and the wound on day one

Back pain without a red flag gets movement, not a week of bed rest and an early MRI. Red flags are cancer, fever, injection drug use, a deficit, urinary retention, trauma, and anticoagulation with a new severe pain. Those get imaged. Testicular torsion does not wait for an ultrasound if the exam is classic. Minutes matter for the testis. A wound infection on the first day is often streptococcal or clostridial. Open it. Do not just change the antibiotic. Necrotizing infection is the operating room. Pain out of proportion and toxicity are the story. Mark simple cellulitis and use an oral drug.

**Vignette.** Day one after surgery. Fever and a wound that is bronze and exquisitely tender. He looks toxic.
**Reasoning.** Open the wound in the operating room. This is not a day-five stitch abscess. The diagnosis is a necrotizing or clostridial early wound infection until the exploration says otherwise. The trap is a broader antibiotic and a dressing change.
**NBME trap.** They MRI a mechanical back pain with no red flag on the first visit, and they ultrasound a classic torsion for an hour. Movement for the back. The operating room for the testis.
**Hook.** Day-one wound toxicity: open it. Classic torsion: do not wait on a picture. A back with no flag: move.
**CCS.** For the wound: surgery, antibiotics, fluid. For torsion: urology now. For ordinary back pain: activity, no early MRI.

### Gout, osteoarthritis, zoster, and the mole

A gout flare gets an anti-inflammatory the kidney and the stomach can tolerate: an NSAID, early colchicine, or a steroid. Do not start allopurinol in the middle of a first attack without a prophylaxis plan. Do not stop it if he is already on it. HLA-B*58:01 is a conversation before allopurinol in higher-risk ancestry. Knee osteoarthritis is weight loss, exercise, and a topical or oral anti-inflammatory if the stomach and kidney allow. Replacement when function is gone. A radiograph that matches the pain does not mandate surgery. A melanoma suspect gets an excisional biopsy, not a shave. Zoster gets an antiviral promptly. The classic teaching is within 72 hours of rash onset. Confirm the current window. Vaccinate later as the CDC schedule says, including older adults.

**Vignette.** A painful dermatomal vesicular rash started yesterday. He is 70. The eye is not involved. He wants a cream.
**Reasoning.** An oral antiviral now, and an eye exam if the tip of the nose or the eye is in the dermatome. The diagnosis is herpes zoster. The trap is a steroid cream and a promise to vaccinate today instead of treating. Vaccinate later, on the schedule, not as the acute treatment.
**NBME trap.** They shave a pigmented lesion that meets melanoma concern. Excise it. A shave is the wrong operation when melanoma is likely.
**Hook.** New dermatomal vesicles: antiviral now, vaccine later. A suspicious mole: excise. A creaky knee that still walks: exercise before a prosthesis.
**CCS.** The antiviral, pain control, eye referral if the eye is threatened, gout treatment matched to the kidney, do not start allopurinol as the only acute order.

### Malignant hyperthermia and the drugs you do not casually stop

A trigger anesthetic, rigidity, and a rising carbon dioxide are malignant hyperthermia. Stop the agent. Dantrolene. This is not a bit of a fever. Endocarditis prophylaxis is a narrow AHA list: a prosthetic valve, previous endocarditis, some congenital heart disease, and cardiac transplant valvulopathy, for dental procedures that manipulate the gingiva. A murmur is not on the list. Do not expand it. Postoperative urinary retention is a bladder scan and a catheter, not a bigger opioid.

**Vignette.** Ten minutes after induction the carbon dioxide is rising, the jaw is rigid, and the temperature is starting up. Someone suggests acetaminophen and another gas.
**Reasoning.** Stop the trigger. Dantrolene. Cool and support. The diagnosis is malignant hyperthermia. The trap is treating it as atelectasis or a little fever.
**NBME trap.** They give amoxicillin before a dental cleaning for a benign flow murmur. Prophylaxis is a narrow list. A murmur is not a prosthetic valve.
**Hook.** Rising carbon dioxide and rigidity: dantrolene. A murmur: no automatic antibiotic.
**CCS.** Stop the volatile agent and succinylcholine, dantrolene, cooling, the malignant hyperthermia protocol. Do not advance the clock on acetaminophen.

### A second set of stems

**Vignette.** He had a drug-eluting stent three weeks ago. The form says hold clopidogrel for a knee arthroscopy that can wait.
**Reasoning.** It should wait, and the antiplatelet drug should not stop, until the cardiologist who placed the stent agrees. The diagnosis is antiplatelet therapy inside the protected window. The trap is holding the drug because the form has a checkbox.
**Hook.** A recent stent beats a preoperative checkbox.

**Vignette.** Diabetes. The morning of surgery someone writes “sliding scale only” and holds the basal insulin.
**Reasoning.** Basal insulin continues in a reduced way. Sliding scale alone is how he wakes in ketoacidosis or, if you overshoot, hypoglycemic. The diagnosis is a perioperative insulin plan that forgot the basal requirement. Confirm the local protocol. Do not invent a unit count here.
**Hook.** Basal stays, adjusted. Sliding scale is not a plan.

**Vignette.** A pulseless, pale, painful foot for two hours. He has atrial fibrillation.
**Reasoning.** Heparin and vascular surgery. The diagnosis is acute limb ischemia. The trap is a clinic ankle-brachial index tomorrow.
**Hook.** The limb has hours. Anticoagulate and call vascular surgery.

**Vignette.** After reperfusion the calf is tense and the pain is worse. The pulse returned.
**Reasoning.** Compartment syndrome after reperfusion. The pulse returning does not clear it. Fasciotomy. The diagnosis is a reperfusion compartment.
**Hook.** The pulse is back and the pain is worse: open the compartment.

**Vignette.** Chronic knee pain, body-mass index high, he can still walk the grocery store, the radiograph shows bone on bone.
**Reasoning.** Weight loss and exercise. The film does not schedule the replacement. The diagnosis is osteoarthritis that has not exhausted conservative care. The trap is a prosthesis because the picture looks dramatic.
**Hook.** Function, not the film, books the operating room.

**Vignette.** He is anticoagulated and has sudden severe back pain and a falling hemoglobin. The abdomen is soft.
**Reasoning.** Retroperitoneal bleed until a CT says otherwise. The diagnosis is a retroperitoneal hemorrhage. The trap is a rectal exam as the only test, or a lumbar strain pathway.
**Hook.** Anticoagulant, back pain, falling blood count: image the retroperitoneum and reverse if the bleed is life-threatening.

**Vignette.** A small skin abscess, well patient, no surrounding redness of consequence.
**Reasoning.** Drain it. The diagnosis is an uncomplicated abscess. Antibiotics are for the halo or the fragile host.
**Hook.** Pus out. A prescription is not a drain.

**Vignette.** Calcium oxalate stones and a urate that is high. He is in the middle of his first gout attack and has never taken allopurinol. The kidney is fine.
**Reasoning.** Treat the attack. Do not start allopurinol tonight as the only new order. The diagnosis is acute gout. The long plan, including any HLA conversation, is not the emergency prescription.
**Hook.** Attack first. Urate-lowering is a plan, not a reflex in a virgin flare.

## Second atlas

The first pass was the fork. These are the cases with the order written out. Emergency surgery still does not wait for a stress test. A recent stent still beats a checkbox that says hold all blood thinners.

### The elective case that does not need a catheterization

He climbs two flights. The hernia is elective. The heart is stable. A catheterization “so anesthesia is comfortable” is a stent he does not need. The 2024 AHA/ACC perioperative guideline asks whether a test would change the operation. Here it would not. Continue the beta blocker he already takes. Do not start one on the morning of surgery if he has never taken one. Continue the statin. Hold the ACE inhibitor or ARB the morning of surgery because of hypotension under anesthesia, and hold the SGLT2 inhibitor beforehand because of ketoacidosis. Confirm the interval on the day you do it. Do not invent a day count in the order.

**Vignette.** Two flights of stairs, a stable echocardiogram, an elective hernia. The order is a coronary angiogram this week.
**Reasoning.** Cancel the angiogram. Schedule the hernia when the medication plan is set. The diagnosis is an unnecessary preoperative revascularization pathway. The trap is comfort mistaken for an indication.
**NBME trap.** They delay a perforated ulcer for the same angiogram. Emergency surgery does not wait. The hernia and the perforation are different stems. Read the word elective.
**Hook.** Stable and active: operate when the medicines are planned. Unstable or perforated: the operating room now.
**CCS.** Functional history, continue the beta blocker and the statin, hold the ACE inhibitor and the SGLT2 inhibitor on the confirmed schedule, no cath, no new beta blocker the morning of surgery.

### The hot knee and the leg that still has a pulse

Tap a hot native joint before antibiotics if you have minutes. Cell count, Gram stain, culture, crystals. Gout on the problem list does not cancel the needle. A prosthetic joint is a phone call to orthopedics, not a copied outpatient plan. Compartment syndrome is pain out of proportion and pain on passive stretch. The pulse can be present. Fasciotomy. After reperfusion, a returning pulse and worse pain is the same diagnosis. An open fracture is antibiotics and the operating room. A hip fracture in an older adult is prompt fixation, pain control, clot prevention, and a later bone-health plan. Do not invent an hour cutoff, and do not spend a week “optimizing” a stable patient.

**Vignette.** Tibial fracture. Pain on passive stretch. The dorsalis pedis pulse is palpable. You schedule a repeat exam in the morning because the pulse is there.
**Reasoning.** Fasciotomy. The pulse is the last finding to disappear. The diagnosis is compartment syndrome. The trap is the pulse used as a clearance.
**NBME trap.** They skip the tap of a hot native knee because he “always has gout,” and they give a steroid. Septic arthritis and gout share joints. The needle is the test.
**Hook.** Pulse present: the compartment can still be dying. Hot native knee: tap, then the drug. Hip fracture: prompt surgery.
**CCS.** Tap if minutes exist, orthopedics, fasciotomy for the compartment, fixation for the hip, clot prophylaxis, pain control. Do not advance to tomorrow on a dying compartment.

### Torsion, the day-one wound, and the back that can move

Classic testicular torsion does not wait for an ultrasound. A wound that is bronze and exquisitely tender on day one is opened in the operating room. It is not atelectasis and it is not a broader antibiotic with the skin left closed. Ordinary back pain, no cancer, no fever, no injection drug use, no deficit, no urinary retention, no trauma, and no anticoagulation with a new severe pain, gets movement. It does not get a week of bed rest and an early MRI. Those red flags get the image.

**Vignette.** Day one. Fever. A bronze, exquisitely tender wound. The order is incentive spirometry for atelectasis.
**Reasoning.** Open the wound. The diagnosis is an early necrotizing or clostridial infection until exploration says otherwise. Atelectasis does not turn the skin bronze.
**NBME trap.** They MRI a back with no red flag, and they ultrasound a classic torsion for an hour. Movement for the back. The operating room for the testis and for the day-one toxic wound.
**Hook.** Day-one toxicity: open it. Classic torsion: do not wait. A back with no flag: walk.
**CCS.** Surgery for the wound and the torsion, antibiotics, fluid. Activity for the ordinary back. No early MRI.

### The rash, the mole, and the flare

A dermatomal vesicular rash that started yesterday gets an oral antiviral. The classic teaching is within 72 hours of onset. Confirm it. Vaccinate later, on the CDC schedule, not instead of treating. If the eye or the tip of the nose is involved, the eye is examined. A melanoma suspect is excised. It is not shaved. A gout flare gets an anti-inflammatory the kidney and the stomach can tolerate. Do not start allopurinol as the only new order in a first attack. Do not stop it if he is already taking it. A creaky knee that still walks the grocery store gets weight loss and exercise. The radiograph does not book the replacement.

**Vignette.** A pigmented lesion with an irregular border. The plan is a shave “to get a sample.”
**Reasoning.** Excise it. The diagnosis is a suspected melanoma. A shave is the wrong operation when melanoma is likely.
**NBME trap.** They start allopurinol tonight for a first flare and call that the treatment, or they stop it in a person who was already stable on it and is having a flare. Treat the inflammation. Keep the urate-lowering drug if it was already his.
**Hook.** Excise the mole. Antiviral now, vaccine later. Attack first, urate-lowering as a plan. Function books the knee replacement, not the film.
**CCS.** Excisional biopsy referral, the antiviral, eye care if the dermatome includes the eye, gout treatment matched to the kidney, exercise and weight for the osteoarthritic knee.

### The anesthetic emergency and the murmur that is not prophylaxis

Rising carbon dioxide, rigidity, and a trigger anesthetic are malignant hyperthermia. Stop the agent. Dantrolene. Acetaminophen is not the treatment. Endocarditis prophylaxis is a narrow list: prosthetic valve, prior endocarditis, some congenital heart disease, and a cardiac transplant with valvulopathy, for dental work that manipulates the gingiva. A flow murmur is not on the list. Do not give amoxicillin to be thorough.

**Vignette.** Induction. The jaw is rigid and the carbon dioxide is climbing. You give acetaminophen and continue the gas.
**Reasoning.** Stop the gas. Dantrolene. Cool. The diagnosis is malignant hyperthermia. The trap is a little fever.
**NBME trap.** They prophylaxis a benign murmur before a cleaning. Ask what the valve is. A murmur is not a prosthetic valve.
**Hook.** Rigidity and a rising carbon dioxide: dantrolene. A murmur: no automatic antibiotic.
**CCS.** Stop the volatile agent, dantrolene, cooling, the protocol. Do not advance the clock on acetaminophen.

### Two more stems

**Vignette.** A drug-eluting stent three weeks ago. The form says hold clopidogrel for a knee arthroscopy that can wait.
**Reasoning.** The arthroscopy waits. The antiplatelet drug does not stop until the cardiologist agrees. The diagnosis is antiplatelet therapy inside the protected window. The trap is the checkbox.
**Hook.** A recent stent beats a form.

**Vignette.** He is anticoagulated. Sudden severe back pain. The hemoglobin is falling. The abdomen is soft.
**Reasoning.** Retroperitoneal blood until a CT says otherwise. Reverse if the bleed is life-threatening, using the reversal that matches the drug. The diagnosis is a retroperitoneal hemorrhage. The trap is a lumbar strain pathway and a rectal exam as the only test.
**Hook.** Thinner, back pain, falling blood count: image and reverse if he is unstable. Vitamin K is for warfarin, not for every anticoagulant.

## Checklist

- [ ] No stress test to “clear” an emergency.
- [ ] Do not revascularize only for a low-risk elective case.
- [ ] Tap a hot joint. Operate a compartment, a cauda equina, a torsion, and a necrotizing infection.
- [ ] Excise, do not shave, a likely melanoma.
- [ ] Red flags before the MRI of ordinary back pain.

## Common traps

- Stopping a beta blocker on the morning of surgery out of habit.
- Antibiotics without drainage.
- Bed rest for nonspecific back pain.
- A feed card that says every back pain needs an MRI. It does not.

Write “tonight’s operating room or not” before you choose a medicine. Perioperative items: which test changes management. The feed can hold a red flag. It should not hold a clearance algorithm that contradicts ACC/AHA.

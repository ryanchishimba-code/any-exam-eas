# Kidney disease over time

## Framework

The emergency potassium plan is in the Step 2 book. Step 3 is the clinic GFR, the drug you stop, and the access you plan before the crash.

KDIGO: CKD is GFR under 60, or kidney damage, for more than 3 months. Stage the GFR and the albumin. An ACE inhibitor or an ARB is for albumin, especially with diabetes. An SGLT2 inhibitor is part of current care when the GFR still allows it. Do not combine an ACE inhibitor with an ARB. ([KDIGO](https://kdigo.org/guidelines/ckd-evaluation-and-management/))

| Visit finding | Order or stop |
| --- | --- |
| Potassium 5.6 on an ACE inhibitor | Stop the offending add-on (a potassium-sparing diuretic, a salt substitute). Recheck. Do not ignore it until the next year. |
| Creatinine up sharply after starting an ACE inhibitor | Look for renal-artery stenosis, volume loss, and a nonsteroidal. A small bump can be expected. A large bump is not. |
| GFR falling toward the point dialysis is a real conversation | Refer before the emergency. Access planning is not a same-day catheter if you had a year. |
| Metformin and a low GFR | Do not use metformin when eGFR is under 30. Confirm the current label. |
| Contrast tomorrow | Hold the drugs the kidney will not forgive that day, hydrate, and do not stack a nonsteroidal. |

Prognosis: a patient with acidosis, volume overload, and a GFR that is not coming back needs renal replacement. Do not wait for a "round number" you memorized if the clinical picture is uremic.

## Checklist

- [ ] Albumin plus blood pressure decides the ACE inhibitor.
- [ ] Do not stack ACE inhibitor and ARB.
- [ ] Recheck potassium after you intensify therapy.
- [ ] Refer before dialysis is an emergency.
- [ ] Metformin and a very low GFR do not mix.

## Common traps

- A year of high potassium because the patient "feels fine."
- Starting an SGLT2 inhibitor and an ACE inhibitor the same day in a volume-depleted patient and not rechecking.
- A nonsteroidal for a gout flare in stage 4 CKD.

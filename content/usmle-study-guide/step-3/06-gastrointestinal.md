# Gastrointestinal management

## Framework

Acute bleeding is in the Step 2 book. Step 3 adds the cirrhosis plan, the ulcer follow-up, and the screen you are late for.

**Cirrhosis, the orders**

| Problem | What you place |
| --- | --- |
| New ascites | Tap if you do not know why, or if there is fever or pain. Salt restriction. A diuretic ratio that respects potassium. Not a large tap without albumin when you remove a large volume. |
| Variceal bleed, after endoscopy | A nonselective beta-blocker when the pressure allows, and a plan for the next endoscopy. Antibiotic during the bleed. |
| Encephalopathy | Lactulose, find the precipitant, do not sedate. |
| Alcohol | Cessation treatment and thiamine. Not a lecture as the only order. |

Peptic ulcer on a nonsteroidal drug: stop the drug if you can, test and treat Helicobacter pylori, and acid suppression. Confirm eradication when the ulcer was complicated. Do not continue the nonsteroidal and add a proton-pump inhibitor as a permanent pair if a different pain plan exists.

Colorectal screening: average risk, start at 45 through 75. A positive stool test is a colonoscopy, scheduled, not "repeat next year." ([USPSTF](https://www.uspreventiveservicestaskforce.org/uspstf/recommendation/colorectal-cancer-screening))

Gallstone pancreatitis that has settled: the gallbladder comes out this admission when the patient is a surgical candidate. Sending them home to "follow up someday" is how the next stone arrives.

## Checklist

- [ ] Fever and ascites: tap.
- [ ] Precipitant for encephalopathy.
- [ ] H. pylori when there is an ulcer.
- [ ] Positive stool test becomes a colonoscopy.
- [ ] Cholecystectomy this admission after mild gallstone pancreatitis, when surgery is safe.

## Common traps

- Repeating the stool test.
- Sedating encephalopathy to "rest."
- A large-volume tap with no albumin plan.

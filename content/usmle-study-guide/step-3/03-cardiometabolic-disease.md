# Cardiometabolic disease in clinic and in the hospital

> **Study aid only.** The 2025 blood-pressure guideline, the ADA 2026 Standards, the heart-failure guideline, and KDIGO. A real patient’s numbers come from those documents, not from memory of this page.

## What this chapter covers

The continuing-care visit for blood pressure, lipids, diabetes, heart failure, and CKD, plus the urgent versions of the same diseases. Cardiovascular is about 9–11% of Step 3 MCQs. Endocrine is about 5–7%. Renal is smaller and travels with these drugs, so it lives here and again in CCS.

## The framework

A Step 3 cardiometabolic item is a visit, a home reading, and a comorbidity. The emergency version is the same disease with a disposition.

| Visit | What changes |
| --- | --- |
| Blood pressure, stage 1, no CVD, diabetes, or CKD, PREVENT risk under 7.5% | Lifestyle for 3 to 6 months, then medication if still at or above 130/80. 2025 AHA/ACC guideline. |
| Stage 1 with diabetes, CKD, clinical CVD, or PREVENT at least 7.5% | Start medication at or above 130/80. |
| Stage 2 | Medication, usually combination therapy, plus lifestyle. |
| On treatment, not at goal | Confirm the readings (home or ambulatory), then intensify. Do not restart the same monotherapy forever. |
| Clinical atherosclerotic disease | A high-intensity statin unless it cannot be used. Add-on therapy is the current cholesterol guideline when LDL stays high. Confirm the threshold. Do not invent one. |
| Heart failure, reduced ejection fraction, euvolemic | The pillars: ARNI or ACE inhibitor or ARB, evidence-based beta blocker, mineralocorticoid antagonist, SGLT2 inhibitor. A diuretic is for fluid, not a pillar. |
| Heart failure, wet and crashing | Airway, nitrates and diuretic as the pressure allows, and the cause. The pillars wait until the person can stand them. |
| Diabetes with heart failure, CKD, or atherosclerotic disease | Agent with evidence for that disease. Metformin is the common choice when glucose is the only goal. See the Step 2 endocrine chapter for the 2026 ADA wording. |
| Diabetes sick day | Hold the SGLT2 inhibitor. Hold metformin if the kidneys are failing or contrast is about to happen. Give basal insulin. Do not leave only a sliding scale. |
| CKD with albuminuria | ACE inhibitor or ARB, not both. SGLT2 inhibitor when the label and KDIGO allow the eGFR. |
| Chest pain in the office | If it could be ACS, the disposition is the emergency department, not a treadmill next week. |

Male reproductive health that belongs with this block because the outline pairs them: a painless testicular mass is cancer until imaged and referred, the same day, not after a course of antibiotics. A prostate symptom score is a quality-of-life conversation and a check for red flags (retention, hematuria, bone pain), not an automatic PSA. Erectile dysfunction is vascular disease until proven otherwise. Ask about the heart before you ask about the prescription.

## Checklist

- [ ] Home readings before you escalate a single clinic number.
- [ ] Stage 2 is medication now.
- [ ] Do not combine ACE inhibitor and ARB.
- [ ] Hold SGLT2 inhibitors when the person is acutely ill.
- [ ] Possible ACS does not leave the office for a routine stress test.
- [ ] A solid testicular mass is a same-day referral.

## Common traps

- Adding a third antihypertensive without checking how the person actually takes the first two.
- Beta blocker as first-line hypertension therapy in an otherwise well adult. It is not, unless a cardiac reason exists.
- Stopping a pillar forever because of a small creatinine rise. A modest rise can be hemodynamic. A big rise means stop and look.
- Antibiotics for a painless testicular mass.

## Practice this in the Qbank

These items are visits. Write the comorbidity that picked the drug. Then do one CCS-style pass: what would you order in the first two minutes if this same person arrived short of breath. The feed card should name the comorbidity. A card that says “start metformin for all type 2” is not the 2026 Standards.

# Cardiometabolic disease in clinic and in the hospital

> **Study aid only.** The 2025 blood-pressure guideline, the ADA 2026 Standards, the heart-failure guideline, and KDIGO. A real patient’s numbers come from those documents, not from memory of this page.

## What this chapter covers

The continuing-care visit for blood pressure, lipids, diabetes, heart failure, and CKD, plus the urgent versions of the same diseases. Cardiovascular is about 9–11% of Step 3 MCQs. Endocrine is about 5–7%. Renal is smaller and travels with these drugs, so it lives here and again in CCS.

## The framework

A Step 3 cardiometabolic item is a visit, a home reading, and a comorbidity. The emergency version is the same disease with a disposition.

| Visit | What changes |
| --- | --- |
| Blood pressure, stage 1, no CVD, diabetes, or CKD, PREVENT risk under 7.5% | Lifestyle for 3 to 6 months, then medication if still at or above 130/80. 2025 AHA/ACC guideline. |
| Stage 1 with diabetes, CKD, clinical CVD, or PREVENT at least 7.5% | Start medication at or above 130/80. |
| Stage 2 | Medication, usually combination therapy, plus lifestyle. |
| On treatment, not at goal | Confirm the readings (home or ambulatory), then intensify. Do not restart the same monotherapy forever. |
| Clinical atherosclerotic disease | A high-intensity statin unless it cannot be used. Add-on therapy is the current cholesterol guideline when LDL stays high. Confirm the threshold. Do not invent one. |
| Heart failure, reduced ejection fraction, euvolemic | The pillars: ARNI or ACE inhibitor or ARB, evidence-based beta blocker, mineralocorticoid antagonist, SGLT2 inhibitor. A diuretic is for fluid, not a pillar. |
| Heart failure, wet and crashing | Airway, nitrates and diuretic as the pressure allows, and the cause. The pillars wait until the person can stand them. |
| Diabetes with heart failure, CKD, or atherosclerotic disease | Agent with evidence for that disease. Metformin is the common choice when glucose is the only goal. See the Step 2 endocrine chapter for the 2026 ADA wording. |
| Diabetes sick day | Hold the SGLT2 inhibitor. Hold metformin if the kidneys are failing or contrast is about to happen. Give basal insulin. Do not leave only a sliding scale. |
| CKD with albuminuria | ACE inhibitor or ARB, not both. SGLT2 inhibitor when the label and KDIGO allow the eGFR. |
| Chest pain in the office | If it could be ACS, the disposition is the emergency department, not a treadmill next week. |

Male reproductive health that belongs with this block because the outline pairs them: a painless testicular mass is cancer until imaged and referred, the same day, not after a course of antibiotics. A prostate symptom score is a quality-of-life conversation and a check for red flags (retention, hematuria, bone pain), not an automatic PSA. Erectile dysfunction is vascular disease until proven otherwise. Ask about the heart before you ask about the prescription.

## High-yield atlas

Step 3 cardiometabolic items are visits. Decide where the patient sleeps tonight, then which guideline changes the prescription. The emergency version of the same disease is in the Step 2 book. Stay here when the question is follow-up, risk, or a drug that should already be on the list.

| Presentation and findings | Next best step | First-line management and the source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Clinic 148/90 but home readings average 128/78, or the reverse. | Believe a properly taken home or ambulatory set over one clinic number. Treat the pattern, not the greeting. | 2025 AHA/ACC guideline. If home readings confirm stage 2, start combination therapy. If home readings are controlled, look for a white-coat pattern and do not stack drugs. |
| Ejection fraction 30%, no rales, weight at baseline, on a loop diuretic only. | Add the missing pillars the pressure and the kidney allow. | 2022 AHA/ACC/HFSA and later updates. ARNI or ACE inhibitor or ARB, evidence-based beta blocker, mineralocorticoid antagonist, SGLT2 inhibitor. Not both an ACE inhibitor and an ARB. |
| Type 2 diabetes plus heart failure, CKD, or a prior infarct. A1C above the individualized goal. | Pick the class with evidence for that comorbidity before you reach for a sulfonylurea. | ADA Standards of Care in Diabetes—2026. SGLT2 inhibitor for heart failure and for many with CKD. GLP-1 receptor agonist when atherosclerotic disease or weight is the driver. Metformin remains a common choice when glucose is the only job. |
| An irregular pulse found on a physical, rate 88, no pain, no edema, walking and talking. | ECG to confirm. Rate is already acceptable. Decide anticoagulation from stroke risk, not from how calm they look. Echo and a thyroid look are part of the new-diagnosis bundle. Confirm the current ACC/AHA list. | ACC/AHA atrial fibrillation guideline. Do not cardiovert an elective stable patient in the office. Anticoagulation is not optional just because you might do a rhythm strategy later. |
| A sudden loss of consciousness with a quick return, or a exertional faint, or a faint with no prodrome in a person with heart disease. | Exertional syncope, syncope without prodrome plus structural heart disease, or an abnormal ECG stays. A classic vasovagal faint with a prodrome in a young person with a normal exam and a normal ECG can go home with advice. | The ACC/AHA syncope guideline. Do not promise a cause you have not earned. A murmur of aortic stenosis plus exertional syncope is the valve until the echo says otherwise. |
| Pressure now, or pressure at rest today, sweat, or pain to the back with unequal pulses. | Emergency department. ECG. Aspirin if there is no contraindication and the pain could be ischemic. Not a treadmill next week. | 2025 ACC/AHA ACS guideline if the tracing or the story is ACS. Dissection is a different pathway and must not get fibrinolysis. |
| A patient after a stent who stopped the statin because of a story he read. | Ask what happened. Muscle symptoms get a check of the CK if they are real. Most people can be rechallenged or switched. Do not leave secondary prevention empty. | ACC/AHA cholesterol guideline. A true severe reaction is rare. A stopped statin after an infarct is a visit you do not adjourn. |
| Ankle swelling on a dihydropyridine calcium blocker, normal neck veins, no dyspnea. | Do not start a loop diuretic and a heart-failure workup as the first move. The drug dilates precapillary vessels. | Change the drug if the swelling bothers him. Look for heart failure only if the story has dyspnea, a gallop, or a raised venous pressure. |
| Calf pain at one block, relieved by rest, weak pulses, no rest pain, no ulcer. | Ankle-brachial index, a statin, an antiplatelet, a supervised walking plan, and a smoking offer. Vascular imaging if an intervention is planned or the limb is threatened. | AHA/ACC peripheral artery guideline. Rest pain or a wound is not a walking program. It is a threatened limb. |
| A patient who has never taken a beta blocker is given one on the morning of major surgery to make the numbers look calm. | Do not start a beta blocker on the morning of surgery in a beta-blocker-naive patient. | ACC/AHA perioperative guideline. Continue a beta blocker he already takes. Emergency surgery does not wait for a stress test. Do not revascularize a heart only to clear a low-risk elective case. |

| Pair | How you tell them apart |
| --- | --- |
| Home blood pressure not at goal versus the neighbor | The opposite log, high at home and calm in the office, is masked hypertension and does need treatment. |
| Heart failure follow-up, dry versus the neighbor | A wet visit is oxygen, a diuretic, and a search for the cause. Do not start a new beta blocker in flash edema. |
| Diabetes drug when the heart or the kidney is sick versus the neighbor | A sick day holds the SGLT2 inhibitor. Ketoacidosis can happen without a very high glucose. |
| New atrial fibrillation in clinic versus the neighbor | Rate 160 with chest pain is the emergency department and electricity, not this visit. |
| Syncope, the disposition versus the neighbor | A teenager who faints in a hot church after standing, with a prodrome of nausea and a normal exam, is the low-risk story. |
| Chest pain that started in the office versus the neighbor | A months-long predictable exertional pain that is gone today is a stable workup, still with an ECG. |
| Statin not taken versus the neighbor | Primary prevention in a low-risk person is a shared decision. This man already has a stent. |
| Edema that is not heart failure versus the neighbor | Orthopnea, a displaced apex, and a raised venous pressure are heart failure until proven otherwise. |
| Peripheral artery disease in clinic versus the neighbor | Pain at rest or a black toe is the emergency version. |
| Preoperative beta blocker, the wrong first dose versus the neighbor | He already takes metoprolol every day. That one is continued. |

### Home blood pressure not at goal

One cuff in a hurry is not the disease.

**Vignette.** A man is 156/94 in the office and frightened. His home log for two weeks is 126/76.
**Reasoning.** The log is the disease. Adding a drug for the office number is the trap. The diagnosis is Home blood pressure not at goal.
**NBME trap.** A new drug every visit based on the number taken in the hallway.
**Hook.** Treat the log.

### Heart failure follow-up, dry

The diuretic treated congestion. It did not remodel the ventricle.

**Vignette.** He is proud that his weight is stable. The medication list is furosemide and aspirin.
**Reasoning.** Dry is the moment for disease-modifying drugs. The trap is a second diuretic. The diagnosis is Heart failure follow-up, dry.
**NBME trap.** Calling a diuretic-only list complete because he feels fine today.
**Hook.** Dry weight unlocks the pillars.

### Diabetes drug when the heart or the kidney is sick

The comorbidity chooses the class.

**Vignette.** Ejection fraction 35%, A1C 8%, eGFR 55. He is on glipizide.
**Reasoning.** A sulfonylurea does not treat the heart failure. An SGLT2 inhibitor does. The trap is raising the sulfonylurea. The diagnosis is Diabetes drug when the heart or the kidney is sick.
**NBME trap.** Metformin first in a person whose main problem is heart failure.
**Hook.** Heart failure and CKD pick the class.

### New atrial fibrillation in clinic

Stroke risk is the disease you prevent. The rate today is fine.

**Vignette.** A 76-year-old with hypertension has an irregular pulse at a well visit. He feels normal. Pressure is 138/80.
**Reasoning.** Age and hypertension put him in a group that is anticoagulated in the current scoring approach. The trap is aspirin because he feels well, or a shock in the office. The diagnosis is New atrial fibrillation in clinic.
**NBME trap.** Aspirin as stroke prevention for atrial fibrillation.
**Hook.** Well and irregular still needs a stroke decision.

### Syncope, the disposition

The dangerous faint is cardiac. The common faint has a prodrome and a trigger.

**Vignette.** A 72-year-old blacked out while climbing stairs. He has a slow carotid upstroke. He feels fine now in the office.
**Reasoning.** Exertional syncope and a delayed pulse do not go home for a Holter next month. The diagnosis to chase is severe aortic stenosis. The trap is a vasovagal label because he recovered. The diagnosis is Syncope, the disposition.
**NBME trap.** A treadmill today for exertional syncope and a murmur of stenosis.
**Hook.** Effort and a murmur stay. A prodrome in a healthy young person can go.

### Chest pain that started in the office

Ongoing possible ACS is not a clinic problem.

**Vignette.** During a blood pressure visit he develops pressure and nausea. The pain is still there.
**Reasoning.** He does not finish the visit. The trap is a prescription for a proton-pump inhibitor and a ride home. The diagnosis is Chest pain that started in the office.
**NBME trap.** Scheduling the stress test before the ECG.
**Hook.** Pain now leaves the office.

### Statin not taken

Secondary prevention is not optional because of a headline.

**Vignette.** He had a stent six months ago. The bottle is full. He feels well.
**Reasoning.** Feeling well is the statin working, or luck. The trap is agreeing that he does not need it because the cholesterol was checked once. The diagnosis is Statin not taken.
**NBME trap.** A vacation from the statin with no plan.
**Hook.** A stent means the statin stays unless it truly cannot.

### Edema that is not heart failure

Not every swollen ankle is a weak ventricle.

**Vignette.** Amlodipine was started last month. The ankles are puffy by evening. He sleeps flat. The lungs are clear.
**Reasoning.** The timing and the flat sleep are the drug. The trap is an echocardiogram and a diuretic before you notice the new medicine. The diagnosis is Edema that is not heart failure.
**NBME trap.** Furosemide for every pair of socks that leave a mark.
**Hook.** New calcium blocker, flat sleep, evening ankles.

### Peripheral artery disease in clinic

Stable claudication is a medical disease plus walking.

**Vignette.** He stops at one block, the pain leaves, and he has no night pain. He still smokes.
**Reasoning.** This is claudication, not an emergency bypass. The highest-yield prescription is stopping tobacco and a walking plan plus the vascular drugs. The trap is an angiogram this week for stable distance pain. The diagnosis is Peripheral artery disease in clinic.
**NBME trap.** Cilostazol in a person with heart failure. It is contraindicated there.
**Hook.** Distance pain walks. Rest pain does not.

### Preoperative beta blocker, the wrong first dose

A new beta blocker on the day of surgery causes harm. Continuation is different from initiation.

**Vignette.** The preop note says start metoprolol this morning. He has never taken it. The case is an elective hernia. He climbs two flights.
**Reasoning.** He does not need a new beta blocker today, and he does not need a stress test. The trap is a drug to decorate the vital signs. The diagnosis is Preoperative beta blocker, the wrong first dose.
**NBME trap.** A cardiology clearance stress test for a patient who climbs stairs and is having a hernia.
**Hook.** Continue, do not initiate, on the morning of surgery.

## Checklist

- [ ] Home readings before you escalate a single clinic number.
- [ ] Stage 2 is medication now.
- [ ] Do not combine ACE inhibitor and ARB.
- [ ] Hold SGLT2 inhibitors when the person is acutely ill.
- [ ] Possible ACS does not leave the office for a routine stress test.
- [ ] A solid testicular mass is a same-day referral.

## Common traps

- Adding a third antihypertensive without checking how the person actually takes the first two.
- Beta blocker as first-line hypertension therapy in an otherwise well adult. It is not, unless a cardiac reason exists.
- Stopping a pillar forever because of a small creatinine rise. A modest rise can be hemodynamic. A big rise means stop and look.
- Antibiotics for a painless testicular mass.

## Practice this in the Qbank

These items are visits. Write the comorbidity that picked the drug. Then do one CCS-style pass: what would you order in the first two minutes if this same person arrived short of breath. The feed card should name the comorbidity. A card that says “start metformin for all type 2” is not the 2026 Standards.

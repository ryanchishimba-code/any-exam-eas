# Pediatrics and development

> **Study aid only.** A generalist sees children. The AAP and the CDC schedule are the sources. Human development is a small MCQ row and a real part of continuing care. Weight-based errors are how children are harmed. This page does not list doses.

## What this chapter covers

The well-child visit, the newborn problems that are surgical, and the adolescent visit that is actually confidential care. Step 3 expects you to function without a pediatric specialist in the hallway for the common problems, and to know when that is no longer true.

## The framework

Well-child care is growth, development, vaccines, safety, and one disease the age suggests. Plot the growth. A single point is not failure to thrive. A falling percentile is. Loss of a skill is not a variant. Refer.

| Age or complaint | Do not miss |
| --- | --- |
| Newborn, bilious vomiting | Malrotation until proven otherwise. Surgery. |
| Young infant, projectile non-bilious vomiting | Pyloric stenosis. Fix the electrolytes. Then operate. |
| Neonatal jaundice | Timing and the bilirubin fraction. Hemolysis and sepsis are the dangerous early causes. Phototherapy thresholds come from the current AAP chart, not from a color. |
| Bronchiolitis | Support. The AAP recommends against routine albuterol, steroids, and antibiotics. |
| Fever in a neonate | A real evaluation. Not reassurance. Use the current febrile-infant guideline. |
| Asthma or croup | See the Step 2 pediatrics chapter for the acute drugs. Disposition is whether the child is tiring. |
| Suspected abuse | Report. A mismatched story and a developmentally impossible injury are the clues. |
| Adolescent | Confidential time. HEADSS-style questions. Vaccines including HPV as the CDC schedule says. Contraception and STI care without a parent in the room when the law allows. Know your jurisdiction. Do not guess it. |
| Vaccines | The CDC schedule. Live vaccines are not for severe immunosuppression. Pertussis vaccine in every pregnancy protects the newborn who is too young to be vaccinated. |
| Dehydration | Oral rehydration if the child drinks. Intravenous if not. |

Development you should be able to defend, then confirm against the current CDC milestones: social smile early, sitting around the middle of the first year, walking and a few words by about a year and a half, and any loss of skill at any time. The exact month on a revised checklist beats the month in an old lecture.

## Checklist

- [ ] Bilious vomiting is an emergency.
- [ ] Plot growth. Look at the trend.
- [ ] Loss of skill is a referral.
- [ ] Bronchiolitis is supportive care.
- [ ] Report suspected abuse. Do not investigate it alone.

## Common traps

- Adult fluid and drug math.
- Aspirin for a viral fever in a child.
- Reassurance for a falling growth curve because today’s weight is “on the chart.”
- HPV vaccine skipped because the adolescent is “not sexually active.” The point is to give it before exposure. Follow the schedule.

## Practice this in the Qbank

Pediatric Step 3 items are a generalist’s scope: finish the otitis, refer the bilious vomit, report the bruise that cannot be explained. CCS pediatric cases should include weight, a glucose when the child is altered, and a dose you look up rather than invent. The feed should not be your vaccine schedule. The CDC page is.

# A Step 3 study plan

> **Study aid only.** A way to divide two different days. Not a forecast.

## What this chapter covers

How to aim a limited number of weeks at Day 1 evidence and Day 2 management, including CCS, without repeating the entire Step 2 book.

## The framework

You already have a clinical base if you are eligible for this exam. The holes are usually specific.

1. **Biostatistics and the paper in the stem.** Largest MCQ range. Do it early and again late.
2. **Continuing care.** Chronic disease over months: diabetes, blood pressure, heart failure, COPD, depression, contraception, prenatal care. The question is the visit, not the emergency you already know.
3. **Disposition.** Home, floor, ICU, operating room, or a consultant. Step 3 loves this because unsupervised practice is a disposition.
4. **CCS reps on the official interface.** A case a day once the books’ CCS chapter is familiar. Quality of the sequence beats the number of cases.
5. **Do not restudy every Step 1 pathway.** Restudy the ones Day 1 still asks: a mechanism that changes a drug, a bug, or a risk.

| Week | Emphasis |
| --- | --- |
| Early | One biostatistics block plus one chronic-disease chapter. A short CCS just to learn the buttons. |
| Middle | Systems by the Step 3 weights: heart, lung, neuro, pregnancy, gut. Prevention folded into each, not saved for the end. |
| Late | Mixed Day-1-style items (abstracts, ethics) and timed CCS. Stop adding new tables. |

The Step 2 book in this series is the differential and the acute guideline. Use it when a Step 3 miss was “I did not know the disease.” Stay in this book when the miss was “I knew the disease and sent them to the wrong place” or “I could not manage the clock.”

## Checklist

- [ ] Biostatistics is on the calendar in week one.
- [ ] CCS is practiced on the official-style interface, not only read about.
- [ ] Each chronic disease has a continuing-care visit, not only an emergency.
- [ ] You can say where the patient sleeps tonight.

## Common traps

- A second full pass of a Step 2 book with no CCS and no papers.
- Collecting CCS “high-yield orders” that you would never place on a real person. Shotgun orders are not good medicine and they are not the point.
- Ignoring male reproductive and skin because the ranges look small. A single missed torsion or melanoma still counts.

## Practice this in the Qbank

Label every miss with the day it belongs to. Day 1 misses go to the evidence and ethics chapters. Day 2 misses go to the system chapter and then to a CCS case of the same complaint. The feed is the reminder for a risk cutoff you keep forgetting, after the chapter has told you which guideline owns it.

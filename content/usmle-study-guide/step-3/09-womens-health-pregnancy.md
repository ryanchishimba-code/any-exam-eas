# Women's health and pregnancy

> **Study aid only.** ACOG for pregnancy. CDC Medical Eligibility Criteria for contraception. USPSTF for the screening ages, which differ from some specialty advice. Pregnancy and the female reproductive system are about 7–9% of Step 3 MCQs. CCS cases include prenatal visits and bleeding.

## What this chapter covers

The prenatal visit, the hypertensive disorders, contraception in a generalist clinic, and the gynecologic problems you either finish or send today. The Step 2 obstetrics chapter holds the emergency detail. This one holds the independent-practice visit.

## The framework

A prenatal visit is blood pressure, symptoms of preeclampsia (headache, visual change, right-upper-quadrant pain, swelling that is sudden), fetal movement when it is time, and the lab that is due. Severe features mean the hospital and magnesium, not a repeat pressure tomorrow. Magnesium toxicity is lost reflexes then respiratory depression. Calcium is the antidote.

| Visit | Finish it yourself or move the person |
| --- | --- |
| Contraception, migraine with aura, or clot history, or smoking over 35 | No estrogen. The CDC chart is the authority. A long-acting method is still available. |
| Prenatal labs | Type and antibody, hemoglobin, HIV, syphilis, hepatitis B, rubella. Treat HIV and syphilis. Do not calendar them for after delivery. |
| Rh-negative | Anti-D at the routine time and after bleeding. |
| GBS | Late-pregnancy screen and intrapartum treatment as ACOG directs. |
| Postpartum check | Mood, bleeding, blood pressure, contraception, and the vaccine that was missed. Preeclampsia can present after birth. |
| Postmenopausal bleeding | Cancer until an endometrial assessment says otherwise. Not reassurance. |
| Breast mass | Imaging that matches age. A negative mammogram does not erase a palpable mass. |
| Screening mammogram | USPSTF: offer every 2 years from 40 to 74. |
| Cervical screen | Start at 21. Interval by age and test. |
| First-trimester bleeding, unstable or pain with dizziness | Ectopic pathway. Emergency. |
| Third-trimester bleeding | Previa until the ultrasound says no. No digital exam. Abruption if painful. Hospital. |

Gestational diabetes screening follows ACOG. Do not mix one-step and two-step cutoffs. Blood pressure treatment in pregnancy is not the adult PREVENT threshold. Use ACOG’s thresholds. The 2025 adult guideline does not run the prenatal clinic.

Medication abortion and emergency contraception are different interventions. Do not describe one as the other. Follow the current FDA label and your jurisdiction’s law. This book is not a legal guide and not a protocol.

## High-yield atlas

A prenatal visit on Step 3 is a blood pressure, the symptoms of preeclampsia, fetal movement when it is time, and the lab that is due. Severe features mean the hospital and magnesium, not a repeat pressure tomorrow. The adult PREVENT threshold of 130/80 does not run the prenatal clinic. Use ACOG. Contraception uses the CDC Medical Eligibility Criteria. Screening ages use USPSTF. CCS prenatal cases fail when HIV or syphilis is “deferred until after delivery,” and when a third-trimester bleed gets a digital exam.

### The hypertensive disorders are different clocks

Gestational hypertension, preeclampsia without severe features, and preeclampsia with severe features are not one disease. Severe features — severe-range pressure, symptoms, laboratory injury, pulmonary edema — mean delivery planning and magnesium for seizure prevention. Magnesium toxicity is lost reflexes, then respiratory depression. Calcium gluconate is the antidote. Eclampsia is the seizure. The treatment is still magnesium, the airway, and delivery. Do not load phenytoin as the plan. Chronic hypertension that long predates the pregnancy is a different label and still uses ACOG’s prenatal thresholds, not the adult stage-1 rule.

| What you see | Where she sleeps | The order and the source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Severe features, or a seizure | Hospital. Magnesium. Delivery planning | [ACOG](https://www.acog.org/) hypertensive disorders |
| Lost reflexes on magnesium | Stop the infusion. Calcium gluconate | Toxicity before respiratory arrest |
| Elevated pressure, no severe features, reliable follow-up | The ACOG outpatient path, not a shrug | Not the adult 130/80 PREVENT rule |
| Headache and a high pressure on day 4 after birth | Hospital. Postpartum preeclampsia is real | The discharge visit does not clear it |

**Vignette.** She is 34 weeks. Blood pressure is 168/112. She has a headache and a new ache under the right ribs. Platelets are falling. A nurse offers a clinic appointment tomorrow because the fetal heart rate is reassuring.
**Reasoning.** Severe features. Hospital, magnesium, and a delivery plan. The fetal heart rate being reassuring does not move her to tomorrow. The diagnosis is preeclampsia with severe features. The trap is labetalol in the office and a next-day visit.
**NBME trap.** They treat the pressure to a normal adult target and skip magnesium because she has not seized. Magnesium is for prevention. The seizure is not the entry criterion.
**Hook.** Symptoms, labs, or severe-range pressure: magnesium and a delivery plan. Reflexes gone: calcium.
**CCS.** Magnesium, labs, fetal monitoring, blood pressure control with an obstetric-safe drug, delivery planning, calcium at the bedside, do not send her home.

### Bleeding in pregnancy

Bleeding with pain in early pregnancy is ectopic until an intrauterine pregnancy is seen. Unstable means surgery. Methotrexate is for the stable person who can follow up and meets the criteria. Shoulder pain and dizziness are rupture clues. Painless third-trimester bleeding is placenta previa until ultrasound says the placenta is clear of the cervix. No digital exam. Painful third-trimester bleeding is abruption. She can be in shock before the blood on the pad looks like much.

**Vignette.** Eight weeks. One-sided pain and light bleeding. She is dizzy when she stands. The ultrasound does not yet show a pregnancy inside the uterus. Pressure is 88/50.
**Reasoning.** Surgery. She is unstable and the uterus is empty. The diagnosis is a ruptured ectopic pregnancy until the operation says otherwise. The trap is methotrexate in a hypotensive patient, or a quantitative hCG and a ride home.
**NBME trap.** They do a digital cervical exam for painless third-trimester bleeding before the ultrasound. That exam is how a previa hemorrhages. Ultrasound first.
**Hook.** Empty uterus, pain, and a falling pressure: the operating room. Painless late bleeding: ultrasound, no fingers.
**CCS.** Two lines, type and screen, obstetric surgery, ultrasound if it will not delay the unstable patient, no methotrexate for shock, no digital exam for late painless bleeding.

### The labs that are treatment, not a recheck

Type and antibody screen, hemoglobin, HIV, syphilis, hepatitis B, and rubella immunity are the early panel. A positive syphilis or HIV test in pregnancy is treatment now. Congenital syphilis and perinatal HIV are what delay looks like. Rh-negative mothers get anti-D at the routine antenatal time and after any bleeding or procedure that can mix blood. A positive antibody screen is a different, higher-risk path. Do not give routine anti-D and call a sensitized patient protected. GBS is a late-pregnancy screen and treatment in labor when the screen is positive or a risk factor says so. Confirm the ACOG timing rather than inventing a week.

**Vignette.** The HIV test is positive at the first prenatal visit. She feels well. A note says “repeat after delivery and refer then.”
**Reasoning.** Treat now. The diagnosis is HIV in pregnancy, and the intervention that protects the infant is treatment during the pregnancy, not a postpartum confirmation as the first step. Confirm the current regimen. Do not invent it.
**NBME trap.** They schedule syphilis treatment for the postpartum visit. Congenital syphilis is the cost of that calendar.
**Hook.** Positive HIV or syphilis: treat in this pregnancy. Rh-negative and bleeding: anti-D, unless she is already sensitized.
**CCS.** Treat the positive test, antibody screen, plan anti-D if she is negative and unsensitized, GBS when the gestational age says so, do not advance the clock past an untreated infection.

### Gestational diabetes is one method, not a blend

Screen as ACOG directs, usually mid-pregnancy, earlier if risk is high. The thresholds are the two-step Carpenter-Coustan path or the one-step 75-gram path. Do not mix the numbers. This chapter does not print them, because mixing them is the actual error. Treatment is diet, then insulin or the oral agent your obstetric protocol allows. Confirm rather than importing a nonpregnant A1C target into the third trimester.

**Vignette.** She failed a one-step test. Someone applies the two-step cutoff to the same number and clears her.
**Reasoning.** The methods are not interchangeable. Use the cutoff of the test she took. The diagnosis is gestational diabetes if that test’s own threshold says so. The trap is shopping for the cutoff that reassures.
**NBME trap.** They set an A1C goal under 7 percent, copied from nonpregnant adults, as the prenatal target. Pregnancy targets are ACOG’s, not the ADA line for the general adult.
**Hook.** One test, one cutoff. Do not average the two methods.

### Postpartum is a visit with four jobs

Mood, bleeding, blood pressure, and contraception. Preeclampsia can present after birth. A hemorrhage is tone, trauma, tissue, and thrombin. Massage and a uterotonic for atony. Methylergonovine is a poor choice when the pressure is high. Look at the laceration and the placenta. A fever and a tender uterus, especially after a cesarean, is endometritis, polymicrobial, and a hospital antibiotic. It is not a pelvic rest pamphlet.

**Vignette.** Day 6 after a vaginal birth. A severe headache and a blood pressure of 170/110. She was normotensive in labor. The bleeding is normal lochia.
**Reasoning.** Postpartum preeclampsia. Hospital and magnesium. The diagnosis is postpartum preeclampsia. The trap is a migraine diagnosis because the pregnancy is “over,” or methylergonovine for a small amount of bleeding in a woman whose real problem is the pressure.
**NBME trap.** They give methylergonovine for atony to a woman whose pressure is already 160. Choose a uterotonic she can survive.
**Hook.** After birth, a high pressure is still preeclampsia. Atony gets a uterotonic the pressure allows.
**CCS.** Blood pressure, magnesium if severe, mood screen, contraception that matches her risk, a uterotonic for atony, examine for trauma and retained tissue.

### Contraception is a chart, not a favorite method

No estrogen if she has migraine with aura, a clot history, or she smokes and is over 35. Some early postpartum days also forbid estrogen. The CDC Medical Eligibility Criteria are the authority. A long-acting reversible method is the most effective and is still available when estrogen is not. Emergency contraception is time-sensitive and is not medication abortion. Do not describe one as the other. This book is not a legal guide and not a protocol for medication abortion. Follow the current label and the jurisdiction’s law when that is the actual question, and do not invent either.

**Vignette.** She is 36, smokes, and has migraine with aura. She wants a combined oral contraceptive because her sister uses one.
**Reasoning.** Two reasons, either of which would do, forbid estrogen. Offer a progestin or nonhormonal method, and a long-acting one if she wants the lowest failure. The diagnosis is a medical ineligibility for combined hormonal contraception. The trap is “she is careful” as a reason to ignore the chart.
**NBME trap.** They call emergency contraception an abortion. It is not. They also withhold every method, including a copper or progestin device, because estrogen was unsafe.
**Hook.** Aura, clot, or smoking over 35: no estrogen. A long-acting method is still on the table.
**CCS.** The chart, the method she can use, counseling, no combined pill as a kindness.

### The breast, the cervix, and postmenopausal bleeding

USPSTF: offer mammography every two years from 40 to 74. A palpable mass is not a screening question. Image it in the way that matches her age, and a negative mammogram does not erase a mass you can feel. Cervical screening starts at 21. Cytology every 3 years from 21 to 29. From 30 to 65, cytology every 3 years, primary HPV every 5, or co-testing every 5, until USPSTF replaces the 2018 recommendation. Postmenopausal bleeding is endometrial cancer until a sampling says otherwise. Do not reassure.

**Vignette.** She is 58 and has not bled in six years. A week of spotting. The exam shows no obvious cervical lesion. She feels well.
**Reasoning.** Endometrial assessment. The diagnosis is postmenopausal bleeding, cancer until proven otherwise. The trap is a repeat exam in three months because the amount was small.
**NBME trap.** They order a screening mammogram as the answer to a palpable lump, and they accept a normal report as clearance. Palpable is diagnostic imaging and a tissue plan, not the screening pathway.
**Hook.** Spotting after menopause: sample the endometrium. A lump: the mammogram does not retire your fingers.
**CCS.** Endometrial biopsy or the imaging-plus-biopsy path your gynecology guidance uses, do not advance to reassurance. For the lump: age-appropriate imaging and a biopsy plan that survives a normal film.

### Infections and the pelvis

Pelvic inflammatory disease that she can tolerate orally, with no abscess and no pregnancy, can be outpatient treatment with follow-up. Pregnancy, vomiting, a tubo-ovarian abscess, or failed oral therapy is a hospital. Treat partners. Test for HIV and syphilis when you find one of these. A Bartholin abscess is drainage. Antibiotics do not replace the knife.

**Vignette.** Cervical motion tenderness, fever, and a pregnancy test that is positive. She is vomiting.
**Reasoning.** This is not outpatient pelvic inflammatory disease. Pregnancy changes the diagnosis list, including ectopic, and she cannot keep oral medicine down. Hospital. The diagnosis is pelvic infection in pregnancy until the location of the pregnancy and the infection are clear. The trap is the outpatient ceftriaxone-and-doxycycline card copied onto a pregnant patient. Doxycycline is not the casual choice in pregnancy. Confirm the regimen.
**NBME trap.** They treat the patient and forget the partner, then forget the HIV test. One infection travels with others.
**Hook.** Pregnant, vomiting, or an abscess: she stays. A partner and an HIV test either way.

### A second set of stems

**Vignette.** She is Rh-negative. The antibody screen is already positive. She has spotting at 12 weeks. Someone orders routine anti-D and says the problem is handled.
**Reasoning.** She is sensitized. Routine anti-D does not fix an antibody that is already there. This is the higher-risk path. The diagnosis is Rh sensitization, not a missed routine dose.
**Hook.** A positive antibody screen is not a cue for the routine shot. It is a different pregnancy.

**Vignette.** Shoulder dystocia. A teammate pushes hard on the fundus.
**Reasoning.** Fundal pressure is the wrong direction. The maneuvers are the ones that relieve the shoulder, not more force from above. The diagnosis is shoulder dystocia. Call for help. Do not invent a force.
**Hook.** Fundal pressure worsens the shoulder you are trying to free.

**Vignette.** A snowstorm ultrasound and a very high hCG. No fetus.
**Reasoning.** Molar pregnancy. Evacuation and follow-up of the hCG. The diagnosis is a hydatidiform mole. The trap is methotrexate dosing as if this were a routine ectopic, or a promise that the pregnancy will appear if you wait.
**Hook.** Snowstorm, no fetus, hCG out of proportion: evacuate and follow the number down.

**Vignette.** She wants a tubal ligation and her husband is not in the room. She has capacity and can explain the permanence.
**Reasoning.** She decides. A spouse does not sign for a capacitated adult. The diagnosis is a capacitated request for sterilization. The trap is requiring his consent.
**Hook.** Capacity and permanence understood: her signature. Not his.

**Vignette.** Postpartum day 2. Fever, uterine tenderness, a cesarean yesterday. The incision looks clean.
**Reasoning.** Endometritis. Polymicrobial antibiotics. The diagnosis is postpartum endometritis. The trap is opening a clean incision because fever means “wound” on a mnemonic. Look, and then treat the uterus.
**Hook.** Tender uterus after a cesarean: the uterine cavity. A clean incision can stay closed.

**Vignette.** She is 42 and smokes. Migraines without aura. No clot history. She wants a combined pill.
**Reasoning.** Smoking over 35 is enough to forbid estrogen even without aura. The diagnosis is medical ineligibility for a combined hormonal method. Aura would have been a second reason. She does not need both.
**Hook.** One MEC prohibition is enough. Do not wait for a second strike.

**Vignette.** A breast mass at 28. Ultrasound is the usual first image at this age. It is reported benign in wording that does not match the exam, and the mass is still there.
**Reasoning.** A mass you can feel survives a reassuring sentence. Tissue or a repeat imaging path that actually explains the lump. The diagnosis is a palpable breast mass that has not been cleared. The trap is “benign imaging” as a goodbye.
**Hook.** Fingers beat the report when they disagree.

**Vignette.** She is due for a mammogram at 76. She asks you to follow the task force.
**Reasoning.** USPSTF found the evidence insufficient at 75 and older. That is an I statement, not a secret grade B, and not a prohibition you invent. Discuss rather than pretend the 40-to-74 recommendation extended itself. The diagnosis is a screening question outside the grade B age band.
**Hook.** Forty to seventy-four is the offer. Seventy-six is a conversation, not an automatic order and not an automatic refusal written as law.

## Checklist

- [ ] Preeclampsia symptoms are asked, not assumed absent.
- [ ] Syphilis and HIV in pregnancy are treated now.
- [ ] Postmenopausal bleeding is evaluated.
- [ ] A palpable breast mass survives a negative mammogram.
- [ ] Estrogen is withheld when the CDC chart says so.

## Common traps

- Adult hypertension targets applied to a pregnant patient.
- Stopping all psychiatric medicines at the first positive pregnancy test, including one the person needs to stay well.
- Reassurance for postmenopausal bleeding because the last Pap was normal. The Pap is not an endometrial test.
- A feed card that starts cervical screening at the onset of sexual activity.

## Practice this in the Qbank

Prenatal items: gestational age plus one danger. Clinic items: screening or a contraindication. CCS prenatal cases should include the blood pressure, the symptom screen, and the lab that is due, not a shotgun of imaging. The feed can hold a contraindication to estrogen. Check it against the CDC chart.

# Psychiatry and substance use

## Framework

Safety, then the drug that can be taken next week, then the follow-up. The syndrome table in the Step 2 book still applies. Step 3 asks you to notice who is withdrawing in the hospital and who is unsafe to discharge.

| Situation | Orders |
| --- | --- |
| Suicidal plan or recent attempt | Stay. One-to-one or the equivalent. Do not discharge because they "contract for safety." |
| Alcohol withdrawal, autonomic | Benzodiazepine protocol. Thiamine before glucose. Recheck the score. CIWA is a tool, not a substitute for looking at the patient. |
| Opioid use, not in acute apnea | Buprenorphine is a treatment, not a moral reward. Start when withdrawal is present if you are inducing. Naloxone in the hand of a patient who uses opioids. |
| Depression, pregnant | Screen (USPSTF). An SSRI can be appropriate. Do not stop a needed antidepressant the day you learn of a pregnancy without a plan. Valproate is not a casual mood stabilizer in a patient who can become pregnant. |
| Psychosis, refusing, dangerous | Emergency hold follows your state's process. The principle on the exam is safety, not a speech about autonomy that leaves a dangerous patient in the lobby. |
| Grief | Not automatically a major depressive episode. Function and duration matter. Do not start a drug to be seen doing something. |

## Checklist

- [ ] Do not discharge an active suicidal plan.
- [ ] Thiamine, benzodiazepine, recheck, for alcohol withdrawal.
- [ ] Naloxone for the patient, not only for the code cart.
- [ ] Valproate and pregnancy are a serious mismatch.
- [ ] A safety contract is not a disposition.

## Common traps

- "Contract for safety" as the answer.
- Glucose before thiamine in a patient with alcohol use disorder.
- Stopping all psychiatric drugs the moment a pregnancy test is positive, with no replacement plan.

# Prevention, prognosis, and decisions

## Framework

Health maintenance and prognosis are large Step 3 ranges. Ethics is what you do when the guideline and the person disagree.

**Screens worth memorizing as intervals**

| Screen | Use this posted recommendation |
| --- | --- |
| Colon | 45–75, average risk. Positive stool test means colonoscopy. ([USPSTF](https://www.uspreventiveservicestaskforce.org/uspstf/recommendation/colorectal-cancer-screening)) |
| Breast | Every 2 years, 40–74. ([USPSTF](https://www.uspreventiveservicestaskforce.org/uspstf/recommendation/breast-cancer-screening)) |
| Lung | Annual low-dose CT, 50–80, 20 pack-years, smoking now or quit within 15 years. ([USPSTF](https://www.uspreventiveservicestaskforce.org/uspstf/recommendation/lung-cancer-screening)) |
| Prostate | 55–69, a decision. Not routine at 70 and older. ([USPSTF](https://www.uspreventiveservicestaskforce.org/uspstf/recommendation/prostate-cancer-screening)) |
| AAA | One-time ultrasound, men 65–75 who have smoked. |
| HIV | At least once. |
| Hepatitis C | Once, 18–79. |

A symptom voids the screening interval. Work up the symptom.

**Prognosis**

Say what you know: this disease, this function, this trajectory. Do not invent a percent chance of living a year. The exam wants the honest frame and the matching plan (curative, life-prolonging, or comfort). Comfort includes treating pain and dyspnea. It is not abandonment.

**Decisions**

A capable adult can refuse. A surrogate uses the patient's wishes. Tell the truth about errors that reached the patient. Offer an interpreter. A goals-of-care talk is an order you place before a crisis when the trajectory is clear. Children of adults do not automatically outrank a named health-care agent.

## Checklist

- [ ] Symptom beats screening interval.
- [ ] Do not invent a survival percent.
- [ ] Comfort includes symptom treatment.
- [ ] Capable refusal stands.
- [ ] Name the decision-maker before the family meeting.

## Common traps

- A screening colonoscopy in a patient with rectal bleeding, as if they were average risk and asymptomatic.
- "There is nothing more we can do" when pain has not been treated.
- Letting the loudest relative override an agent's decision.

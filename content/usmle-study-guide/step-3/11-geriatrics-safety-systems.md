# Geriatrics, goals of care, and systems

> **Study aid only.** Social sciences are about 7–9% of Step 3 MCQs. Day 1 scores communication, ethics, and safety on purpose. This is not your state’s statute book.

## What this chapter covers

The older adult as a physiologic setup, the goals-of-care conversation, and the systems answers that feel like etiquette and are actually the key.

## The framework

Aging changes the therapeutic index. Kidneys clear less. Falls follow drugs that sedate or drop the pressure. Delirium follows infection, a new medicine, or a full bladder. A new antipsychotic for “agitation” is often the cause of the next fall. Start low, but do not under-treat pain or depression because of age. Under-treatment is its own harm.

| Situation | The move |

| Many medicines | Reconcile. Stop the one with no current indication, especially an anticholinergic, a benzodiazepine, or a duplicate. |
| Fall | Orthostatic pressure, drugs, vision, footwear, and a home hazard. A CT is for the head injury or the focal sign, not for every trip. |
| Goals of care | What the person understands, what they are hoping for, what they would not want, and who speaks if they cannot. Do this before the crisis when you can. |
| Code status | A recommendation is allowed. “What do you want us to do?” with no medical frame is not informed consent. |
| A surrogate and a living person with capacity | The person with capacity decides. The surrogate does not overrule them. |
| Interpreter | A professional. Not the child. |
| Harmful error | Disclose it. |
| Impaired colleague | A real reporting path. Not a permanent silent cover. |
| Time-out | Stop the line. |
| Discharge of an older adult | Teach-back, a reconciled list, the daughter or facility who will actually give the medicines, and a follow-up that is on a calendar. |
| Hospice versus palliative care | Hospice is a prognosis and a goal. Palliative care is the symptom skill and can happen alongside treatment. Do not wait for hospice to treat pain. |

Systems items also include a quality gap: an infection rate, a readmission, a missed screening. The answer is often a checklist, a protocol, or a measured change, not “try harder.” Practice-based learning on Day 1 is this muscle.

## High-yield atlas

Aging narrows the therapeutic index. Kidneys clear less. Falls follow drugs that sedate or drop the pressure. Delirium follows a new medicine, an infection, or a full bladder. A new antipsychotic for agitation is often the cause of the next fall. Start low when the drug requires it, and do not under-treat pain or depression because of age. Under-treatment is its own harm. Day 1 of Step 3 scores this chapter on purpose: communication, a goals conversation, and a system that is a checklist rather than a plea to try harder.

### The medication list is the disease

Reconcile every visit. Stop the drug with no current indication, especially an anticholinergic, a benzodiazepine, or a duplicate. Diphenhydramine for sleep, a bladder drug that was never revisited, and a benzodiazepine for a grief that ended last year are the usual finds. The AGS Beers Criteria name high-risk classes. Do not invent a cutoff dose. Pain still gets treated. Depression still gets treated. “He is old” is not a contraindication to a drug that would help, and it is not a reason to continue a drug that only sedates.

| What you see | What you change | The source of the habit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Many drugs, a fall, and an anticholinergic | Stop the anticholinergic. Review the rest | AGS Beers Criteria. High-risk classes, not a homemade dose cap |
| Severe depression at 80 | Treat it. An SSRI is still allowed | Under-treatment is a harm |
| Severe pain at the end of life | Dose the opioid to the pain | Fear of respiratory depression is managed by skill, not by leaving the pain |
| A duplicate proton-pump inhibitor and a stopped indication | Stop it | Reconciliation is the visit |

**Vignette.** He is 84. He fell last week. The list includes diphenhydramine at night, oxybutynin, and a benzodiazepine from a hospitalization two years ago. He is drowsy at noon.
**Reasoning.** Stop the sedating, anticholinergic drugs that have no current job. The diagnosis is a medication-related fall risk. The trap is a brain MRI as the first move, or a new antipsychotic for the daytime sleepiness the drugs are causing.
**NBME trap.** They stop his antidepressant because “older adults should not take psychiatric drugs,” while continuing the benzodiazepine. The benzodiazepine is the Beers problem. The antidepressant may be the treatment.
**Hook.** Sedating drugs with no job: stop them. Pain and depression: do not.
**CCS.** Reconcile, stop the offenders, orthostatic vitals, fall precautions, follow-up. Do not add a drug for each symptom the last drug caused.

### A fall is a medication, a pressure, and a home

Orthostatic blood pressure, the drug list, vision, footwear, and a home hazard. A CT is for a head injury or a focal sign, not for every trip over a rug. Orthostatic hypotension is a drop of at least 20 mm Hg systolic or 10 mm Hg diastolic within three minutes of standing. That definition is the measurement, not a reason to start midodrine before you have stopped the culprit drug. Urge incontinence gets a bladder-training plan and, if a drug is used, a bladder relaxant whose fall risk you have said out loud. Overflow from an anticholinergic is the opposite problem. Do not add another anticholinergic to a bladder that will not empty.

**Vignette.** She stands and the systolic pressure falls from 148 to 118. She is on a high-dose alpha blocker for blood pressure and tamsulosin. No head strike. The neurologic exam is normal.
**Reasoning.** Orthostatic hypotension from two drugs that drop the pressure. Stop or reduce them. The diagnosis is drug-related orthostasis. The trap is a head CT and a carotid study for a trip with no injury and no focal sign.
**NBME trap.** They add oxybutynin for urgency in a man who already cannot empty, and they call the frequency “overactive bladder.” A high residual is overflow. The anticholinergic makes it worse.
**Hook.** Measure the standing pressure. Image the head when the head was hurt or the exam is focal. A residual tells you which incontinence you have.
**CCS.** Orthostatics, medication changes, home safety, physical therapy if gait is the issue, no CT for an uncomplicated trip.

### Delirium, dementia, and the family who wants a pill

Acute and fluctuating is delirium. Find the infection, the drug, the bladder, the oxygen, the sodium, the withdrawal. Chronic and progressive is dementia. B12, thyroid, depression, and medicines still get checked. A cholinesterase inhibitor is modest. Say so. Do not promise a reversal. Do not start an antipsychotic so the night shift is quieter. If agitation is dangerous, the lowest dose for the shortest time, after the cause has been chased.

**Vignette.** He has known mild dementia and was himself at dinner. At midnight he is shouting. A new sleeping pill was given at 9. The oxygen saturation is 89 percent. The nurse asks for haloperidol.
**Reasoning.** This is delirium. The new pill and the hypoxia are causes. Oxygen and stop the pill. The diagnosis is delirium superimposed on dementia. The trap is a new antipsychotic as the plan and a note that “the dementia worsened.”
**NBME trap.** They treat chronic nighttime wandering, present for a year, with the same emergency workup as an acute delirium, and they also do the reverse: they call an acute change “just his dementia.” The baseline sentence is the fork.
**Hook.** Yesterday he was himself: find the cause. A year of the same pattern: safety and an honest drug conversation, not a nightly antipsychotic.
**CCS.** The cause, oxygen, the bladder, medication review, nonpharmacologic care, family counseling that a cholinesterase inhibitor is modest.

### Goals, code status, and who speaks

Ask what the person understands, what they are hoping for, what they would not want, and who speaks if they cannot. Do this before the crisis when you can. A recommendation is allowed. “What do you want us to do?” with no medical frame is not informed consent. If the person has capacity, the person decides. A surrogate does not overrule them. If capacity is gone, the surrogate uses substituted judgment: what the patient would have wanted, not what the family would want for themselves. Best interests are the fallback when that is unknowable. Hospice is a prognosis and a goal. Palliative care is the symptom skill and can happen alongside disease treatment. Do not wait for hospice to treat pain.

**Vignette.** He has capacity and has said he does not want intubation. His son, who just arrived, demands “everything.” The patient repeats the refusal.
**Reasoning.** The patient decides. The diagnosis is a capacitated refusal. The trap is following the loudest relative. Document, and keep treating everything he did not refuse.
**NBME trap.** They withhold opioid pain medicine until a hospice enrollment is complete. Palliative care does not wait for a prognosis form. Dose to the pain.
**Hook.** Capacity beats the family. Pain treatment does not wait for hospice. Hospice is a goal. Palliative care is a skill you can use today.
**CCS.** The goals conversation, a recommendation, the order that matches what he chose, pain treatment now, no intubation if that is the refusal.

### Systems, errors, and the older discharge

A professional interpreter, not the child. Disclose a harmful error: what happened, what it means, what you will do. An impaired colleague gets a real reporting path, not a permanent silent cover. A time-out stops the line. Discharge of an older adult is teach-back, a reconciled list, the person who will actually give the medicines, and a follow-up that is on a calendar. “Any questions?” is not teach-back. A quality gap, an infection rate or a readmission, is answered with a checklist or a measured change, not with “try harder.”

**Vignette.** He is going home on warfarin, a new antibiotic, and insulin. His daughter nods. No one has heard him say the plan. The daughter lives two states away. A neighbor “might check.”
**Reasoning.** Teach-back, and a person who will actually be there. The diagnosis is an unsafe discharge. The trap is a nod counted as understanding, and a follow-up of “call us if you need us.”
**NBME trap.** They use the bilingual grandson to explain a new cancer diagnosis. A child is not the interpreter. A professional is.
**Hook.** Teach-back means he says it back. The person giving the pills is named. A child does not interpret the news.
**CCS.** Reconcile, teach-back, a real follow-up appointment, home services if he cannot manage, interpreter if needed, do not advance the clock on a nod.

### Weight loss, wounds, and abuse

Unintentional weight loss is a workup, not an appetite lecture. Cancer, depression, a drug, a dental problem, and not enough food at home are the list. Pressure injury: stage 1 is intact nonblanchable redness, stage 2 is partial thickness, stage 3 is full thickness, stage 4 reaches bone. Unstageable is eschar. Offload and feed. Do not massage a deep injury and call it circulation. Elder abuse is a report. A bruise that does not match the story, a fear of a caregiver, or a depletion of the person’s money with a new “friend” is enough suspicion to act. Confirm the local reporting path. Do not invent a statute.

**Vignette.** She has lost 20 pounds in four months. She says she is “just not hungry.” A caregiver answers every question for her and will not leave the room. There is a bruise on the upper arm in the shape of a hand.
**Reasoning.** The weight loss is a medical workup, and the bruise plus the control of the interview is a report. The diagnosis is unintentional weight loss and suspected elder abuse. The trap is an appetite stimulant and a private shrug.
**NBME trap.** They stage an eschar-covered wound as a stage 2 because the visible rim looks shallow. Eschar is unstageable until it is off. Do not guess the depth.
**Hook.** Weight loss is a disease until proven otherwise. A hand-shaped bruise is a report. Eschar is not a stage you invent.
**CCS.** The medical workup of weight loss, nutrition, wound offloading, a private interview, the report, do not send her home with the same caregiver unexamined.

### A second set of stems

**Vignette.** A systolic pressure drops 22 mm Hg on standing. You already stopped the alpha blocker. He is still symptomatic and dry.
**Reasoning.** Volume, compression stockings, and a slow stand before any pressor. The diagnosis is orthostatic hypotension that may now be volume or autonomic. The trap is midodrine as the first order while he is volume depleted.
**Hook.** Stop the drug and fill the tank before you add a pressor.

**Vignette.** He wants “do not resuscitate” and also wants antibiotics and a hospital if he has pneumonia. The family thinks those contradict.
**Reasoning.** They do not. Code status is one decision. Treatment of a reversible infection is another. The diagnosis is a limited code status with full treatment otherwise. Write it that way.
**Hook.** No resuscitation is not “no care.”

**Vignette.** The night nurse asks for a standing benzodiazepine so he will sleep. He is not withdrawing from alcohol.
**Reasoning.** A benzodiazepine is the wrong sleep plan and a fall. Daytime light, a quiet night, and treatment of pain or a bladder. The diagnosis is a request for a high-risk sedative. Decline it.
**Hook.** Sleep drugs that sedate are how the morning starts with a hip fracture.

**Vignette.** Teach-back: he says he will take the warfarin “whenever I eat greens.” That is not the plan you stated.
**Reasoning.** The teach-back worked. It found the misunderstanding. Reteach. The trap is documenting “teaching done” because you spoke.
**Hook.** If he cannot say it, he does not have it yet.

**Vignette.** A colleague smells of alcohol before a procedure. A resident offers to “just do the case and not make a thing of it.”
**Reasoning.** Remove the colleague from care and use the reporting path. The diagnosis is an impaired clinician. Covering the case forever is not the treatment.
**Hook.** Impairment is a report. A favor is not a safety plan.

**Vignette.** Pain from bone metastases. He is opioid-naive and in severe pain. A clinician orders a dose suitable for a person who has taken opioids for years, or the reverse: a homeopathic dose because of “respiratory depression.”
**Reasoning.** Start at a dose matched to his tolerance, and titrate to the pain. The diagnosis is cancer pain. The trap is either a huge dose in a naive patient or a dose that cannot touch the pain. Skill is the middle.
**Hook.** Naive patients get a naive dose. Severe pain still gets relieved. Both sentences are true.

**Vignette.** A living will refuses a feeding tube. He now lacks capacity. The family wants a tube “so he does not starve.”
**Reasoning.** Substituted judgment is already written down. Follow the directive. The diagnosis is a valid refusal of artificial nutrition. The trap is calling the family’s love a reason to override it.
**Hook.** The document is the patient’s voice when he cannot speak.

**Vignette.** Readmission for heart failure. The last discharge listed a drug he never filled because it cost too much. The quality meeting proposes “remind doctors to try harder.”
**Reasoning.** The gap is cost and a missing fill, which is a system: a check that the drug was obtained, a cheaper alternative, a pharmacist. The diagnosis is a discharge process failure. Trying harder is not a measurement.
**Hook.** A quality answer is a checklist or a measured change.

## Second atlas

The first pass named the drug classes and the goals conversation. This pass is the older adult in front of you, including the night the same person becomes a disposition. No state’s statute is stated. Capacity still beats a surrogate. Pain and depression are still treated.

### The fall you do not CT, and the fall you do

A trip over a rug, a normal exam, no head strike, and a drug list full of sedatives is a medication visit and a home visit. Computed tomography is for a head injury, anticoagulant plus a head strike, or a new focal sign. It is not how you look compassionate. Orthostatic hypotension is a drop of at least 20 mm Hg systolic or 10 mm Hg diastolic within three minutes of standing. Measure it. Stop the drug that caused it before you add midodrine. Volume comes before a pressor if he is dry.

**Vignette.** She tripped on a rug. No head strike. Exam is normal. She takes diphenhydramine and a benzodiazepine. Her daughter wants a head CT “to be safe.”
**Reasoning.** Stop the sedatives. Look at the home and the standing pressure. The diagnosis is a mechanical fall with a medication contribution. The trap is a CT and a new walker while the bottles stay full.
**NBME trap.** They skip the CT in a person on warfarin who hit his head and seems fine. That head gets imaged. The rug without a head strike is the other stem. Read whether the head was hit and whether a drug thins the blood.
**Hook.** Head strike or a focal sign or a thinner: image. A rug and a sedative: stop the sedative.
**CCS.** Orthostatics, reconcile, stop the offender, home safety, no CT unless the head or the exam earns it.

### Delirium has a cause you can touch

A new sleeping pill, a full bladder, a saturation of 89 percent, a sodium of 122, and a urinary infection can all be true on the same night. Chase more than one. A benzodiazepine is the treatment of alcohol withdrawal and the cause of every other delirium. An antipsychotic is for dangerous agitation after the cause is being treated, at the lowest dose, for the shortest time. It is not a standing order for the night shift. Yesterday he was himself is the sentence that makes this delirium rather than the dementia the family already knew.

**Vignette.** Mild dementia for two years, the same every evening. Tonight he does not recognize his wife, and he was sharp at dinner. A bladder anticholinergic was started three days ago. Oxygen saturation is 91 percent.
**Reasoning.** Acute on chronic. Stop the new drug, give oxygen, examine the lungs and the bladder. The diagnosis is delirium superimposed on dementia. The trap is “sundowning” as a complete assessment, or haloperidol as the only order.
**NBME trap.** They treat a year of identical nighttime wandering as a new emergency sepsis workup every evening, and they also miss the one night that is actually new. The baseline sentence is the whole question.
**Hook.** New tonight: find the cause. The same for a year: safety, not a nightly antipsychotic.
**CCS.** Glucose, oxygen, bladder, medication review, the infection the story suggests, nonpharmacologic care, family counseling. Do not end on a new antipsychotic.

### Depression at 80 is still depression

Do not withhold an antidepressant because of age. Do not continue a benzodiazepine because it was started “for mood” two years ago. Ask about suicide directly. A plan and the means are an emergency at any age. Grief that is functioning and without a wish to die is not an immediate prescription. A wish to die, or a collapse of eating and function, is. Screen, then treat or refer by severity, and see the person again soon.

**Vignette.** He is 82. He has stopped eating. He says he would be better off dead and has saved his cardiac pills. The list includes a benzodiazepine and no antidepressant. A clinician says older adults should not start psychiatric drugs.
**Reasoning.** This is severe depression with a plan and means. He stays for an evaluation. An antidepressant is allowed. The benzodiazepine is the drug to stop, carefully, not the reason to avoid treatment. The diagnosis is severe late-life depression with suicidal intent. The trap is age used as a contraindication.
**NBME trap.** They stop the only drug that was helping a prior severe depression because a Beers table was misread, and they continue the benzodiazepine. Beers flags the sedative. It does not forbid treating depression.
**Hook.** Age is not a reason to leave severe depression untreated. It is a reason to stop the sedative that has no job.
**CCS.** The suicide question, remove the means, start or continue the antidepressant, stop the benzodiazepine with a plan, follow-up soon.

### Pain at the end of life is dosed to the pain

Hospice is a prognosis and a goal. Palliative care is a skill you can use while disease treatment continues. Do not wait for a hospice form to treat pain from bone metastases. An opioid-naive patient gets a naive dose. A patient who has taken opioids for months does not get a tiny PRN that cannot touch the pain. Respiratory depression is real and is managed by skill and by watching, not by leaving the person in pain. Code status is a separate decision. “Do not resuscitate” is not “do not treat the pneumonia” and it is not “do not treat the pain.”

**Vignette.** Metastatic cancer. He has asked not to be intubated. He is in severe pain and has never taken an opioid. The order is a dose meant for a person on a fentanyl patch, because “cancer patients need a lot,” and the nurse is told to hold it if the respiratory rate is 18.
**Reasoning.** Naive dose, titrated to the pain, and a respiratory rate of 18 is not a reason to withhold. The diagnosis is severe cancer pain in an opioid-naive patient with a limited code status. The trap is either the huge first dose or the held dose. Both abandon skill.
**NBME trap.** They withhold all opioids until hospice enrollment is finished. The pain is today’s problem. Hospice is a program. Palliative care is the order you can write now.
**Hook.** Naive dose for a naive patient. Titrate. Do not wait for a form. No intubation is not no morphine.

### The discharge that fails on a nod

Teach-back means he says the plan in his own words. A nod is not teach-back. “Any questions?” is not teach-back. The person who will actually give the medicines is named, and that person is not a daughter two states away who “might call.” Warfarin plus a new antibiotic needs a plan for the INR, said back to you. Follow-up is a date, not “call if you need us.” Reconcile before he leaves. The drug he cannot afford is a different drug or a help with cost, not a lecture about adherence.

**Vignette.** Heart failure discharge. He cannot say what the daily weight means. The new drug costs more than his rent. His daughter nods. She lives across the country. You document “teaching complete.”
**Reasoning.** Teaching is not complete. Change the drug he cannot fill, reteach the weight, and name a person or a service who will see him. The diagnosis is an unsafe discharge. The trap is the nod and the note.
**NBME trap.** They call a quality meeting and conclude the doctors should try harder. The miss was cost and a missing fill. The fix is a check that the drug was obtained, or a cheaper equivalent, measured the next month.
**Hook.** He says it back, or he does not have it. The person giving the pills is in the room or on the calendar. Cost is a system, not a character flaw.
**CCS.** Reconcile, teach-back, a real follow-up, a drug he can obtain, home services if he lives alone. Then end.

### Capacity, the directive, and the son who just arrived

If he can understand, appreciate, reason, and tell you his choice, he decides. A son does not overrule a refusal of intubation. If capacity is gone, a directive or a named agent is the voice. Substituted judgment asks what he would have wanted, not what the family would want for themselves. Best interests are the fallback when that is unknowable. A recommendation is allowed. “What do you want us to do?” with no medical frame is not informed consent. Tell him what the intensive care unit can and cannot do, then ask.

**Vignette.** He has capacity and repeats that he does not want a ventilator. His son arrives and demands everything. The patient is tired and says, again, no.
**Reasoning.** The patient decides. Document it and keep treating what he did not refuse, including antibiotics and comfort. The diagnosis is a capacitated refusal of intubation. The trap is the loudest relative.
**NBME trap.** They follow a living will that refuses a feeding tube, then place the tube because the family cannot stand the idea of “starving.” The document is his voice. Love is not a substitute judgment.
**Hook.** Capacity beats the family. A directive beats a new relative. Recommend, do not dump the decision unframed.
**CCS.** The conversation, the order that matches it, pain treatment, no ventilator if that was the refusal, no tube if the directive refused it.

### Abuse, weight loss, and the wound you do not stage through eschar

Unintentional weight loss is a workup: cancer, depression, dentition, a drug, and not enough food. An appetite stimulant is not the workup. A hand-shaped bruise, a caregiver who will not leave, and a depleted bank account are a report. Confirm the local path. Do not invent the statute. Pressure injury: stage 1 is intact nonblanchable redness, stage 2 is partial thickness, stage 3 is full thickness, stage 4 reaches bone. Eschar is unstageable until it is off. Offload and feed. Do not massage a deep injury.

**Vignette.** Twenty pounds gone in four months. The caregiver answers every question. A bruise on the upper arm is the shape of a hand. You prescribe an appetite stimulant and a week of observation.
**Reasoning.** The weight loss is a medical workup and the bruise is a report, done privately. The diagnosis is unintentional weight loss and suspected elder abuse. The trap is the stimulant and the shared interview.
**NBME trap.** They call an eschar a stage 2 because the edge looks shallow. You cannot stage what you cannot see. Offload it and get help from wound care.
**Hook.** Weight loss is a disease. A hand-shaped bruise is a report. Eschar is unstageable.
**CCS.** The cancer and depression and nutrition workup, a private interview, the report, wound offloading, do not send her home with the same story unexamined.

### The systems answers that feel like etiquette

A professional interpreter, not the grandchild, for a new diagnosis or a consent. Disclose an error that reached him: what happened, what it means, what will change. An impaired colleague is removed from care and reported. Covering the shift forever is not the treatment. A time-out stops the line even if the schedule is late. A readmission because the drug was never filled is a checklist, not a speech about trying harder. These are Day 1 points. They are also the last orders on a CCS case that was otherwise medically correct.

**Vignette.** You gave the wrong dose. He is stable now after the reversal. A colleague says not to worry him.
**Reasoning.** Tell him. The diagnosis of the duty is disclosure of a harmful error. The trap is a quiet chart correction and a friendly visit that never names the dose.
**NBME trap.** They use a child to interpret the disclosure because the family is in the room. A professional interpreter is the disclosure, too.
**Hook.** Say what happened. Use an interpreter who is not the family. Stop the line. Report the impaired colleague. Measure the fix.

### A last set of older-adult stems

**Vignette.** Standing systolic pressure falls 24 mm Hg. He is dry and on two alpha-blocking drugs. You start midodrine.
**Reasoning.** Stop the drugs and give volume first. The diagnosis is drug-related orthostasis with a volume deficit. A pressor is what you consider after those two are addressed.
**Hook.** Stop the offender. Fill the tank. Then, maybe, a pressor.

**Vignette.** Urinary frequency. The post-void residual is 400 mL. Someone starts oxybutynin.
**Reasoning.** This is overflow, or obstruction, until proven otherwise. An anticholinergic makes it worse and adds a fall. The diagnosis is urinary retention. The trap is treating every frequency as overactive bladder.
**Hook.** A residual before a bladder relaxant. High residual: do not add the anticholinergic.

**Vignette.** He wants no resuscitation and also wants antibiotics if he gets pneumonia. The family says those contradict.
**Reasoning.** They do not. Write both. The diagnosis is a limited code status with otherwise full treatment. The trap is “comfort only” inferred from a single refusal.
**Hook.** No resuscitation is not no care.

**Vignette.** Teach-back. He says he will take warfarin whenever he eats greens. You had said something else.
**Reasoning.** The teach-back worked. Reteach. The trap is documenting success because you spoke.
**Hook.** If he cannot say it, he does not have it.

**Vignette.** A colleague smells of alcohol before a procedure. A resident offers to do the case quietly.
**Reasoning.** Remove the colleague and use the reporting path. The diagnosis is impairment. A quiet favor is not safety.
**Hook.** Impairment is a report. A cover is not a plan.

**Vignette.** Insomnia. No alcohol withdrawal. The night nurse wants a standing benzodiazepine.
**Reasoning.** Decline it. Daytime light, pain control, and a bladder he can empty. The diagnosis is a request for a high-risk sedative. The trap is kindness that becomes a hip fracture.
**Hook.** A benzodiazepine for sleep in an older adult is a fall you are scheduling.

**Vignette.** Readmission for heart failure. The discharge drug was never filled because of cost. The committee says “remind physicians to try harder.”
**Reasoning.** The fix is a check that the medicine was obtained, or a drug he can afford, and a measurement next month. The diagnosis is a discharge process failure. Effort is not a metric.
**Hook.** A quality answer is a checklist or a measured change.

**Vignette.** Bone pain, opioid naive, respiratory rate 18. The dose is held because someone fears depression of breathing.
**Reasoning.** Eighteen is not depression. Give a naive dose and watch. The diagnosis is untreated severe pain. The trap is a fear used as a protocol.
**Hook.** Watch the rate. Do not invent a hold at a normal rate. Relieve the pain.

## Common traps

- A new benzodiazepine for insomnia in an 82-year-old.
- Withholding opioids at the end of life because of a vital-sign anxiety.
- Letting a family member forbid you to tell a capacitated patient the diagnosis.
- “Be more careful” as the answer to a systems failure. The answer is a change in the process.

## Practice this in the Qbank

Geriatrics items are the drug you stop and the diagnosis you do not miss (delirium versus dementia). Ethics items are the person who holds the decision. Feed cards that say “start low and go slow” without saying “do not omit the treatment” are half of the truth. Write both halves.

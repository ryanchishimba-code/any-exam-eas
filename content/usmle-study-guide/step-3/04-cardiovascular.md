# Cardiovascular management

## Framework

Step 2 asked for the next test. Step 3 asks whether the patient is still on the right drugs at the next visit, and what you order on CCS when the pressure or the rhythm changes.

**Longitudinal blood pressure**

Confirm the reading. The 2025 AHA/ACC goal used in this book is under 130/80 mm Hg, with medicine at 130/80 or higher when cardiovascular disease, diabetes, CKD, or PREVENT risk of 7.5% or higher is present. Recheck after a change. Do not add a third drug before you ask about doses actually taken. ([2025 AHA/ACC](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/CIR.0000000000001356))

**Heart failure across visits**

A patient with HFrEF who is "a little more short of breath" needs a weight, a lung exam, and a look at the four drug classes: ARNI or ACE inhibitor or ARB, evidence-based beta-blocker, mineralocorticoid receptor antagonist if the kidney and potassium allow, and an SGLT2 inhibitor. Missing one of the four is a more common exam miss than a rare device. Hold an ACE inhibitor and an ARNI apart. Acute pulmonary edema on CCS is oxygen, a nitrate if the pressure allows, and a loop diuretic. Then recheck the oxygen and the breathing. Do not discharge from that screen.

**Prevention that belongs in the cardiac visit**

A high-intensity statin for secondary prevention. Ask about tobacco and offer cessation treatment. Aspirin is for disease that is already there, not a vitamin for a low-risk adult. Confirm the current USPSTF aspirin statement. It has narrowed.

Atrial fibrillation in clinic: rate or rhythm as the symptoms require, and anticoagulation at the 2023 guideline's risk threshold (CHA2DS2-VASc 2 or more in men, 3 or more in women), DOAC preferred unless a mechanical valve or moderate to severe mitral stenosis is the reason for warfarin. Recheck adherence. A stopped DOAC is not "stable."

## Checklist

- [ ] Ask what the patient is actually taking before you add a drug.
- [ ] HFrEF: look for the missing pillar.
- [ ] Acute edema: oxygen, diuretic, recheck. Do not end the case.
- [ ] Anticoagulation in fibrillation is a risk decision, not a symptom decision.
- [ ] Secondary prevention includes a statin and tobacco treatment.

## Common traps

- Adding a beta-blocker and a non-dihydropyridine calcium blocker together for rate, in a low ejection fraction.
- Stopping a beta-blocker for a mild cold.
- Discharging unstable pulmonary edema because the diagnosis was correct.

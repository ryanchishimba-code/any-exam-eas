# Exam day for Step 3

> **Study aid only.** Two days, two jobs. The Bulletin wins on any conflict. This chapter does not promise a result.

## What this chapter covers

How the published days are built and how to spend the break and the CCS clock. Confirm every figure on usmle.org the month you test.

## The framework

![Two days, two jobs](visuals/two-day-map.svg)

| Day | Published shape | What to protect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Day 1, FIP | About 7 hours. About 232 items. Twelve 30-minute blocks. At least 55 minutes of break. A 5-minute optional tutorial. | Papers, ethics, and diagnosis. Fatigue makes you skim an abstract. Do not. |
| Day 2, ACM | About 9 hours. About 180 items in nine 30-minute blocks. Then a CCS tutorial and 13 to 14 cases, each at most 10 or 20 minutes. At least 45 minutes of break. | The cases. Do not donate the CCS time to a postmortem of the morning blocks. |

Early finishes can become break, as on the other Steps. Skipping the multiple-choice tutorial is reasonable if you know the interface. Do not skip the CCS tutorial the first time you see that software, unless you have already done it in the official practice and you are sure.

Between the days, sleep and food. A new chapter the night between Day 1 and Day 2 will not land. A review of your CCS order (site, emergency treatment, clock, counsel) is the only note worth rereading.

If something goes wrong, tell the test center that day and write the USMLE program inside the Bulletin’s window. A survey comment is not that notice.

You sit Step 3 only after Step 1 and Step 2 CK are passed. The registration entity and the FSMB rules about state eligibility are not this book. Read them.

## Checklist

- [ ] Official multiple-choice interface and official CCS practice both done before the week of the exam.
- [ ] Break food for a 7-hour day and a 9-hour day.
- [ ] A one-page CCS order, already memorized, so you do not invent one under the clock.
- [ ] No new guideline the morning of either day.
- [ ] Bulletin checked for ID and what you may bring.

## Common traps

- Treating Day 1 like Day 2 and hunting for a treatment on an item that asks you to interpret an ad.
- Rushing CCS because the morning felt bad. The cases are part of the result. The Bulletin says so, without giving you a percent to game.
- Skipping breaks on Day 2 and starting CCS depleted.
- A forum’s “must-order” list that includes tests you cannot defend.

## Practice this in the Qbank

One timed mixed block for Day 1 skills, then one timed CCS case, on separate sittings, in the week before the exam. Stop when the method is boring. The feed is not an exam-morning activity unless the card is one you already know and you are calming down, not learning.

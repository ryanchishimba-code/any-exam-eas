# Diabetes and thyroid over time

## Framework

The Step 2 chapter has the diagnostic numbers and DKA. Step 3 is the visit after the emergency, and the orders on a CCS clinic screen.

Use the ADA standards. Confirm the diagnosis unless it is already obvious. Pick therapy from comorbidity: atherosclerotic disease, a GLP-1 receptor agonist or an SGLT2 inhibitor; heart failure, an SGLT2 inhibitor; CKD, an SGLT2 inhibitor when it fits. Metformin is a common foundation when none of those force a different first move. Individualize the A1C. Do not chase a normal A1C in a frail patient who is hypoglycemic. ([ADA](https://diabetesjournals.org/care))

**CCS orders for a new type 2 diagnosis**

- Education, not a pamphlet as the only plan.
- Home glucose plan if you are using a drug that can cause hypoglycemia.
- Blood pressure, a statin decision, urine albumin, eye and foot plan.
- Vaccines that are due.
- A follow-up soon enough to see the A1C move. An A1C the next morning will not show the new drug.

Hypoglycemia under 70 mg/dL: 15 g if awake, glucagon if not, and a medication review so it does not repeat tonight.

Thyroid replacement: levothyroxine on an empty stomach, TSH in about 6 weeks, lower starting dose in older adults and in coronary disease. Do not adjust the dose on a TSH drawn three days after a change. A sick, hospitalized patient's thyroid tests are often the illness. Do not start levothyroxine for a single odd TSH in the intensive care unit.

## Checklist

- [ ] Comorbidity before the drug.
- [ ] A1C goal matches frailty.
- [ ] Albumin and the eye are part of the diabetes visit.
- [ ] Recheck TSH on the right clock.
- [ ] Do not treat euthyroid-sick with a new prescription.

## Common traps

- Insulin sliding scale as the entire hospital plan, with no basal insulin, in a patient who needs basal insulin.
- Adding a sulfonylurea in an older adult who lives alone and then not asking about lows.
- Doubling levothyroxine because the patient still feels tired a week later.

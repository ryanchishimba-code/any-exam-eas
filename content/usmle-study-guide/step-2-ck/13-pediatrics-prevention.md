# Pediatrics, development, and prevention

> **Study aid only.** The CDC schedule and the AAP policy are revised. Ages below are the ones to confirm, not to tattoo. Pediatrics is about 17–27% of Step 2 CK as a clinical science. Human development is a smaller system row and still shows up inside pediatrics.

## What this chapter covers

The pediatric emergencies, the developmental and preventive visits, and the adult prevention that USMLE scores as health maintenance. Vaccines and screening are one skill: who, and why not this person.

## The framework

A sick infant is airway, breathing, circulation, glucose, and a weight-based dose. Then the specific syndrome.

| Syndrome | What changes the next minute |
| --- | --- |
| Neonatal resuscitation | Warm, dry, stimulate. Ventilate if the heart rate stays low. Most babies respond to ventilation, not to a drug. |
| Bilious vomiting in an infant | Malrotation with volvulus until proven otherwise. Surgical emergency. |
| Non-bilious projectile vomiting, young infant | Hypertrophic pyloric stenosis. Correct the alkalosis and operate. Not a same-hour ex-lap before fluids if the baby is stable. |
| Bronchiolitis | Supportive care. The AAP recommends against routine bronchodilators, steroids, and antibiotics. |
| Croup | Steroid. Nebulized epinephrine if the stridor is at rest, then watch for rebound. |
| Epiglottitis | Do not examine the throat in a way that upsets the child. Airway in a controlled setting. Hib vaccine made this rare and did not make it impossible. |
| Fever in a young infant | Age decides the workup. Do not apply the “viral syndrome, go home” script to a neonate. Follow the current AAP febrile-infant guideline rather than a cutoff you remember from a senior. |
| Non-accidental trauma | Injury that does not match the development or the story. Report. Reporting is the action, not a suspicion you keep. |
| Dehydration | Oral rehydration if the child can drink. Intravenous fluid if not. The WHO/AAP principle is replace what is lost, then maintain. |

Development items give you a single milestone and an age. Know the ones that, if missing, mean you refer: no social smile by a few months, no sitting by about 9 months, no walking by about 18 months, no words by about 18 months, and loss of a skill at any age. Loss of skill is not “a late bloomer.” Confirm exact ages against the current CDC Milestone materials rather than a chart from a decade ago. The CDC revised the checklists.

Vaccines: follow the current CDC schedule. A few rules the exam loves because they are about who should not get a vaccine. Live vaccines (MMR, varicella, live attenuated influenza) are not for a severely immunocompromised child. MMR is not given before the first birthday for routine immunization because maternal antibody interferes, with an outbreak exception you should read rather than invent. Pertussis vaccine in every pregnancy protects the newborn. That is ACOG and CDC together.

Adult prevention that belongs in the same mental drawer:

| Service | USPSTF, confirm before you use it |
| --- | --- |
| Breast cancer | Biennial mammography, 40 to 74 |
| Cervical cancer | Start at 21. See the women’s health chapter. |
| Colorectal cancer | 45 to 75 |
| Lung cancer | Annual low-dose CT, 50 to 80, 20 pack-years, current smoker or quit within 15 years |
| Abdominal aortic aneurysm | One-time ultrasound in men 65 to 75 who have ever smoked |
| Depression | Screen adults, including pregnant and postpartum people |
| Chlamydia and gonorrhea | Sexually active women 24 and younger, and older women at increased risk |
| Osteoporosis | Women 65 and older |
| Unhealthy alcohol and tobacco | Ask, and offer treatment that works |

Grade A and B services are the ones to offer. An I statement means the evidence was not enough. Do not sell an I statement as a mandate.

## High-yield atlas

Pediatric items are an age plus one dangerous diagnosis. Bilious vomit is surgical. Bronchiolitis is supportive care. A lost milestone is a referral. Prevention items ask whether this person meets a USPSTF or CDC frame. Grade A and B services are the ones to offer. An I statement is not a mandate. Confirm the current page before a number is used on a patient.

Do not give MMR or varicella during pregnancy. Do not invent a schedule exception. |

### Midgut volvulus

The midgut twists on a narrow pedicle. Bile-stained vomit means the obstruction is distal to the ampulla. A soft abdomen does not mean the bowel is alive.

**Vignette.** A 6-day-old term baby has sudden green vomiting and is screaming. The abdomen is soft and not distended. A parent is offered a reflux precaution sheet and a visit next Tuesday.
**Reasoning.** Green vomit in an infant is a surgical problem until an urgent study or an operation says otherwise. Softness is early, not reassuring. Reflux and a formula change do not turn vomit green. The diagnosis is Midgut volvulus.
**NBME trap.** Reassurance, a reflux prescription, or an outpatient study for bilious emesis.
**Hook.** Green vomit in a baby is surgical until proven otherwise.

### Bronchiolitis

Small airways are plugged with edema and debris. The illness is viral. A trial of a bronchodilator is not the guideline.

**Vignette.** An 8-month-old has two days of cough and wheeze. A sibling has a cold. He is alert, pulling a bit at the breast, and his saturation is 94%. Orders are written for albuterol, a steroid, a chest film, and an antibiotic.
**Reasoning.** The age, the season, and the wheeze are this illness. Saturation above 90% does not mandate oxygen, and none of the four orders is routine care. Hydration and observation are the treatment. A truly hypoxic or exhausted infant is admitted for support, still without those drugs as the plan. The diagnosis is Bronchiolitis.
**NBME trap.** A routine bronchodilator trial, a steroid, or an antibiotic for a first wheezing infant.
**Hook.** Support the infant. Do not invent a bronchodilator benefit.

### Hypertrophic pyloric stenosis

The pyloric muscle has hypertrophied. Acid and chloride are lost in the vomit. The kidney trades hydrogen for sodium and the urine can be paradoxically acid.

**Vignette.** A 4-week-old boy vomits milk in a projectile arc after feeds. There is no green color. An olive is felt. The chloride is low, the potassium is low, and the bicarbonate is high. The board says ex-lap now, before labs are corrected.
**Reasoning.** The vomit is not bilious, so this is not a twist of the midgut. The metabolic picture is the pylorus, and a stable baby is resuscitated first. The operation is a pyloromyotomy after the alkalosis is corrected, not a damage-control laparotomy tonight. The diagnosis is Hypertrophic pyloric stenosis.
**NBME trap.** Immediate surgery in a stable, severely alkalotic baby, or reassurance because the vomit is not green.
**Hook.** Nonbilious projectile and an alkalosis: resuscitate, then the pylorus.

### A newborn who needs ventilation

The newborn problem is almost always ventilation. A low heart rate is a sign the lungs have not been inflated, until effective breaths have been given and the heart rate is still under 60.

**Vignette.** A term baby is born limp. After drying and stimulation he is not breathing and the heart rate is 70. A colleague reaches for a drug before anyone has put a mask on the face.
**Reasoning.** Heart rate under 100 after the initial steps is a ventilation problem. The mask comes first. Compressions and a drug are for a heart rate that stays under 60 after the breaths are actually moving the chest. Most babies never need the drug. The diagnosis is A newborn who needs ventilation.
**NBME trap.** A drug first, or chest compressions, before effective ventilation.
**Hook.** Ventilate the newborn. The heart rate tells you if it worked.

### Croup

Subglottic edema. The bark is the clue. Rest stridor means the airway is tight enough to treat and observe.

**Vignette.** A 2-year-old has a seal-like bark and a hoarse cry. Stridor is loud when he is upset and gone when he is calm in his parent's arms. He is not drooling. The plan is albuterol and a throat culture.
**Reasoning.** A bark without toxicity is this airway, and a corticosteroid is the drug. Stridor only with crying is not the epinephrine indication. Stridor at rest would be epinephrine plus a period of observation, because the epinephrine wears off. The diagnosis is Croup.
**NBME trap.** Albuterol, or discharge immediately after epinephrine.
**Hook.** Bark gets a steroid. Rest stridor gets epinephrine and a watch.

### Epiglottitis

The supraglottic structures are swollen. Agitation can close the airway. The diagnosis is clinical and the treatment is a controlled airway.

**Vignette.** A 4-year-old who has not received vaccines sits forward on the stretcher, drools, and will not lie down. His voice is muffled and he looks toxic. A tongue blade and a lateral neck film are proposed before anyone calls anesthesia.
**Reasoning.** Tripod posture, drooling, and toxicity are a supraglottic emergency. A tongue blade is how the airway is lost in the hallway. The film is for a stable child if it is done at all. The operating room or an equivalent controlled setting is where the airway is secured. The diagnosis is Epiglottitis.
**NBME trap.** A tongue blade, or a trip to radiology, in a toxic drooling child.
**Hook.** Drooling and leaning forward: leave the throat alone and control the airway.

### Fever in a young infant

Invasive bacterial infection is common enough in the youngest that a calm exam does not clear the spinal fluid.

**Vignette.** A 12-day-old term infant is feeding and looks comfortable. The rectal temperature is 38.3 C. A parent asks whether this can be a virus and a clinic visit tomorrow. No urine, blood, or spinal fluid has been sent.
**Reasoning.** Twelve days is the youngest band. Comfort and a good feed do not move him into the older pathway. He needs urine, blood, cerebrospinal fluid, parenteral antibiotics, and a hospital watch. A 45-day-old with normal inflammatory markers is a different conversation. The diagnosis is Fever in a young infant.
**NBME trap.** The viral-syndrome script applied to a neonate.
**Hook.** The younger the infant, the less you trust a calm exam.

### Nonaccidental trauma

The history and the milestone do not match, or the fracture pattern is inflicted. You do not need certainty or a confession.

**Vignette.** A 3-month-old has posterior rib fractures. The caregiver says the baby rolled off a bed. The baby does not roll. The plan in the note is to document the story and see the family in clinic, with no report.
**Reasoning.** A non-rolling infant did not generate posterior rib fractures by rolling off a bed. Reasonable suspicion is enough to report. The skeletal survey looks for the fractures you have not found yet. Waiting for proof is how the next injury happens. The diagnosis is Nonaccidental trauma.
**NBME trap.** Keeping the suspicion because you are not sure of the perpetrator.
**Hook.** If the milestone cannot make the injury, report.

### Intussusception

Ileum telescopes into colon. The pain is ischemic and intermittent. Bloody mucus means mucosa has been injured and is a late sign.

**Vignette.** A 9-month-old screams for a few minutes, draws his knees up, then smiles and plays. This has repeated all afternoon. There is no blood in the stool. A right-sided sausage is felt. The plan is observation overnight.
**Reasoning.** Intermittent pain and a sausage are the telescope. Currant jelly is not required. Ultrasound is the next image, and an air enema is the treatment if the abdomen is not peritoneal. Overnight observation is how the bowel dies. The diagnosis is Intussusception.
**NBME trap.** Reassurance because the stool is not yet bloody.
**Hook.** Intermittent screaming and a sausage. Do not wait for currant jelly.

### Developmental regression

Skills are not shed in typical development. Loss means a neurologic, metabolic, epileptic, or autistic process until a specialist says otherwise.

**Vignette.** An 18-month-old had two words and pointed at pictures. He now has no words and no longer points. His parents call him a late bloomer. The visit ends with reassurance and a 6-month follow-up.
**Reasoning.** Loss is the opposite of delay. A late bloomer is slow to gain a skill, not a child who had the skill and lost it. This is a referral, a hearing check, and a search for regression, not a longer leash. The diagnosis is Developmental regression.
**NBME trap.** Calling loss of skill a late bloomer.
**Hook.** Lost a skill: refer. Do not wait on a decade-old chart.

### A live vaccine in severe immunocompromise

A live attenuated virus can replicate in a child who cannot contain it. Inactivated vaccines do not do that.

**Vignette.** A child in intensive chemotherapy is due for MMR and for the nasal influenza vaccine. A sibling at home is well. The nurse asks if both live vaccines should be given today so the child does not fall behind.
**Reasoning.** Severe immunosuppression is a contraindication to those live vaccines. Falling behind is repaired later, from the CDC catch-up table, not today. The well sibling can usually receive MMR. The nasal vaccine is the live one. An injected inactivated influenza vaccine is a different product. The diagnosis is A live vaccine in severe immunocompromise.
**NBME trap.** Giving MMR or the nasal influenza vaccine during intensive chemotherapy, or giving routine MMR at 9 months and counting it as the series.
**Hook.** Severe immunosuppression and live vaccines do not mix.

### Pertussis vaccine in pregnancy

The newborn is too young for a complete pertussis series and is protected by maternal antibody. That antibody has to be made in this pregnancy.

**Vignette.** A woman at 30 weeks had a tetanus booster as a teenager and asks if she is done. Nobody has offered a pertussis-containing vaccine in this pregnancy. She is also due for a catch-up MMR because her rubella titer is negative.
**Reasoning.** The teenage dose does not protect this newborn. Tdap now is the intervention. MMR waits until after delivery because it is live. Inactivated influenza does not wait. The diagnosis is Pertussis vaccine in pregnancy.
**NBME trap.** Skipping Tdap because she was vaccinated years ago, or giving MMR during pregnancy.
**Hook.** Every pregnancy gets Tdap. The teenage dose does not count for this baby.

### Breast cancer screening

Grade B means offer the service. Grade I means the evidence was not enough to mandate it. Average risk here includes a family history that is not a known high-risk mutation.

**Vignette.** A 42-year-old woman at average risk has never had a mammogram. She wants to know if she should wait until 50, or if she must be imaged every year. Her breasts are dense. A pamphlet offers an ultrasound as a required add-on.
**Reasoning.** Forty-two is inside 40 to 74. The USPSTF interval is every other year, not a wait until 50 and not an annual mandate. Density does not create a grade B requirement for ultrasound or MRI. Seventy-six would be an I statement and a conversation, not the same offer. The diagnosis is Breast cancer screening.
**NBME trap.** Waiting until 50, requiring annual mammograms as if USPSTF said so, or ordering supplemental MRI as a mandate for dense breasts.
**Hook.** 40 to 74, every other year. Age 75 and dense-breast add-ons are I, not a mandate.

### Colorectal cancer screening

Grade A and grade B are services to offer. Grade C is individualized. A symptom such as bleeding is no longer a screen.

**Vignette.** A 46-year-old adult has never been tested. There is no bleeding and no family syndrome. They ask if screening starts at 50, and whether a stool card is a refusal of care. Last year a friend's positive card was followed by another card.
**Reasoning.** Forty-six is inside the window that used to start at 50. A stool test is a real screen. A positive one is not repeated as the next step. It is a colonoscopy. Seventy-eight would be selective, not automatic. The diagnosis is Colorectal cancer screening.
**NBME trap.** Starting at 50, or repeating a positive fecal test instead of looking inside.
**Hook.** 45 to 75. A positive stool test earns a colonoscopy.

### Lung cancer screening

The screen finds cancer early enough to treat in people whose smoking history makes the yield worth the false positives. Outside the frame, the harm dominates.

**Vignette.** A 62-year-old smoked one pack a day from age 20 to age 50 and quit 12 years ago. There is no cough. A chest radiograph is ordered as the screen. A neighbor with a 10 pack-year history asks for the same test.
**Reasoning.** Thirty pack-years and a quit inside 15 years, at age 62, is the frame. The test is a low-dose CT every year, not a chest film. The neighbor with 10 pack-years is outside the frame. A new cough would leave the screening pathway and become a diagnostic one. The diagnosis is Lung cancer screening.
**NBME trap.** A chest radiograph, a 10 pack-year history, or a quit 20 years ago used as a reason to screen.
**Hook.** 50 to 80, 20 pack-years, still smoking or quit within 15 years, low-dose CT.

### Abdominal aortic aneurysm screening

One ultrasound finds a silent aneurysm in the group where repair prevents rupture often enough to justify the screen. The other groups do not share that balance.

**Vignette.** A 68-year-old man smoked for two years in his twenties. He has not smoked since. He asks if he needs a yearly ultrasound. In the next room, a 68-year-old woman who never smoked is offered the same test as a required screen.
**Reasoning.** He has ever smoked and he is inside 65 to 75, so he gets one ultrasound, not a yearly habit. She has never smoked, and the USPSTF recommends against routine screening in that group. A man who never smoked is a selective conversation, grade C, not a grade B requirement. The diagnosis is Abdominal aortic aneurysm screening.
**NBME trap.** Screening a woman who never smoked, repeating a negative ultrasound every year, or skipping a man because he quit decades ago.
**Hook.** Men 65 to 75 who ever smoked: one ultrasound.

### Cervical cancer screening

Most early HPV infection clears. Screening too young finds transient infection. Screening on the interval finds the lesions that matter.

**Vignette.** A 19-year-old who is sexually active asks for a Pap smear because her friends had one. A 25-year-old is due and is told she can skip cytology until 30. A 66-year-old with a decade of negative tests and no high-risk history is told she needs a Pap every year for life.
**Reasoning.** Nineteen is before the start, and sexual activity does not move it. Twenty-five is cytology every 3 years, not a skip to 30. Sixty-six with adequate prior negatives can stop. None of these is an annual lifetime schedule. The diagnosis is Cervical cancer screening.
**NBME trap.** A Pap before 21, primary HPV alone as the USPSTF plan under 30, or never stopping after adequate negatives.
**Hook.** Start at 21. Do not start because she is sexually active.

### Kawasaki disease

A medium-vessel vasculitis. The coronary arteries are the organ. The classic count is fever plus four of five mucocutaneous features, and incomplete disease is real.

**Vignette.** A 3-year-old has had fever for 6 days. The eyes are red without discharge, the lips are cracked, the hands are swollen, there is a rash, and one cervical node is large. He is playful between fever spikes. The plan is supportive care and no cardiac imaging.
**Reasoning.** Five days of fever and the mucocutaneous cluster is this vasculitis, even when he plays between spikes. The coronaries are imaged and treatment starts. Waiting for all five features in a toxic infant is how aneurysms are missed. Aspirin here is deliberate. It is not the drug you give for a routine viral fever. The diagnosis is Kawasaki disease.
**NBME trap.** Supportive care only, or aspirin for an ordinary viral fever in a different child.
**Hook.** Five days of fever and the mucocutaneous cluster: treat, and look at the coronaries.

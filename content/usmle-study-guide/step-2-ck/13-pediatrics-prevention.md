# Pediatrics, development, and prevention

> **Study aid only.** The CDC schedule and the AAP policy are revised. Ages below are the ones to confirm, not to tattoo. Pediatrics is about 17–27% of Step 2 CK as a clinical science. Human development is a smaller system row and still shows up inside pediatrics.

## What this chapter covers

The pediatric emergencies, the developmental and preventive visits, and the adult prevention that USMLE scores as health maintenance. Vaccines and screening are one skill: who, and why not this person.

## The framework

A sick infant is airway, breathing, circulation, glucose, and a weight-based dose. Then the specific syndrome.

| Syndrome | What changes the next minute |
| --- | --- |
| Neonatal resuscitation | Warm, dry, stimulate. Ventilate if the heart rate stays low. Most babies respond to ventilation, not to a drug. |
| Bilious vomiting in an infant | Malrotation with volvulus until proven otherwise. Surgical emergency. |
| Non-bilious projectile vomiting, young infant | Hypertrophic pyloric stenosis. Correct the alkalosis and operate. Not a same-hour ex-lap before fluids if the baby is stable. |
| Bronchiolitis | Supportive care. The AAP recommends against routine bronchodilators, steroids, and antibiotics. |
| Croup | Steroid. Nebulized epinephrine if the stridor is at rest, then watch for rebound. |
| Epiglottitis | Do not examine the throat in a way that upsets the child. Airway in a controlled setting. Hib vaccine made this rare and did not make it impossible. |
| Fever in a young infant | Age decides the workup. Do not apply the “viral syndrome, go home” script to a neonate. Follow the current AAP febrile-infant guideline rather than a cutoff you remember from a senior. |
| Non-accidental trauma | Injury that does not match the development or the story. Report. Reporting is the action, not a suspicion you keep. |
| Dehydration | Oral rehydration if the child can drink. Intravenous fluid if not. The WHO/AAP principle is replace what is lost, then maintain. |

Development items give you a single milestone and an age. Know the ones that, if missing, mean you refer: no social smile by a few months, no sitting by about 9 months, no walking by about 18 months, no words by about 18 months, and loss of a skill at any age. Loss of skill is not “a late bloomer.” Confirm exact ages against the current CDC Milestone materials rather than a chart from a decade ago. The CDC revised the checklists.

Vaccines: follow the current CDC schedule. A few rules the exam loves because they are about who should not get a vaccine. Live vaccines (MMR, varicella, live attenuated influenza) are not for a severely immunocompromised child. MMR is not given before the first birthday for routine immunization because maternal antibody interferes, with an outbreak exception you should read rather than invent. Pertussis vaccine in every pregnancy protects the newborn. That is ACOG and CDC together.

Adult prevention that belongs in the same mental drawer:

| Service | USPSTF, confirm before you use it |
| --- | --- |
| Breast cancer | Biennial mammography, 40 to 74 |
| Cervical cancer | Start at 21. See the women’s health chapter. |
| Colorectal cancer | 45 to 75 |
| Lung cancer | Annual low-dose CT, 50 to 80, 20 pack-years, current smoker or quit within 15 years |
| Abdominal aortic aneurysm | One-time ultrasound in men 65 to 75 who have ever smoked |
| Depression | Screen adults, including pregnant and postpartum people |
| Chlamydia and gonorrhea | Sexually active women 24 and younger, and older women at increased risk |
| Osteoporosis | Women 65 and older |
| Unhealthy alcohol and tobacco | Ask, and offer treatment that works |

Grade A and B services are the ones to offer. An I statement means the evidence was not enough. Do not sell an I statement as a mandate.

## Checklist

- [ ] Bilious vomiting in a baby is a surgical emergency.
- [ ] Bronchiolitis is supportive. Do not invent a bronchodilator benefit.
- [ ] Loss of developmental skill is a referral.
- [ ] Live vaccines and severe immunosuppression do not mix.
- [ ] USPSTF grade A or B, or do not pretend it is required.

## Common traps

- Adult sepsis fluid math on a newborn. Use a pediatric source.
- Aspirin for a fever in a child with a viral illness. Reye syndrome is the reason pediatric services avoid it.
- Reassurance for bilious emesis.
- A vaccine card that skips pregnancy pertussis or that gives MMR at 9 months as the routine.

## Practice this in the Qbank

Pediatric items are an age plus one dangerous diagnosis. Prevention items are “does this person meet the USPSTF frame.” Feed cards on milestones and vaccine ages should be checked against the CDC pages after any schedule change. This chapter will go stale on a number before it goes stale on the danger signs.

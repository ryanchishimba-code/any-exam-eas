# Gastrointestinal

## Framework

Bleeding, obstruction, and the liver each have one first move. Screening is a separate question and should not be mixed into an acute stem.

| Picture | Next step |
| --- | --- |
| Vomiting blood or black stool, unstable | Two large intravenous lines, blood, and reverse a coagulopathy. Endoscopy after resuscitation, not instead of it. |
| Vomiting blood, stable | Endoscopy. A proton-pump inhibitor is adjunctive, not the diagnosis. |
| Pain out of proportion, lactic acidosis, atrial fibrillation | Mesenteric ischemia until imaging says otherwise. |
| Right-upper-quadrant pain, fever, jaundice | Ascending cholangitis. Fluids, antibiotics that cover gut organisms, and biliary drainage. Not a clinic antibiotic alone. |
| Epigastric pain through to the back, lipase up | Pancreatitis. Fluids, pain control, and the cause (stone or alcohol). Do not feed a "bowel rest forever" plan. |

Colorectal cancer screening for average risk starts at 45 and continues through 75 (USPSTF). A positive stool test leads to colonoscopy, not to another stool test. ([USPSTF](https://www.uspreventiveservicestaskforce.org/uspstf/recommendation/colorectal-cancer-screening))

Hepatitis C: screen once in adults 18 to 79. Antibody with reflex RNA. RNA means current infection. ([USPSTF hepatitis C](https://www.uspreventiveservicestaskforce.org/uspstf/recommendation/hepatitis-c-screening))

Cirrhosis with ascites and fever is spontaneous bacterial peritonitis until the tap says otherwise. Tap before the antibiotic when it is safe to wait a few minutes. Albumin and a specific antibiotic are for confirmed infection. Variceal bleeding gets octreotide and an antibiotic, plus endoscopy.

## Checklist

- [ ] Resuscitate before you endoscope an unstable bleed.
- [ ] Cholangitis is drainage plus antibiotics.
- [ ] Lipase diagnoses pancreatitis. The CT is for complications, not for the first hour in a clear case.
- [ ] Colon screening starts at 45 for average risk.
- [ ] Ascites plus fever gets a tap.

## Common traps

- A repeat fecal immunochemical test after a positive one.
- CT first for a textbook pancreatitis that is not severe and not in doubt.
- Holding the antibiotic for hours in cholangitis while you "wait for sensitivities."

# Renal and electrolytes

## Framework

Potassium and volume kill people before the creatinine does. Name the emergency, then the cause.

**Potassium**

| Situation | Act |
| --- | --- |
| ECG changes or severe weakness | Calcium first if the ECG is changing, then shift potassium in (insulin with glucose, a beta-agonist), then remove it (a binder, dialysis if the kidney is gone). |
| Mild, with a reason you can stop | Stop the cause. Recheck. Do not start with dialysis. |
| Low potassium on a diuretic | Replace potassium and magnesium. The magnesium must be there or the potassium will not stay. |

**Sodium**

Correct a chronic low sodium slowly. A rapid jump risks osmotic demyelination. Acute severe symptoms (seizure) are a different problem and need a careful, small, hypertonic correction in a monitored setting. Do not memorize a casual bolus from a forum. Follow a current hyponatremia guideline.

**Kidney injury**

KDIGO defines acute kidney injury as creatinine up by at least 0.3 mg/dL within 48 hours, creatinine at least 1.5 times baseline within 7 days, or urine output under 0.5 mL/kg/h for 6 hours. CKD is a GFR under 60, or markers of kidney damage, for more than 3 months. ([KDIGO CKD](https://kdigo.org/guidelines/ckd-evaluation-and-management/))

| AKI bucket | The clue | The move |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pre-renal | Dry, high urea out of proportion, bland urine | Volume if the patient is volume-down. Not if the lungs are wet. |
| Intrinsic | Muddy casts (acute tubular necrosis), red-cell casts (glomerulonephritis), white-cell casts (interstitial nephritis) | Stop the toxin. Steroids are for allergic interstitial nephritis when the story fits, not for every white cell. |
| Post-renal | Obstruction. A full bladder. Hydronephrosis. | Relieve it. |

ACE inhibitors and ARBs help albuminuric CKD, including diabetic kidney disease, and they raise creatinine a little and potassium a lot if you are not watching. An SGLT2 inhibitor is part of current CKD care when the GFR allows. Do not start them the morning of a shock admission and forget to hold them. ([KDIGO](https://kdigo.org/guidelines/ckd-evaluation-and-management/))

## Checklist

- [ ] ECG changes from potassium: calcium before insulin.
- [ ] Replace magnesium with potassium.
- [ ] Do not correct chronic hyponatremia in a hurry.
- [ ] Casts sort intrinsic AKI.
- [ ] A rising creatinine on a new ACE inhibitor needs a potassium and a look for renal-artery stenosis, not automatic panic at a small bump.

## Common traps

- Insulin without glucose in a patient whose sugar is already low.
- Fluids for cardiorenal syndrome with wet lungs.
- Calling every high creatinine "dehydration."

# Renal and electrolyte disorders

> **Study aid only.** The decision, not a dialysis prescription. KDIGO is the kidney source. The 2025 blood-pressure guideline matters when CKD and hypertension meet.

## What this chapter covers

How to name an acute kidney injury, what to do with potassium, sodium, and acid, and the CKD interventions that change outcome. Renal and reproductive content together are about 7–13% of Step 2 CK. Reproductive disease is in the women’s health chapter.

## The framework

Acute kidney injury is prerenal, intrinsic, or postrenal until you have looked. A bladder scan or a catheter answers obstruction faster than a long differential. Urine sediment answers glomerulonephritis (red-cell casts) and acute tubular injury (muddy casts). The fraction of excreted sodium helps only when diuretics are not already in the story.

| Problem | The move |
| --- | --- |
| Hyperkalemia with ECG changes | Stabilize the membrane with calcium, shift potassium inward, then remove it. Calcium does not lower the potassium. It buys time. |
| Severe hyponatremia with seizures | Hypertonic saline, in a monitored setting. Chronic asymptomatic hyponatremia is corrected slowly because the brain has adapted. |
| Hypernatremia | Calculate the free-water deficit and replace it at a pace the brain can follow. |
| CKD with albuminuria | An ACE inhibitor or an ARB, not both. SGLT2 inhibitors have a kidney indication in diabetic kidney disease and in many patients without diabetes. Confirm the eGFR floor in the current label and in KDIGO before you start one. |
| Hypertension and CKD | 2025 AHA/ACC guideline: ACE inhibitor or ARB when eGFR is under 60 and albuminuria is at least 30 mg/g. |
| Dialysis indications | Acidosis, electrolyte emergency you cannot move, intoxicant, overload, uremic complication (pericarditis, encephalopathy, refractory bleeding). The letters are a memory hook. The patient is the indication. |

Contrast, NSAIDs, and ACE inhibitors are the three outpatient drugs that show up in a creatinine bump. NSAIDs constrict the afferent arteriole. ACE inhibitors dilate the efferent. Together, in a low-flow kidney, filtration falls. A small creatinine rise after starting an ACE inhibitor can be hemodynamic and acceptable. A large rise means stop and look for renal artery disease or volume loss. KDIGO and the drug label own the percent. Do not invent one.

Nephrotic syndrome is edema, heavy protein, and a thrombotic tendency. Nephritic syndrome is blood, casts, and hypertension. The Step 2 question is the complication and the next test (biopsy when it will change treatment), not the Step 1 immunofluorescence pattern, unless the stem is clearly asking for the mechanism.

Stones: a noncontrast CT is the usual adult test when the diagnosis is uncertain. Infection behind an obstructing stone is a drainage emergency, the same logic as cholangitis. Alpha blockers may be offered for some distal stones. Confirm size cutoffs rather than memorizing a millimeter from an old lecture.

## Checklist

- [ ] Obstruction is a bedside question, not a lab question.
- [ ] Calcium for ECG changes does not remove potassium.
- [ ] Slow correction of chronic hyponatremia.
- [ ] One of ACE inhibitor or ARB, plus think about an SGLT2 inhibitor.
- [ ] Infected obstructing stone gets drained.

## Common traps

- Insulin and beta agonist without a plan to remove potassium. The shift reverses.
- Normal saline as a reflex for every sodium problem. It is the wrong fluid for hypernatremia’s free-water deficit and it can worsen some acidoses.
- Combining ACE inhibitor and ARB “for the protein.” More hyperkalemia and acute injury, without a benefit you want.
- Biopsying every diabetic with protein and retinopathy. The diagnosis is often already clinical. Biopsy when the story does not fit.

## Practice this in the Qbank

Electrolyte items are a sequence: ECG or symptoms, shift or replace, then the cause. CKD items are albuminuria and the two drug classes that change the slope. Feed cards with eGFR cutoffs expire when labels change. Learn the class, then check the floor.

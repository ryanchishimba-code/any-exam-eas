# Hematology and oncology

## Framework

Step 2 wants the next test and the emergency, not the translocation.

| Picture | Next step |
| --- | --- |
| Microcytic anemia, low ferritin | Iron deficiency. Find the blood loss. In an adult man or a postmenopausal woman, that means a colon look, not a multivitamin. |
| Macrocytic anemia, neurologic symptoms | B12, not folate alone. Folate will not fix the cord. |
| Pancytopenia and blasts | Urgent hematology. This is not an outpatient iron trial. |
| Transfusion, fever, back pain, dark urine during the unit | Stop the blood. Clerical check. Support. This is hemolytic until proven otherwise. |
| Heparin, platelets falling by half, and a clot | Stop all heparin, including flushes. Do not give platelets for the number alone. Another anticoagulant is required. This is HIT physiology. |
| Febrile neutropenia | See the infectious disease chapter. Do not send the patient home on an oral drug you use for sinusitis. |
| Spinal cord compression from cancer | Steroids and urgent imaging, then definitive treatment. Do not wait on a clinic MRI next week if the patient cannot walk. |

Tumor lysis: high potassium, high phosphate, high uric acid, low calcium, after treatment of a bulky responsive cancer. Hydrate. Rasburicase is for high risk. Do not alkalinize by reflex.

## Checklist

- [ ] Iron deficiency in an older adult is a source hunt.
- [ ] B12 before you declare folate the whole story when the cord is involved.
- [ ] Stop the transfusion when the patient crashes during it.
- [ ] HIT: stop heparin. Do not platelet-transfuse the number.
- [ ] Cord compression is a same-day problem.

## Common traps

- Oral iron for a colon cancer.
- Platelets for HIT.
- Folate alone for subacute combined degeneration.

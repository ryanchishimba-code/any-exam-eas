# Endocrine

## Framework

The numbers diagnose. The comorbidity picks the drug. The emergency is fluids and potassium, not a new oral agent.

**Diabetes, ADA Standards of Care**

Diagnose with any one of these, confirmed unless the patient is clearly symptomatic: A1C 6.5% or higher, fasting plasma glucose 126 mg/dL or higher, 2-hour glucose 200 mg/dL or higher on a 75 g test, or a random glucose 200 mg/dL or higher with classic symptoms. Prediabetes is an A1C of 5.7% to 6.4%. USPSTF screens adults 35 to 70 who have overweight or obesity. ([ADA Standards of Care](https://diabetesjournals.org/care), [USPSTF](https://www.uspreventiveservicestaskforce.org/uspstf/recommendation/screening-for-prediabetes-and-type-2-diabetes))

| Comorbidity | Drug with proven benefit |
| --- | --- |
| Atherosclerotic disease | GLP-1 receptor agonist or SGLT2 inhibitor |
| Heart failure | SGLT2 inhibitor |
| CKD | SGLT2 inhibitor, or a GLP-1 receptor agonist if the SGLT2 inhibitor is not appropriate |
| None of these | Metformin remains a common foundation. Combine drugs to reach an individualized A1C. A younger healthy adult is often treated toward an A1C under 7%. Do not chase a normal A1C in a frail adult who falls. |

Hypoglycemia is a glucose under 70 mg/dL. Awake: 15 g of carbohydrate and recheck. Cannot swallow: glucagon.

**DKA versus hyperosmolar state**

DKA is hyperglycemia, an anion-gap acidosis, and ketones. Fluids first. If potassium is under 3.3 mEq/L, replace potassium before insulin. Insulin drives potassium into cells. Hyperosmolar hyperglycemic syndrome is extreme glucose, high osmolality, little ketoacidosis, and profound dehydration, usually in an older adult. Fluid first. Find the precipitant.

**Thyroid**

Primary hypothyroidism is a high TSH and a low free T4. Levothyroxine on an empty stomach. Recheck TSH in about 6 weeks. Start lower in older adults and in coronary disease (ATA hypothyroidism guidance). A nodule gets a TSH first. A low TSH goes to a radionuclide scan. A normal or high TSH goes to ultrasound. Do not biopsy a hot nodule as if it were cancer.

Thyroid storm: beta-blocker, thionamide, iodine after the thionamide, and a corticosteroid. Do not give iodine first.

## Checklist

- [ ] Confirm the diagnostic number unless the patient is symptomatic.
- [ ] Pick the diabetes drug from the comorbidity, not from habit.
- [ ] Potassium before insulin when it is under 3.3 in DKA.
- [ ] TSH before imaging a nodule.
- [ ] Iodine after the thionamide in storm.

## Common traps

- Insulin first in hypokalemic DKA.
- Metformin as the hero in heart failure when an SGLT2 inhibitor is the drug that changes outcomes.
- Biopsying a hot nodule.

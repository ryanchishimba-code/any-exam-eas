# Neurology

## Framework

Time and glucose decide the first minute. The localization decides the disease.

| Emergency | Do not skip |
| --- | --- |
| Sudden focal deficit | Glucose, then a noncontrast CT if stroke is possible. Do not give thrombolysis on a clinical hunch before the CT. The window is a guideline number. Confirm the current AHA/ASA window. Do not invent one here. |
| Worst headache of life | CT, then lumbar puncture if the CT is negative and the story is still a subarachnoid hemorrhage. |
| Seizure that will not stop | Benzodiazepine first, then a loading antiseizure drug. Glucose if it might be low. |
| Fever, headache, stiff neck | Antibiotics that cover the age group's organisms. Do not delay them for a CT when there is no focal sign, no papilledema, and no new seizure. Add ampicillin when the patient is over 50 or immunocompromised, for Listeria (IDSA meningitis guidance). Dexamethasone with or just before the first dose when pneumococcus is likely. |
| Temporal arteritis story, age over 50, new headache, jaw claudication | Steroids. Do not wait on the biopsy to start them if vision is threatened. ESR supports. It does not rule out. |

A first seizure with a return to baseline and a normal exam is not the same as status. Do not load every first seizure with a lifetime drug in the stem if the question is "next step." Often the next step is glucose, labs, and imaging, not a prescription.

Multiple sclerosis items want lesions separated in time and space, and they want you not to give a beta-interferon story when the question is an acute flare (steroids for the flare).

## Checklist

- [ ] Glucose before you call it a stroke.
- [ ] CT before thrombolysis.
- [ ] Thunderclap: CT, then lumbar puncture if needed.
- [ ] Status: benzodiazepine first.
- [ ] Steroids for suspected giant-cell arteritis when vision is at risk. Do not wait on the biopsy.

## Common traps

- A lumbar puncture first in a focal meningitis with papilledema.
- Thrombolysis without a CT.
- Phenytoin as the first drug in status, before a benzodiazepine.

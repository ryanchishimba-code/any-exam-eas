# Endocrine disease and nutrition

> **Study aid only.** Diagnostic thresholds from the ADA Standards of Care in Diabetes—2026, and the nutrition decisions USMLE now scores as their own discipline. Confirm the current Standards before you treat someone. They are revised every year.

## What this chapter covers

How diabetes is diagnosed, what to start when the person also has heart, kidney, or liver disease, the thyroid and adrenal emergencies, and the nutrition moves that are double-coded onto other systems. Endocrine is about 3–7% as a system. Nutrition is about 15–20% as a discipline that also belongs to a system. This chapter is where those meet.

## The framework

Diagnose diabetes, per the 2026 ADA Standards, with one of these, confirmed unless the picture is already unequivocal (a random glucose at or above 200 mg/dL with classic symptoms):

| Test | Diabetes threshold |
| --- | --- |
| A1C | 6.5% or higher, on a laboratory method, not a casual point-of-care excuse |
| Fasting glucose | 126 mg/dL or higher |
| 2-hour glucose on a 75 g load | 200 mg/dL or higher |
| Random glucose plus symptoms | 200 mg/dL or higher |

A1C is wrong when red-cell life is wrong: hemoglobinopathy, hemolysis, pregnancy, recent transfusion, advanced kidney disease. Use glucose or CGM then.

For many nonpregnant adults the A1C goal is under 7%, if that can be reached without severe hypoglycemia. Older adults and people with limited life expectancy get a looser goal. Pregnancy gets a tighter, different set of targets. Do not export the 7% into the obstetric chapter.

Drug choice is no longer “metformin for everyone, forever, first.” The 2026 pharmacologic Standards say: if atherosclerotic disease, heart failure, or CKD is present, choose an agent with evidence for that condition (GLP-1 receptor agonist and/or SGLT2 inhibitor, matched to the condition). If the only job is glucose, metformin remains a commonly used, inexpensive, low-hypoglycemia option. Weight and fatty-liver disease are their own goals, not side notes. Stop an SGLT2 inhibitor when a person is acutely ill and not eating. The ketoacidosis risk is real and can happen with a glucose that is not sky-high.

| Emergency | The pattern |
| --- | --- |
| DKA | Gap acidosis, ketones, often a glucose that is high but not always extreme in SGLT2-associated cases. Fluids, insulin, and potassium replacement. Potassium may be high in the blood and low in the body. |
| Hyperosmolar state | Very high glucose, high osmolality, little ketoacidosis, profound dehydration. Fluids first. |
| Thyroid storm | Fever, tachycardia, altered mentation, a trigger. Beta blocker, antithyroid drug, iodine after the drug, steroid, and the trigger. |
| Myxedema coma | Hypothermia, bradycardia, altered mentation, a trigger. Intravenous thyroid hormone and stress-dose steroid until adrenal failure is excluded. |
| Adrenal crisis | Shock out of proportion, often with hyperpigmentation if the failure is primary. Do not wait for a cortisol to give the steroid if the person is crashing. |

Nutrition on this Step is counseling that changes risk: a dietary pattern for blood pressure and lipids, protein and sodium in CKD, folate before and during early pregnancy, and screening for food insecurity because a perfect diet plan fails if the person cannot get food. USMLE points at the 2024 nutrition-competencies statement for what “nutrition” means here. It is not a vitamin poster.

## Checklist

- [ ] Confirm an asymptomatic lab. Do not confirm a crashing, classic presentation before you treat.
- [ ] A1C fails when red cells lie.
- [ ] Heart failure, CKD, or atherosclerotic disease picks the diabetes drug class before “whatever is cheapest” does.
- [ ] Hold SGLT2 inhibitors in acute illness.
- [ ] Storm and crisis are triggers plus hormone. Find the trigger.

## Common traps

- Metformin as the automatic first drug in a person with heart failure. The heart-failure evidence is the SGLT2 inhibitor.
- Sliding-scale insulin alone for a hospitalized patient who is not eating. It chases glucose. It does not a plan.
- Iodine before the antithyroid drug in storm. You can fuel hormone synthesis.
- A single low TSH treated as hyperthyroidism in a sick inpatient. Non-thyroidal illness exists. Repeat when the person is well unless the clinical picture is toxic.

## Practice this in the Qbank

Diabetes items on Step 2 are “which drug, given this comorbidity,” not “which receptor.” Thyroid items are storm versus outpatient. Feed cards with A1C goals should say for whom. A card that says “everyone under 7” is wrong for pregnancy and often wrong for a frail older adult.

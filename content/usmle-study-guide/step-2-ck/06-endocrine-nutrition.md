# Endocrine disease and nutrition

> **Study aid only.** Diagnostic thresholds from the ADA Standards of Care in Diabetes—2026, and the nutrition decisions USMLE now scores as their own discipline. Confirm the current Standards before you treat someone. They are revised every year.

## What this chapter covers

How diabetes is diagnosed, what to start when the person also has heart, kidney, or liver disease, the thyroid and adrenal emergencies, and the nutrition moves that are double-coded onto other systems. Endocrine is about 3–7% as a system. Nutrition is about 15–20% as a discipline that also belongs to a system. This chapter is where those meet.

## The framework

Diagnose diabetes, per the 2026 ADA Standards, with one of these, confirmed unless the picture is already unequivocal (a random glucose at or above 200 mg/dL with classic symptoms):

| Test | Diabetes threshold |
| --- | --- |
| A1C | 6.5% or higher, on a laboratory method, not a casual point-of-care excuse |
| Fasting glucose | 126 mg/dL or higher |
| 2-hour glucose on a 75 g load | 200 mg/dL or higher |
| Random glucose plus symptoms | 200 mg/dL or higher |

A1C is wrong when red-cell life is wrong: hemoglobinopathy, hemolysis, pregnancy, recent transfusion, advanced kidney disease. Use glucose or CGM then.

For many nonpregnant adults the A1C goal is under 7%, if that can be reached without severe hypoglycemia. Older adults and people with limited life expectancy get a looser goal. Pregnancy gets a tighter, different set of targets. Do not export the 7% into the obstetric chapter.

Drug choice is no longer “metformin for everyone, forever, first.” The 2026 pharmacologic Standards say: if atherosclerotic disease, heart failure, or CKD is present, choose an agent with evidence for that condition (GLP-1 receptor agonist and/or SGLT2 inhibitor, matched to the condition). If the only job is glucose, metformin remains a commonly used, inexpensive, low-hypoglycemia option. Weight and fatty-liver disease are their own goals, not side notes. Stop an SGLT2 inhibitor when a person is acutely ill and not eating. The ketoacidosis risk is real and can happen with a glucose that is not sky-high.

| Emergency | The pattern |
| --- | --- |
| DKA | Gap acidosis, ketones, often a glucose that is high but not always extreme in SGLT2-associated cases. Fluids, insulin, and potassium replacement. Potassium may be high in the blood and low in the body. |
| Hyperosmolar state | Very high glucose, high osmolality, little ketoacidosis, profound dehydration. Fluids first. |
| Thyroid storm | Fever, tachycardia, altered mentation, a trigger. Beta blocker, antithyroid drug, iodine after the drug, steroid, and the trigger. |
| Myxedema coma | Hypothermia, bradycardia, altered mentation, a trigger. Intravenous thyroid hormone and stress-dose steroid until adrenal failure is excluded. |
| Adrenal crisis | Shock out of proportion, often with hyperpigmentation if the failure is primary. Do not wait for a cortisol to give the steroid if the person is crashing. |

Nutrition on this Step is counseling that changes risk: a dietary pattern for blood pressure and lipids, protein and sodium in CKD, folate before and during early pregnancy, and screening for food insecurity because a perfect diet plan fails if the person cannot get food. USMLE points at the 2024 nutrition-competencies statement for what “nutrition” means here. It is not a vitamin poster.

## High-yield atlas

Endocrine items on Step 2 CK are a threshold, then the comorbidity that picks the drug, then the emergency you treat before the assay returns. Nutrition items are the same kind of decision: thiamine before glucose, phosphate before a full tray, and food on the table before another hypoglycemia-prone drug. The framework above is the map. This atlas is the set.

### Diabetes mellitus, the diagnosis

One asymptomatic number can be a laboratory error. Symptoms plus a glucose at or above 200 cannot.

**Vignette.** A 52-year-old feels well. A laboratory A1C is 6.7 percent. Hemoglobin is normal. He has not been transfused. The fasting glucose has not been repeated.
**Reasoning.** He meets an A1C threshold and has no symptoms, so confirm it. A random glucose of 320 with polyuria and weight loss would be treated today, without waiting for a second A1C. The diagnosis is Diabetes mellitus, the diagnosis.
**NBME trap.** One A1C after a transfusion, or a demand for a confirmatory A1C before insulin in a patient who is acidemic with a glucose of 400.
**Hook.** Asymptomatic: confirm. Classic and sick: treat.

### Type 2 diabetes with heart failure

The SGLT2 inhibitor changes heart-failure outcomes. Euglycemic ketoacidosis is the risk if he keeps taking it while fasting and ill.

**Vignette.** Ejection fraction is 30 percent. A1C is 8.0 percent. He takes metformin only. He is euvolemic, eating, and not hospitalized. Kidney function allows the class.
**Reasoning.** Heart failure is present, so the next drug is an SGLT2 inhibitor. A sulfonylurea does not change that outcome. If he returns unable to eat, the same drug is held. The diagnosis is Type 2 diabetes with heart failure.
**NBME trap.** Metformin as the whole plan in heart failure, or continuing the SGLT2 inhibitor through an NPO admission.
**Hook.** Heart failure picks the SGLT2 inhibitor. Illness holds it.

### Type 2 diabetes with atherosclerotic disease

Glucose is one target. A prior infarct is the other, and only some drugs have been shown to move it.

**Vignette.** He had a myocardial infarction two years ago. A1C is 7.8 percent. There is no heart failure. He takes metformin. BMI is 34. He is well and eating.
**Reasoning.** Atherosclerotic disease calls for an agent with outcome evidence. The BMI makes a GLP-1 receptor agonist particularly coherent. Another metformin titration, while the artery is ignored, is the outdated sequence. The diagnosis is Type 2 diabetes with atherosclerotic disease.
**NBME trap.** A sulfonylurea because the A1C moved, or delaying the outcome drug for another metformin step.
**Hook.** A prior infarct picks a drug with outcome evidence.

### Diabetic ketoacidosis

Insulin deficiency generates ketoacids. Potassium has left the cells and the urine, so the serum number lies about the stores.

**Vignette.** Glucose is 480 mg/dL. pH is 7.15. Bicarbonate is 10 mEq/L. The anion gap is 24. Beta-hydroxybutyrate is high. Potassium is 3.1 mEq/L. He has been vomiting.
**Reasoning.** The gap and the ketones are the crisis. Potassium under 3.3 means potassium before insulin, or the insulin will drop the potassium further. When glucose falls, add dextrose and keep the insulin going until the gap closes. A glucose of 160 on an SGLT2 inhibitor with this same gap is the same crisis. The diagnosis is Diabetic ketoacidosis.
**NBME trap.** Insulin at a potassium of 3.1, stopping insulin when glucose normalizes, or missing the crisis because glucose was only 160.
**Hook.** Potassium under 3.3: delay insulin. Gap open: do not stop it.

### Hyperosmolar hyperglycemic state

Enough insulin remains to suppress ketones and not enough to clear the glucose. The deficit is water.

**Vignette.** A 78-year-old from a nursing home is stuporous and profoundly dry. Glucose is 980 mg/dL. Measured osmolality is 350. pH is 7.38. Bicarbonate is 22 mEq/L. Ketones are negative.
**Reasoning.** No ketoacidosis and a very high osmolality mean volume is the first treatment. An insulin bolus before fluid, or bicarbonate because the glucose is frightening, misses the physiology. Search for a trigger, often infection or an inability to drink. The diagnosis is Hyperosmolar hyperglycemic state.
**NBME trap.** Bicarbonate, insulin before any fluid in a hypotensive dry patient, or the ketoacidosis label because the glucose is high.
**Hook.** Very high glucose, no ketones: fluid first.

### Thyroid storm

A surge of hormone plus a precipitant: infection, surgery, childbirth, or stopped medicine.

**Vignette.** She stopped her antithyroid pills. Temperature is 40 C. Heart rate is 150 in atrial fibrillation. She is vomiting and agitated. The film shows a pneumonia. A radioiodine uptake and aspirin are suggested.
**Reasoning.** Fever, fibrillation, vomiting, and agitation on this background are storm. Give the beta blocker and the antithyroid drug, wait before iodine, give a glucocorticoid, and treat the pneumonia. A scan delays care. Aspirin displaces hormone. The diagnosis is Thyroid storm.
**NBME trap.** Iodine before the antithyroid drug, aspirin for the fever, or a radioiodine uptake while she is in storm.
**Hook.** Drug, then iodine. Never iodine first. No aspirin.

### Myxedema coma

Profound hormone deficiency plus a precipitant. The airway and the cortisol will not wait for the assay.

**Vignette.** Temperature is 33 C. Heart rate is 40. She is barely rousable. Sodium is 126. Glucose is 58. There is a thyroidectomy scar. Carbon dioxide is retained. The TSH is drawn and not back.
**Reasoning.** Hypothermia, bradycardia, hypercapnia, and a scar are this coma. Give intravenous thyroid hormone and a stress-dose steroid, support the airway, and warm her passively. The diagnosis is Myxedema coma.
**NBME trap.** Waiting for the TSH, oral hormone only, active rewarming as the treatment, or skipping the steroid.
**Hook.** Cold, slow, and not waking: intravenous hormone and a steroid.

### Adrenal crisis

Without cortisol the vasculature does not hold a response to catecholamines. Volume and pressors alone fail.

**Vignette.** Blood pressure is 72/40 after a liter of saline. Sodium is 124. Potassium is 6.2. Glucose is 55. He stopped long-term prednisone three days ago. He is vomiting.
**Reasoning.** Steroid withdrawal plus refractory shock and this pattern is crisis. Intravenous hydrocortisone and saline now. A stimulation test before the drug leaves him hypotensive. Fludrocortisone tablets are the outpatient mineralocorticoid. The diagnosis is Adrenal crisis.
**NBME trap.** Waiting for the cosyntropin result, pressors without hydrocortisone, or fludrocortisone as the emergency drug.
**Hook.** Crashing after steroids stop: hydrocortisone now.

### Subacute thyroiditis

Inflammation, release, a possible hypothyroid dip, then recovery. Not autonomous production.

**Vignette.** Two weeks after a sore throat, a 30-year-old man has an exquisitely painful thyroid, heart rate 110, a low TSH, and a high free T4. There are no eye findings. ESR is high. He is not pregnant.
**Reasoning.** Pain after a virus plus thyrotoxic labs is release of stored hormone. A beta blocker and an anti-inflammatory. Methimazole is the drug for a nontender, high-uptake gland, not this one. The diagnosis is Subacute thyroiditis.
**NBME trap.** Methimazole for a tender gland, radioiodine ablation, or iodine.
**Hook.** A tender thyroid: a beta blocker, not methimazole.

### Thyroid nodule

Most lumps are benign. A suppressed TSH means the lesion may be making hormone, and a hot one is rarely cancer.

**Vignette.** A 45-year-old has a 1.2 cm lump in the thyroid found on exam. She feels well. TSH has not been ordered. The next line on the chart is a referral for lobectomy.
**Reasoning.** TSH comes first. A normal TSH leads to ultrasound and a needle only if the pattern and the size earn it. A low TSH leads to a scan, and a hot lesion is not a cancer operation. The diagnosis is Thyroid nodule.
**NBME trap.** Aspiration before the TSH, a radionuclide scan when the TSH is normal, or surgery for a small spongiform lesion.
**Hook.** TSH first. A hot lesion is not biopsied first.

### Primary hyperparathyroidism

One or more glands are making PTH out of turn. Urine calcium separates this from a genetic set-point disorder.

**Vignette.** Calcium is 11.4 mg/dL with an upper limit of 10.2. PTH is 88 and is not suppressed. Phosphorus is low. She is 42 and takes neither a thiazide nor lithium. The proposed plan is cinacalcet. No urine calcium has been measured.
**Reasoning.** Age under 50 and a calcium more than 1 mg/dL above the limit are already surgical indications, once urine calcium excludes the familial disorder. A scan localizes. It does not diagnose. A detectable PTH at this calcium is inappropriate, not normal. The diagnosis is Primary hyperparathyroidism.
**NBME trap.** Cinacalcet as definitive therapy in a young surgical candidate, an operation despite very low urine calcium, or canceling the diagnosis because the scan is negative.
**Hook.** Under 50, or calcium a full point high: surgery, after the familial mimic is out.

### Cushing syndrome

Cortisol excess. Most look-alikes are exogenous steroid or ordinary weight gain. The screen prevents a needless adrenal CT.

**Vignette.** A 38-year-old has abdominal weight gain, wide purple striae, proximal weakness, and a new fasting glucose of 140. She uses no steroid, no injections, and no megestrol. One morning cortisol was normal. The next order is an adrenal CT.
**Reasoning.** The phenotype is real and exogenous steroid is out, so she needs a proper screen. A random morning cortisol does not clear her, and a CT of an incidental nodule would not prove it makes cortisol. The diagnosis is Cushing syndrome.
**NBME trap.** An adrenal CT first, or one random morning cortisol used as exclusion.
**Hook.** Screen the cortisol. Do not CT the adrenal first.

### Primary aldosteronism

Aldosterone is on and renin is off. Potassium is wasted. The pressure will not yield until the aldosterone is dealt with.

**Vignette.** Blood pressure is 168/100 on amlodipine, lisinopril, and chlorthalidone. Potassium is 3.1 mEq/L. He is 62. No ratio has been drawn. The plan is an adrenal CT and removal of whatever is seen.
**Reasoning.** Resistant hypertension plus hypokalemia is this workup. Correct the potassium and draw the ratio before any image. A 1 cm nodule on CT in a 62-year-old is not an automatic adrenalectomy. The diagnosis is Primary aldosteronism.
**NBME trap.** CT first, adrenalectomy from the CT in an older adult, or spironolactone started the week of the blood draw.
**Hook.** The ratio before the picture. Sampling before the knife in most adults.

### Inpatient hyperglycemia

Scheduled insulin matches basal needs and meals. A scale only chases the last number.

**Vignette.** He is eating three meals after orthopedic surgery. Glucoses are 240, 280, and 260. The only insulin order is a correction scale. His home SGLT2 inhibitor is still on the list. He is not in an ICU.
**Reasoning.** Eating and persistently high means basal, prandial, and correction, with a target of 140 to 180. Hold the SGLT2 inhibitor because he is acutely ill. A scale alone will keep chasing. A target under 110 is the old ICU harm pattern. The diagnosis is Inpatient hyperglycemia.
**NBME trap.** A correction scale alone, continuing the SGLT2 inhibitor while intake is unreliable, or a ward target under 110.
**Hook.** Eating in the hospital: basal, mealtime, and correction. Hold the SGLT2 inhibitor.

### Wernicke encephalopathy

Thiamine deficiency in a high-turnover brain region. A glucose load consumes what little thiamine remains.

**Vignette.** Daily heavy alcohol use, confusion, ataxia, and nystagmus. The order set starts dextrose now and a multivitamin pill in the morning. No thiamine is charted. MRI is listed as the next step.
**Reasoning.** This picture is the thiamine emergency. Parenteral thiamine comes before the glucose. An oral vitamin does not treat it in time. The MRI does not change the decision to give the dose. The diagnosis is Wernicke encephalopathy.
**NBME trap.** Glucose first, an oral multivitamin as the only replacement, or an MRI before the dose.
**Hook.** Parenteral thiamine before glucose.

### Vitamin B12 deficiency

Demyelination of the posterior and lateral columns. The marrow finding is optional. The cord finding is not.

**Vignette.** Numb feet, a wide-based gait, and bilateral Babinski signs. MCV is 112. He takes metformin and a proton-pump inhibitor. The plan is folic acid and a blood count in a month. No cobalamin level has been drawn.
**Reasoning.** Cord findings plus macrocytosis are cobalamin deficiency until the level and the methylmalonic acid say otherwise. Folate first can improve the MCV and miss the cord. Neurologic disease is treated parenterally. The diagnosis is Vitamin B12 deficiency.
**NBME trap.** Folate as the first vitamin, a normal MCV used as exclusion, or oral replacement as the whole plan when he cannot walk.
**Hook.** Cord signs: B12 and methylmalonic acid before folate.

### Refeeding syndrome

Insulin drives phosphate, potassium, and magnesium into cells that are rebuilding. The serum levels crash.

**Vignette.** BMI is 14 after months of restriction. On the second day of an unrestricted meal plan she is weak. Phosphate is 1.2 mg/dL. Potassium and magnesium are low. Thiamine has not been given.
**Reasoning.** Calories arrived before the electrolytes and the thiamine. Hold the full tray, replace phosphate, potassium, and magnesium, give thiamine, and restart more slowly with daily labs. The diagnosis is Refeeding syndrome.
**NBME trap.** A full-calorie tray on day one with no phosphate checked, or watching the phosphate and forgetting thiamine.
**Hook.** Low BMI and a full tray: check phosphate and give thiamine.

### Food insecurity in diabetes

The glucose swings because the meals swing. A higher insulin dose makes the next missed meal dangerous.

**Vignette.** A1C rose from 7.4 to 9.1 percent. He reports glucose under 60 on days he skipped dinner. He says he worries that food will run out. The plan on the chart is to add a sulfonylurea and a stricter diet sheet.
**Reasoning.** The screen is positive and he is already hypoglycemic when a meal is missed. A sulfonylurea makes the next missed meal dangerous. Food resources and a regimen that tolerates a skipped meal come before another secretagogue. The diagnosis is Food insecurity in diabetes.
**NBME trap.** Adding a sulfonylurea, intensifying insulin, or writing nonadherence without asking the hunger questions.
**Hook.** Ask if the food will run out before you add a drug that can bottom out the glucose.

## Checklist

- [ ] Confirm an asymptomatic lab. Do not confirm a crashing, classic presentation before you treat.
- [ ] A1C fails when red cells lie.
- [ ] Heart failure, CKD, or atherosclerotic disease picks the diabetes drug class before “whatever is cheapest” does.
- [ ] Hold SGLT2 inhibitors in acute illness.
- [ ] Storm and crisis are triggers plus hormone. Find the trigger.

## Common traps

- Metformin as the automatic first drug in a person with heart failure. The heart-failure evidence is the SGLT2 inhibitor.
- Sliding-scale insulin alone for a hospitalized patient who is not eating. It chases glucose. It does not a plan.
- Iodine before the antithyroid drug in storm. You can fuel hormone synthesis.
- A single low TSH treated as hyperthyroidism in a sick inpatient. Non-thyroidal illness exists. Repeat when the person is well unless the clinical picture is toxic.

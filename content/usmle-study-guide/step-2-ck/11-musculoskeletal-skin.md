# Musculoskeletal, skin, and rheumatology

> **Study aid only.** The joint you must not miss and the rash that is a systemic disease. Drug choice follows a current ACR or dermatology guideline.

## What this chapter covers

Septic arthritis, fracture and compartment decisions, the inflammatory arthritides at the level of “what confirms it and what damages an organ,” and the skin findings that change the next hour. This block is about 6–12% of Step 2 CK.

## The framework

One hot joint is a tap. Do it before antibiotics if a few minutes are safe, and do not skip it because you already “know” it is gout. Septic arthritis and gout can share a joint. The tap is the cell count, the gram stain, and the crystals. Blood cultures too, because the joint was seeded from the blood often enough to matter.

| Problem | Decision |
| --- | --- |
| Open fracture | Antibiotics and operative care now. A closed fracture with intact nerves and perfusion can often wait for the reduction plan. |
| Compartment syndrome | Pressure and pain on stretch. Fasciotomy. A present pulse does not clear the diagnosis. |
| Cauda equina | Saddle anesthesia, urinary retention, bilateral leg symptoms. MRI and decompression. Not physical therapy. |
| Hip fracture in an older adult | Surgery on a prompt timeline, pain control, and a reason to look at bone health after. Delay costs function and clots. |
| Suspected septic joint with a prosthetic | Orthopedics early. The rules of a native joint tap do not copy over cleanly. |
| Giant cell arteritis | Steroids. See the neurology chapter. Vision does not wait. |
| Lupus flare versus infection | Steroids make infection worse. Fever in lupus is infection until you have a reason. |
| Melanoma | ABCDE is a screen for the eye, not a diagnosis. Excisional biopsy, not a shaved fragment of a suspicious lesion, when melanoma is likely. |

Rheumatoid arthritis is a disease-modifying problem. Symptom drugs are not the long-term plan. Early referral for a disease-modifying drug reduces joint destruction. Screen for latent tuberculosis and hepatitis before you start a biologic. That screen is the question more often than the biologic’s brand.

Gout treatment has two clocks. The acute attack is an anti-inflammatory (NSAID, colchicine early, or steroid) if the kidney and the stomach allow it. Urate lowering is the long plan, usually continued through an attack if the person is already on it, and not started in the middle of a first attack without a plan for prophylaxis. Confirm the ACR gout guideline before you change a start-time rule you learned years ago. Allopurinol in a high-risk ancestry (including many people of Southeast Asian and African descent, and people with known HLA-B*58:01 risk) needs a conversation about testing. The hypersensitivity is severe.

Skin infections: necrotizing fasciitis is pain out of proportion, systemic toxicity, and a surgical emergency. Imaging must not delay the operating room when the story is clear. Mark the border of cellulitis so you can see whether it moves.

## High-yield atlas

Musculoskeletal and skin items are a next action. A hot native joint is a tap. A tense compartment, a cauda equina, and dead fascia go to the operating room even when a pulse is still present or a scan would be comforting. Rheumatology items ask which organ is about to be hurt and what you must screen before the drug. This atlas is the set. Confirm the current ACR, IDSA, or AAOS file before a threshold is used on a patient.

### Septic arthritis of a native joint

The joint was seeded, often from the blood. A high synovial white-cell count supports infection and does not prove it. A negative Gram stain does not clear it.

**Vignette.** A 62-year-old man has a hot swollen knee and cannot stand on it. He is febrile. Three years ago a similar knee showed crystals. The plan on the board is an antibiotic and a steroid, with no needle.
**Reasoning.** A crystal history does not exclude a seeded joint. The culture is useless if the antibiotic goes in first and the patient is stable enough for the tap. Tenosynovitis plus a few pustules in a younger patient is the gonococcal pattern, and that joint culture is often negative, so mucosal testing matters. The diagnosis is Septic arthritis of a native joint.
**NBME trap.** Steroids or antibiotics for a hot native joint you have not tapped, because the old chart said crystals.
**Hook.** One hot native joint is a tap. Crystals do not cancel infection.

### Acute compartment syndrome

Swollen muscle inside a closed fascia stops its own perfusion. The artery at the ankle can still be felt while the muscle dies.

**Vignette.** Six hours after a tibial shaft fracture the calf is wooden. Dorsiflexion of the toes makes him cry out. The foot is pink and the dorsalis pedis pulse is easy to feel. He asks whether the pulse means he can wait for the morning.
**Reasoning.** Pain on passive stretch with a tense compartment is the diagnosis while the pulse is still there. Waiting for a lost pulse waits for dead muscle. A cast that is part of the story comes off on the way to the operating room. The diagnosis is Acute compartment syndrome.
**NBME trap.** A present pulse, a warm foot, or a normal radiograph used to cancel the fasciotomy.
**Hook.** The pulse is a late and unreliable friend. Open the fascia.

### Cauda equina syndrome

The nerve roots below the cord are compressed. The bladder fails by retention. Overflow can look like the patient is still voiding.

**Vignette.** A 45-year-old man has two days of pain down both legs. The perineum is numb. He says he is urinating, but he has not emptied, and a catheter returns a large residual. Rectal tone is reduced. A referral for therapy next week is on the chart.
**Reasoning.** Saddle sensation, retention, and bilateral roots are compression of the cauda. Overflow is not proof the bladder works. The residual is the clue. Imaging is MRI, and the treatment is decompression, not a stretch program. The diagnosis is Cauda equina syndrome.
**NBME trap.** Physical therapy, or a steroid pack, for saddle anesthesia and retention.
**Hook.** Saddle, residual, both legs: MRI and the operating room.

### Necrotizing fasciitis

The fascia dies faster than the skin shows. Pain out of proportion and toxicity are the disease. Crepitus is a late gift, not a requirement.

**Vignette.** A man has a small red patch on the thigh that was the size of a coin this morning. By afternoon the pain is unbearable, he is hypotensive, and a bulla has appeared. Someone orders a CT and a cellulitis admission for the morning.
**Reasoning.** Toxicity plus pain beyond the skin is a surgical disease. Imaging is allowed only if it does not delay the knife when the story is this clear. A low score on a laboratory tool does not send him to the floor. The diagnosis is Necrotizing fasciitis.
**NBME trap.** A CT, a score, or oral antibiotics used to postpone exploration.
**Hook.** Pain out of proportion and a sick patient: the operating room is the test.

### Open fracture

The fracture hematoma is contaminated. Hours matter for infection. A photograph is not debridement.

**Vignette.** A man falls from a ladder. The tibia is tenting through a dirty laceration with grass in it. The foot is warm and he can move his toes. The plan is a dressing, a picture for the group chat, and antibiotics in the morning.
**Reasoning.** Bone that has met the outside is an open injury even when the foot is perfused. Antibiotics and the operating room start now. Farm dirt is a broader set of organisms than a clean puncture. Tetanus status is part of the same hour. The diagnosis is Open fracture.
**NBME trap.** Morning antibiotics, or primary closure in the emergency department as the definitive care.
**Hook.** If the outside world touched the bone, the clock has started.

### Hip fracture in an older adult

The fracture is urgent, not elective. Delay costs function and clots. A medical screen looks for active instability, not a theoretical stenosis.

**Vignette.** An 82-year-old woman slipped in the kitchen. The leg is short and turned out. She cannot lift it. She walks two flights at home without chest pain. A colleague wants a preoperative stress test tomorrow and surgery next week.
**Reasoning.** The leg tells you the fracture. She has functional capacity and no active cardiac syndrome, so the stress test does not change a safe urgent fixation. Waiting a week is how these patients lose function and form clots. The diagnosis is Hip fracture in an older adult.
**NBME trap.** A nonurgent cardiac clearance that pushes fixation out by days.
**Hook.** Short, externally rotated, older adult: fix it promptly.

### Acute gout

Monosodium urate crystals. Negatively birefringent needles. The flare is inflammation. The urate plan is the long disease.

**Vignette.** A man wakes with a toe so tender that the sheet hurts. He has chronic kidney disease and heart failure. He is not on a urate-lowering drug. The only order is indomethacin. Nobody has mentioned a tap, and he is febrile.
**Reasoning.** The toe is the classic site, and he is also febrile, so a tap still has to be considered before you commit. An NSAID is a poor first flare drug when the kidney and the heart are already failing. A steroid or another guideline option fits better. Starting allopurinol tonight with no prophylaxis is the other trap. The diagnosis is Acute gout.
**NBME trap.** An NSAID for every flare, including the patient in heart failure or chronic kidney disease, or a steroid into a joint you have not tapped.
**Hook.** Two clocks: the flare tonight, the urate plan on purpose.

### Allopurinol hypersensitivity

The allele marks a severe cutaneous and systemic reaction: rash, eosinophils, hepatitis, kidney injury. The starting dose and the kidney also change the risk.

**Vignette.** A man of Han Chinese descent has had three attacks this year and a tophus. Allopurinol is the intended long-term drug. The kidney is moderately impaired. The prescription is a full starting dose today, and no one has mentioned a genetic test.
**Reasoning.** He has an indication for urate-lowering therapy, and allopurinol is still the first-line drug. His ancestry is exactly the group the ACR guideline conditionally tests before the first dose. A high start in a reduced kidney is the second risk. A rash with fever and a rising creatinine later is a stop, not a dose increase. The diagnosis is Allopurinol hypersensitivity.
**NBME trap.** Skipping the allele test in a higher-risk ancestry, or starting high and calling it standard.
**Hook.** Han Chinese, Korean, Thai, or African American: test before allopurinol.

### Rheumatoid arthritis

Synovium destroys cartilage and bone. Symptom control does not stop the erosion. Early disease-modifying therapy is how joints are saved.

**Vignette.** A 38-year-old woman has six weeks of swollen knuckles and wrists and two hours of morning stiffness. Rheumatoid factor is positive. The only prescription is a standing NSAID, with a plan to recheck in six months.
**Reasoning.** This duration and pattern is inflammatory arthritis, and waiting on a symptom drug is how erosions happen. The disease-modifying drug is the treatment. Pregnancy, the liver, and the kidney change which drug, not whether a disease-modifying drug is needed. The diagnosis is Rheumatoid arthritis.
**NBME trap.** NSAIDs alone for months, or a biologic with no tuberculosis or hepatitis B screen.
**Hook.** Symptom drugs are not the long plan. Modify the disease.

### Latent tuberculosis before a biologic

TNF blockade lets a walled-off focus reactivate. Hepatitis B can reactivate the same way. The screen is the question more often than the brand name.

**Vignette.** A woman with erosive synovitis has failed methotrexate. A TNF inhibitor is ordered for tomorrow. She lived in a high-prevalence country and had a cough last winter that was never imaged. No release assay is on the chart.
**Reasoning.** The drug class is the risk. The missing assay is the next step, and a cough needs a film so you are not about to immunosuppress active disease. Active disease would be combination therapy and a delay. Latent disease is treated before the TNF inhibitor, not after the first infection declares itself. The diagnosis is Latent tuberculosis before a biologic.
**NBME trap.** The biologic today, the assay next month.
**Hook.** No screen, no TNF inhibitor.

### Giant cell arteritis

Granulomatous inflammation of medium and large arteries, including the vessels to the optic nerve. Vision is the organ you cannot get back.

**Vignette.** A 74-year-old woman has a week of temporal headache and pain in the jaw when she chews. This morning the left eye dimmed for a few minutes. The sedimentation rate is not back. The biopsy is scheduled in four days. No steroid has been given.
**Reasoning.** Jaw pain with chewing and a visual symptom over age 50 are enough to treat. The biopsy does not come first. Four days of waiting is how the other eye is lost. Skip lesions mean a negative sample would not have saved her anyway. The diagnosis is Giant cell arteritis.
**NBME trap.** Waiting on the biopsy, or on the sedimentation rate, while vision is changing.
**Hook.** Vision does not wait on the pathology report.

### Suspected melanoma

Melanocytes have invaded. The depth is the prognosis and the surgical plan. A transected base destroys the measurement.

**Vignette.** A 52-year-old man has a mole on the back that darkened and grew over three months. The border is notched and the color is mixed. A plan is written for a superficial shave so the specimen is easy to remove in clinic.
**Reasoning.** Change, border, and color are the lesion you excise in full thickness. A shave that stops inside the pigment leaves you unable to measure the depth, which is the fact the next operation needs. The diagnosis is Suspected melanoma.
**NBME trap.** A superficial shave, a freeze, or reassurance for a changing ugly duckling.
**Hook.** If you think it is melanoma, do not shave through the base.

### Infection in systemic lupus

Fever is not a lupus vital sign you can ignore. Complement and anti-dsDNA can support a flare. They do not clear bacteremia.

**Vignette.** A woman with a malar rash, painless oral ulcers, and a positive ANA is on hydroxychloroquine and a low dose of prednisone. She is febrile and coughing. The plan is to double the prednisone for a flare and skip the chest film.
**Reasoning.** The background is lupus. The new problem is fever, and fever is infection until cultures and a film say otherwise. Doubling the steroid before that workup is how a pneumonia is mistaken for a flare. The diagnosis is Infection in systemic lupus.
**NBME trap.** Steroids first for fever, because the chart says lupus.
**Hook.** Fever in lupus is infection until you have a reason.

### Slipped capital femoral epiphysis

The femoral head slips off the neck through a weak growth plate. Knee pain is referred. Both hips are at risk.

**Vignette.** A 13-year-old boy with obesity has three weeks of left knee pain and a limp. The knee film is normal. Flexing the hip rotates the leg outward. He is told to stretch and to follow up if the knee stays sore.
**Reasoning.** Knee pain with a normal knee and a rotation block at the hip is the slip. Weight-bearing while you wait is how a stable slip becomes unstable. The frog-leg view is the film. Pinning is the treatment. The diagnosis is Slipped capital femoral epiphysis.
**NBME trap.** A normal knee film and a physical-therapy referral.
**Hook.** Adolescent knee pain: examine the hip.

### Axial spondyloarthritis

The enthesis and the sacroiliac joint are inflamed. The pain improves when he moves, which is the opposite of mechanical back pain.

**Vignette.** A 26-year-old man has a year of low-back stiffness that is worst when he gets out of bed and eases when he walks. He had a painful red eye last spring. HLA-B27 is ordered as the only test, and methotrexate is proposed for the spine.
**Reasoning.** Inflammatory back pain plus uveitis is axial disease until imaging says otherwise. The allele is not the diagnosis. Methotrexate does not treat the spine. An NSAID is the first drug, and a biologic aimed at TNF or IL-17 is the next step if the axis stays active. The diagnosis is Axial spondyloarthritis.
**NBME trap.** A positive HLA-B27 as the whole diagnosis, or methotrexate for axial disease.
**Hook.** Better with activity, worse at rest, young back: think the sacroiliac joint.

### Acute osteomyelitis

Bacteria are in bone. Radiographs lag by days to a couple of weeks. Probe-to-bone in a diabetic ulcer is already strong evidence.

**Vignette.** A man with diabetes has a heel ulcer. A probe passes to hard bone. He is afebrile and eating. The radiograph is normal. The plan is five days of an oral antibiotic for skin infection and no culture.
**Reasoning.** Probe-to-bone is osteomyelitis until proven otherwise, and a normal early film is expected. He is stable, so the culture should come before the antibiotic. A short skin course does not treat bone. If he were septic, blood cultures and antibiotics would not wait. The diagnosis is Acute osteomyelitis.
**NBME trap.** A normal radiograph, or a few days of a skin antibiotic, used to close the case.
**Hook.** Probe-to-bone is bone infection. The film lags.

### Stevens-Johnson syndrome

The epidermis is dying and separating. Mucosa is involved. This is not a simple morbilliform drug rash.

**Vignette.** Two weeks after a new prescription for allopurinol, a man has fever, painful red skin, erosions in the mouth, and eyes that he cannot open. The plan is to continue the drug at a lower dose and add a prophylactic antibiotic.
**Reasoning.** Mucosa plus detachment plus a culprit drug is this spectrum. The drug stops. Lowering the dose is a rechallenge. Antibiotics without an infection add another drug injury. The eyes need a specialist the same day. The diagnosis is Stevens-Johnson syndrome.
**NBME trap.** Continuing the culprit, or treating the rash with a new sulfa antibiotic.
**Hook.** Mucosa, detachment, a new drug: stop the drug and move the patient.

### Prosthetic joint infection

Biofilm on the implant. A native-joint tap-and-pill pathway does not clear it. Acute hematogenous infection and a chronic sinus are not the same operation.

**Vignette.** A woman with a knee replacement has had a draining sinus for a month. She is walking and afebrile today. The clinic plan is ten days of an oral antibiotic and a wound check. Orthopedics has not been called.
**Reasoning.** A sinus to a prosthesis is infection. Oral antibiotics without a culture and without a surgical plan suppress the culture and leave the biofilm. She is stable, so the antibiotics can wait until the cultures that will actually guide care are taken. The diagnosis is Prosthetic joint infection.
**NBME trap.** A short oral antibiotic course for a draining prosthesis.
**Hook.** A sinus to a replacement is an infected replacement.

## Checklist

- [ ] Tap a hot native joint before you commit to “gout.”
- [ ] Compartment and cauda equina and necrotizing infection do not wait on a comfortable test.
- [ ] Disease-modifying therapy for rheumatoid arthritis, plus infection screens before biologics.
- [ ] Do not shave a lesion you think is melanoma.
- [ ] Mark cellulitis. Pain out of proportion is a different disease.

## Common traps

- Antibiotics before the tap in a stable septic-joint workup, then a useless culture.
- Allopurinol started during an untreated acute attack with no prophylaxis, causing a worse attack.
- Steroids for a hot joint you have not tapped.
- A feed card that says “NSAID for every gout flare.” The kidney and the heart may forbid it.

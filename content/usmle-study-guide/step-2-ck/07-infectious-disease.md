# Infectious disease

> **Study aid only.** Site, severity, and the likely organism. The regimen is the current IDSA or CDC page, not a mnemonic from a previous year.

## What this chapter covers

How to choose an empiric spectrum without pretending one drug list fits every hospital, plus the syndromes that are emergencies. Blood and immune content is about 5–10% of Step 2 CK. Multisystem infection sits in the 4–8% multisystem range as well.

## The framework

Three questions before any antibiotic name. Where is the infection? How sick is the person? What did they already receive, and what is the local resistance? Then start the narrowest regimen that still covers the killers, take cultures first when a few minutes will not harm, and shrink the regimen when the organism is known.

| Syndrome | Do not miss |
| --- | --- |
| Sepsis and shock | Source, cultures, prompt antibiotics, fluid for hypoperfusion, vasopressor if fluid is not enough. The Surviving Sepsis Campaign discusses a 30 mL/kg starting volume that must be individualized. Lactate is a perfusion marker, not a diagnosis. |
| Meningitis, adult | Do not delay antibiotics for the lumbar puncture if the puncture will be delayed by imaging. Cover the usual bacteria and add the age-and-risk organisms. Dexamethasone is given with or just before the first antibiotic in many adult bacterial cases. Confirm the current IDSA timing. |
| Community pneumonia | Severity decides ward, ICU, or home. See the pulmonary chapter. |
| Cellulitis versus abscess | Abscess is a drainage procedure. Antibiotics do not replace the knife. |
| Sexually transmitted infection | Treat the syndrome and the partners as the CDC guidelines say. Test for HIV and syphilis when you find one of these. |
| HIV, new | Start antiretroviral therapy. The current regimen is a DHHS choice and it changes. Opportunistic prophylaxis is a CD4-threshold table you should open, not memorize from an old card. |
| Tuberculosis | Respiratory isolation when you suspect it. Never treat active disease with one drug. |
| Tick-borne disease | Doxycycline is the drug for suspected Rocky Mountain spotted fever in all ages. Do not withhold it from a child because of an outdated tooth myth. CDC is explicit. |
| C. difficile | Stop the inciting antibiotic when you can. Severity decides oral vancomycin versus a milder regimen. Confirm the current IDSA/SHEA table. Do not reach for metronidazole as a reflex from an old edition. |

Prevention is part of infectious disease. Vaccines in adults, including the ones around pregnancy and the ones after splenectomy, are CDC schedules. Post-exposure HIV prophylaxis is a time-sensitive CDC/DHHS protocol. This chapter will not invent the hours. Open the page.

Fever in a returning traveler and fever in a neutropenic patient are both “do not send home with reassurance.” Neutropenic fever gets an examination for a source and prompt antipseudomonal coverage. The exact drug is your hospital’s protocol, built on IDSA.

### Sepsis

Pipes are open and tissues are underfed.

**Vignette.** Pneumonia, warm skin, pressure 100/40, lactate 4, confusion.
**Reasoning.** Distributive shock. Antibiotics and a careful fluid plan. The trap is waiting for a blood pressure of 70, or pouring fluid into a wet heart-failure patient. The diagnosis is Sepsis.
**NBME trap.** A carbapenem for a simple cellulitis because the word sepsis was used loosely.
**Hook.** Source, drug, fluid, reassess.

### Bacterial meningitis

Bacteria in the CSF. Minutes matter.

**Vignette.** Fever and a stiff neck. The CT scanner is busy. He is immunocompetent and has no focal sign.
**Reasoning.** You can puncture without a CT in this classic low-risk exam, and you still do not delay antibiotics if imaging is going to happen. The trap is a perfect CSF an hour late. The diagnosis is Bacterial meningitis.
**NBME trap.** A CT before antibiotics in a patient who will wait an hour.
**Hook.** Drug before a delayed tap.

### Community cellulitis

Dermis infection, usually streptococcal when it is nonpurulent.

**Vignette.** A red shin. He is walking. No abscess. The border is easy to draw.
**Reasoning.** Oral therapy and a mark. The trap is two intravenous antibiotics. The diagnosis is Community cellulitis.
**NBME trap.** A carbapenem for a marked shin.
**Hook.** Mark it. Drain pus. Do not broaden for sport.

### Necrotizing infection

The fascia is dying faster than the skin shows.

**Vignette.** Mild redness, terrible pain, hypotension, and a blister.
**Reasoning.** Necrotizing infection. The trap is oral antibiotics and a clinic tomorrow, or a CT that delays the knife. The diagnosis is Necrotizing infection.
**NBME trap.** A normal white count used as reassurance.
**Hook.** Pain out of proportion is the operating room.

### C. difficile

A toxin injures the colon after the flora was wiped out.

**Vignette.** Ten days of a cephalosporin. Now watery stools. He is walking. White count is 12.
**Reasoning.** Nonfulminant C. difficile. Stop the cephalosporin. The trap is metronidazole because that is what an older book said. The diagnosis is C. difficile.
**NBME trap.** Antidiarrheals as the main treatment.
**Hook.** Stop the culprit drug. Do not start with metronidazole.

### HIV, new diagnosis

A virus that removes cell-mediated defense.

**Vignette.** The confirmatory test is positive. He has no symptoms and wants to wait until he feels sick.
**Reasoning.** Start therapy. The trap is waiting for an AIDS illness. Confirm the current first-line regimen rather than inventing one. The diagnosis is HIV, new diagnosis.
**NBME trap.** A CD4 cutoff from memory used to withhold prophylaxis or to delay therapy.
**Hook.** Start treatment. Open the current regimen.

### Tuberculosis, active

The organism is slow and develops resistance if you use one agent.

**Vignette.** A cavity and a month of cough. Someone wants to start isoniazid alone tonight.
**Reasoning.** Active disease. Isolate and use combination therapy. The trap is the latent regimen. The diagnosis is Tuberculosis, active.
**NBME trap.** One drug for a cavity.
**Hook.** Cavity means combination therapy and a mask.

### Rocky Mountain spotted fever

A vasculitic rickettsia. The lab lags the illness.

**Vignette.** A child in the summer has fever and a petechial rash on the wrists. A tick was removed last week.
**Reasoning.** Treat now. The trap is waiting for a titer, or avoiding doxycycline because he is eight. The diagnosis is Rocky Mountain spotted fever.
**NBME trap.** A macrolide because he is a child.
**Hook.** Doxycycline at any age when this is the disease.

### Sexually transmitted infection, the syndrome

A mucosal or ulcer infection. One organism often travels with another.

**Vignette.** Purulent discharge. He may not return for a test result.
**Reasoning.** Treat the syndrome and test anyway. The trap is a culture and a callback he will miss, or a drug from a 2010 card. The diagnosis is Sexually transmitted infection, the syndrome.
**NBME trap.** One drug and no HIV test.
**Hook.** Treat the syndrome. Test for the company it keeps.

### Neutropenic fever

No neutrophils means gram-negatives, including Pseudomonas, can kill quickly.

**Vignette.** Day 10 after chemotherapy. Temperature 38.6. He feels fine. The differential is not back.
**Reasoning.** You treat. The trap is waiting for the absolute neutrophil count while he sits in the waiting room. The diagnosis is Neutropenic fever.
**NBME trap.** Oral amoxicillin while you wait for the count.
**Hook.** Fever plus recent chemotherapy: do not wait for the reprint.

### Pyelonephritis

The kidney is infected, not just the bladder.

**Vignette.** Pregnant, fever, and flank pain. She is vomiting.
**Reasoning.** Admission. The trap is an oral prescription and a prenatal visit next week. The diagnosis is Pyelonephritis.
**NBME trap.** Nitrofurantoin for a febrile flank. It does not treat the kidney.
**Hook.** Pregnant or vomiting: she stays.

### Infective endocarditis, the first hour

A vegetation.

**Vignette.** He injects drugs. Fever and three round lung lesions. A murmur gets louder on inspiration.
**Reasoning.** Right-sided endocarditis. The trap is a left-sided stroke workup as the only plan, or one culture after antibiotics. The diagnosis is Infective endocarditis, the first hour.
**NBME trap.** Antibiotics before any cultures in a stable patient, then a negative culture used to stop.
**Hook.** Three cultures if you have minutes. Then the drug.

### Influenza and the complication

A virus, then sometimes a bacterium in the damaged airway.

**Vignette.** Day 6. He was better and now has a new fever and a cavity on the film.
**Reasoning.** Post-influenza bacterial pneumonia. The trap is oseltamivir alone for a cavity. The diagnosis is Influenza and the complication.
**NBME trap.** A macrolide for every winter ache.
**Hook.** Better, then worse: think staph, not more oseltamivir alone.

### Diabetic foot infection

Neuropathy hid the wound. Poor flow keeps it from healing.

**Vignette.** A painless ulcer. The probe hits bone. Pulses are weak.
**Reasoning.** Osteomyelitis and ischemia. The trap is two weeks of an oral antibiotic and no vascular look. The diagnosis is Diabetic foot infection.
**NBME trap.** A surface swab as the culture that chooses the drug.
**Hook.** Probe to bone and weak pulses: not a skin cream.

### Traveler's fever

The exposure names the organism. Falciparum does not wait.

**Vignette.** He returned from West Africa five days ago. Fever and headache. He took prophylaxis most days.
**Reasoning.** Smears now. The trap is a viral syndrome label because he took most of his pills. The diagnosis is Traveler's fever.
**NBME trap.** One negative smear used to clear falciparum.
**Hook.** Fever after a malaria zone: smears now.

### Opportunistic infection, the idea

Cell-mediated defense is gone. The organism was waiting.

**Vignette.** CD4 is very low. Dry cough. LDH is high. The film is ground glass.
**Reasoning.** Pneumocystis. The trap is community pneumonia drugs that miss it, or a CD4 cutoff you invented. The diagnosis is Opportunistic infection, the idea.
**NBME trap.** A five-year-old prophylaxis table.
**Hook.** Match the syndrome. Open the current CD4 page.

## Another pass on the stems

The atlas named the emergencies. These are the office and the pregnancy forks it did not finish.

### Asymptomatic bacteriuria is a pregnancy problem

A positive culture and no symptoms in a man, or in a nonpregnant woman, is not an antibiotic. In pregnancy it is treatment, because it becomes pyelonephritis. Confirm the current obstetric regimen. Do not use a drug that is wrong for the trimester because it was the cystitis drug you memorized for everyone else. Nitrofurantoin does not treat pyelonephritis. It can treat a bladder infection, including bacteriuria, when the trimester allows it. Confirm.

**Vignette.** She is 16 weeks. A screening culture grows more than 100,000 colonies of E. coli. She has no dysuria and no fever.
**Reasoning.** Treat. The diagnosis is asymptomatic bacteriuria of pregnancy. The trap is the adult rule, “no symptoms, no drug,” applied to a pregnant patient, or nitrofurantoin continued after she develops a fever and flank pain.
**NBME trap.** They treat the same culture in a 70-year-old woman who feels well and has no pregnancy. That culture is not an indication.
**Hook.** Pregnancy plus a positive culture: treat. Everyone else without symptoms: do not.

### Syphilis in pregnancy is penicillin

Primary is a painless ulcer. Secondary is a rash that includes the palms and soles. Late disease is years later. Neurosyphilis is a cerebrospinal-fluid problem and needs intravenous penicillin, not a single intramuscular dose. In pregnancy the drug is penicillin. If she reports a penicillin allergy, the path is desensitization, not a switch to doxycycline. Doxycycline is not the pregnancy regimen. Confirm the current CDC stage table rather than inventing the number of doses.

**Vignette.** She is pregnant. The treponemal test is positive. She says penicillin “makes her throat close.”
**Reasoning.** Desensitize and give penicillin. The diagnosis is syphilis in pregnancy. The trap is doxycycline because it treats nonpregnant adults with a penicillin allergy.
**NBME trap.** One intramuscular dose for a person with a positive cerebrospinal fluid and ocular symptoms. That is neurosyphilis. Intravenous therapy.
**Hook.** Pregnancy: penicillin, even if you have to desensitize. Eyes or a positive spinal fluid: intravenous, not one shot.

### Lyme disease is the rash, then the drug the host can take

Erythema migrans after a tick in an endemic area is Lyme disease. Do not wait for a titer to treat the classic rash. Doxycycline is the usual adult drug. Pregnancy and early childhood are the amoxicillin conversation. Confirm the current IDSA choice. A facial palsy after a tick is still Lyme until proven otherwise, and doxycycline treats the infection. Steroids are not the substitute. A later swollen knee can be Lyme arthritis. Early disseminated disease with a heart block is admission, not a clinic refill.

**Vignette.** A circular expanding rash with central clearing, a week after a hike. She is pregnant. No titer is back.
**Reasoning.** Treat now with the pregnancy-appropriate drug. The diagnosis is erythema migrans. The trap is waiting for the serology, or doxycycline because that is the nonpregnant default.
**NBME trap.** They give a steroid for a tick-associated facial palsy and no antibiotic. The palsy is the infection.
**Hook.** Classic rash: treat. Do not wait for the lab. Pregnancy changes the drug, not the decision to treat.

### Acute HIV can look like mono, and the antibody test can be negative

Fever, rash, sore throat, and a recent exposure. The antigen-antibody test can be negative in the first days. The RNA test is the one that catches it. Do not diagnose streptococcal pharyngitis and stop. Do not start chronic antiretroviral therapy from one symptom and no test. If the RNA is positive, start therapy and look at the current DHHS regimen. This chapter still does not print a CD4 cutoff.

**Vignette.** Two weeks after a new sexual partner. Fever, a maculopapular rash, and a sore throat. The antibody test is negative. A rapid strep test is negative.
**Reasoning.** Order the viral load. The diagnosis is possible acute HIV. The trap is amoxicillin for pharyngitis, which also risks a rash if this is Epstein-Barr virus, and a closed case because the antibody was negative.
**NBME trap.** Amoxicillin for a mono-like illness. If it is EBV, the drug causes a rash that gets called a penicillin allergy. If it is acute HIV, the antibody was the wrong test.
**Hook.** Mono-like illness and a new exposure: RNA, not only an antibody. Do not give amoxicillin for a sore throat you have not explained.

### A bite is a specific organism and a specific wound

A cat or dog bite is Pasteurella until proven otherwise. Amoxicillin-clavulanate is the usual oral drug when the person can go home. Confirm. Do not use a drug that misses Pasteurella because it was the cellulitis drug. A bite on the hand, a closed-fist injury, or a wound that already looks infected stays for a surgical look. Rabies prophylaxis is a protocol with a clock and a wound wash. Open the CDC page for the animal and the region. Do not invent the vaccine schedule.

**Vignette.** A cat bite to the hand this morning. The hand is swelling. He is otherwise well. Someone prescribes cephalexin.
**Reasoning.** Change the drug to one that covers Pasteurella, and have a low threshold to explore a hand. The diagnosis is a cat bite. The trap is the cellulitis cephalosporin, which misses the organism the cat carries.
**NBME trap.** They suture a grossly contaminated bite closed as if it were a clean laceration, and they forget to wash it.
**Hook.** Cat or dog: a drug that covers Pasteurella. Hand: someone who operates should see it. Wash first.

### Epstein-Barr virus, the spleen, and the drug rash

Fever, pharyngitis, posterior cervical nodes, and fatigue. The heterophile antibody supports it after the first days. No contact sport until the spleen is no longer at risk. Confirm the interval rather than inventing a week. Amoxicillin causes a rash and is not the treatment. A strep test can be positive and still not be the whole story. Airway obstruction or severe thrombocytopenia is the admission. Most adolescents go home.

**Vignette.** A teenager with a week of sore throat and fatigue. Posterior nodes. You start amoxicillin. The next day he has a diffuse rash.
**Reasoning.** Stop the amoxicillin. This is the EBV drug rash, not a lifelong penicillin allergy, until you have thought it through. The diagnosis is infectious mononucleosis. The trap is labeling him penicillin-allergic and clearing him for Saturday’s rugby match.
**NBME trap.** They clear contact sports because he feels a little better. The spleen is the reason he sits out.
**Hook.** Posterior nodes and fatigue: do not give amoxicillin. Do not clear contact sports.

## Common traps

- A broad carbapenem for a simple cellulitis because it feels thorough. Spectrum is a side effect.
- Waiting for the CT to treat meningitis.
- Metronidazole as the default for C. difficile because that is what an older book said.
- A feed card with a CD4 cutoff from five years ago. Open the current opportunistic-infection guideline.

## Practice this in the Qbank

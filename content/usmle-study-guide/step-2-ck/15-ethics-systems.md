# Ethics and systems

## Framework

Step 2 ethics is what you say and who decides. It is not a statute quiz.

| Situation | The move |
| --- | --- |
| Capable adult refuses a treatment | The refusal stands. You explain, you do not coerce, you do not ask the family to overrule. |
| Patient lacks capacity | Surrogate, using the patient's known wishes, then values, then best interest. |
| Family asks you to hide a diagnosis | You ask the patient what they want to know. You do not lie. |
| Error that reached the patient | Tell the patient. Do not wait for a perfect root-cause meeting. |
| Colleague is impaired at work | Patients come first. Report through the channel your hospital uses. Do not "cover the shift quietly" as the whole answer. |
| A child, parents refuse a life-saving transfusion | Emergency care of the child is not the same as an adult refusal. Involve ethics and the legal path your hospital uses. Do not let a child die for an adult's refusal of a simple life-saving therapy. |
| Confidentiality and a serious threat to an identifiable person | Duty to protect can override confidentiality. Know that the principle exists. The local law is local. |

Interpreter: use a professional interpreter for a serious decision. A child does not interpret a cancer conversation. A bilingual nurse in a hurry is not a plan.

Goals of care belong in the room before a code, not after. A do-not-resuscitate order is about resuscitation. It is not an order to stop treating pain.

## Checklist

- [ ] Capacity is about this decision.
- [ ] Do not lie because a family asked.
- [ ] Disclose errors that reached the patient.
- [ ] A child does not interpret.
- [ ] Comfort care is not "do nothing for pain."

## Common traps

- Calling risk management before you tell the patient about a clear error, and never telling them.
- Treating a surrogate's preference as stronger than a known advance directive.
- Using a family member as the interpreter for consent.

# A Step 2 CK study plan

> **Study aid only.** A way to aim hours. Not a forecast.

## What this chapter covers

How to pair this book with the question bank and the feed so you learn decisions, not paragraphs. Medicine is the majority clinical science. Pediatrics, obstetrics and gynecology, and psychiatry are large enough to schedule on purpose. Surgery is smaller and still shows up as “next step,” often the decision to operate or not.

## The framework

For each system, one sitting has four products:

1. The unstable-patient move.
2. The diagnosis you should be able to say from the stem alone.
3. The one test that changes treatment, and the test that does not.
4. The guideline name and the decision it actually changes.

If you cannot name the guideline, you are not done with a management topic. “I think we start a medicine” is not a decision.

| Week shape | Use |
| --- | --- |
| Two systems | Pick from the heavier ranges first: cardiovascular, renal and reproductive, musculoskeletal and skin, then rotate pediatrics and obstetrics through the month. |
| Same day | Bank items on that system. Sort misses into “wrong diagnosis,” “right diagnosis, wrong next step,” and “did not know the guideline.” |
| Cards | Only for the third pile, and only after the chapter table. |
| Mixed block | At least one a week so the chapter title is not a hint. |
| Prevention | A recurring slot. USPSTF and vaccines are a system of their own in practice even though they are spread across the outline. |

Social-science items are not a separate weekend if you fold one ethics or safety stem into every mixed block.

## Checklist

- [ ] Every management chapter you finish has a guideline named in your own notes.
- [ ] Pediatrics and obstetrics are on the calendar, not leftover.
- [ ] Misses are sorted by the kind of error.
- [ ] You are not opening a second textbook to soothe a reasoning miss.

## Common traps

- Reading a guideline PDF cover to cover. Take the decision the exam can test, and leave the rest for residency.
- Doing only medicine items because they feel like “the real exam.” The clinical-science table says otherwise.
- Saving prevention for a pamphlet the night before. It is an explicit physician task.

## Practice this in the Qbank

The bank is the main teacher on this Step. This book is the map. The feed is the reminder. If a card states a dose or an age cutoff, check it against the chapter’s named source before you trust it. Cards are not guidelines.

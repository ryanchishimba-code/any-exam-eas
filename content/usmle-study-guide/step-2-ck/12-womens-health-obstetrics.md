# Women's health and obstetrics

> **Study aid only.** ACOG is the source for pregnancy care. USPSTF is the source for the screening ages below. They disagree sometimes with specialty societies. The exam usually wants the preventive-services answer unless the stem is clearly a specialist problem. Re-check ages. They move.

## What this chapter covers

Prenatal danger signs, the hypertensive disorders of pregnancy, bleeding in pregnancy, and the screening and contraception decisions of outpatient gynecology. Reproductive systems are grouped with renal at about 7–13%. Pregnancy itself is about 3–7%. Obstetrics and gynecology as a clinical science is about 10–20%. This chapter is sized for that, not for the small-looking pregnancy row alone.

## The framework

Pregnancy care is a calendar plus a list of things that are never normal. Bleeding with pain in early pregnancy is ectopic until an intrauterine pregnancy is seen. Painless third-trimester bleeding is placenta previa until the ultrasound says the placenta is clear of the cervix, and you do not do a digital exam. Painful third-trimester bleeding is abruption until proven otherwise, and the mother can be in shock before the vaginal blood looks impressive.

Prenatal labs that change management: blood type and antibody screen, hemoglobin, HIV, syphilis, hepatitis B, rubella immunity, and the later GBS and diabetes screens. A positive syphilis or HIV test in pregnancy is treatment, not a recheck next month. Congenital syphilis and perinatal HIV are preventable and still happen when someone delays.

Ectopic precautions are a script: come back for dizziness, shoulder pain, worsening pain, or heavy bleeding. A methotrexate candidate must be able to follow up. A ruptured ectopic is surgery.

### Preeclampsia with severe features

A placental disease with maternal endothelial injury. The adult stage-1 blood-pressure pathway is a different disease and a different document.

**Vignette.** On postpartum day 4 a woman has a severe headache and a blood pressure of 182/112. Platelets are falling and the liver enzymes are up. She is sent home on the adult lifestyle advice used for a PREVENT risk under 7.5%, with a primary-care visit in a month. No magnesium is given.
**Reasoning.** Headache, severe-range pressure, and lab injury are severe features, and they still count after delivery. She needs magnesium, a pregnancy-safe antihypertensive, and monitoring. The adult PREVENT threshold does not belong in this note. The diagnosis is Preeclampsia with severe features.
**NBME trap.** Adult PREVENT rules, an ACE inhibitor, or magnesium used as the only blood-pressure drug.
**Hook.** Severe features: magnesium and a pregnancy-safe pressure drug. Not PREVENT.

### Eclampsia

The seizure of preeclampsia. It can be the first sign, and it can happen after the birth. Imaging is for a patient who does not wake or who has a focal deficit that does not fit, after the magnesium is in.

**Vignette.** A woman at 36 weeks has a generalized seizure. Her blood pressure is 170/110. The fetal heart is being monitored. A phenytoin load is ordered, and the CT scanner is called before any magnesium.
**Reasoning.** A seizure plus severe-range pressure in late pregnancy is eclampsia. Magnesium and the airway come first. Phenytoin is not the preferred drug. The CT can wait until she is treated and only if the picture stays wrong. The diagnosis is Eclampsia.
**NBME trap.** Phenytoin as the plan, or a CT that delays magnesium.
**Hook.** The seizure gets magnesium first. Then deliver if she is still pregnant.

### Suspected ectopic pregnancy

A pregnancy implanted outside the uterus, usually in the tube. A single hCG does not locate it. The empty uterus plus a level that should show a sac is the warning.

**Vignette.** A woman with a positive pregnancy test has pelvic pain and a blood pressure of 84/50. The ultrasound shows an empty uterus and free fluid. Shoulder pain has started. Methotrexate is proposed so she can avoid an operation, and serial hCG values are planned for tomorrow.
**Reasoning.** Hypotension, shoulder pain, free fluid, and an empty uterus are a ruptured ectopic. She needs the operating room and blood, not methotrexate and a next-day lab. Methotrexate is for the stable patient who can follow up. The diagnosis is Suspected ectopic pregnancy.
**NBME trap.** Methotrexate for an unstable patient, or reassurance without an ultrasound.
**Hook.** Unstable and empty uterus: the operating room. Methotrexate is for the stable patient who will come back.

### Placenta previa

The placenta covers the internal os. A finger creates a hemorrhage you cannot see coming. Pain is not the feature. Pain means think about abruption, including a previa that has also separated.

**Vignette.** At 34 weeks a woman has a gush of bright blood and no pain. The clinic note from 20 weeks mentioned a low placenta that was never repeated. A digital exam is proposed to check dilation before the ultrasound.
**Reasoning.** Painless third-trimester bleeding is previa until the ultrasound says the placenta is clear of the cervix. The finger is the harm. Resuscitate if the bleeding is heavy, update the ultrasound, and do not induce through an unexamined os. The diagnosis is Placenta previa.
**NBME trap.** A digital cervical exam to see how dilated she is.
**Hook.** Painless bleeding: ultrasound. No fingers in the cervix.

### Placental abruption

The placenta has separated. Blood can stay inside the uterus. The fetus loses surface area. The mother loses volume and can lose clotting factors.

**Vignette.** At 37 weeks a woman has sudden abdominal pain and a rigid uterus. Vaginal bleeding is only a small trickle. Her blood pressure is 80/50 and the fetal heart rate is bradycardic. The team waits for an ultrasound to prove the separation before starting blood.
**Reasoning.** Pain, a rigid uterus, shock, and a bad fetal heart rate are abruption. The small amount of vaginal blood is the concealed-hemorrhage trap. Resuscitation and delivery do not wait on a scan that often misses this disease. The diagnosis is Placental abruption.
**NBME trap.** A negative ultrasound used to send her home, or delayed transfusion because the vaginal pad looks only moderately soaked.
**Hook.** Pain and shock out of proportion to the blood. Do not wait on the scan.

### Postpartum hemorrhage from uterine atony

The myometrium is not contracting, so the spiral arteries keep bleeding. A full bladder holds the uterus up and open. Retained placenta and a cervical tear bleed even when the fundus is firm.

**Vignette.** Minutes after delivery the uterus is boggy and the pad is soaking. She has preeclampsia and asthma. Methylergonovine is drawn up, and no one has emptied the bladder or looked at the cervix.
**Reasoning.** Atony is the most common cause and massage plus oxytocin plus an empty bladder come first. Methylergonovine is the wrong second drug in preeclampsia, and carboprost would be the wrong drug in asthma. The cervix still has to be looked at so a laceration is not missed. The diagnosis is Postpartum hemorrhage from uterine atony.
**NBME trap.** Methylergonovine in a hypertensive patient, or carboprost in asthma.
**Hook.** Boggy uterus: massage and oxytocin. Skip methergine if the pressure is high.

### Intrapartum group B Streptococcus prophylaxis

Colonization becomes early neonatal sepsis when the birth canal is the exposure. Antepartum oral therapy does not clear that exposure reliably. The drug has to be in the mother during labor.

**Vignette.** A woman at 37 weeks had a positive group B culture and took a week of oral amoxicillin. She is now in labor with ruptured membranes. No intravenous penicillin is ordered because she was already treated. She has no penicillin allergy.
**Reasoning.** The oral course does not replace intrapartum prophylaxis. She needs intravenous penicillin while she is in labor. The exception you did not use is a cesarean before labor with intact membranes. The diagnosis is Intrapartum group B Streptococcus prophylaxis.
**NBME trap.** Oral antibiotics at 36 weeks instead of intravenous penicillin in labor, or clindamycin without a susceptibility result.
**Hook.** The screen is treated in labor, with penicillin, not with a pill the week before.

### Rh-negative mother, unsensitized

Fetal Rh-positive cells can immunize an Rh-negative mother. Anti-D coats those cells before she makes her own antibody. Once she has made it, the fetus is at risk for hemolytic disease.

**Vignette.** An Rh-negative woman at 28 weeks has a negative antibody screen. The clinic skips anti-D because she had a first-trimester bleed and someone assumes it is too early in pregnancy for the routine dose to matter. No dose was given at the bleed either.
**Reasoning.** A negative screen means she is still unsensitized and should receive anti-D at the routine time, and she should have received it at the bleed. Early bleeding is not too early for a mixing event. A positive screen would have been the opposite order: no false reassurance from anti-D. The diagnosis is Rh-negative mother, unsensitized.
**NBME trap.** Skipping anti-D after early bleeding, or giving anti-D to a sensitized mother and telling her the risk is gone.
**Hook.** Negative screen: give anti-D. Positive screen: she is already sensitized.

### Gestational diabetes

Placental hormones create insulin resistance. The two screening methods use different loads and different thresholds. Mixing them mislabels the patient.

**Vignette.** At 26 weeks a woman has a 50 g one-hour glucose above the screen cutoff. Insulin is started that afternoon. No 100 g test is ordered. She is also offered her sister's nonpregnant SGLT2 inhibitor.
**Reasoning.** In a two-step protocol the 50 g result is a screen. The diagnostic test comes next. Starting insulin on the screen, and starting a nonpregnant drug class, are both the wrong next step. If the clinic uses the one-step 75 g test, say so and use only those thresholds. The diagnosis is Gestational diabetes.
**NBME trap.** Mixing one-step and two-step numbers, or an SGLT2 inhibitor because that is the adult algorithm.
**Hook.** Do not mix the two glucose protocols. Insulin is the preferred drug if diet fails.

### Combined hormonal contraception, a contraindication

Estrogen raises clot risk and can worsen migraine with aura. Effectiveness and safety are different columns. The most effective method she can safely use is the goal.

**Vignette.** A 22-year-old with migraine with aura asks for a combined oral contraceptive. She does not smoke. The patch is offered instead because it is not a pill. No MEC chart is opened.
**Reasoning.** Migraine with aura is a category 4 for every combined hormonal method. The patch does not remove the estrogen. She needs a non-estrogen method. Age and smoking are a different category 4 row and are not required once aura is present. The diagnosis is Combined hormonal contraception, a contraindication.
**NBME trap.** A patch or a ring offered as the safe version of a forbidden pill.
**Hook.** Aura, heavy smoking at 35, early postpartum, clot: no estrogen. Open the MEC.

### Breast cancer screening, average risk

Screening finds an asymptomatic cancer. A symptom converts the visit to a diagnostic workup. High-risk mutations are a different, earlier pathway and are not this average-risk recommendation.

**Vignette.** A 42-year-old average-risk woman with a normal exam asks when mammograms start. She is told to wait until 50 and then to come every year. She has no lump.
**Reasoning.** The 2024 USPSTF recommendation is biennial mammography from 40 through 74. Waiting until 50 uses the retired recommendation. Annual is not the USPSTF interval. She has no mass, so this is screening, not a diagnostic study. The diagnosis is Breast cancer screening, average risk.
**NBME trap.** Start at 50, or annual mammography as the USPSTF answer, or a screening appointment for a breast lump.
**Hook.** USPSTF 2024: every two years, ages 40 to 74.

### Cervical cancer screening

Most cervical infection in adolescents clears. Screening them finds transient HPV and leads to procedures she does not need. The start age is 21.

**Vignette.** An 18-year-old who has been sexually active for two years requests a Pap smear before she leaves for college. She has no symptoms and a normal exam. HPV co-testing is also requested because her friend had it.
**Reasoning.** USPSTF screening starts at 21 regardless of sexual activity. She does not need a Pap today, and she does not need an HPV test. At 21 the test is cytology every 3 years, still without routine HPV testing, until 30. The diagnosis is Cervical cancer screening.
**NBME trap.** A Pap smear before age 21 because she is sexually active, or HPV co-testing at 22.
**Hook.** Cervical screening starts at 21. Sexual activity does not move it.

### Intimate-partner violence

Coercive control and physical or sexual violence by a partner. The visit is often about something else. Disclosure depends on privacy.

**Vignette.** A woman has a bruised arm and says she fell. Her partner stays in the room and corrects her story. The discharge packet, including a domestic-violence brochure, is handed to the couple together. No private interview occurs.
**Reasoning.** The brochure in front of him is the hazard. She needs a private question and a resource she can use without being watched. A couples session is not the intervention. A report, if any, follows the actual law, not a guess. The diagnosis is Intimate-partner violence.
**NBME trap.** A pamphlet handed over in front of the partner, or couples therapy as the safety plan.
**Hook.** Ask her alone. The resource stays in her control.

### Syphilis in pregnancy

Treponema crosses the placenta. Treatment of the mother is treatment of the fetus, and only penicillin does that job reliably.

**Vignette.** At the first prenatal visit the RPR is reactive and the confirmatory treponemal test is positive. She has no symptoms. She had a rash after penicillin as a child. Doxycycline is prescribed, and a recheck is planned in a month before any penicillin.
**Reasoning.** A confirmed test in pregnancy is treatment today. Doxycycline is the nonpregnant alternative and it is contraindicated here. Allergy means desensitization to penicillin, not a different class and not a month of delay. The diagnosis is Syphilis in pregnancy.
**NBME trap.** Doxycycline in pregnancy, or a month's delay to repeat the titer.
**Hook.** Pregnant and positive: penicillin. Desensitize if you must.

### HIV in pregnancy

Perinatal transmission falls when the maternal viral load is suppressed. Delay and monotherapy are how infants are infected.

**Vignette.** A woman at 12 weeks has a confirmed positive HIV test. She is asymptomatic. The plan is to repeat the test next month and then discuss a single older drug if it is still positive. No combination regimen is started.
**Reasoning.** A confirmed diagnosis is treatment now, with a full regimen, not a repeat visit and not monotherapy. The load will matter again near delivery. It does not matter for the decision to start. The diagnosis is HIV in pregnancy.
**NBME trap.** Delaying therapy until a later trimester, or a single-drug regimen from an old protocol.
**Hook.** Start a full regimen now. The viral load later guides delivery.

### Preterm prelabor rupture of membranes

The membranes are open and the clock to labor and to infection has started. Each digital exam pushes bacteria upward. The gestational age decides whether waiting helps the fetus more than it risks infection.

**Vignette.** At 30 weeks a woman has a confirmed gush and a sterile-speculum exam that shows pooling. She is afebrile and the fetus looks reassuring. Three digital exams are done to score the cervix. No antibiotics, no corticosteroids, and no neuroprotection discussion are started. She is told this is managed like adult hypertension.
**Reasoning.** Expectant management at this preterm age includes latency antibiotics and corticosteroids, and a discussion of magnesium for fetal neuroprotection inside the ACOG window. Digital exams are the infection risk. Adult blood-pressure rules are the wrong document. The diagnosis is Preterm prelabor rupture of membranes.
**NBME trap.** Serial digital exams, or tocolysis continued after she develops an intra-amniotic infection.
**Hook.** Speculum, not fingers. Latency antibiotics and steroids if you are waiting.

### Postpartum endometritis

Ascending polymicrobial infection of the decidua, more common after cesarean. Atelectasis is an earlier, first-day fever. Mastitis is a focal breast. Pyelonephritis has flank pain.

**Vignette.** On day 2 after a cesarean a woman has a fever of 39 C and a tender uterus. The incision is clean and she has no flank pain. Trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole is prescribed for a presumed urinary infection, and heparin is started for a presumed pelvic clot before any antibiotic aimed at the uterus.
**Reasoning.** Fever and uterine tenderness after a cesarean is endometritis. She needs an intravenous regimen that covers anaerobes, such as clindamycin plus gentamicin. Heparin is not the opening move. A urinary antibiotic misses the anaerobes. The diagnosis is Postpartum endometritis.
**NBME trap.** Oral therapy aimed only at the urine, or anticoagulation before the uterine infection is treated.
**Hook.** Tender uterus after a cesarean: intravenous anaerobe coverage, not heparin first.

### Emergency contraception

Emergency contraception prevents or delays ovulation or, for the copper device, interferes with fertilization and implantation. It is not an abortifacient of an established pregnancy.

**Vignette.** A woman had unprotected sex 12 hours ago. Her urine pregnancy test is negative. She asks to prevent a pregnancy. A mifepristone and misoprostol regimen is ordered, and she is told emergency contraception is the same thing as a medication abortion.
**Reasoning.** A negative test and an exposure 12 hours ago is emergency contraception, not a medication abortion. Mifepristone plus misoprostol is the wrong pair. Offer a levonorgestrel method, ulipristal, or a copper intrauterine device, and confirm the hours on the CDC page. The diagnosis is Emergency contraception.
**NBME trap.** Mifepristone and misoprostol given as emergency contraception, or refusal of emergency contraception because it was confused with abortion.
**Hook.** Last night's exposure is emergency contraception. It is not a medication abortion.

## Common traps

- Treating blood pressure of 148/96 in pregnancy with the adult 2025 PREVENT rules. Different disease.
- Methylergonovine in a hypertensive patient with atony. It raises pressure. Use a different uterotonic.
- A Pap smear before age 21 because the patient is sexually active. USPSTF starts at 21.
- Emergency contraception confused with a medication abortion. Different drugs, different purpose, different follow-up.

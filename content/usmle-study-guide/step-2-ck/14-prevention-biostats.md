# Prevention and biostatistics

## Framework

Health maintenance is a large Step 2 task range. Learn the posted USPSTF grade and the interval. Biostatistics is the same square as Step 1, now attached to a paper.

| Screen | Posted recommendation to use on items |
| --- | --- |
| Colorectal cancer | Start at 45, through 75, average risk. A positive stool test means colonoscopy. |
| Breast cancer | Mammogram every 2 years, ages 40–74. |
| Lung cancer | Annual low-dose CT, ages 50–80, 20 pack-years, current smoker or quit within 15 years. |
| Cervical cancer | Start at 21. See the reproductive chapter for intervals. |
| Depression | Screen adults. |
| HIV | At least once in adults. |
| Hepatitis C | Once, adults 18–79. |
| Prostate cancer | Men 55–69: individual decision. Do not screen men 70 or older. ([USPSTF](https://www.uspreventiveservicestaskforce.org/uspstf/recommendation/prostate-cancer-screening)) |
| Abdominal aortic aneurysm | One-time ultrasound in men 65–75 who have smoked. |
| Diabetes | Adults 35–70 with overweight or obesity. |

A grade I statement means the evidence is insufficient for a universal screen. It does not mean the disease is unimportant. A patient with symptoms is not in the screening chapter.

**Papers**

Intention-to-treat preserves randomization. A composite endpoint can be driven by the softest piece. A subgroup that was not prespecified is a hypothesis, not a result. Absolute risk matters more than a relative risk when the disease is rare. Number needed to treat is 1 over the absolute risk reduction.

## Checklist

- [ ] Symptoms cancel the screening rules. Work up the symptom.
- [ ] Know which USPSTF items are "start," "stop," and "discuss."
- [ ] Do not quote a relative risk as if it were an absolute one.
- [ ] A positive stool test is a colonoscopy.
- [ ] Grade I is not "do not care."

## Common traps

- Screening a 70-year-old man for prostate cancer because his friend had it, and calling that the USPSTF recommendation.
- Treating lead-time bias as proof a scan saves lives.
- Applying a screening interval to a patient who already has the disease.

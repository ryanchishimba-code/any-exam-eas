# Pulmonary disease

> **Study aid only.** Next step and the guideline that owns it. Ventilator manipulation for a crashing patient is a supervised skill.

## What this chapter covers

The outpatient and emergency decisions in asthma, COPD, pulmonary embolism, and pneumonia. Respiratory content is about 5–10% of Step 2 CK. The mechanism of gas exchange was Step 1. This chapter is what you do.

## The framework

Shortness of breath is the same first question as chest pain. Is the airway open, is oxygenation failing, is ventilation failing? Oxygen for hypoxemia. Noninvasive ventilation when the COPD exacerbation is acidotic and tiring, if no contraindication. Intubation when those fail or the airway is not protected. The rest of the chapter assumes you have already done that.

| Problem | Decision | Source to trust |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Asthma, any persistent symptoms | Do not treat with a short-acting beta agonist alone. Use an inhaled corticosteroid–containing regimen. | GINA strategy report, current year |
| Asthma exacerbation | Oxygen as needed, repeated bronchodilator, systemic corticosteroid. Magnesium and escalation when severe. | GINA and the emergency chapter |
| COPD maintenance | Group by symptoms and exacerbations, not by FEV1 alone. Long-acting bronchodilators are the base. Inhaled steroids are for higher exacerbation risk, not for everyone. | GOLD, current report |
| COPD exacerbation | Bronchodilators, steroid, antibiotic when sputum is more purulent or ventilation is needed, noninvasive ventilation for respiratory acidosis. | GOLD |
| Suspected PE, unstable | Treat and image. Do not send an unstable person to the CT scanner alone. | AHA / ESC PE guidance |
| Suspected PE, stable | Gestalt first. PERC only if gestalt is already low. Otherwise D-dimer if PE is unlikely, CT pulmonary angiography if likely. | ACEP and specialty PE guidance |
| Community pneumonia | Severity (PSI or CURB-65) plus judgment decides the site of care. Cover the typical and atypical organisms appropriate to the ward versus the ICU. | IDSA/ATS community-acquired pneumonia guideline |
| Light’s criteria | Pleural protein and LDH ratios separate exudate from transudate. An exudate needs a cause: infection, cancer, embolus, inflammation. | Standard pulmonary teaching; confirm a borderline case with the full criteria |

Smoking cessation is the disease-modifying treatment in COPD. Inhalers are symptom and exacerbation tools. Long-term oxygen is for chronic hypoxemia meeting criteria, not for breathlessness with adequate saturation. Confirm the saturation threshold in the current CMS/ATS oxygen guidance before you quote a number. A commonly taught threshold is a resting saturation at or below 88%. Flag it and check it.

Lung cancer screening is not a pulmonary-clinic aside. USPSTF recommends annual low-dose CT for adults 50 to 80 with at least a 20 pack-year history who smoke now or quit within 15 years. That lives again in the prevention chapter. Do not start screening someone outside that frame because they cough.

## High-yield atlas

Pulmonary items are a disposition. Say whether the patient can go home, then the guideline that picks the drug. A normal number in a tiring patient is not reassuring.

| Presentation and findings | Next best step | First-line management and the source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Night symptoms, a reliever used most days, no inhaled corticosteroid on the list. | Start an inhaled corticosteroid-containing regimen. Do not leave them on a short-acting beta agonist alone. | GINA. SABA-only treatment is not maintenance. A written action plan belongs with the inhaler. |
| Speaks in words, a quiet chest, a normal or rising CO2, falling peak flow. | Oxygen as needed, repeated bronchodilator, systemic corticosteroid, and a monitored bed. Magnesium if severe. | GINA and emergency pathways. A normal CO2 in a tiring patient is a warning, not reassurance. |
| Dyspnea and a smoking history. Group by symptoms and exacerbations, not by FEV1 alone. | Long-acting bronchodilator. Inhaled steroid only if exacerbations are frequent. Offer smoking cessation with a drug. | GOLD. Oxygen for chronic hypoxemia that meets criteria, not for breathlessness with a normal saturation. Confirm the saturation threshold before you quote 88%. |
| More dyspnea, more sputum, or purulent sputum, on a known COPD background. | Bronchodilators, a steroid burst, antibiotics when sputum is purulent or ventilation is needed. Noninvasive ventilation if acidemic and tiring. | GOLD. Target the saturation. Do not park high-flow oxygen on a sleepy retainer. |
| Fever, cough, a new infiltrate. The PSI or CURB-65 plus whether they can drink and who is at home. | Oral therapy and home if low severity and reliable. Hospital if hypoxic, confused, hypotensive, or unable to drink. | IDSA/ATS. The tool does not override a person who cannot keep water down. Confirm the current empiric regimen. Do not invent one. |
| Pleuritic pain, a clear film, a normal blood pressure. Recent surgery or a swollen leg. | Gestalt. PERC only if gestalt is already low. D-dimer if unlikely. CT pulmonary angiography if likely. | Anticoagulation once confirmed, unless bleeding forbids it. Thrombolysis is for hypotension, not for a stable patient. |
| Hypotension, right-heart strain, a sudden collapse, clear lungs. | Resuscitation and a reperfusion decision. Do not send an unstable patient to the scanner alone. | Thrombolysis or a procedure for the crashing patient. Anticoagulation alone is for the stable one. AHA and ESC PE guidance. |
| A round opacity found on a scan done for something else. No old films yet. | Compare with old images before you biopsy. Follow-up interval depends on size and risk. | Fleischner-style follow-up. Open the current table. Do not invent an interval. A growing nodule or a high-risk large nodule is not a one-year wait. |
| A cough, or a 30-year quit, used as a reason for a low-dose CT. | Screening is for the USPSTF frame only. A symptom is a diagnostic workup. | USPSTF: 50 to 80, 20 pack-years, current or quit within 15 years, annual low-dose CT. A chest radiograph is not the screening test. |
| Dullness, decreased sounds, trachea pushed away if large. New in a patient with cancer, heart failure, or a pneumonia. | Tap a new effusion unless the cause is obvious heart failure that is responding. Send protein and LDH. | Light's criteria separate exudate from transudate. An exudate needs a cause: infection, cancer, clot, inflammation. Heart failure is the usual transudate. |
| Absent sounds, trachea away, hypotension, after a procedure or trauma. | Decompress. Do not wait for a film if the patient is crashing. | Clinical diagnosis when unstable. A chest tube follows. ATLS. |
| Snoring, daytime sleepiness, a thick neck, hypertension that is hard to treat. A partner saw pauses. | A sleep study. Not a stimulant as the first drug. | Positive airway pressure is the treatment that fixes the airway. Weight loss helps. The sleep medicine guideline. Do not clear someone for elective surgery without telling anesthesia if the story is this strong. |
| Streaks versus cups of blood. A smoker. Or a patient with bronchiectasis. | Massive hemoptysis is the bad lung down, airway protection, and a procedural consult. Streaks in a stable patient are a chest film and a cancer or infection workup. | Protect the good lung. Do not lay the bleeding side up if you can help it. Bronchoscopy or embolization is specialist care. Confirm the local pathway. |
| A positive interferon-gamma release assay or a positive skin test. A normal chest film. No symptoms. | Rule out active disease before you treat latent infection. Then treat latent infection so it does not become active. | CDC. Do not start a single drug if the film or the symptoms say active disease. Active disease is never one drug. |
| Cough for a few days, no focal findings, no hypoxia, a well-appearing adult. | No antibiotic. No chest film if the exam is clear and the patient is well. | IDSA and Choosing Wisely style guidance: uncomplicated acute bronchitis is viral. Purulent sputum does not equal bacteria. |
| Any smoker with or without COPD. | Advise and offer varenicline or combination nicotine replacement plus support. | USPSTF and CDC. A pamphlet is not the whole answer. Pregnancy changes the drug. Confirm before you prescribe. |

| Pair | How you tell them apart |
| --- | --- |
| Asthma, not controlled versus the neighbor | COPD maintenance is long-acting bronchodilators first. Inhaled steroid is for higher exacerbation risk, not for everyone. |
| Severe asthma attack versus the neighbor | A patient who speaks in sentences, has a good peak flow response, and is observed can go home with steroids. |
| COPD maintenance versus the neighbor | Asthma is reversible and usually younger, with a preserved diffusion capacity on the Step 1 side of the story. |
| COPD exacerbation versus the neighbor | A mild increase in sputum in a patient who is walking and talking is bronchodilators, a steroid, and maybe an antibiotic, then home with follow-up. |
| Community-acquired pneumonia, site of care versus the neighbor | A PSI class II patient who eats, drinks, and has a ride can go home. |
| Pulmonary embolism, stable versus the neighbor | Hypotension is reperfusion territory, not this pathway. |
| Massive pulmonary embolism versus the neighbor | Stable embolism gets a diagnostic pathway. This gets resuscitation. |
| Lung nodule versus the neighbor | A 2 cm spiculated nodule in a 40-pack-year smoker is not a one-year follow-up. |
| Solitary lung cancer screening mistake versus the neighbor | A 62-year-old current 40-pack-year smoker with no symptoms meets the frame. |
| Pleural effusion versus the neighbor | Bilateral effusions in a wet patient with a known cardiomyopathy can be treated and watched if they improve. |
| Pneumothorax, tension versus the neighbor | A small stable pneumothorax in a healthy person can be observed. This cannot. |
| Obstructive sleep apnea versus the neighbor | Narcolepsy is sleep attacks and cataplexy, not snoring and pauses. |
| Hemoptysis, the amount versus the neighbor | Blood-streaked sputum in a stable smoker is a cancer evaluation, not an intubation. |
| Latent tuberculosis versus the neighbor | Cough, night sweats, and a cavity are active disease: isolation and combination therapy. |
| Acute bronchitis versus pneumonia versus the neighbor | Fever, a focal crackle, and hypoxia are pneumonia until the film says otherwise. |
| Smoking cessation as the pulmonary treatment versus the neighbor | Nicotine replacement alone is weaker than combination or varenicline for many people. Offer the effective option. |

### Asthma, not controlled

Airway inflammation, not just bronchodilator deficiency.

**Vignette.** A 24-year-old uses albuterol every day and wakes twice a week. No controller is on the list.
**Reasoning.** Daily reliever use means the airway is inflamed. The diagnosis is uncontrolled asthma. The trap is a refill of the reliever. The diagnosis is Asthma, not controlled.
**NBME trap.** Another albuterol inhaler as the whole plan.
**Hook.** No SABA-only asthma treatment.

### Severe asthma attack

Ventilation is failing. The usual low CO2 of an attack has been lost.

**Vignette.** She can say only one word at a time. The chest is quiet. The arterial CO2 is 40.
**Reasoning.** A severe attack should blow CO2 down. Forty means she is tiring. The diagnosis is life-threatening asthma. The trap is discharge because the number looks normal. The diagnosis is Severe asthma attack.
**NBME trap.** A prescription and the door for a silent chest.
**Hook.** Normal CO2 in bad asthma is bad.

### COPD maintenance

Destroyed recoil or a chronically inflamed airway. The cigarette is the disease-modifying exposure.

**Vignette.** He stops walking at one block. Two exacerbations last year. He is on albuterol only and still smokes.
**Reasoning.** Symptoms plus exacerbations pick the inhalers. The diagnosis is COPD that is undertreated. The trap is an inhaled steroid for everyone, or oxygen for a saturation of 95%. The diagnosis is COPD maintenance.
**NBME trap.** FEV1 alone choosing the inhaler.
**Hook.** Symptoms and exacerbations, not the FEV1 percent.

### COPD exacerbation

An acute worsening. Acidosis means the pump is losing.

**Vignette.** Known COPD. He is sleepy. pH is 7.28. CO2 is 70. He is still protecting his airway.
**Reasoning.** Acidemic and tiring gets noninvasive ventilation if nothing contraindicates it. The diagnosis is a severe exacerbation. The trap is immediate intubation of a patient who can still try a mask, or a cannula and a nap. The diagnosis is COPD exacerbation.
**NBME trap.** Unlimited oxygen for comfort.
**Hook.** Acid and sleepy: noninvasive ventilation.

### Community-acquired pneumonia, site of care

Severity is the disposition. The organism follows the host.

**Vignette.** CURB-65 is 0. She cannot keep fluids down and lives alone. Oxygen is 96% on room air.
**Reasoning.** The score says home. The person cannot drink. The diagnosis is pneumonia that needs observation or admission. The trap is discharging to an empty apartment. The diagnosis is Community-acquired pneumonia, site of care.
**NBME trap.** The score used as a discharge order.
**Hook.** The tool plus the living situation.

### Pulmonary embolism, stable

Dead space and clot. A normal CO2 does not clear it.

**Vignette.** Three weeks after pelvic surgery, pleuritic pain, oxygen 96%, pressure 128/76.
**Reasoning.** High gestalt. Do not PERC her. Do not stop at a D-dimer if she is likely. The diagnosis is suspected pulmonary embolism. The trap is a d-dimer in a high-probability patient. The diagnosis is Pulmonary embolism, stable.
**NBME trap.** PERC after recent surgery.
**Hook.** PERC is only a low-gestalt tool.

### Massive pulmonary embolism

Obstruction of the pulmonary arteries. Output falls.

**Vignette.** She collapses. Pressure is 70. The chest film is clear. A leg is swollen.
**Reasoning.** This is obstructive shock from clot until proven otherwise. The trap is a CT first while the pressure is 70 and nobody is with her. The diagnosis is Massive pulmonary embolism.
**NBME trap.** A heparin drip as the only move in a patient with no blood pressure.
**Hook.** Low pressure is reperfusion, not a dimer.

### Lung nodule

Most small nodules are benign. Growth is the malignant behavior.

**Vignette.** A 6 mm nodule in a 45-year-old who never smoked. A film from two years ago shows the same nodule.
**Reasoning.** Stability for two years is reassuring. The trap is a biopsy of a stable tiny nodule. The diagnosis is a stable nodule, not cancer. The diagnosis is Lung nodule.
**NBME trap.** Biopsy before old films.
**Hook.** Old films before a needle.

### Solitary lung cancer screening mistake

Screening and diagnosis are different visits.

**Vignette.** He quit 20 years ago after 10 pack-years and has a winter cough. He wants the screening CT his brother had.
**Reasoning.** He is outside the frame. The cough, if persistent, is a diagnostic question, not a screening checkbox. The diagnosis is Solitary lung cancer screening mistake.
**NBME trap.** A chest radiograph called screening.
**Hook.** Pack-years and recency, not a cough.

### Pleural effusion

Pressure or protein versus a leaky pleura.

**Vignette.** New dyspnea. A large right effusion. No heart failure history. He has lost weight.
**Reasoning.** This is not a diuretic trial. Tap it. The trap is calling every effusion heart failure. The diagnosis is Pleural effusion.
**NBME trap.** A therapeutic tap of a tiny effusion that does not explain the dyspnea, before you have a diagnosis.
**Hook.** A new unexplained effusion gets a needle.

### Pneumothorax, tension

Air under pressure kinks venous return.

**Vignette.** After a subclavian line the pressure falls, one side is silent, and the trachea shifts.
**Reasoning.** That is tension. Tamponade does not silence one hemithorax. The trap is a chest film while the pressure is 70. The diagnosis is Pneumothorax, tension.
**NBME trap.** A radiograph as the first move in a crashing patient.
**Hook.** Shift and shock: needle, then film.

### Obstructive sleep apnea

The upper airway collapses in sleep. Hypoxia and arousals follow.

**Vignette.** His wife says he stops breathing. He falls asleep in the chair. Blood pressure is hard to control.
**Reasoning.** The story is enough to test. The diagnosis to pursue is obstructive sleep apnea. The trap is a new sedative for the daytime sleep. The diagnosis is Obstructive sleep apnea.
**NBME trap.** A benzodiazepine at night.
**Hook.** Pauses and sleepiness: a study, not a sedative.

### Hemoptysis, the amount

Airway blood is an airway problem first.

**Vignette.** He coughs up a cup of blood. He is tachycardic. The left chest is the one full of sounds he cannot clear.
**Reasoning.** This is massive. Position and airway before a clinic CT appointment. The trap is a routine chest film and a follow-up visit. The diagnosis is Hemoptysis, the amount.
**NBME trap.** Both lungs up and a long history while he is drowning.
**Hook.** Cups of blood: airway. Streaks: a workup.

### Latent tuberculosis

Infection without disease. The immune system is holding it.

**Vignette.** A positive blood test. No cough. The film is normal. He feels well.
**Reasoning.** This is latent infection if active disease is truly absent. The trap is isoniazid alone for a cavitary cough. The diagnosis is Latent tuberculosis.
**NBME trap.** One drug for a cavity.
**Hook.** Normal film and no symptoms: latent. Cavity: never one drug.

### Acute bronchitis versus pneumonia

Airway inflammation after a virus.

**Vignette.** Five days of cough. Lungs are clear. Oxygen is 98%. He wants a z-pak because the sputum is yellow.
**Reasoning.** Color of sputum is not pneumonia. The diagnosis is acute bronchitis. The trap is the antibiotic. The diagnosis is Acute bronchitis versus pneumonia.
**NBME trap.** An antibiotic for yellow sputum.
**Hook.** Clear lungs and a well patient: no antibiotic.

### Smoking cessation as the pulmonary treatment

The exposure causes the disease. The inhaler does not erase the cigarette.

**Vignette.** He wants a stronger inhaler and will not discuss the pack a day.
**Reasoning.** You still offer the drug and the advice. The inhaler is added, not substituted. The trap is a new inhaler and silence about tobacco. The diagnosis is Smoking cessation as the pulmonary treatment.
**NBME trap.** Waiting until he asks.
**Hook.** The cigarette is the disease. Offer a drug.

## Checklist

- [ ] No SABA-only asthma treatment.
- [ ] COPD inhalers follow symptoms and exacerbations.
- [ ] Unstable PE is a resuscitation, not a PERC item.
- [ ] PERC is only for low gestalt.
- [ ] Site of care for pneumonia is a severity tool plus the person in front of you.

## Common traps

- Steroids for every wheeze forever. COPD and asthma do not have the same inhaled-steroid indication.
- A negative D-dimer “ruling out” PE when the pretest probability was high. D-dimer is for the unlikely group.
- Waiting for a fever to call pneumonia. Older adults may not mount one.
- Quoting an antibiotic by habit from medical school. The IDSA/ATS regimen depends on severity and on local resistance. Check the current table.

## Practice this in the Qbank

Pulmonary misses are usually “sent them for a test while unstable” or “used the wrong severity tool.” Feed cards on GOLD groups go stale when GOLD revises the letters. Learn the principle (symptoms plus exacerbations) so a renamed group does not break you.

# Cardiovascular disease

> **Study aid only.** Decisions tied to named guidelines. A supervisor and the current document outrank this page for any real patient.

## What this chapter covers

The next step in chest pain, heart failure, blood pressure, and the common arrhythmias, using the ACC/AHA documents that actually changed the decision. Cardiovascular content is about 6–12% of Step 2 CK.

## The framework

Chest pain is two clocks. Is this acute coronary syndrome or dissection or embolus or a pneumothorax, right now? If yes, the next step is the ECG and the resuscitation, not a clever outpatient test. If the person is stable and the pain is clearly reproducible or pleuritic with a normal ECG and a low-risk story, you earn the right to think about a different diagnosis. Most stems sit in between. Say “unstable or not” before you say the disease.

| Situation | The decision the guideline is about |
| --- | --- |
| Suspected ACS | ECG immediately. Aspirin if there is no contraindication. Reperfusion strategy for ST-elevation, not a long history. The 2025 ACC/AHA ACS guideline is the source for the pathway. Do not memorize a minute number from a forum. Use the pathway. |
| Chronic coronary disease | Antianginal therapy plus risk reduction. High-intensity statin for clinical atherosclerotic disease is an ACC/AHA cholesterol-guideline idea. Confirm the current lipid document before quoting a target. |
| Heart failure with reduced ejection fraction | Four pillars when they can be used: an ARNI (or an ACE inhibitor or ARB), an evidence-based beta blocker, a mineralocorticoid receptor antagonist, and an SGLT2 inhibitor. Diuretics treat congestion. They do not change the remodeling story the way the pillars do. Source: 2022 AHA/ACC/HFSA heart failure guideline, with later updates to be checked. |
| Stage 1 blood pressure (130–139 or 80–89) | 2025 AHA/ACC high blood pressure guideline: start medication if there is clinical CVD, diabetes, CKD, or a PREVENT 10-year CVD risk of at least 7.5%. If risk is under 7.5% and there is no compelling condition, lifestyle for 3 to 6 months, then medication if still at or above 130/80. |
| Stage 2 (at or above 140 or 90) | Start medication, usually as combination therapy, along with lifestyle. The 2025 guideline moved this clearly. |
| Pregnancy blood pressure | Different document. Do not apply the adult PREVENT threshold to a pregnant patient. ACOG owns that decision. See the obstetrics chapter. |

The 2025 blood pressure guideline uses the PREVENT equations, not the old pooled-cohort calculator, and it does not use race to pick the drug. ACE inhibitor or ARB is recommended when diabetes and CKD (eGFR under 60 or albuminuria at least 30 mg/g) travel with hypertension. Do not combine an ACE inhibitor with an ARB.

Atrial fibrillation items are rate or rhythm, plus whether to anticoagulate. The decision to anticoagulate is a stroke-risk score plus bleeding context, not a vibe. Confirm the current CHA2DS2-VASc-style factors in the ACC/AHA atrial fibrillation guideline before you freeze a list. Do not invent a point value in your notes if you are not looking at the table.

## Checklist

- [ ] ECG before the story is finished, when ACS is possible.
- [ ] Heart-failure pillars versus diuretic for fluid. Different jobs.
- [ ] Stage 1 hypertension is a risk decision, using PREVENT, in the 2025 guideline.
- [ ] No ACE inhibitor plus ARB.
- [ ] Pregnancy has its own blood-pressure rules.

## Common traps

- Nitroglycerin as a diagnostic test. Esophageal spasm can also ease. It does not prove a coronary lesion.
- Starting a beta blocker in decompensated shock because “heart failure gets beta blockers.” The pillar is for the compensated reduced-ejection-fraction patient.
- Treating a blood pressure of 148/92 with lifestyle alone for months. That is stage 2.
- Using race as a reason to pick a drug. The 2025 guideline removed that approach.

## Practice this in the Qbank

Sort cardiology misses into “did not stabilize,” “wrong syndrome,” and “wrong pillar.” Feed cards on drug classes should name which pillar, not just a brand. Re-check any card that still teaches pooled-cohort risk or race-based drug choice. Those cards are out of date if they contradict the 2025 guideline.

# Cardiovascular disease

> **Study aid only.** Decisions tied to named guidelines. A supervisor and the current document outrank this page for any real patient.

## What this chapter covers

The next step in chest pain, heart failure, blood pressure, and the common arrhythmias, using the ACC/AHA documents that actually changed the decision. Cardiovascular content is about 6–12% of Step 2 CK.

## The framework

Chest pain is two clocks. Is this acute coronary syndrome or dissection or embolus or a pneumothorax, right now? If yes, the next step is the ECG and the resuscitation, not a clever outpatient test. If the person is stable and the pain is clearly reproducible or pleuritic with a normal ECG and a low-risk story, you earn the right to think about a different diagnosis. Most stems sit in between. Say “unstable or not” before you say the disease.

| Situation | The decision the guideline is about |
| --- | --- |
| Suspected ACS | ECG immediately. Aspirin if there is no contraindication. Reperfusion strategy for ST-elevation, not a long history. The 2025 ACC/AHA ACS guideline is the source for the pathway. Do not memorize a minute number from a forum. Use the pathway. |
| Chronic coronary disease | Antianginal therapy plus risk reduction. High-intensity statin for clinical atherosclerotic disease is an ACC/AHA cholesterol-guideline idea. Confirm the current lipid document before quoting a target. |
| Heart failure with reduced ejection fraction | Four pillars when they can be used: an ARNI (or an ACE inhibitor or ARB), an evidence-based beta blocker, a mineralocorticoid receptor antagonist, and an SGLT2 inhibitor. Diuretics treat congestion. They do not change the remodeling story the way the pillars do. Source: 2022 AHA/ACC/HFSA heart failure guideline, with later updates to be checked. |
| Stage 1 blood pressure (130–139 or 80–89) | 2025 AHA/ACC high blood pressure guideline: start medication if there is clinical CVD, diabetes, CKD, or a PREVENT 10-year CVD risk of at least 7.5%. If risk is under 7.5% and there is no compelling condition, lifestyle for 3 to 6 months, then medication if still at or above 130/80. |
| Stage 2 (at or above 140 or 90) | Start medication, usually as combination therapy, along with lifestyle. The 2025 guideline moved this clearly. |
| Pregnancy blood pressure | Different document. Do not apply the adult PREVENT threshold to a pregnant patient. ACOG owns that decision. See the obstetrics chapter. |

The 2025 blood pressure guideline uses the PREVENT equations, not the old pooled-cohort calculator, and it does not use race to pick the drug. ACE inhibitor or ARB is recommended when diabetes and CKD (eGFR under 60 or albuminuria at least 30 mg/g) travel with hypertension. Do not combine an ACE inhibitor with an ARB.

Atrial fibrillation items are rate or rhythm, plus whether to anticoagulate. The decision to anticoagulate is a stroke-risk score plus bleeding context, not a vibe. Confirm the current CHA2DS2-VASc-style factors in the ACC/AHA atrial fibrillation guideline before you freeze a list. Do not invent a point value in your notes if you are not looking at the table.

## High-yield atlas

Cardiovascular items on Step 2 CK are a next step. Say whether the patient is stable, then the syndrome, then the one action that changes today. The feed cards are single decisions. This atlas is the set.

| Presentation and findings | Next best step | First-line management and the source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Crushing pain, ST elevation in two neighboring leads, diaphoresis. The first troponin may still be normal. | ECG already in hand. Aspirin if no contraindication. Activate the reperfusion pathway. Do not wait for the troponin. | The 2025 ACC/AHA ACS guideline owns the reperfusion choice and the antithrombotic regimen. Do not memorize a minute count from a forum. Do not give fibrinolytic therapy if the patient is going immediately to angiography and a contraindication is present. |
| Inferior ST elevation, clear lungs, and a pressure that falls after nitrate. | Stop the nitrate. Give fluid if the lungs are clear. Right-sided leads. | Preload is the cardiac output. The ACS guideline still applies to the infarct. The volume decision is the right ventricle. |
| Tearing pain to the back, unequal pulses, a wide mediastinum, no ST elevation. | Two large-bore lines, blood pressure control, a surgical consult, and a CT angiogram if the patient is stable enough to go. | The ACC/AHA aortic disease guideline. Type A (ascending) is an operation. Do not thrombolyse a dissection that was mistaken for an infarct. |
| A dilated ventricle, a low ejection fraction, and congestion or fatigue. The patient is euvolemic today. | Start or continue the pillars the blood pressure and the kidneys allow. A diuretic only if they are wet. | 2022 AHA/ACC/HFSA and later updates: ARNI or ACE inhibitor or ARB, an evidence-based beta blocker, a mineralocorticoid antagonist, and an SGLT2 inhibitor. Not an ACE inhibitor plus an ARB. |
| Home readings of 134/84, or an office reading of 152/94, confirmed. | Calculate PREVENT risk and look for diabetes, CKD, or clinical CVD before you start a drug at stage 1. Stage 2 starts a drug now. | 2025 AHA/ACC high blood pressure guideline. Stage 1 medication if risk is at least 7.5% or a compelling disease is present. Otherwise lifestyle for 3 to 6 months. Stage 2 is combination therapy plus lifestyle. No race-based choice. No ACE inhibitor plus ARB. |
| An irregular narrow tachycardia, hypotension, and ischemic pain or pulmonary edema. | Synchronized cardioversion. Do not wait for a rate-control pill. | The ACC/AHA atrial fibrillation guideline. Anticoagulation is a separate decision based on stroke risk, not on whether the rhythm just converted. |
| Pressure on exertion, relieved by rest, a normal rest ECG. | Risk reduction and an antianginal. Not an emergent catheterization unless the pattern is changing. | High-intensity statin for clinical atherosclerotic disease is the ACC/AHA cholesterol approach. Confirm the current lipid document before you quote an LDL number. Aspirin is typical. A beta blocker is a common antianginal. |
| Positional pain, a rub, diffuse ST elevation, PR depression. | ECG and an echocardiogram to look for fluid. An anti-inflammatory. Not fibrinolysis. | NSAIDs and colchicine are the usual first combination in the ESC and AHA pericarditis documents. Confirm the current file. Anticoagulation is dangerous if an effusion is growing. |
| Hypotension, distended neck veins, muffled sounds, a pulsus paradoxus, a clear chest or a known effusion. | Fluid bolus if you are waiting, and drainage. Not a rate-control drug. | Drainage is the treatment. The European Society of Cardiology and AHA pericardial documents describe urgent pericardiocentesis for tamponade. A diuretic is the wrong direction. |
| Calf pain at a fixed distance, relieved by rest, weak distal pulses. Or a rest pain and a wound. | Ankle-brachial index. A statin, an antiplatelet, a walking program, and a smoking stop. Imaging if the limb is threatened or an intervention is planned. | The AHA/ACC peripheral artery guideline. Cilostazol can help claudication if heart failure is absent. A threatened limb is a vascular emergency, not a walking program. |
| A swollen leg, or pleuritic pain with a normal chest film, and a stable blood pressure. | Gestalt first. PERC only if gestalt is already low. Otherwise D-dimer if unlikely, CT pulmonary angiography if likely. | Anticoagulation per the CHEST/ACC approach for a confirmed clot. Thrombolysis is for the hypotensive embolus, not for a stable one. A proximal leg clot and a lung clot are the same disease. |
| Exertional syncope, angina, or dyspnea, a late-peaking systolic murmur, a delayed carotid upstroke. | Echocardiography. If severe and symptomatic, valve replacement. Do not exercise the patient. | The ACC/AHA valvular guideline. Afterload reduction is dangerous when the valve is fixed and severe. Replacement is the disease-modifying step. |
| Fever and a new regurgitant murmur, or a fever and a valve plus embolic lesions. | Blood cultures before antibiotics if minutes are safe, then an echocardiogram. Start antibiotics if the patient is unstable. | The AHA/IDSA endocarditis guidance. Dental prophylaxis is a narrow list, not everyone with a murmur. Confirm the list before you prescribe it. |
| Very high pressure plus acute organ injury: encephalopathy, a dissecting pain, pulmonary edema, or a failing kidney. | A controlled reduction in a monitored bed, with an intravenous agent. Not a normalized pressure in an hour. | The 2025 high blood pressure guideline and the emergency-medicine approach. The injured organ sets the pace and the target. Asymptomatic severe hypertension without organ injury is not the same disease and does not need an intravenous drip. |
| Hypotension, cold skin, pulmonary edema, distended neck veins, after an infarct or in a known cardiomyopathy. | Airway, a small fluid challenge only if you are not sure the lungs are wet, vasopressors or inotropes, and the cause. Reperfusion if this is an infarct. | The shock is the pump. Norepinephrine is a common first vasopressor. An inotrope is added when the pump is the problem and the pressure allows. Confirm the current SCCM/ACC approach. Do not flood a wet patient. |

| Pair | How you tell them apart |
| --- | --- |
| ST-elevation myocardial infarction versus the neighbor | NSTEMI has a rising troponin without ST elevation. Unstable angina has ischemic symptoms and no troponin rise. Both are ACS. Only elevation means the artery is usually shut. |
| Right-ventricular infarction versus the neighbor | Cardiogenic shock from the left ventricle is wet. This shock is dry. |
| Aortic dissection versus the neighbor | An expanding aneurysm hurts and can rupture, but it does not create a false lumen and unequal pulses the same way. |
| Heart failure with reduced ejection fraction versus the neighbor | Heart failure with preserved ejection fraction is a stiff ventricle. The pillars are not identical. Do not copy the reduced-ejection-fraction list blindly. Confirm the current guideline. |
| Stage 1 versus stage 2 hypertension versus the neighbor | 148/92 is stage 2 even if the patient feels well. Lifestyle alone for months is the wrong plan. |
| Atrial fibrillation with instability versus the neighbor | Stable atrial fibrillation with a rate of 110 and no pain is a rate or rhythm discussion and an anticoagulation decision, not a shock. |
| Stable angina versus the neighbor | Unstable angina is new, rest, or crescendo. Stable is reproducible. |
| Acute pericarditis versus the neighbor | Myocarditis has a troponin rise and a sick muscle. Pericarditis can have a small troponin if the epicardium is irritated. A large troponin and a failing ventricle is the muscle. |
| Tamponade versus the neighbor | Constriction has a sharp y descent and a knock and is not an acute post-procedure collapse. |
| Peripheral artery disease versus the neighbor | Spinal stenosis is a posture disease. Vascular claudication is a distance disease. |
| Deep vein thrombosis and pulmonary embolism, stable versus the neighbor | Massive embolism is hypotension. Submassive is strain without shock. Stable is neither. The drug changes. |
| Aortic stenosis, symptomatic versus the neighbor | Hypertrophic obstruction softens with more preload. This murmur will not. |
| Endocarditis, the next test versus the neighbor | Left-sided disease embolizes to brain, kidney, and spleen. Right-sided disease embolizes to the lung. |
| Hypertensive emergency versus the neighbor | Urgency is a high number without acute injury. Emergency is injury. |
| Cardiogenic shock versus the neighbor | Distributive shock is warm and the diastolic pressure is low. This patient is cold and wet. |

### ST-elevation myocardial infarction

A coronary artery is usually occluded. Full-thickness injury shows as ST elevation. Troponin lags the tracing.

**Vignette.** A 58-year-old man has 40 minutes of pressure, nausea, and sweat. ST elevation is in V2 through V4. He asks if you can wait for the blood test.
**Reasoning.** The tracing has already made the diagnosis. Waiting for a protein that rises later delays reperfusion. The diagnosis is ST-elevation myocardial infarction.
**NBME trap.** A normal first troponin used to cancel the catheterization lab.
**Hook.** The tracing outruns the troponin.

### Right-ventricular infarction

The right ventricle is stiff and underfilled. Nitrates empty the venous reservoir it needed.

**Vignette.** ST elevation in II, III, and aVF. After one nitroglycerin tablet the systolic pressure is 80 and the chest is clear.
**Reasoning.** Inferior wall plus nitrate collapse plus no pulmonary edema is the right ventricle. Papillary rupture would bring a murmur and wet lungs. The diagnosis is Right-ventricular infarction.
**NBME trap.** More nitrate because the pain is still there.
**Hook.** Inferior, clear, nitrate-down: fill the right heart.

### Aortic dissection

A tear in the intima lets blood into the media. It is not a dilated sac.

**Vignette.** A 70-year-old has sudden pain between the scapulae. The right arm pulse is weak. The ECG has no ST elevation. The chest film is wide.
**Reasoning.** Unequal pulses and a tear to the back are a flap. An aneurysm can be silent. An infarct would light up a territory. The diagnosis is Aortic dissection.
**NBME trap.** Fibrinolysis for the pain.
**Hook.** Unequal pulses, no ST elevation, back.

### Heart failure with reduced ejection fraction

Systolic failure. Remodeling is the disease. Congestion is a symptom the diuretic treats.

**Vignette.** A 64-year-old with an ejection fraction of 30% is at dry weight. He is on furosemide only.
**Reasoning.** The diuretic did its job. The pillars change the disease. The trap is adding a second diuretic and calling the regimen complete. The diagnosis is Heart failure with reduced ejection fraction.
**NBME trap.** A beta blocker started today in florid pulmonary edema.
**Hook.** Dry weight is when the pillars matter. Wet is when the diuretic matters.

### Stage 1 versus stage 2 hypertension

Stage 1 is 130–139 or 80–89. Stage 2 is at or above 140 or 90.

**Vignette.** Repeated home readings average 136/86. No diabetes, no CKD, no prior infarct. PREVENT 10-year risk is 4%.
**Reasoning.** This is stage 1 at low risk. Lifestyle first, then a drug if the pressure is still high in 3 to 6 months. The trap is a two-drug start, which is the stage 2 move. The diagnosis is Stage 1 versus stage 2 hypertension.
**NBME trap.** Using the old pooled-cohort calculator or a race-based drug rule.
**Hook.** Stage 2 is a drug now. Stage 1 asks about risk.

### Atrial fibrillation with instability

The rate or the loss of atrial kick dropped the output.

**Vignette.** A patient is cold and confused. The monitor is irregular at 160. Blood pressure is 80/50.
**Reasoning.** Unstable tachycardia is electricity. A beta blocker is for a stable rate. The trap is a pill because the complex is narrow. The diagnosis is Atrial fibrillation with instability.
**NBME trap.** Adenosine for an irregular wide or an irregular narrow rhythm you have already called fibrillation.
**Hook.** Unstable means electricity.

### Stable angina

Fixed stenosis. Demand exceeds supply. The cap has not ruptured.

**Vignette.** Climbing one flight causes pressure that stops when he stops. It has been the same for four months. The rest ECG is normal.
**Reasoning.** Predictable demand ischemia. Unstable angina would be rest pain or a changing threshold. The trap is an emergency department referral for a stable, months-long pattern that has not changed. The diagnosis is Stable angina.
**NBME trap.** A troponin chase for a symptom that has been identical for months and is gone.
**Hook.** Same hill, same pain, same relief: stable.

### Acute pericarditis

The sac is inflamed. The elevation is not one coronary territory.

**Vignette.** Pain is worse supine and better leaning forward. ST segments are up in many leads. There is no reciprocal depression.
**Reasoning.** Distribution and posture are the sac. The trap is a STEMI pathway. The diagnosis is Acute pericarditis.
**NBME trap.** Thrombolysis for diffuse elevation.
**Hook.** Many leads, better sitting up.

### Tamponade

The heart cannot fill. Diastolic pressures equalize. The y descent is lost.

**Vignette.** After a procedure, blood pressure falls, the neck veins rise, and the heart sounds are distant. The lungs are clear.
**Reasoning.** Obstruction to filling. Tension pneumothorax would move the trachea and silence one side. The diagnosis is tamponade. The diagnosis is Tamponade.
**NBME trap.** Diuresis because the neck veins are up.
**Hook.** Up veins, clear lungs, quiet heart: drain.

### Peripheral artery disease

A fixed arterial stenosis. Demand of walking exceeds supply.

**Vignette.** Calf pain every time he walks one block. It stops when he stands. Pulses are weak. There is no rest pain and no ulcer.
**Reasoning.** Classic claudication. The trap is a spinal stenosis, which is relieved by leaning forward and not by simply standing, and which has normal pulses. The diagnosis is Peripheral artery disease.
**NBME trap.** An emergency angiogram for stable one-block claudication.
**Hook.** Distance and pulses versus posture and normal pulses.

### Deep vein thrombosis and pulmonary embolism, stable

Virchow. Stasis, injury, or a sticky blood.

**Vignette.** A woman three weeks after a pelvic surgery has a unilaterally swollen calf and pleuritic pain. Blood pressure is 128/76. Oxygen is 96%.
**Reasoning.** She is stable, so this is a diagnostic pathway, not a thrombolytic. High gestalt after recent surgery means you do not PERC her out. The diagnosis is Deep vein thrombosis and pulmonary embolism, stable.
**NBME trap.** A D-dimer in a high-probability patient, or PERC in a high-probability patient.
**Hook.** PERC is only for a low gestalt.

### Aortic stenosis, symptomatic

Fixed outflow obstruction. The ventricle has hypertrophied. Cardiac output cannot rise.

**Vignette.** A 78-year-old nearly faints climbing stairs. The carotid upstroke is slow. A systolic murmur radiates to the neck.
**Reasoning.** The delayed pulse is fixed stenosis, not hypertrophic obstruction, which has a brisk pulse and a murmur that softens when he squats. The diagnosis is Aortic stenosis, symptomatic.
**NBME trap.** A vasodilator to help the blood pressure in severe symptomatic stenosis.
**Hook.** Slow carotid, late murmur, symptoms: replace the valve.

### Endocarditis, the next test

A vegetation. Acute disease destroys a normal valve. Subacute disease sits on a damaged one.

**Vignette.** A man who injects drugs has a fever and a new murmur that gets louder on inspiration. The lungs have round lesions.
**Reasoning.** The right-sided valve throws emboli to the lung. Inspiration loads that valve. The diagnosis is right-sided endocarditis. The trap is a left-sided stroke story, which would be systemic emboli. The diagnosis is Endocarditis, the next test.
**NBME trap.** One set of cultures after antibiotics have already started, then a negative culture used to stop the drug.
**Hook.** Right heart throws to the lung. Left heart throws to the body.

### Hypertensive emergency

The pressure is injuring an organ now.

**Vignette.** Blood pressure is 220/120. The patient is confused and the fundi show papilledema. There is no chest pain.
**Reasoning.** The brain is the injured organ. This is an emergency. The trap is a clonidine load and a clinic appointment because the number is the only thing you saw, or the opposite trap of dropping the pressure to 120 in twenty minutes and causing a watershed infarct. The diagnosis is Hypertensive emergency.
**NBME trap.** Normalizing the pressure immediately.
**Hook.** Injury makes it an emergency. A number alone does not.

### Cardiogenic shock

Cardiac output is down and filling pressure is up.

**Vignette.** After an anterior infarct, the patient is cold, the pressure is 80, the neck veins are up, and rales are halfway up.
**Reasoning.** The left ventricle is not ejecting. Fluid will drown him. Lactate means poor perfusion, not a mandate for liters. The diagnosis is Cardiogenic shock.
**NBME trap.** A sepsis-sized fluid bolus into a wet chest.
**Hook.** Cold, wet, full neck veins: do not flood.

## Checklist

- [ ] ECG before the story is finished, when ACS is possible.
- [ ] Heart-failure pillars versus diuretic for fluid. Different jobs.
- [ ] Stage 1 hypertension is a risk decision, using PREVENT, in the 2025 guideline.
- [ ] No ACE inhibitor plus ARB.
- [ ] Pregnancy has its own blood-pressure rules.

## Common traps

- Nitroglycerin as a diagnostic test. Esophageal spasm can also ease. It does not prove a coronary lesion.
- Starting a beta blocker in decompensated shock because “heart failure gets beta blockers.” The pillar is for the compensated reduced-ejection-fraction patient.
- Treating a blood pressure of 148/92 with lifestyle alone for months. That is stage 2.
- Using race as a reason to pick a drug. The 2025 guideline removed that approach.

## Practice this in the Qbank

Sort cardiology misses into “did not stabilize,” “wrong syndrome,” and “wrong pillar.” Feed cards on drug classes should name which pillar, not just a brand. Re-check any card that still teaches pooled-cohort risk or race-based drug choice. Those cards are out of date if they contradict the 2025 guideline.

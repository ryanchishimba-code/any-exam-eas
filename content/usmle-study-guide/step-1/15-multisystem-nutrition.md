# Multisystem disease and nutrition

> **Study aid only.** Processes that do not stay in one organ, and the deficiencies that are a missing cofactor. Diet prescriptions for a clinic patient follow the current ADA, AHA, or USPSTF source.

## What this chapter covers

Shock and sepsis as whole-body physiology, the nutritional deficiencies that explain a weird finding, and the fluid shifts of burns and trauma. Multisystem processes are about 8–12% of Step 1. Nutrition as a discipline is listed at about 15–20% because those items are also coded to a system. They are not an extra fifth of the exam piled on top.

## The framework

A multisystem item has one circulating problem: a cytokine storm, a toxin, an embolus of clot or fat or amniotic fluid, or a missing cofactor. Ask what is in the blood.

Sepsis physiology, at the mechanism level, is distributive shock plus a source. Vessels dilate, fluid leaks, lactate rises when perfusion fails. Fever, a high white count, and a warm periphery are common and not required. The older adult can be hypothermic. You do not need the hour-by-hour bundle here. You need to recognize that the pipes are open and the tissues are underfed. The bundle itself is a Step 2 and Step 3 topic and follows the Surviving Sepsis Campaign.

Emboli that are not thrombus: fat after a long-bone fracture (respiratory distress, petechiae, neurologic change), amniotic fluid during delivery (sudden collapse, coagulopathy), air (a procedure in a vein above the heart), septic emboli (from a right-sided valve to the lung, or a left-sided valve to anywhere). Name the passenger.

Nutrition items are cofactors.

| Missing piece | The finding that is the mechanism |
| --- | --- |
| Thiamine (B1) | Impaired glucose oxidation in brain and heart. Wernicke is confusion, ataxia, eye-movement failure. Giving glucose without thiamine to a depleted person can precipitate it. |
| Niacin (B3) | Pellagra: dermatitis, diarrhea, dementia. Tryptophan can become niacin, so a tryptophan problem can look the same. |
| Cobalamin (B12) | Megaloblastic anemia plus demyelination of the cord (vibration and proprioception). Folate fixes the anemia and not the cord. |
| Folate | Megaloblastic anemia without the cord lesion. Needed early in neural-tube closure. |
| Pyridoxine (B6) | Used by many amino-acid reactions. Isoniazid increases the need. Neuropathy. |
| Vitamin C | Collagen cross-linking fails. Bleeding gums, poor wounds, corkscrew hairs. |
| Vitamin D | Calcium and phosphate absorption fail. Rickets or osteomalacia. |
| Vitamin K | Clotting factors II, VII, IX, X and proteins C and S cannot be carboxylated. |
| Vitamin A | Night vision and epithelial differentiation. Excess is teratogenic and causes high intracranial pressure. |
| Iron | Low heme. Fatigue, pale conjunctiva, pagophagia in the classic stem. |
| Iodine | Thyroid hormone cannot be made. Goiter. |

Energy: marasmus is calorie deficiency, wasted, no edema. Kwashiorkor is protein deficiency with edema and a fatty liver, sometimes with calories still present. Refeeding a starved person drives insulin up, phosphate and potassium into cells, and can stop the heart. The mechanism is the shift, not a protocol.

Burns and trauma, as physiology: capillary leak, evaporative water loss, and a hypermetabolic state after the first day. Inhalation injury is an airway problem before it is a skin-percent problem. Carbon monoxide and cyanide are the two gases of a closed-space fire. Carbon monoxide occupies hemoglobin. Cyanide stops the electron transport chain, so venous blood stays red and lactate rises.

### Thiamine deficiency

Thiamine is the cofactor for pyruvate dehydrogenase and transketolase. Glucose cannot be oxidized. Giving glucose alone to a depleted person can precipitate Wernicke encephalopathy. Wet beriberi is the heart.

**Vignette.** A man with alcohol use disorder is given intravenous glucose and becomes confused, with nystagmus and a wide gait.
**Reasoning.** The remaining thiamine was consumed by the glucose load. The diagnosis is Wernicke encephalopathy. The diagnosis is Thiamine deficiency.
**Pair.** Korsakoff is the memory gap that can follow, with confabulation. Wernicke is the acute triad.
**NBME trap.** Glucose first, thiamine never.
**Hook.** Eyes, gait, confusion. Thiamine with the glucose.

### Niacin and tryptophan

Niacin is NAD. Tryptophan can become niacin, so a tryptophan problem (Hartnup, a carcinoid that consumes tryptophan) can look the same. Isoniazid can interfere with the vitamin B6 that the pathway needs.

**Vignette.** A person on a corn-heavy diet has a necklace-like rash on sun-exposed skin, diarrhea, and a new confusion.
**Reasoning.** Three systems, one cofactor. The diagnosis is pellagra. The diagnosis is Niacin and tryptophan.
**Pair.** B12 deficiency has anemia and a cord finding, not a photosensitive rash.
**NBME trap.** Treating the dementia and never looking at the diet or the rash.
**Hook.** Diarrhea, dermatitis, dementia.

### B12 versus folate

Both are needed for DNA in the marrow. Only B12 maintains myelin in the cord. Folate will fix the anemia of B12 deficiency and leave the cord to worsen. That is why you do not treat a macrocytic anemia with folate alone until B12 is known.

**Vignette.** A person who had an ileal resection has large red cells and numb feet. Vibration is gone.
**Reasoning.** The terminal ileum was the B12 gate. The cord finding means this is not folate. The diagnosis is B12 versus folate.
**Pair.** Folate deficiency is diet, pregnancy, or a goat-milk story, and the cord is spared.
**NBME trap.** The anemia improved, so the vitamin was the right one. Check the feet.
**Hook.** Folate fixes the film. Only B12 fixes the cord.

### Vitamin C

Collagen cannot be hydroxylated, so the triple helix is weak. Vessels and wounds fail.

**Vignette.** An older adult who eats tea and toast has perifollicular hemorrhages and a bruise from a blood-pressure cuff.
**Reasoning.** The collagen in small vessels failed. The diagnosis is scurvy. The diagnosis is Vitamin C.
**Pair.** Vitamin K deficiency bleeds because clotting factors were not carboxylated, not because collagen failed. The PT is long in vitamin K deficiency and the story is different.
**NBME trap.** Iron for the bleeding gums.
**Hook.** Corkscrew hairs and a diet with no plants.

### Vitamin A, too little and too much

Vitamin A is the visual pigment and an epithelial differentiation signal. Deficiency blinds at night and dries the eye. Excess raises intracranial pressure and is teratogenic.

**Vignette.** A child with a limited diet cannot see in dim light. The conjunctiva looks dry.
**Reasoning.** The retina cannot regenerate rhodopsin. The diagnosis is vitamin A deficiency. The diagnosis is Vitamin A, too little and too much.
**Pair.** Excess is a different patient: supplements, bone pain, and a high intracranial pressure.
**NBME trap.** More vitamin A for a pregnant person who is already taking a high-dose supplement.
**Hook.** Night vision needs it. Too much hurts the embryo and the pressure.

### Vitamin D and vitamin K

Vitamin D raises calcium and phosphate absorption. Deficiency is soft bone. Vitamin K carboxylates factors II, VII, IX, X and proteins C and S. A warfarin start can drop protein C first.

**Vignette.** A breastfed infant who sees no sun has a soft skull and a rachitic rosary. Calcium is low.
**Reasoning.** The mineralization cofactor never arrived. The diagnosis is vitamin D deficiency rickets. The diagnosis is Vitamin D and vitamin K.
**Pair.** Vitamin K deficiency in a newborn is hemorrhagic disease, not soft bones.
**NBME trap.** A bisphosphonate for rickets.
**Hook.** D is bone mineral. K is the clotting carboxyl.

### Vitamin E and vitamin B6

Vitamin E deficiency can hemolyze and can injure the cord and the cerebellum. Pyridoxine is the cofactor isoniazid consumes. The neuropathy is preventable.

**Vignette.** A person treated for tuberculosis develops numb feet. The regimen includes isoniazid. B12 is normal.
**Reasoning.** The drug used up B6. The diagnosis is isoniazid neuropathy. The diagnosis is Vitamin E and vitamin B6.
**Pair.** B12 deficiency has large red cells. This neuropathy often does not.
**NBME trap.** Stopping the tuberculosis drugs forever instead of replacing the vitamin, when the vitamin was the missing piece. Confirm the current tuberculosis regimen before you change it.
**Hook.** Isoniazid eats B6.

### Iron, iodine, and zinc, briefly and exactly

Iron is heme. Iodine is thyroid hormone. Zinc is a cofactor in many enzymes including taste and healing. Each deficiency is the reaction that stopped, not a personality.

**Vignette.** A child in an inland region has a neck mass and slow growth. The mother had little access to iodized salt.
**Reasoning.** Thyroid hormone could not be iodinated. The diagnosis is iodine deficiency. The diagnosis is Iron, iodine, and zinc, briefly and exactly.
**Pair.** Iron deficiency is small red cells. Iodine deficiency is a thyroid. Do not swap the minerals.
**NBME trap.** Thyroid hormone replacement without asking why the gland grew, when the cause is a missing element in the diet.
**Hook.** Iodine is the goiter mineral. Iron is the red-cell mineral.

### Marasmus, kwashiorkor, and refeeding

Marasmus is calorie starvation. Kwashiorkor is protein deficiency with edema and a fatty liver, sometimes with calories still present. Refeeding drives insulin, and phosphate, potassium, and magnesium enter cells. The heart can stop.

**Vignette.** A severely underweight adult is fed aggressively. Two days later the phosphate is very low and the person is weak.
**Reasoning.** Insulin pushed phosphate into cells that were suddenly asked to rebuild. The diagnosis is refeeding hypophosphatemia. The diagnosis is Marasmus, kwashiorkor, and refeeding.
**Pair.** Simple starvation does not drop phosphate until the insulin arrives.
**NBME trap.** A high-calorie start with no electrolyte plan.
**Hook.** Insulin on an empty cell pulls phosphate in.

### Emboli that are not thrombus

Fat embolus, amniotic-fluid embolus, and air embolus are passengers. Fat embolus is a fracture plus a respiratory and a neurologic picture and a petechial rash. Amniotic fluid is peripartum collapse and coagulopathy. Air is a procedure in a vein above the heart.

**Vignette.** A day after a femur fracture, a patient is confused and hypoxic and has a rash on the chest. The leg ultrasound is not the whole story.
**Reasoning.** Marrow fat entered the blood. The diagnosis is fat embolism. The trap is only a venous thromboembolism, which does not cause the petechial rash. The diagnosis is Emboli that are not thrombus.
**Pair.** Thromboembolism is a clot from a vein. Fat embolism is marrow. The rash and the timing after instrumentation of bone are the split.
**NBME trap.** Heparin as the only thought after an orthopedic instrumentation when the rash is there.
**Hook.** Fracture, hypoxia, petechiae.

### Shock as a multisystem final common path

Shock is demand for perfusion that is not met. The Step 1 split is pump, tank, or pipes, drawn in the cardiovascular chapter. Lactate is a perfusion marker. It is not a diagnosis. Distributive shock is warm until the heart fails.

**Vignette.** A patient with a pneumonia is warm, wide-pulse-pressure, and confused. Lactate is high. The blood pressure is 100/40.
**Reasoning.** The pipes are open. Perfusion is failing even though the systolic number is not yet 70. The diagnosis is distributive shock, here from sepsis. The diagnosis is Shock as a multisystem final common path.
**Pair.** Cardiogenic shock is cold and the neck veins are full. This patient is warm and the diastolic pressure is low.
**NBME trap.** A normal systolic pressure used to send the person to the floor.
**Hook.** Lactate and the mental status, not the systolic number alone.

### Glycogen and lysosomal diseases, only the classic stems

Von Gierke is glucose-6-phosphatase. Glycogen cannot become free glucose. Hypoglycemia, a large liver, and a high lactate. Pompe is lysosomal acid maltase. The heart is huge and the tongue may be. McArdle is muscle phosphorylase. Cramps and a burgundy urine after a sprint, and no lactate rise. Tay-Sachs is hexosaminidase A. A cherry-red spot and no hepatosplenomegaly. Niemann-Pick has the spot and a large liver and spleen. Gaucher is glucocerebrosidase. A crumpled-paper cytoplasm and bone crises.

**Vignette.** An infant has a large tongue and a large heart and profound weakness. Glycogen is inside lysosomes.
**Reasoning.** The lysosomal enzyme that breaks glycogen is missing. The diagnosis is Pompe disease. The diagnosis is Glycogen and lysosomal diseases, only the classic stems.
**Pair.** Von Gierke has hypoglycemia and a liver. Pompe has a heart. McArdle has a muscle and a flat lactate.
**NBME trap.** Calling every large liver Von Gierke. Ask about the heart and the sugar.
**Hook.** Von Gierke is sugar. Pompe is the heart. McArdle is the sprint.

## Stem craft

One missing cofactor, or one passenger in the blood.

The vignette withholds the name. The reasoning earns it.

**Vignette 1.** Confusion, nystagmus, and a wide gait after a glucose infusion in a malnourished patient.
**Reasoning.** Wernicke encephalopathy. Thiamine was the cofactor the glucose used up. Withdrawal is a sympathetic storm without this eye finding. The trap is more glucose.

**Vignette 2.** Large red cells and numb feet after an ileal resection. Folate was already replaced. The anemia improved and the feet did not.
**Reasoning.** B12. Folate fixes the marrow and not the myelin. The trap is declaring victory because the hemoglobin rose.

**Vignette 3.** Corkscrew hairs and bleeding gums on a tea-and-toast diet. The PT is normal.
**Reasoning.** Scurvy. Vitamin K deficiency would prolong the PT. The collagen, not the clotting factor, failed.

**Vignette 4.** A day after a femur fracture: confusion, hypoxia, and a petechial rash on the chest.
**Reasoning.** Fat embolism. A venous thrombus does not paint the chest with petechiae. The trap is only heparin.

**Vignette 5.** An infant with a huge heart and a huge tongue. Glycogen is inside lysosomes. The blood sugar is not the headline.
**Reasoning.** Pompe, lysosomal acid maltase. Von Gierke is hypoglycemia and a liver. McArdle is a sprint and a flat lactate. The trap is one glycogen disease.

## Filing the stem

A last set of stems that are easy to misfile.

The vignette withholds the name. The reasoning earns it.

**Vignette 1.** A child sprints and the muscles cramp. The urine is dark. Lactate does not rise.
**Reasoning.** Muscle phosphorylase is missing. McArdle disease. The muscle cannot break glycogen, so lactate does not rise and myoglobin spills. Von Gierke would be a liver and a low glucose between meals, not a sprint.

**Vignette 2.** A cherry-red spot and no enlarged liver or spleen. The infant stops meeting milestones.
**Reasoning.** Tay-Sachs. Hexosaminidase A. Niemann-Pick has the spot and a large liver and spleen. The organomegaly is the split.

**Vignette 3.** Crumpled-paper macrophages, bone pain, and a large spleen. The child is not blind.
**Reasoning.** Gaucher disease. Glucocerebrosidase. The bone crises are not osteomyelitis until proven otherwise, but the cell is the storage disease.

**Vignette 4.** Diarrhea, a rash on sun-exposed skin, and dementia. The diet has almost no niacin and little tryptophan.
**Reasoning.** Pellagra. A carcinoid that consumes tryptophan can mimic it. The trap is a primary dementia when the rash and the diarrhea are in the stem.

**Vignette 5.** A refeeding phosphate of 1.0 and weakness two days after a starved patient is fed aggressively.
**Reasoning.** Insulin drove phosphate into cells. The body was not phosphate-rich. The trap is more calories. Replace the phosphate and slow the feed.

## Checklist

- [ ] One circulating cause for a multisystem stem.
- [ ] B12 hits the cord. Folate does not.
- [ ] Thiamine before, or with, glucose in a person who may be depleted.
- [ ] Vitamin K factors are the carboxylated ones, including protein C and S.
- [ ] Refeeding drops phosphate.

## Common traps

- Treating nutrition percentages as “15–20% of your study time on vitamins only.” Those items are double-coded to organs. Study the deficiency inside the organ it breaks.
- Calling every edema kwashiorkor. Heart, kidney, and liver still exist.
- Quoting a Parkland fluid number from memory as if it were physiology. The mechanism is capillary leak and evaporation. The formula is a clinical tool and it has been revised in practice. Do not treat a memorized milliliter as a law.
- Forgetting that protein C and S are vitamin K–dependent, so a warfarin start can briefly tilt toward clotting in the veins if protein C falls first.

## Practice this in the Qbank

Multisystem items are “what is in the blood.” Nutrition items are “which reaction stopped.” Work them as pairs with the organ chapter they belong to. Feed cards on vitamin names are useful after the finding, not before. Sepsis bundles and burn resuscitation are in the clinical books.

# Biochemistry and genetics

## Framework

This chapter is the energy map and the inheritance map. The feed cards name one enzyme. Here you decide which pathway is on, which step cannot run backward, and which inheritance pattern the pedigree is forcing.

**Fed versus fasting**

| State | Liver is doing | What turns it on |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Just ate carbohydrate | Glycolysis, glycogen synthesis, fatty-acid synthesis | Insulin. PFK-1 is the committed step of glycolysis. ATP and citrate slow it. |
| A few hours hungry | Glycogenolysis, then gluconeogenesis | Glucagon lowers fructose-2,6-bisphosphate, so PFK-1 slows and the phosphatase of gluconeogenesis speeds. |
| Prolonged fast | Ketone production, gluconeogenesis from amino acids | Oxaloacetate is used to make glucose, so acetyl-CoA from fat goes to ketones. |

Gluconeogenesis is not glycolysis run backward. The irreversible steps need pyruvate carboxylase (biotin), PEPCK, fructose-1,6-bisphosphatase, and glucose-6-phosphatase. Muscle has no glucose-6-phosphatase, so muscle glycogen cannot feed the blood glucose.

**A poison or a block**

| Finding | Mechanism |
| --- | --- |
| Oxygen use up, ATP down, heat up | Uncoupling. The chain runs. The gradient becomes heat. |
| Oxygen use and ATP both down | The chain itself is blocked. |
| Hemolysis after an oxidant, bite cells | Red-cell G6PD cannot refill NADPH. The oldest cells lyse first, so an assay that day can look normal. |
| Lactate, pyruvate, and alanine all high after sugar | Pyruvate dehydrogenase is not moving pyruvate into the cycle. More glucose makes more pyruvate. |

**Inheritance you can read off a pedigree**

| Pattern | What you see | Classic use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Autosomal dominant | Every generation, both sexes | Structural proteins. One bad copy shows. |
| Autosomal recessive | Skips, both sexes, often enzymes | Both copies must fail. Carriers are usually well. |
| X-linked recessive | Boys affected, no male-to-male | Carrier mother. Ask who the mother is. |
| Mitochondrial | From the mother to all children | Heteroplasmy. Severity varies in one family. |

Imprinting is not ordinary dominance. Prader-Willi and Angelman are the same region with a different parent's copy silenced. A triplet repeat that grows in the next generation is anticipation. Name the repeat disease by the tract it hits, not by a list you half-remember.

## Checklist

- [ ] Say which hormone is high before you name an enzyme.
- [ ] Irreversible glycolysis steps are the only ones gluconeogenesis replaces.
- [ ] G6PD assay during the hemolysis can miss the diagnosis.
- [ ] Mitochondrial disease comes from the mother. Fathers do not pass it.
- [ ] A dominant pedigree with incomplete penetrance is still dominant.

## Common traps

- Calling hexokinase the committed step. It is regulated. PFK-1 commits the glucose to glycolysis.
- Giving more carbohydrate to a pyruvate-dehydrogenase block.
- Reading an X-linked pedigree as autosomal because one girl is mildly affected. Ask about skewed inactivation only after the pedigree is X-linked.

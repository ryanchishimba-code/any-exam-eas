# Immunology and microbiology

## Framework

Step 1 microbes are mechanisms with a gram stain attached. Learn the toxin, the missing defense, and the drug's target. A list of every agar plate will not move a score.

**Hypersensitivity, by what actually happens**

| Type | Mechanism | The finding the stem loves |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I | IgE, mast cell, immediate | Anaphylaxis, asthma, wheal |
| II | Antibody against a cell or receptor | Hemolysis, Goodpasture, Graves, myasthenia |
| III | Immune complexes | Serum sickness, Arthus, many vasculitides |
| IV | T cells, delayed | Contact dermatitis, TB skin test, transplant rejection that is cellular |

**Host defense, matched to the organism**

| Defense that failed | Organisms that appear |
| --- | --- |
| Neutrophils | Candida, Aspergillus, many bacteria. Staph in CGD. |
| T cells | Viruses, fungi, Pneumocystis, intracellular bacteria |
| Antibody or complement | Encapsulated bacteria. Neisseria when the terminal complement is gone. |
| Spleen | Encapsulated bacteria, malaria more severe |

A toxin that ADP-ribosylates EF-2 is diphtheria or exotoxin A. A toxin that ADP-ribosylates Gi is pertussis. Cholera ADP-ribosylates Gs, so chloride and water pour into the gut. Superantigens cross-link MHC and a T-cell receptor outside the groove. That is toxic shock, not a normal peptide response.

Vaccines: a toxoid is an inactivated toxin (tetanus, diphtheria). A conjugate vaccine hands a polysaccharide to a T cell so a child under two can respond. Live vaccines are not for a patient who cannot control a living organism. Confirm the current CDC schedule before you advise a real patient. The exam wants the mechanism, not this year's age cut.

## Checklist

- [ ] Name the hypersensitivity type from the timing and the cell, not from the disease list.
- [ ] Encapsulated organisms plus a missing spleen or missing antibody.
- [ ] Terminal complement and Neisseria.
- [ ] Toxoid versus conjugate versus live, by the immune problem each solves.
- [ ] Superantigen is not processed peptide.

## Common traps

- Calling every rash type IV. Immediate wheal and anaphylaxis are type I.
- Picking a live vaccine for a T-cell defect because "vaccines are prevention."
- Treating a toxin disease as if killing the organism, after the toxin is already made, is the whole answer. Sometimes you need the antitoxin.

# Multisystem processes

## Framework

Shock, inflammation, and the processes that hit more than one organ sit in their own outline range. They are physiology with a blood pressure attached.

**Shock by the numbers you can reason**

| Type | Pump | Vessels | What you feel |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Hypovolemic | Fast, weak volume | Constricted | Cold. Flat neck veins. Bleeding or dehydration. |
| Cardiogenic | The pump failed | Constricted | Cold. Full neck veins. Pulmonary edema. |
| Distributive | Often fast | Dilated | Warm sepsis is the classic. Pressure is low because the pipes are open. |
| Obstructive | The pump cannot fill or eject | — | Tamponade, tension pneumothorax, a massive pulmonary embolus. |

Inflammation over time: neutrophils first, then macrophages. Chronic inflammation brings lymphocytes and fibrosis. A granuloma is macrophages that became epithelioid because they could not digest the target. Caseation is the necrosis pattern of tuberculosis on exams, not of every granuloma.

Edema is high hydrostatic pressure, low oncotic pressure, leaky vessels, or a blocked lymphatic. Heart failure is hydrostatic. Nephrotic syndrome and liver failure are oncotic. Infection is leaky. A filarial or surgical lymph block is lymphatic.

Amyloid is misfolded protein in tissue. The exam stain is Congo red with apple-green birefringence. AA is chronic inflammation. AL is a plasma-cell clone. Do not treat them as the same disease.

## Checklist

- [ ] Cold and full neck veins is not hypovolemia.
- [ ] Warm shock is distributive until proven otherwise.
- [ ] Neutrophils first. Macrophages stay.
- [ ] Name the edema force.
- [ ] Congo red is amyloid. The letter (AA or AL) is the cause.

## Common traps

- Pouring fluid into cardiogenic shock because the pressure is low.
- Calling every granuloma tuberculosis.
- Treating lymphatic edema with a diuretic as if it were heart failure.

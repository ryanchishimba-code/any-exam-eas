# Step 1 questions people ask

> **Study aid only.** Short answers. Where a fact can change, the official page wins. This book does not promise a pass.

## What this chapter covers

The questions that waste a week of studying if you leave them fuzzy: what the exam is, what this book is not, and how it fits the question bank and the feed.

## The framework

If an answer below and a page on usmle.org disagree, use usmle.org. These books were written to the specifications and Bulletin available while they were drafted. USMLE states that ranges and standards can change.

| Question | Answer |
| --- | --- |
| Who owns the exam? | The USMLE program is a joint program of the FSMB and the NBME. AnyExamEasy is not part of either, and is not endorsed by them. |
| Is Step 1 a basic-science quiz? | No. The specifications say most items ask you to apply a principle to a patient, a graph, or a specimen. |
| Why is there no “general principles” block in the system table? | USMLE moved that material into the organ systems. Biochemistry still shows up. It shows up inside a disease. |
| Do the percents add to 100? | System ranges are built to describe a form. Discipline ranges overlap on purpose and add to more than 100%. Nutrition is double-coded to a system. |
| What score do I need? | This book will not tell you. The Bulletin says the standard can change, that passing is not a fixed percent of examinees, and that the percent correct that corresponds to passing varies by form. Look up the current standard on usmle.org. |
| Can I take Step 2 CK first? | If you are eligible, the Bulletin allows Step 1 and Step 2 CK in either order. Step 3 comes after both are passed. |
| How is this book different from the feed? | The feed is one association per card. This book is the mechanism those associations sit on. Neither replaces the question bank. |
| How is this book different from a question explanation? | An explanation answers one stem. A chapter gives you the table you can reuse on the next stem. |
| Will following this plan make me pass? | No one can honestly say that. Use it to aim your hours at the published outline and at your misses. |
| What if a chapter and a guideline disagree? | For Step 1, prefer the mechanism. For a number you might use on a patient, prefer the current guideline and your supervisor. These chapters are study aids. |
| Where are the doses? | Mostly not here. A dose you memorize wrong is worse than a mechanism you can rebuild. Doses that matter clinically are in the Step 2 and Step 3 books, tied to a named source, and still need a human check. |

## Checklist

- [ ] You can say, in one sentence, what Step 1 measures.
- [ ] You know which two organizations own the exam.
- [ ] You are not carrying a made-up passing percent.
- [ ] You know the order: chapter, questions, then cards.
- [ ] You will re-check the Bulletin the month you test.

## Common traps

- Buying a sense of safety from a finished chapter. Fluency at a table is not fluency on a mixed block.
- Treating AnyExamEasy, a commercial book, or a bank as if it were the USMLE program. They are not.
- Using Step 2 management answers on a Step 1 mechanism item. The task is different. Read the stem’s verb.
- Ignoring a small published range (biostatistics, communication) because it is not an organ.

## Practice this in the Qbank

If you are asking “am I ready,” the evidence you have is your performance on mixed, timed blocks that you have not seen before. A percent on a block is information about that block. It is not a forecast. Use misses to pick the next chapter. Use the feed only to rebuild an association you already understand.

# Exam day

> **Study aid only.** Logistics from the published Bulletin, plus a way to spend attention. Not a promise about stamina, score, or outcome.

## What this chapter covers

What the published Step 1 day looks like, how to spend breaks, and what to do with a stem that feels unfair. Confirm every time and rule on usmle.org the week you test. This page will be wrong the day the Bulletin changes, and the Bulletin wins.

## The framework

The current published day is one session of about 8 hours, about 280 items, fourteen blocks of 30 minutes, no more than 20 items in a block, at least 55 minutes of break, and a 5-minute optional tutorial. Time you do not use in the tutorial or in a block can move to the break. That is the whole logistics model. Anything more specific (what you may bring, what ID works, how a testing irregularity is reported) is the Bulletin and the testing vendor’s rules, not this book.

| Moment | A useful default |
| --- | --- |
| Tutorial | Skip it if you have already seen the interface in the official practice format. The minutes become break. |
| Each block | First pass the items you can answer from the mechanism. Mark the ones that need a drawing. |
| A stuck item | Name the normal process in the stem. If you still cannot, pick the option that breaks that process and move. |
| Breaks | Eat and move on a schedule you practiced. Do not spend the whole break on one item you already submitted. |
| Last block | Same method. Fatigue makes people add facts the stem did not give. |

You may take Step 1 and Step 2 CK in either order if you are eligible. Step 3 requires both to be passed. That sequence is a Bulletin rule. It is not advice about when you personally should sit.

## If something goes wrong

The Bulletin’s irregularity process has two parts: tell the test-center staff that day, and write the USMLE program within the stated number of days. A comment on the post-exam survey is not that notice. Read the current Bulletin for the address and the day count. Do not rely on a number you remember from a forum.

If you are ill before you start, the Bulletin’s own advice is to consider rescheduling. This book will not tell you to “push through.”

## Checklist

- [ ] You have read the current Bulletin section on the test day, not a blog summary.
- [ ] You have used the official free practice interface so the tutorial is optional.
- [ ] Your break plan is written in minutes, and it includes food.
- [ ] You know that early finishes become break, so you do not need to sit and stare after you are done with a block.
- [ ] You are not carrying a target percent into the room. The passing standard is not a number this book will give you.

## Common traps

- Re-reading an entire block “to be safe” and donating your break.
- Changing answers without a new reading of the stem. Most late changes replace a mechanism with a vibe.
- Studying new material the morning of the exam. Sleep and a normal meal beat a new table.
- Treating a practice-test percent as a promise. Forms differ. The Bulletin says so.

## Practice this in the Qbank

Do at least one timed block under the real constraint: 30 minutes, no pausing, then a short break. The point is the clock, not a score to post. The feed is a bad exam-morning activity if it makes you collect new exceptions. If you use it, use cards you already know.

# Respiratory mechanisms

> **Study aid only.** Ventilation, perfusion, and the curve. Oxygen devices and ventilator settings for a sick patient are later Steps.

## What this chapter covers

How air and blood meet, why A-a gradient separates lung failure from hypoventilation, and the obstructive-versus-restrictive patterns. Respiratory disease is grouped with renal disease at about 11–15% of Step 1. This chapter is the lung half.

## The framework

Arterial oxygen can be low for five reasons: low inspired oxygen, hypoventilation, diffusion limitation, shunt, and V/Q mismatch. Only the first two have a normal A-a gradient. Shunt does not fully correct with supplemental oxygen, because the blood that never saw alveoli stays mixed in. That is the practical split.

| Pattern | FEV1/FVC | What else moves |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Obstruction | Low | Residual volume up. Air trapping. |
| Restriction | Normal or high | Total lung capacity down. The bellows is small. |

Compliance is how much volume you get per unit of pressure. Fibrosis and edema make the lung stiff (compliance down). Emphysema destroys recoil (compliance up, airway collapse on exhale). Resistance is the airway. Asthma and chronic bronchitis are resistance problems. Emphysema is a recoil problem that behaves like obstruction because airways collapse.

The hemoglobin curve shifts right (easier unloading) with heat, acid, CO2, and 2,3-BPG. It shifts left when those fall, and with fetal hemoglobin and carbon monoxide. Carbon monoxide occupies sites and also shifts the remaining curve left, so the dissolved oxygen looks falsely reassuring on a standard pulse oximeter that cannot tell carboxyhemoglobin from oxyhemoglobin. A co-oximeter can.

Pulmonary hypertension is high pressure in the lung arteries. Group the causes by where they sit: arterial disease, left-heart backup, lung disease and hypoxia, chronic clot, and miscellaneous. Hypoxic vasoconstriction is local and useful. Global hypoxia raises the pressure the right heart must beat.

## Gas exchange details that earn points

- Dead space is ventilated and not perfused. Embolus creates dead space. The CO2 often stays normal because the patient breathes more.
- Shunt is perfused and not ventilated. Airway occlusion, dense consolidation.
- Zone 1 of the lung, when it exists, is dead-space-like (alveolar pressure exceeds arterial). Base gets more blood and more ventilation. Blood flow increases down the lung more than ventilation does, so the base has a lower V/Q.
- Fetal circulation: the placenta is the oxygen source, pulmonary resistance is high, and the foramen ovale and ductus arteriosus bypass the lungs. First breaths drop pulmonary resistance. The ductus feels oxygen and closes functionally.

## High-yield atlas

The lung question is almost always one of five reasons the arterial oxygen is low, or a curve that moved, or a pattern of obstruction versus restriction. Name which of those it is before you name a disease.

### Five reasons the oxygen is low

| Cause | A-a gradient | Does extra oxygen fix it | A stem |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Low inspired oxygen | Normal | Yes | Altitude. The lung is fine. The air is thin |
| Hypoventilation | Normal | Yes | A opioid, a neuromuscular disease, or a chest that will not move. CO2 is high |
| Diffusion limitation | High | Usually yes, with time and oxygen | Fibrosis, or exercise when the blood races past a thick membrane |
| V/Q mismatch | High | Usually yes | Most lung diseases. Airway obstruction, a moderate pneumonia |
| Shunt | High | No, not fully | Blood that never saw an alveolus. A dense consolidation, an arteriovenous malformation, an intracardiac right-to-left shunt |

The A-a gradient asks whether the lung itself is failing at exchange. Hypoventilation and thin air do not raise it. Everything else does. Shunt is the one that refuses supplemental oxygen, because the shunted blood never touches the oxygen you provided.

**Vignette.** After a surgery, a patient is sleepy from pain medicine. The arterial CO2 is high. The A-a gradient is normal. Oxygen by mask raises the arterial oxygen.
**Reasoning.** The lung is exchanging. The drive is low, so alveolar ventilation fell and CO2 rose. The diagnosis is hypoventilation. The trap is pulmonary embolism, which raises the A-a gradient because it creates dead space and mismatch. A normal gradient sends you away from a lung-parenchyma disease.

**Hook.** Normal gradient: air or drive. High gradient: the lung. Will not correct: shunt.

### Dead space, shunt, and the zones

Dead space is ventilated and not perfused. The anatomic dead space is the conducting airway. Alveolar dead space is an embolus or a zone where alveolar pressure exceeds capillary pressure. Shunt is perfused and not ventilated. A peanut in a bronchus, a dense pneumonia, and a right-to-left cardiac shunt are shunts.

Blood flow increases from the top of the lung to the base more steeply than ventilation does. The base therefore has a lower V/Q. An embolus raises V/Q toward dead space. An airway block drops V/Q toward shunt.

**Vignette.** A sudden pleuritic pain and a clear chest film. The arterial CO2 is normal or low because the person is breathing hard. The dead-space fraction is up.
**Reasoning.** Ventilation continues in alveoli whose blood supply stopped. The person can still blow off CO2 by hyperventilating the rest of the lung, so a normal CO2 does not clear an embolus. The diagnosis is pulmonary embolus physiology. The trap is “CO2 is normal, so the lungs are fine.”

### The hemoglobin curve

Right shift unloads oxygen to tissues: heat, acid, high CO2, and 2,3-BPG. Left shift holds oxygen: the opposite of those, fetal hemoglobin, and carbon monoxide. Carbon monoxide also occupies sites, so the content falls even when a two-wavelength pulse oximeter looks falsely reassuring. A co-oximeter tells carboxyhemoglobin from oxyhemoglobin.

Fetal hemoglobin is left-shifted on purpose. The fetus holds oxygen that the mother’s hemoglobin is willing to release. That is why a left shift in the fetus is not a disease.

**Vignette.** A person pulled from a closed-space fire has a headache, a cherry-colored flush, and a pulse oximeter reading that is not as low as the person looks. Lactate is high.
**Reasoning.** Carbon monoxide occupies hemoglobin and shifts the remainder left, so tissues starve while a standard oximeter is fooled. The diagnosis is carbon monoxide poisoning. The trap is calling the oximeter the truth. Cyanide, also from a fire, stops the electron chain so venous blood stays red and lactate rises. They can travel together. The split is a co-oximeter versus a story of a blocked chain that oxygen cannot feed.

**Pair.** Carbon monoxide occupies the carrier. Cyanide blocks the chain. Both make oxygen useless. The test that sees carbon monoxide does not see cyanide.

### Obstruction versus restriction, and the diseases that wear them

| Pattern | FEV1/FVC | Lung volumes | Compliance or resistance | The diseases |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Obstruction | Low | Residual volume high | Resistance high, or recoil lost | Asthma, chronic bronchitis, emphysema, a foreign body |
| Restriction | Normal or high | Total lung capacity low | Compliance low if the lung is stiff. The bellows can also be the chest wall or the nerve | Fibrosis, edema, a neuromuscular disease, a pleural peel, obesity |

Emphysema destroys alveolar walls and the elastic recoil that holds small airways open on exhale. Compliance is high. Airways collapse. Chronic bronchitis is a conducting-airway and mucus disease. Resistance is high. Recoil can be intact. Both obstruct airflow. Asthma is reversible bronchial hyperreactivity. The obstruction comes and goes, and the diffusion capacity is usually preserved, unlike emphysema.

**Vignette.** A long-time smoker has a quiet chest, a barreled look, and a low diffusion capacity. He purses his lips to exhale.
**Reasoning.** Lost surface area drops the diffusion capacity. Pursed lips keep a back-pressure that stents collapsing airways. The diagnosis is emphysema. The trap is chronic bronchitis, which is the noisy, mucus-heavy phenotype and does not drop diffusion the same way unless emphysema is mixed in. Many real people have both. The stem’s diffusion number is the split.

**Vignette.** A young adult wheezes at night and after cold air. Between attacks the volumes are normal. Diffusion capacity is normal.
**Reasoning.** Reversible, trigger-linked obstruction with a preserved surface area is asthma. The diagnosis is asthma. The trap is emphysema because of the wheeze. Age, reversibility, and diffusion separate them.

### Pulmonary hypertension and the right ventricle

Hypoxic vasoconstriction is useful in one blocked airway and harmful when the whole lung is hypoxic. Chronic hypoxia, left-heart backup, chronic clot, and primary arterial disease all raise the pressure the right ventricle must beat. The right ventricle fails by backing up into the neck veins and the liver.

| Group, in plain words | The pressure comes from |
| --- | --- |
| Arterial disease | The lung arteries themselves |
| Left heart | Backup through the pulmonary veins |
| Lung disease and hypoxia | The vasoconstriction and the destroyed bed |
| Chronic clot | Blocked arteries that organized |
| Everything else | A named cause the stem will give, such as a vasculitis |

**NBME trap.** They put a left-heart story (orthopnea, a mitral murmur) and ask the group. Students pick “primary pulmonary hypertension” because the right heart is loud. The left heart is the cause.

### Airway defenses and the organisms that follow a pattern

This is mechanism, not a regimen. The organism follows the host.

| Host | The organism the stem wants | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A previously healthy adult, a lobar consolidation | Streptococcus pneumoniae | The encapsulated diplococcus |
| A young adult, a walking pneumonia, cold agglutinins | Mycoplasma | No cell wall. A beta-lactam cannot see it |
| An older smoker, hyponatremia, a gastrointestinal complaint, a contaminated water aerosol | Legionella | Inside cells. A special medium. The urine antigen is the test the later books use |
| After influenza, a cavitary or a necrotizing pneumonia | Staphylococcus aureus | The virus cleared the epithelium |
| Alcohol use, a currant-jelly sputum | Klebsiella | An upper-lobe aspiration picture in the classic stem |
| A ventilated patient or a person with cystic fibrosis, a green pigment | Pseudomonas | The water organism with the EF-2 toxin |
| Neutropenia, a halo on the scan | Aspergillus | The narrow-branching mold |
| AIDS, a dry cough, a high lactate dehydrogenase, ground glass | Pneumocystis | A fungus that T cells used to hold down. The treatment threshold is a CD4 number you confirm in the HIV guideline, not a number you invent |

**Vignette.** A young adult has a week of a dry cough, a patchy film, and hemolysis when the blood is cold. He can still go to class.
**Reasoning.** A mild pneumonia with cold agglutinins is an organism with no wall. The diagnosis is Mycoplasma. The trap is choosing a beta-lactam, which needs a wall. The later books name the macrolide or the related drug. Here the point is the missing wall.

### Neonatal lung and the vessels around it

A newborn’s first breaths drop pulmonary resistance. The ductus arteriosus, which shunted blood away from the fluid-filled lung, feels the oxygen and closes functionally. If the lung stays stiff and hypoxic, the resistance stays high and the shunt continues: persistent pulmonary hypertension of the newborn.

Surfactant, from type II cells, lowers surface tension so alveoli do not collapse at the end of expiration. Prematurity lacks it. The film is a ground-glass, low-volume lung. Maternal diabetes delays surfactant. A cesarean without labor also raises the risk of retained fluid, which is a different, usually faster-clearing disease (transient tachypnea).

**Vignette.** A baby born at 28 weeks grunts and retracts. The film shows small lungs with a diffuse haze. There is no focal lobar consolidation.
**Reasoning.** Immature type II cells, high surface tension, collapse. The diagnosis is respiratory distress from surfactant deficiency. The trap is meconium aspiration, which is a term or post-term baby with a patchy, overexpanded film and a history of stained fluid.

**Pair.** Surfactant deficiency is a small, hazy lung in a premature baby. Meconium is a patchy, obstructing, often hyperexpanded lung in a stressed term baby. Transient tachypnea is retained fluid and clears.

### Pleura and the chest wall

A pleural effusion is transudate (pressure or protein: heart failure, nephrotic syndrome, cirrhosis) or exudate (inflammation: infection, cancer, embolus, autoimmune disease). Light’s criteria are the lab split used on Step 2. On Step 1, the mechanism is enough: high hydrostatic pressure versus a leaky, cell-rich pleura.

Tension pneumothorax shifts the mediastinum away from the air and kinks venous return. The diagnosis is clinical when the person is crashing. A simple pneumothorax has air but not that shift and not that shock.

**Vignette.** After a central line attempt, a patient becomes hypotensive. The trachea is pushed to the opposite side. Breath sounds are absent on the side of the procedure. The neck veins are up.
**Reasoning.** Air under pressure is compressing the vena cava and the other lung. The diagnosis is tension pneumothorax. The trap is tamponade, which also has distended veins and hypotension but does not move the trachea or silence one hemithorax.

### More worked vignettes

**Vignette.** A person at high altitude hyperventilates. The arterial CO2 is low. The A-a gradient is normal. The oxygen is low but appropriate for the altitude.
**Reasoning.** The inspired oxygen fell. The lung did not break. Respiratory alkalosis is the compensation, and the kidneys will dump bicarbonate over days. The diagnosis is hypoxic hypoxemia from atmosphere, not from shunt.

**Vignette.** A patient with a massive pleural effusion on the left has a dull percussion note and a trachea pushed away from the fluid. He is uncomfortable but not in shock.
**Reasoning.** Fluid takes space and shifts the mediastinum away, like a tension pneumothorax, but the percussion is dull, not drum-like, and breath sounds are gone for a different reason. The diagnosis is a large effusion. The trap is pneumothorax because of the shift. Percussion splits them.

**Vignette.** Clubbing, a chronic productive cough, and a film with tram-track bronchi. The person had a severe pneumonia years ago.
**Reasoning.** Permanently dilated bronchi that pool sputum. The diagnosis is bronchiectasis. The trap is chronic bronchitis, which does not focally dilate bronchi this way. Cystic fibrosis and a post-infectious injury are the classic soils.

## Stem craft

Gas exchange stems that hide the mechanism in a number.

The vignette withholds the name. The reasoning earns it.

**Vignette 1.** FEV1/FVC is low and the residual volume is high. Diffusion capacity is low. The patient has smoked for decades.
**Reasoning.** Obstruction with lost surface area is emphysema. Asthma keeps diffusion. The trap is calling every low ratio asthma because the patient wheezed once.

**Vignette 2.** FEV1/FVC is high. Total lung capacity is low. The patient has a neuromuscular disease and a weak cough.
**Reasoning.** The bellows, not the airway, is the problem. Restriction from the chest wall or the nerve. Fibrosis is also restrictive but the lung itself is stiff and the diffusion capacity falls. The stem’s weak cough points at the pump.

**Vignette 3.** A right shift of the curve in a febrile, acidotic patient.
**Reasoning.** Heat and acid unload oxygen. That is useful at the tissue. A left shift would hold oxygen on the hemoglobin. Carbon monoxide shifts left and also occupies sites. The trap is saying the patient is better because saturation looks acceptable.

**Vignette 4.** Zone 1 physiology appears after a hemorrhage. Alveolar pressure exceeds capillary pressure at the apex.
**Reasoning.** Those alveoli are dead space. The base still has blood. The trap is calling the apex a shunt. Shunt is blood without air. This is air without blood.

**Vignette 5.** A baby of a mother with diabetes is born at 37 weeks and grunts. The film is hazy and small.
**Reasoning.** Insulin delays surfactant. The mechanism is the same deficiency as prematurity even though the dates are late preterm. The trap is transient tachypnea only, which is retained fluid and clears faster and is more common after a cesarean without labor.

## Another pass on the stems

More gas-exchange and pattern stems.

The vignette withholds the name. The reasoning earns it.

**Vignette 1.** A patient with pulmonary fibrosis has a normal FEV1/FVC ratio, a low total lung capacity, and a low diffusion capacity. He is not a smoker.
**Reasoning.** The lung is stiff and the surface is gone. Restriction from parenchymal disease, not from a weak chest wall. A neuromuscular restriction keeps diffusion closer to normal until the lung itself is sick. The trap is calling a high ratio asthma.

**Vignette 2.** Oxygen saturation falls during exercise and recovers at rest. The A-a gradient is high. The membrane is thick on the biopsy.
**Reasoning.** Blood crosses a thick membrane too fast to equilibrate when cardiac output rises. Diffusion limitation. At rest there may be enough time. The trap is calling every exertional desaturation a shunt. Shunt does not fully correct even at rest with oxygen.

**Vignette 3.** A person with a known right-to-left cardiac shunt is given 100% oxygen. The arterial oxygen rises only a little.
**Reasoning.** Shunted blood never sees the alveolus. That is why oxygen fails. V/Q mismatch usually yields. The trap is turning the oxygen up and declaring the lung the only problem.

**Vignette 4.** Chronic bronchitis phenotype: a noisy chest, a productive cough for years, and a diffusion capacity that is relatively preserved.
**Reasoning.** The lesion is the airway and the gland, not the alveolar wall. Emphysema destroys the wall and drops diffusion. Many patients have both. The stem that preserves diffusion is asking for the airway disease.

## Checklist

- [ ] Normal A-a means the lung is exchanging. Look at drive or inspired oxygen.
- [ ] Shunt resists oxygen. V/Q mismatch usually yields to it.
- [ ] FEV1/FVC low is obstruction. Lung volumes tell you restriction.
- [ ] Carbon monoxide fools a two-wavelength pulse oximeter.
- [ ] Right shift unloads oxygen. Left shift holds it.

## Common traps

- Calling every low oxygen a shunt. Calculate the company the gradient keeps.
- Treating emphysema and chronic bronchitis as the same lesion. One loses recoil. One is airway and mucus. Both can obstruct airflow.
- Forgetting fetal hemoglobin’s left shift. The fetus holds oxygen. That is the point.
- Quoting an oxygen saturation target. That is a clinical guideline, not this chapter.

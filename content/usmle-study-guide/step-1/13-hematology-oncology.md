# Hematology and oncology mechanisms

> **Study aid only.** How a cell line fails and how a clone escapes. Chemotherapy regimens and transfusion thresholds are later Steps.

## What this chapter covers

Anemia as a production-versus-destruction problem, coagulation as two linked paths, and the cancer mechanisms that are just cell-cycle brakes removed. Blood and immune content is about 9–13% of Step 1. Immune defense was the microbiology chapter. This is the marrow and the clot.

## The framework

Anemia is three bins. Not enough production. Lost or destroyed. Diluted or mismeasured. The reticulocyte count tells you whether the marrow is answering. A low reticulocyte count in anemia means the marrow is not answering. A high one means it is, and you should look for bleeding or hemolysis.

| Clue | Lean toward |
| --- | --- |
| Small cells | Iron deficiency, thalassemia, anemia of chronic disease (sometimes) |
| Large cells | B12 or folate, some drugs, liver disease, marrow failure syndromes, hemolysis with a reticulocytosis |
| Schistocytes | Mechanical destruction: microangiopathy, a bad valve |
| Spherocytes | Membrane loss: hereditary spherocytosis, immune hemolysis |
| Target cells | Hemoglobin problem, liver disease, a spleen that is missing |
| Bite cells, Heinz bodies | Oxidative damage. G6PD when the oxidant hit. |

Hemolysis is intravascular (hemoglobinuria, very low haptoglobin) or extravascular (spleen, bilirubin up, haptoglobin low but urine hemoglobin absent). Direct Coombs means antibody or complement is on the red cell.

Coagulation. The platelet plug is primary hemostasis: adhesion (von Willebrand factor and GPIb), aggregation (GPIIb/IIIa), then the fibrin clot. Mucosal bleeding and petechiae suggest platelets or vessels. Deep joints and delayed bleeding suggest clotting factors. Hemophilia A is factor VIII, B is IX, both X-linked, both with a normal platelet count and a prolonged PTT that corrects when mixed with normal plasma unless an inhibitor is present. Von Willebrand disease is the most common inherited bleeding disorder in teaching sets: the factor that carries VIII and sticks platelets to collagen is weak, so the PTT may be long and the bleeding is often mucosal.

Thrombosis is Virchow: stasis, endothelial injury, and a hypercoagulable blood. A clot on the arterial side is usually platelet-rich on a damaged wall. A clot on the venous side is usually fibrin and red cells in slow flow. That is why the drugs differ later. The mechanism is enough for Step 1.

## Oncology as brakes and accelerators

Proto-oncogenes accelerate. One mutant copy can drive. Tumor suppressors brake. Both copies must fail. p53 answers DNA damage. Rb holds the cycle. BCL2 blocks apoptosis. A translocation that puts a growth gene next to an immunoglobulin promoter is the classic “wrong place, always on” trick in lymphoid malignancy. You do not need every translocation. You need to recognize that a translocation can create a fusion protein or a mis-expressed normal protein.

Metastasis is a sequence: detach, invade, enter a vessel, survive, exit, grow. The organ a cancer favors is often the next capillary bed (gut to liver, most systemic veins to lung) plus a biological preference. Sentinel nodes exist because lymph has a first stop.

## High-yield atlas

Anemia is production, destruction, or loss. The reticulocyte count says whether the marrow is answering. Coagulation is platelets versus factors. Oncology is one hit versus two, and a cell that would not die.

### Anemia by the red cell and the reticulocyte

| Clue | Lean toward | The trap |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Small cells, high platelet count, a craving for ice | Iron deficiency | Thalassemia, which is also small but has a high red-cell count and a normal iron |
| Small cells, low iron, low transferrin, a chronic disease | Anemia of inflammation | Iron deficiency, which has a high transferrin |
| Very small cells, target cells, a normal iron, a family history | Thalassemia | Iron deficiency. Do not give iron forever |
| Large cells, neurologic symptoms | B12 | Folate, which enlarges the cell and spares the cord |
| Large cells, tea-and-toast or a pregnancy, no neurologic loss | Folate | B12 |
| Large cells because the reticulocytes are big | Hemolysis or blood loss with a responding marrow | A vitamin, which would have a low reticulocyte count |
| Spherocytes | Membrane loss: hereditary spherocytosis or immune hemolysis | The Coombs test splits them |
| Schistocytes | Mechanical destruction | A vitamin. Look for a bad valve, a microangiopathy |
| Bite cells, Heinz bodies, an oxidant drug, in a person of Mediterranean or African ancestry | G6PD | The enzyme level can be normal during the attack because the old cells died. Repeat later |
| Target cells and a sickle film | Hemoglobinopathy | Iron deficiency |
| Low reticulocytes, a low white count, a low platelet count | Marrow failure | A peripheral destruction, which raises the reticulocyte count |

**Vignette.** A woman with heavy periods has a low hemoglobin, small cells, a high platelet count, and a high transferrin. The ferritin is low.
**Reasoning.** Iron left with the blood and was not replaced. The marrow is hungry, so transferrin is up. The diagnosis is iron deficiency. The trap is thalassemia, which does not lower ferritin.

**Pair.** Iron deficiency: low ferritin, high transferrin. Inflammation: low iron, low transferrin, ferritin normal or high because it is an acute-phase protein.

### Hemolysis, intravascular versus extravascular

Intravascular hemolysis spills hemoglobin into the plasma and the urine and consumes haptoglobin completely. Extravascular hemolysis is the spleen eating marked cells: bilirubin rises, haptoglobin falls, and the urine has no free hemoglobin. A direct Coombs test means antibody or complement is on the red cell.

**Vignette.** Dark urine during the night, a low haptoglobin, and a thrombosis in an unusual vein. The blood is lysed by acid in the older Ham test teaching, and flow cytometry shows missing GPI-anchored proteins.
**Reasoning.** Complement is chewing red cells because the brake (CD55, CD59) is missing. The diagnosis is paroxysmal nocturnal hemoglobinuria. The trap is G6PD, which is oxidant-triggered and does not cause unusual clots the same way.

### Coagulation

| Story | The limb | The lab |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mucosal bleeding, petechiae, a low platelet count | Platelets, number | ITP, a production problem, or a consumptive process |
| Mucosal bleeding, a normal count, a long bleeding time or a platelet-function story | Platelets, function | von Willebrand disease, aspirin, uremia |
| Deep joint bleeding, a long PTT that corrects when mixed | Factor VIII or IX | Hemophilia. X-linked. VIII is A. IX is B |
| The same, and the PTT does not correct | An inhibitor | A factor VIII inhibitor, or a lupus anticoagulant which paradoxically clots in the body |
| A long PT and PTT, a sick liver, or a vitamin K problem | The shared factors | Vitamin K is II, VII, IX, X, and proteins C and S. Factor VII is the short one, so the PT moves first |
| A low platelet count, a long PT and PTT, a low fibrinogen, a high D-dimer | Consumption | DIC. The trigger is sepsis, trauma, a cancer, or a obstetric catastrophe |
| A low platelet count, schistocytes, a sick kidney or a brain, a normal coagulation panel | A microangiopathy | TTP or HUS. Not DIC, because the clotting times are normal |

**Vignette.** A boy bleeds into a knee after a small twist. The platelet count is normal. The PTT is long and corrects with a mix. The PT is normal.
**Reasoning.** A factor in the intrinsic path is missing, and the platelets work. The diagnosis is hemophilia. The trap is von Willebrand disease, which is usually mucosal and often autosomal, and which carries factor VIII so the PTT can be long, but the bleeding story is the mucosa and the ristocetin test, not a hemarthrosis as the headline.

**Hook.** Petechiae are platelets. Hemarthroses are factors. Schistocytes with a normal PT are a microangiopathy, not DIC.

### White cells and the clones

| Disease | The cell | The one association |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Acute lymphoblastic leukemia | Lymphoblast. A child | Bone pain, a low blood counts, a mediastinal mass if it is T cell |
| Acute myeloid leukemia | Myeloblast. Auer rods if present | An adult. DIC if it is the promyelocytic form, which is a translocation that a vitamin A derivative can treat. The drug is the later book. The translocation is the mechanism |
| Chronic lymphocytic leukemia | Mature lymphocytes. Smudge cells. An older adult | An indolent high count. Autoimmune hemolysis can join it |
| Chronic myeloid leukemia | A neutrophil series with a left shift. A low leukocyte alkaline phosphatase | The Philadelphia chromosome, BCR-ABL, a tyrosine kinase. The drug that blocks that kinase is the classic targeted story |
| Hodgkin lymphoma | Reed-Sternberg cell, CD15 and CD30 | Contiguous spread. A young person. A fever and a night sweat |
| Burkitt | A starry-sky B cell | MYC next to a loud immunoglobulin promoter. EBV. A jaw or an abdominal mass |
| Follicular lymphoma | A nodular B cell | BCL2, so the cell will not die. Waxing nodes |
| Multiple myeloma | A plasma cell | CRAB: calcium, renal failure, anemia, bone lytic lesions. Rouleaux. A light-chain kidney |
| Waldenstrom | A lymphoplasmacytic cell making IgM | Hyperviscosity. Not the lytic bone of myeloma |

**Vignette.** An older adult has a very high neutrophil count, a low leukocyte alkaline phosphatase, and a translocation between chromosomes 9 and 22.
**Reasoning.** A kinase is always on. The diagnosis is chronic myeloid leukemia. The trap is a leukemoid reaction, which has a high leukocyte alkaline phosphatase and a reason (infection). The enzyme is the split.

**NBME trap.** They ask the gene product, not the disease name. BCR-ABL is a tyrosine kinase. BCL2 blocks apoptosis. MYC is a transcription factor. RAS is a signal. p53 and Rb are the brakes.

### Transfusion reactions as mechanisms

| When | What happened | The move, in one line |
| --- | --- | --- |
| During the unit, fever, back pain, dark urine | ABO incompatibility. Preformed antibody | Stop the blood. It is clerical until proven otherwise |
| During the unit, anaphylaxis, in an IgA-deficient person | Antibody against IgA | Stop. Epinephrine for the anaphylaxis |
| During the unit, a mild fever and little else | Cytokines, or an antibody against a white cell | Stop and evaluate. Do not assume it is mild until the clerical check is done |
| Hours later, a falling hemoglobin and a fever | A delayed hemolytic reaction, an anamnestic antibody | Support. The Coombs test may be the clue |
| Lung injury during or soon after | Donor antibodies against recipient white cells | TRALI. Support the lung. It is not fluid overload, which has a high pressure |
| Volume overload | Too much volume | A high pressure and a responding diuretic. Split it from TRALI |

**Vignette.** Minutes into a transfusion, fever and flank pain. The plasma is pink.
**Reasoning.** Preformed antibody fixed complement on the new red cells. The diagnosis is an acute hemolytic transfusion reaction. The trap is a febrile nonhemolytic reaction, which does not turn the plasma pink.

## Stem craft

The red cell, the clot, and the clone.

The vignette withholds the name. The reasoning earns it.

**Vignette 1.** Small cells, a very high red-cell count, a normal ferritin, and a family history.
**Reasoning.** Thalassemia. Iron deficiency lowers ferritin and often raises the platelet count, and the red-cell count is not high. The trap is years of iron for a trait.

**Vignette 2.** Bite cells after a sulfa drug. The enzyme assay during the attack is normal.
**Reasoning.** G6PD. The old cells that lacked the enzyme already lysed, so the assay of the survivors can look normal. Repeat later. The trap is a negative assay used to clear the diagnosis during the crisis.

**Vignette 3.** A low platelet count, schistocytes, a fever, and a normal PT and PTT.
**Reasoning.** A microangiopathy, TTP until plasma exchange is arranged. DIC would prolong the clotting times and drop fibrinogen. The trap is platelets for a low count.

**Vignette 4.** A very high neutrophil count and a low leukocyte alkaline phosphatase. A 9;22 translocation.
**Reasoning.** Chronic myeloid leukemia, BCR-ABL. A leukemoid reaction has a high enzyme and an infection. The trap is the word leukocytosis.

**Vignette 5.** Smudge cells in an older adult and an autoimmune hemolysis.
**Reasoning.** Chronic lymphocytic leukemia. The hemolysis is the antibody, not the crowding alone. The trap is calling it iron deficiency because the hemoglobin fell.

### Iron deficiency versus thalassemia

Iron left the body or never arrived. Thalassemia is a globin story with plenty of iron and too many small cells.

**Vignette.** A man has small cells, a red-cell count that is high for the hemoglobin, and a normal ferritin. His father had the same count.
**Reasoning.** The marrow is pumping out many small cells and the iron stores are full. The diagnosis is Iron deficiency versus thalassemia.
**Pair.** Iron deficiency lowers ferritin and usually does not raise the red-cell count.
**NBME trap.** A trial of iron that never gets a ferritin.
**Hook.** High red-cell count and normal ferritin is not iron loss.

### B12 cord versus folate marrow

Folate will repair the marrow of a B12-deficient patient and leave the myelin to die.

**Vignette.** After folate, the hemoglobin rose. The feet are number and the Babinski is new.
**Reasoning.** The marrow was fed. The cord was not. This was B12. The diagnosis is B12 cord versus folate marrow.
**Pair.** Folate deficiency does not hit the dorsal columns.
**NBME trap.** A normal hemoglobin used to retire the neurologic question.
**Hook.** The film can lie. The tuning fork does not.

### G6PD after an oxidant

Older cells with the least enzyme died. The survivors are the younger cells that still have enzyme.

**Vignette.** Dark urine two days after a new antibiotic. The smear shows bite cells. The G6PD level is reported as normal.
**Reasoning.** You tested the cells that lived. Repeat the assay later. The diagnosis is G6PD after an oxidant.
**Pair.** Autoimmune hemolysis is Coombs-positive and is not tied to an oxidant.
**NBME trap.** Clearing the diagnosis because the level was normal on day one.
**Hook.** Test after the crisis, not during it.

### Warm versus cold autoimmune hemolysis

Warm is extravascular and splenic. Cold fixes complement in the cool periphery and can be intravascular.

**Vignette.** Hemoglobin falls in the winter. The fingers turn blue. The Coombs test for complement is positive.
**Reasoning.** This is a cold agglutinin picture. Warm disease is IgG and does not care about the season in that way. The diagnosis is Warm versus cold autoimmune hemolysis.
**Pair.** Warm disease answers steroids more often. Cold disease wants you to keep the patient warm and to think about the underlying clone.
**NBME trap.** Splenectomy logic applied to a cold agglutinin.
**Hook.** Season and the finger. Complement versus IgG.

### DIC versus TTP

DIC consumes clotting factors. TTP is platelet thrombi from a missing ADAMTS13 activity. The clotting times stay normal.

**Vignette.** Schistocytes, fever, confusion, a platelet count of 15,000, and a normal PT.
**Reasoning.** The coagulation panel is intact, so this is not DIC. Plasma exchange is the TTP pathway. Platelets are not the plan. The diagnosis is DIC versus TTP.
**Pair.** DIC has a trigger you can name, a long PT, and a low fibrinogen.
**NBME trap.** A platelet transfusion as the first order.
**Hook.** Normal PT means it is not DIC.

### Hemophilia versus von Willebrand

Factor VIII or IX is a deep bleed and an X-linked story. von Willebrand factor is the platelet glue and the carrier of VIII, so the bleeding is mucosal and the PTT may be long.

**Vignette.** A woman has nosebleeds and heavy periods. The PTT is slightly long. The platelet count is normal.
**Reasoning.** Mucosa, both sexes, a long PTT that is really a missing carrier. The diagnosis is von Willebrand disease. The diagnosis is Hemophilia versus von Willebrand.
**Pair.** Hemophilia is joints and muscles and almost always a boy in the classic teaching.
**NBME trap.** A normal platelet count used to clear a platelet-function disease.
**Hook.** Joints are factors. Mucosa is platelets or von Willebrand factor.

### CML versus a leukemoid reaction

BCR-ABL is a tyrosine kinase that is always on. A reaction is the marrow answering an infection.

**Vignette.** The white count is 80,000 with a left shift. The patient is not febrile. The alkaline phosphatase of the white cells is low.
**Reasoning.** No infection and a low enzyme. The translocation will confirm chronic myeloid leukemia. The diagnosis is CML versus a leukemoid reaction.
**Pair.** A leukemoid reaction has a reason and a high enzyme.
**NBME trap.** The word leukocytosis treated as infection.
**Hook.** Low LAP, no fever, 9;22.

### Acute promyelocytic leukemia

A translocation that a retinoid can differentiate. The emergency is DIC. The drug is the later book. The translocation and the bleeding are this page.

**Vignette.** A young adult has pancytopenia, bleeding gums, and a smear with abnormal promyelocytes. Fibrinogen is low.
**Reasoning.** This subtype consumes clot. The diagnosis is acute promyelocytic leukemia until the genetics return. The trap is waiting for the final karyotype before you tell anyone the patient is bleeding. The diagnosis is Acute promyelocytic leukemia.
**Pair.** Other AML subtypes are less famous for DIC on day one.
**NBME trap.** A routine admit without a clotting panel.
**Hook.** Promyelocytes plus bleeding is DIC until proven otherwise.

### Multiple myeloma

A plasma-cell clone. Lytic bone, not the blastic bone of prostate cancer. Light chains hurt the tubule. The gap looks low because the paraprotein is a cation.

**Vignette.** Back pain, calcium 12, creatinine 2.4, and a blood smear that looks like stacked coins.
**Reasoning.** A paraprotein and lytic bone. The diagnosis is myeloma. The diagnosis is Multiple myeloma.
**Pair.** Waldenstrom makes IgM and hyperviscosity and does not punch lytic holes in bone the same way.
**NBME trap.** A bone scan that is cold, which myeloma often is, used to clear the disease. Plain films or a CT see the lytic lesions better.
**Hook.** Lytic, calcium, rouleaux, kidney.

### Transfusion, hemolytic versus febrile

Pink plasma is complement on the wrong red cells, usually a clerical ABO error. A fever alone can be cytokines, but you still stop the blood and check the identity.

**Vignette.** Ten minutes into the unit the patient has back pain. The spun plasma is pink.
**Reasoning.** Free hemoglobin. Stop the blood. The diagnosis is an acute hemolytic reaction. The diagnosis is Transfusion, hemolytic versus febrile.
**Pair.** TRALI is lung injury and a normal pressure. Volume overload is a high pressure. Neither turns the plasma pink.
**NBME trap.** Acetaminophen and a faster rate.
**Hook.** Pink plasma stops the unit.

## Checklist

- [ ] Reticulocyte count before the vitamin.
- [ ] Mucosa and petechiae: platelets. Joints: factors.
- [ ] Mixing study: corrects means a missing factor. Does not correct means an inhibitor.
- [ ] Oncogene, one hit. Tumor suppressor, two hits.
- [ ] Arterial thrombus is a platelet story. Venous thrombus is a stasis story.

## Common traps

- Calling every microcytic anemia iron deficiency. Thalassemia has a very low MCV and a normal or high red-cell count. Chronic disease often has a low iron and a low transferrin, the opposite of iron deficiency’s high transferrin.
- Treating a high platelet count as “well protected.” Function can be terrible (uremia, aspirin) with a normal count.
- Memorizing chemo adverse-effect lists with no mechanism. The ones worth keeping are the ones that are the mechanism: a drug that hits rapidly dividing cells hits marrow, gut, and hair.
- Quoting a hemoglobin number that “requires transfusion.” That is a clinical guideline and it depends on the patient.

## Practice this in the Qbank

Anemia items are a reticulocyte and a smear. Coagulation items are a bleeding story plus which lab is long. Do not open a regimen table. Feed cards on factor names are fine if you can say whether the bleeding is mucosal or deep.

# Reproduction and development

> **Study aid only.** Embryology and reproductive physiology. Prenatal management is an ACOG topic in the Step 2 and Step 3 books.

## What this chapter covers

Sex development, the menstrual cycle as feedback, pregnancy physiology, and the embryology errors that become named congenital disease. Human development is a small explicit range (about 1–3%) and also sits inside the heavy reproductive and endocrine block.

## The framework

Gonads and internal ducts are a fork. The SRY gene on Y pushes the undifferentiated gonad toward testis. Sertoli cells make anti-Müllerian hormone, so the female internal ducts regress. Leydig cells make testosterone, so the Wolffian ducts become epididymis, vas, and seminal vesicles. Dihydrotestosterone, made by 5-alpha-reductase, virilizes the external genitalia. Without SRY, the gonad becomes an ovary, Müllerian structures persist, and external genitalia develop along female lines. A defect at each step has a different phenotype. Name the step.

| Step that failed | Internal ducts | External genitalia |
| --- | --- | --- |
| No anti-Müllerian hormone effect in an XY fetus | Both systems can persist | Male, if androgen worked |
| Androgen receptor does not work | Wolffian structures do not mature | Female or under-virilized. Testes are present. |
| No 5-alpha-reductase | Wolffian structures present | Under-virilized until puberty raises testosterone |
| Congenital adrenal hyperplasia, XX, androgen excess | Uterus present | Virilized |

The menstrual cycle is negative feedback until it isn’t. Early follicular growth is FSH. Estrogen then feeds back negatively. A sustained high estrogen switches to positive feedback, the LH surge, and ovulation. The corpus luteum makes progesterone, which quiets the endometrium into a secretory lining. No fertilization: the corpus luteum fades, progesterone falls, the lining sheds. Fertilization: hCG keeps the corpus luteum alive until the placenta takes over steroid production.

## Pregnancy physiology worth the points

Blood volume and cardiac output rise. Systemic resistance falls, so blood pressure often dips mid-pregnancy. Tidal volume rises and PCO2 falls. A “normal” nonpregnant PCO2 is a warning in pregnancy. GFR rises, so creatinine should look a little lower. The placenta is an organ of gas exchange. Fetal hemoglobin holds oxygen (left-shifted curve). Oxygenated blood prefers the ductus venosus and the foramen ovale on the way to the brain and heart.

Teratogens are mechanism plus timing. The all-or-none period is very early. Organogenesis is the vulnerable window. Learn a short list by mechanism (folate antagonism, vascular disruption, ACE inhibitor effect on fetal kidney, isotretinoin on neural crest and the face) rather than a poster of drug names. Confirm any drug you might actually prescribe against a current label. This page is not a prescribing guide.

## Embryology that explains a lesion

- Neural tube closes early. Folate deficiency raises the risk of a failure to close. The lesion level decides motor and continence findings.
- Branchial apparatus: a pouch, groove, or arch error explains a lateral neck cyst or a missing parathyroid and thymus (third pouch).
- Neural crest builds peripheral neurons, melanocytes, the adrenal medulla, and parts of the face and conotruncal cushions. A crest failure can therefore be pigment plus heart plus gut innervation.
- Vitelline duct that stays open is a Meckel diverticulum. It can contain gastric mucosa and bleed.
- Physiologic midgut herniation returns. If it does not, you have an omphalocele (covered, midline). Gastroschisis is a wall defect, usually to the right of the cord, without a sac. Do not swap them.

## Checklist

- [ ] Name the sexual-differentiation step, not the syndrome first.
- [ ] LH surge needs sustained estrogen. Progesterone is the luteal hormone.
- [ ] Pregnancy lowers PCO2 and the expected creatinine.
- [ ] Timing of a teratogen matters as much as the drug.
- [ ] Omphalocele is covered. Gastroschisis is not.

## Common traps

- Calling every virilized XX newborn “androgen exposure” without asking whether a uterus is present. The uterus tells you the gonadal and Müllerian story.
- Treating hCG and LH as unrelated. hCG can stimulate the LH receptor. That is why it maintains the corpus luteum, and why some tumors cause hormone effects.
- Memorizing week numbers you cannot defend. If an item needs a week, it usually also gives a clue. Confirm standard obstetric weeks in the ACOG-based chapters rather than inventing them here.
- Studying development as trivia disconnected from the adult disease it becomes.

# Musculoskeletal, skin, and connective tissue

> **Study aid only.** Structure, inflammation pattern, and the autoantibody as a clue, not as a diagnosis by itself. Treatment choices follow current rheumatology guidance on later Steps.

## What this chapter covers

Bone and joint as mechanics, the rashes that are the disease rather than decoration, and the connective-tissue patterns. Musculoskeletal plus skin is about 8–12% of Step 1.

## The framework

A joint item asks two questions. How many joints, and is it inflammatory? One joint plus fever is infection until proven otherwise. Many small joints of the hands, lasting, with morning stiffness, is inflammatory arthritis. Pain that worsens through the day and spares true inflammation is more degenerative. The mechanism of osteoarthritis is cartilage failure and bone remodeling, not a primary synovitis. Rheumatoid arthritis is synovium becoming an invasive tissue.

Bone is collagen plus mineral. Osteoblast builds. Osteoclast resorbs, stimulated by RANKL, held back by osteoprotegerin. PTH raises RANKL when calcium is low, so chronic high PTH thins bone while it raises serum calcium. Estrogen loss lets resorption outrun formation. That is the menopause mechanism, not a slogan.

| Pattern | Mechanism worth keeping |
| --- | --- |
| Osteoporosis | Less bone mass, normal mineralization. Fracture from a fall that should not break a bone. |
| Osteomalacia / rickets | Poor mineralization. Soft bone. Vitamin D pathway. |
| Paget | Disordered remodeling. Mosaic bone. High-output state if the disease is extensive. |
| Osteomyelitis | Direct or hematogenous infection. Sickle cell and Salmonella is the classic pair, but Staphylococcus remains the overall common organism in many series. Do not let the classic pair erase the common one. |
| Compartment syndrome | Pressure above perfusion. Pain out of proportion, pain on stretch. The pulse can still be present. |
| Gout | Monosodium urate. Negatively birefringent needles. The big toe is common because of temperature and pH, not because of a rule you must memorize. |
| Pseudogout | Calcium pyrophosphate. Positively birefringent rhomboids. |

Skin is a window. A vesicle at different stages, in different dermatomes, in an adult, is varicella until you prove otherwise. A dermatomal stripe of vesicles is zoster, which is reactivation in one sensory ganglion. Target lesions after an infection or a drug are erythema multiforme’s pattern. Pemphigus is flaccid bullae because the split is inside the epidermis (desmosomes). Bullous pemphigoid is tense bullae because the split is at the basement membrane. Nikolsky (skin shears with pressure) tracks the intra-epidermal split.

Autoantibodies are clues that travel with a mechanism. ANA is sensitive for several connective-tissue diseases and not specific. Anti-dsDNA and anti-Smith pull toward lupus. Anti-histone is the drug-induced lupus clue. Anti-centromere is limited systemic sclerosis. Anti-Scl-70 is diffuse disease with more internal organ risk. Anti-Jo-1 is an inflammatory myopathy clue. c-ANCA (PR3) travels with granulomatosis with polyangiitis. p-ANCA (MPO) travels with microscopic polyangiitis and eosinophilic granulomatosis, and also with other diseases, so it is not a nameplate. Confirm a rare association rather than stacking eponyms.

### Septic arthritis

A single joint plus fever is infection until a tap says otherwise. Crystals and infection can share a joint. The tap is the cell count, the Gram stain, and the crystals.

**Vignette.** A 60-year-old with a chronic gout history has a knee that is hot, and he is shivering. He cannot walk.
**Reasoning.** The old diagnosis does not clear a new fever. The joint needs a needle before the antibiotic if minutes are safe. The diagnosis is Septic arthritis.
**Pair.** Gout is one joint and is often afebrile or low-grade. Sepsis is a systemically ill person. When you cannot tell, you tap.
**NBME trap.** Steroids into a joint you have not tapped.
**Hook.** One hot joint gets a needle.

### Gout versus pseudogout

Urate needles are negatively birefringent. Calcium pyrophosphate rhomboids are positively birefringent. The big toe is common for urate because the joint is cool and acidic. The knee and the wrist like pyrophosphate.

**Vignette.** An older knee is swollen. The film shows a line in the cartilage. Crystals are rhomboid and blue when parallel to the compensator.
**Reasoning.** Cartilage calcification plus a positive sign is calcium pyrophosphate. The diagnosis is pseudogout. The diagnosis is Gout versus pseudogout.
**Pair.** Urate is needle-negative. Pyrophosphate is rhomboid-positive. The words are easy to swap. The shape is the backup.
**NBME trap.** Treating every birefringent crystal as gout.
**Hook.** Needles negative. Rhomboids positive.

### Osteoarthritis versus rheumatoid arthritis

Osteoarthritis is cartilage failure. Rheumatoid arthritis is synovium becoming invasive. Osteoarthritis likes the DIP and the base of the thumb. Rheumatoid arthritis likes the MCP and the wrist and spares the DIP.

**Vignette.** A 70-year-old has bony enlargement of the distal finger joints and pain that is worse after gardening. Morning stiffness lasts a few minutes.
**Reasoning.** The joint, the bony nodes, and the short stiffness are cartilage, not synovium. The diagnosis is osteoarthritis. The diagnosis is Osteoarthritis versus rheumatoid arthritis.
**Pair.** Rheumatoid stiffness lasts an hour and hits the MCP. The DIP is Heberden country.
**NBME trap.** A positive rheumatoid factor used to override a DIP-only exam. The factor is not specific.
**Hook.** DIP is osteoarthritis. MCP is rheumatoid.

### Osteoporosis versus osteomalacia

Osteoporosis is less bone, normally mineralized. Osteomalacia is poorly mineralized matrix. A DEXA number is a risk tool. The mechanism is the quantity versus the mineral.

**Vignette.** A woman years after menopause breaks a wrist when she trips on a rug. Calcium and PTH are normal.
**Reasoning.** The matrix was mineralized and there was less of it. Estrogen loss let resorption win. The diagnosis is osteoporosis. The diagnosis is Osteoporosis versus osteomalacia.
**Pair.** Osteomalacia has a mineralization defect, often a low vitamin D, a low calcium, and a high PTH or a low phosphate depending on the cause.
**NBME trap.** A bisphosphonate for osteomalacia. You needed the vitamin.
**Hook.** Quantity versus mineral.

### Paget disease

Disordered remodeling. Mosaic bone. The alkaline phosphatase is the osteoblast. Calcium stays normal because the mineral is being recycled, not dumped.

**Vignette.** A 75-year-old has a enlarging hat size and a warm tibia. ALP is high. Calcium is normal. The film is mixed lytic and sclerotic.
**Reasoning.** Chaotic bone turnover in one or a few bones. The diagnosis is Paget disease. The diagnosis is Paget disease.
**Pair.** Osteosarcoma can arise in Paget bone and hurts more and looks destructive. A new pain in old Paget disease is not just the Paget.
**NBME trap.** Calling a high ALP a liver disease when the GGT is normal and the bone is warm.
**Hook.** High ALP, normal calcium, one misshapen bone.

### Compartment syndrome

Pressure in a closed fascial space exceeds perfusion. Nerves and muscle die before the artery is occluded. Fasciotomy is the mechanism of treatment.

**Vignette.** Six hours after a tibial fracture, the calf hurts more than the film explains. Toes can still feel a pulse. Stretching the toes is agony.
**Reasoning.** The pulse is a late and unreliable friend. The diagnosis is compartment syndrome. The diagnosis is Compartment syndrome.
**Pair.** A clot in an artery loses the pulse early and the limb is cold. A compartment keeps a pulse and a terrible stretch test.
**NBME trap.** Reassurance because the Doppler still finds a pulse.
**Hook.** Pain on stretch. Do not wait for pulselessness.

### Pemphigus versus bullous pemphigoid

Pemphigus splits inside the epidermis because desmosomes failed. The antibody is often against desmoglein. Pemphigoid splits under the epidermis at the basement membrane, so the blister roof is tougher.

**Vignette.** A 50-year-old has erosions in the mouth and flaccid blisters. Rubbing nearby skin shears it off.
**Reasoning.** The split is inside the epidermis, so the roof is thin and the mucosa is involved. The diagnosis is pemphigus vulgaris. The diagnosis is Pemphigus versus bullous pemphigoid.
**Pair.** Bullous pemphigoid has tense bullae, less mucosal disease, and an older patient. Nikolsky is negative.
**NBME trap.** Steroids for a blister you have not assigned a level to, when it might be a burn or a staphylococcal scald.
**Hook.** Flaccid and Nikolsky is inside. Tense is under.

### Melanoma clues, mechanism level

Melanocytes that invaded. Depth is the prognostic measurement. A shave that cannot measure depth is the wrong biopsy when melanoma is likely. The ABCDE list is a screen for the eye, not a diagnosis.

**Vignette.** A mole on the back has three colors and an irregular edge. It grew over two months.
**Reasoning.** Change and irregular pigment are invasion until a full-thickness sample says otherwise. The diagnosis to rule out is melanoma. The diagnosis is Melanoma clues, mechanism level.
**Pair.** A basal-cell carcinoma is a pearly nodule with telangiectasias. A squamous-cell carcinoma is a scaly ulcer on sun-damaged skin. Neither is a multicolored macule.
**NBME trap.** A superficial shave of a lesion you think is melanoma.
**Hook.** Depth is the number. Do not shave it away.

### Bone tumors by age, only the ones that answer

Osteoid osteoma is a small benign nidus that makes prostaglandin pain. Osteosarcoma is a malignant bone-forming tumor in the metaphysis of a teenager, or in an older person with Paget disease. Ewing is a diaphyseal, onion-skin, small round cell tumor in a child. Giant cell tumor is an epiphyseal lytic lesion after the growth plates close.

**Vignette.** A 14-year-old has months of tibial night pain that melts with ibuprofen. The film shows a tiny round lucency with a lot of surrounding bone.
**Reasoning.** Prostaglandin from a small nidus. The diagnosis is osteoid osteoma. The trap is osteosarcoma, which does not politely answer ibuprofen and does not stay tiny. The diagnosis is Bone tumors by age, only the ones that answer.
**Pair.** Osteosarcoma is a big, bone-making metaphyseal mass. Ewing is diaphyseal and onion-skinned.
**NBME trap.** A biopsy plan that ignores the age and the location.
**Hook.** Night pain that ibuprofen erases is a nidus, not a sarcoma.

### Nerve injuries the brachial plexus items use

Upper trunk (Erb) is a fall that pulls the neck: waiter's tip, loss of shoulder abduction and elbow flexion. Lower trunk (Klumpke) is a hand claw from a grab that pulls the arm up. Wrist drop is the radial nerve. Ape hand and a thenar loss is the median nerve. Claw with intrinsic loss and a sensory split is the ulnar nerve.

**Vignette.** A newborn after a difficult delivery holds one arm adducted and internally rotated, with the elbow extended.
**Reasoning.** The upper trunk was stretched. The diagnosis is an Erb palsy. The trap is a clavicle fracture, which hurts but does not produce that posture once the pain is still. Both can coexist. The posture is the plexus. The diagnosis is Nerve injuries the brachial plexus items use.
**Pair.** Klumpke grabs the intrinsics and can include a Horner syndrome if the T1 root is in it.
**NBME trap.** Calling every neonatal arm a broken clavicle.
**Hook.** Waiter's tip is the upper trunk. Claw with a Horner is the lower trunk.

### Skin infections that are an emergency

Necrotizing fasciitis is a surgical disease. The organism can be a mix or a group A strep. Cellulitis is a dermis infection that you mark and recheck. Erythema nodosum is a septal panniculitis and a reaction, often to a strep or a drug or an inflammatory bowel disease, not an infection of the fat that you incise.

**Vignette.** A leg is mildly red but the pain is enormous. The person is hypotensive. Crepitus is present.
**Reasoning.** The fascia is dying faster than the skin shows. The diagnosis is necrotizing infection. The trap is oral antibiotics and a clinic visit tomorrow. The diagnosis is Skin infections that are an emergency.
**Pair.** Cellulitis has pain proportional to the redness. Necrotizing pain is out of proportion.
**NBME trap.** A CT scan that delays the operating room when the story is already clear.
**Hook.** Pain out of proportion is the knife.

### Autoantibodies as clues, not diagnoses

ANA is sensitive for several connective-tissue diseases and not specific. Anti-dsDNA and anti-Smith pull toward lupus. Anti-centromere is limited systemic sclerosis. Anti-Scl-70 is diffuse disease with more lung and kidney risk. Anti-Jo-1 is an inflammatory myopathy clue. c-ANCA (PR3) travels with granulomatosis with polyangiitis. p-ANCA (MPO) is less specific. The organs in the stem are the diagnosis.

**Vignette.** A young woman has a malar rash, painless oral ulcers, a low complement, and a positive anti-dsDNA. The urine has red-cell casts.
**Reasoning.** Several systems and a specific antibody. The diagnosis is systemic lupus erythematosus with nephritis. The trap is rosacea because of the cheeks. Rosacea does not drop complement or throw casts. The diagnosis is Autoantibodies as clues, not diagnoses.
**Pair.** Drug-induced lupus likes anti-histone antibodies and spares the kidney more often. Anti-dsDNA is the native disease.
**NBME trap.** An antibody without an organ. Do not treat a titer.
**Hook.** The antibody suggests. The organs decide.

## Stem craft

One joint, the level of a split, and the age of a bone lesion.

The vignette withholds the name. The reasoning earns it.

**Vignette 1.** Distal finger joints, bony nodes, minutes of stiffness, pain after use.
**Reasoning.** Osteoarthritis. Rheumatoid arthritis wants the MCP and the wrist and an hour of stiffness. The trap is a rheumatoid factor that is positive in an older person and does not match the joints.

**Vignette 2.** A teenager, a tiny tibial lucency, night pain that ibuprofen erases.
**Reasoning.** Osteoid osteoma. Osteosarcoma does not stay tiny and does not politely answer a prostaglandin inhibitor. The trap is a sarcoma workup as the first and only thought.

**Vignette 3.** Flaccid mouth erosions and skin that shears when rubbed.
**Reasoning.** Pemphigus, an intraepidermal split. Bullous pemphigoid is tense, older, and Nikolsky-negative. The trap is steroids for a staphylococcal scald, which is also intraepidermal and is a child.

**Vignette 4.** Pain out of proportion in a cast, a present pulse, agony when the toes are stretched.
**Reasoning.** Compartment syndrome. The artery is not the first thing to die. The trap is waiting for pulselessness.

**Vignette 5.** A positive ANA only, no organ findings, a tired young woman.
**Reasoning.** A positive ANA is not lupus. Anti-dsDNA, low complement, casts, or a real rash would be. The trap is treating a titer.

## Filing the stem

Bone, joint, and skin stems that are filed wrong.

The vignette withholds the name. The reasoning earns it.

**Vignette 1.** A swollen knee in an older adult. The radiograph shows a line of calcium in the cartilage. The crystal is a rhomboid.
**Reasoning.** Calcium pyrophosphate. Positively birefringent rhomboids. Urate is a needle and negative. The knee and the film are the split, not the word gout. Pseudogout is the diagnosis the stem is building. Do not start a urate-lowering drug for a pyrophosphate disease.

**Vignette 2.** Morning stiffness for an hour, swollen wrists and knuckles, and a spleen that is not the story. The distal finger joints are spared.
**Reasoning.** Rheumatoid arthritis hits the synovium of the MCP and the wrist. Osteoarthritis hits the DIP and is worse with use, not with an hour of morning stiffness. A positive rheumatoid factor in an older person without those joints is not the disease.

**Vignette 3.** A pearly nodule with telangiectasias on a sun-exposed face. It has not changed color through three shades.
**Reasoning.** Basal-cell carcinoma. Melanoma is the irregular multicolored macule. Squamous-cell carcinoma is the scaly ulcer. The trap is a wide excision planned as if it were melanoma before the biopsy says what it is. The level of aggression follows the diagnosis.

**Vignette 4.** A child has a slapped cheek and then a lacy rash. A sibling with sickle cell disease is suddenly very anemic and not in terrible bone pain.
**Reasoning.** Parvovirus B19 stopped red-cell production. In a normal child you see the rash. In a child who depends on a fast marrow, you see an aplastic crisis. The trap is a pain crisis protocol for a hemoglobin that fell without pain.

**Vignette 5.** Pain in the anatomic snuffbox after a fall on an outstretched hand. The first radiograph is normal.
**Reasoning.** Scaphoid fracture until a repeat film or a dedicated study says otherwise. The blood supply enters distally, so the proximal pole can die. The trap is a sprain diagnosis on a normal day-one film.

**Vignette 6.** A teenager with a high fever, a bone that hurts, and a normal plain film on day one.
**Reasoning.** Osteomyelitis can have a normal early film. MRI or the clinical picture leads. Staphylococcus is the common organism. Salmonella is the sickle-cell classic and does not replace Staphylococcus as the usual bug. The trap is a negative film used to send the child home.

**Vignette 7.** Heberden nodes and a squared thumb base. The erythrocyte sedimentation rate is 25. The patient is 72.
**Reasoning.** Osteoarthritis. A mildly raised sedimentation rate at that age is not rheumatoid arthritis. The joints are the diagnosis. The trap is methotrexate for bony nodes.

**Vignette 8.** A positive anti-centromere antibody and tight skin of the fingers, with a long story of cold white-blue fingers. The trunk skin is relatively spared.
**Reasoning.** Limited systemic sclerosis. Anti-Scl-70 is the diffuse disease with more internal-organ risk. The trap is one word, scleroderma, and one drug, for two different risks.

## Common traps

- Treating osteoarthritis with the rheumatoid mechanism. Different tissue.
- Calling every birefringent crystal gout. Check the sign and the shape.
- Waiting for pulselessness in compartment syndrome. Pain and stretch come first.
- Memorizing a steroid ladder. Not this chapter.

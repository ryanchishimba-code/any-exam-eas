# How to use the Step 1 book

**Study aid only.** This book helps you prepare for an exam. It is not medical advice, not a medical school, and not a promise of any result. AnyExamEasy is independent of the USMLE program. USMLE is a joint program of the NBME and the FSMB. We are not affiliated with either, and we do not invent pass rates.

> The live page on usmle.org is the source of truth for what the exam contains. Ranges in the next chapter were recorded from that outline on 2026-09-06. If the page has moved, follow the page.

## Study plan

Step 1 rewards the mechanism that makes a finding inevitable. The level is the consensus a First Aid-style review expects: pathways, receptors, and classic pairings. This book does not reproduce that text. Pathology, physiology, and pharmacology carry the day. A fact with no mechanism will not transfer to the next vignette.

| Plan | Use it when | Weekly shape |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2 weeks | The science is fresh and you need a pass through the map | One heavy system each day, then a mixed block |
| 6 weeks | You can protect a daily block | Two systems a week, mechanisms before drugs |
| 12 weeks | You are early, working, or rebuilding | One system a week, plus a short mixed set twice a week |

Give reproductive, endocrine, respiratory, renal, and nervous-system time that matches their outline ranges. A rare enzyme with a pretty name is not a plan.

**Daily loop**

1. Read one checklist until you can say the mechanism out loud.
2. Do a timed Step 1 block on that system.
3. Write the miss as a mechanism, not as a disease name.
4. The next day, start from the misses.

## What this book will not do

It will not repeat every flashcard in the USMLE feed. The feed is the single fact. The book is the framework that tells you which fact the vignette is testing. It will not list every organism, every stain, or every drug dose.

## Common traps

- Studying a list of trivia because it feels like progress.
- Treating a practice score as a forecast. This book does not do that, and you should not either.
- Skipping biostatistics and ethics because they "are not science." They are on the outline.

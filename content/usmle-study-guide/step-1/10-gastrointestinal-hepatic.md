# Gastrointestinal and hepatic mechanisms

> **Study aid only.** Secretion, absorption, and the liver zones. Bleeding protocols and drug choice are Step 2 and Step 3, using current ACG, AASLD, or AGA guidance.

## What this chapter covers

How the gut decides to digest, where each nutrient is absorbed, and how the liver handles bilirubin and drugs. Gastrointestinal content is about 6–10% of Step 1. The items are physiology plus one lesion.

## The framework

Three hormones run the meal. Gastrin, from the gastric antrum, raises acid when the stomach is distended, when peptides arrive, and when the vagus fires. Secretin, from the duodenum, sees acid and tells the pancreas to send bicarbonate. CCK sees fat and protein and tells the gallbladder to contract and the pancreas to send enzymes. Somatostatin is the brake.

Acid is made by the parietal cell. Gastrin, acetylcholine (M3), and histamine (H2) all push it. The proton pump is the final common step. Intrinsic factor from the same cell is required for ileal B12 absorption. Lose the parietal cell and you lose acid and, later, B12.

| Site | What depends on it |
| --- | --- |
| Duodenum | Iron, folate, and a large share of mineral absorption. Celiac disease hits this carpet. |
| Jejunum | Most calories. |
| Terminal ileum | B12–intrinsic factor and bile acids. Loss means B12 deficiency and fat-soluble vitamin trouble from bile-acid wasting. |
| Colon | Water, short-chain fatty acids. A villous adenoma can secrete enough to cause diarrhea and low potassium. |

Bilirubin comes from heme. Unconjugated bilirubin is not water-soluble and rides on albumin. The hepatocyte conjugates it. Conjugated bilirubin can enter urine. A stone or pancreatic head cancer blocks excretion: conjugated bilirubin in blood and urine, pale stool, dark urine, and a dilated duct if the block is outside the liver. Hemolysis floods the system with unconjugated bilirubin. The urine stays free of bilirubin because unconjugated bilirubin is not filtered. Hepatocellular disease can do both and usually raises ALT and AST.

Liver zones matter because blood and oxygen enter at the portal triad and exit at the central vein. Zone 3, around the central vein, is most hypoxic and most affected by many toxins and by right-heart backup (nutmeg liver). Zone 1 sees toxins that come straight from the portal blood and is where chronic viral inflammation often starts its fibrosis story. Do not memorize a zone without the reason.

## Portal flow

Portal hypertension shunts blood around the liver: esophageal varices, splenomegaly, and hemorrhoids are the same pressure in different veins. Ascites is portal pressure plus low albumin plus salt retention. Hepatic encephalopathy is failure to clear nitrogenous compounds, worsened by bleeding, infection, and hypokalemia. The mechanism is the point. The drug list for encephalopathy is a later chapter.

## High-yield atlas

The gut question is a hormone, a segment that was removed, or a bilirubin that can or cannot enter the urine. Name which of those three it is.

### The meal, hormone by hormone

| Hormone | The trigger | The effect | The trap |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Gastrin | Distension, peptides, vagus. Low acid if the antrum is alkaline | Acid from the parietal cell | A gastrinoma keeps acid high. A PPI raises gastrin because acid fell. Do not call every high gastrin a tumor |
| Secretin | Acid in the duodenum | Bicarbonate from the pancreas | It is not the fat hormone |
| CCK | Fat and protein in the duodenum | Gallbladder contraction, pancreatic enzymes, a slower stomach | It is not the acid hormone |
| Somatostatin | Acid in the lumen, and as a drug | The brake on gastrin, glucagon, and growth hormone | Octreotide is this hormone as a medicine |
| GIP and GLP-1 | Glucose in the gut | The incretin effect. More insulin when sugar arrives by mouth than by vein | The reason an oral glucose load and an IV load are not the same |
| Motilin | Fasting | Migrating motor complexes. Erythromycin tickles this receptor | A side effect, not a reason to use an antibiotic as a laxative |
| VIP | A tumor, rarely | Watery diarrhea, low potassium, low acid | Not a cholera infection. The organism is not there |

**Vignette.** A man has ulcers in the duodenum and the jejunum, diarrhea, and a gastrin that stays high when acid is high.
**Reasoning.** Acid should have turned gastrin off. It did not. The diagnosis is a gastrinoma. The trap is ordinary peptic ulcer disease, which does not put ulcers past the duodenum or ignore feedback. MEN clustering with parathyroid disease is the associated question, not the proof.

**Hook.** Secretin sees acid. CCK sees fat. Gastrin sees a meal and a vagus nerve.

### Absorption, so a resection is a vitamin

| Segment lost or diseased | What fails | The finding |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Duodenum, as in celiac disease | Iron and folate first, and the surface for everything | Iron deficiency, and dermatitis herpetiformis as the itchy antibody on the skin. Villous blunting |
| Jejunum | Most calories | Weight loss out of proportion to a single vitamin |
| Terminal ileum | B12–intrinsic factor and bile acids | Megaloblastic anemia plus neurologic disease if B12 is low, and fat-soluble vitamin loss if bile acids are wasted. Crohn disease loves this segment |
| Exocrine pancreas | Lipase | Greasy stools, and vitamins A, D, E, K. Glucose comes later if the islet also fails |
| Parietal cell | Acid and intrinsic factor | B12 deficiency years later, and a high gastrin because acid is low. Pernicious anemia is the autoimmune version |
| Colon | Water | Diarrhea. A villous adenoma can secrete enough to drop potassium |

**Vignette.** A child has bulky stools, a rash of grouped vesicles, and a low iron. The biopsy shows flat villi. The symptoms quiet when wheat, barley, and rye leave the diet.
**Reasoning.** An immune attack on the surface, triggered by gluten. The diagnosis is celiac disease. The trap is cystic fibrosis, which also makes bulky stools but does not flatten villi or cause that skin finding. The skin is dermatitis herpetiformis.

**Pair.** Pancreatic insufficiency has a normal D-xylose absorption (the mucosa works) and a low fecal elastase. Mucosal disease fails D-xylose. Both make steatorrhea.

### Bilirubin, urine, and the enzymes

Unconjugated bilirubin rides albumin and does not enter urine. Conjugated bilirubin is water-soluble and darkens urine. Hemolysis floods unconjugated bilirubin. The urine stays free of bilirubin. A stone or a pancreatic head cancer blocks excretion of conjugated bilirubin: dark urine, pale stool, a dilated duct if the block is outside the liver. Hepatocellular disease can do both and raises the transaminases.

| Pattern | Bilirubin | Urine | Enzymes | The lesion |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Hemolysis | Unconjugated | No bilirubin. Urobilinogen can be high | LDH high, haptoglobin low | Red cells, not the liver |
| Gilbert | Unconjugated, mild, during a fast or an illness | No bilirubin | Normal | A lazy conjugating enzyme. Benign |
| Crigler-Najjar | Unconjugated, severe in type 1 | No bilirubin | Normal liver otherwise | The conjugating enzyme is absent or nearly so |
| Dubin-Johnson | Conjugated | Dark | Roughly normal | A black liver. Excretion into bile is the defect |
| Stone in the duct | Conjugated | Dark | ALP and GGT high | Pain, then jaundice. The gallbladder may be dilated |
| Pancreatic head cancer | Conjugated, painless | Dark | ALP high | A painless jaundice and a palpable, nontender gallbladder is the classic, not a law |
| Alcoholic hepatitis | Mixed | Variable | AST higher than ALT, often under a few hundred | The ratio is a clue, not a diagnosis. Confirm with the history |
| Viral hepatitis, acute | Mixed | Dark | ALT and AST very high | The virus and the exposure are the rest of the stem |
| Ischemic hepatitis | Mixed | — | Astronomical transaminases after a shock | The liver was downstream of a low-flow state |

**Vignette.** Mild jaundice during examination weeks. The bilirubin is unconjugated. The blood count and the liver enzymes are normal. An older relative has the same story.
**Reasoning.** Conjugation is a bit slow. There is no hemolysis and no sick liver. The diagnosis is Gilbert syndrome. The trap is hemolysis, which would drop haptoglobin or raise LDH, or hepatitis, which would raise the enzymes.

**NBME trap.** They say “dark urine” and want conjugated bilirubin. Students pick hemolysis because jaundice was the only word they saw.

### Liver zones, viruses, and the drugs that hit zone 3

Blood and oxygen enter at the portal triad and exit at the central vein. Zone 3, around the central vein, is the most hypoxic and sees many toxins after they have been metabolized: acetaminophen, and the nutmeg congestion of right-heart failure. Zone 1 sees incoming toxins that do not need much metabolism, and regenerates first.

| Virus | The fact | Chronic? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A | Fecal-oral. A traveler or shellfish | No |
| B | Blood, sex, birth. Surface antigen is the vaccine. e antigen means infectious. Ground-glass cells | Yes |
| C | Blood. Cryoglobulins, a membranoproliferative kidney, porphyria cutanea tarda | Yes, often |
| D | Needs B. A superinfection is worse than a coinfection | Yes, with B |
| E | Fecal-oral. Dangerous in pregnancy | No, in the usual teaching |

**Vignette.** A woman who takes several extra acetaminophen tablets for a toothache becomes ill on day two. The transaminases are enormous. The center of each lobule is necrotic.
**Reasoning.** A toxic metabolite, normally quenched by glutathione, killed the hypoxic center of the lobule. The diagnosis is acetaminophen toxicity. The trap is viral hepatitis, which is more portal and panlobular and has an exposure. The antidote replaces glutathione capacity. The later books name it. The zone is this page.

### Vascular and inflammatory gut

| Disease | The mechanism | The stem |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ischemic colitis | Watershed, often the splenic flexure, in a low-flow older adult | Pain then bloody stool. Not the small-bowel ileus of mesenteric ischemia, which is pain out of proportion and often no blood yet |
| Mesenteric ischemia | Embolus, thrombus, or venous clot to the small bowel | Pain out of proportion to the exam |
| Intussusception | A lead point, or lymphoid tissue in a child | Currant-jelly stool, a mass, intermittent pain |
| Volvulus | A twist. Sigmoid in an older adult, midgut in a baby | Bilious vomiting in the baby is a surgical emergency |
| Hirschsprung | No ganglion cells in a distal segment. Neural crest | A newborn who does not pass meconium. The dilation is upstream of the aganglionic part |
| Meckel | A vitelline remnant with gastric mucosa | Painless bleeding in a child. The rule-of-2s is a memory hook, not an anatomy law |
| Celiac | Gluten, villous blunting | Iron, the skin, the diet |
| Tropical sprue | An infectious injury to the mucosa, after travel | Folate and B12. It does not answer a gluten-free diet the same way |
| Whipple | Tropheryma. PAS-positive macrophages | A middle-aged man, diarrhea, arthralgia, and a neurologic or a cardiac clue |
| Crohn | Transmural, skip, any part, often ileum | Fistulas, cobblestones, noncaseating granulomas |
| Ulcerative colitis | Mucosa, continuous from the rectum | Bloody diarrhea, a toxic megacolon risk, a primary sclerosing cholangitis association, and a cancer risk that surveillance is for |
| Diverticulitis | A perforated diverticulum, usually sigmoid | Left-lower pain and fever in an older adult. Not the same as the painless bleeding of diverticulosis |

**Vignette.** A baby vomits green fluid. The abdomen is distended. An upper study shows the small bowel corkscrewed.
**Reasoning.** Bilious vomiting is obstruction below the ampulla until proven otherwise. A twist of the midgut around a narrow pedicle is volvulus from malrotation. The diagnosis is that surgical emergency. The trap is pyloric stenosis, which is non-bilious and projectile, in a slightly older infant, with a metabolic alkalosis.

**Pair.** Crohn is transmural and skippy and can be anywhere. Ulcerative colitis is mucosal and continuous from the rectum. Fistula means Crohn. Continuous bloody mucosa from the rectum means ulcerative colitis until the biopsy says otherwise.

### Portal pressure

Portal hypertension shunts blood around the liver: esophageal varices, a large spleen, and hemorrhoids are one pressure in three veins. Ascites is that pressure plus a low albumin plus salt retention. Encephalopathy is a failure to clear nitrogenous compounds, worse after a bleed, an infection, or a low potassium. The drugs are the later books. The shunt is this page.

**Vignette.** A person with cirrhosis vomits blood. The spleen tip is palpable. There is no ulcer history.
**Reasoning.** Portal blood found a thin submucosal vein. The diagnosis is variceal bleeding. The trap is calling the large spleen a separate leukemia. The spleen is congested by the same pressure.

### Eight stems, worked

**Vignette.** Painless jaundice, a palpable nontender gallbladder, and weight loss. The duct is dilated. Transaminases are only mildly up. ALP is high.
**Reasoning.** Conjugated bilirubin cannot leave. A painless, distended gallbladder means the block is below the cystic duct’s join, classically a pancreatic head mass. The diagnosis is extrahepatic obstruction from that mass until imaging names it. The trap is a stone, which hurts.

**Vignette.** A traveler returns with watery diarrhea and no fever. Stool has no white cells.
**Reasoning.** A toxin turned on a secretory pathway. Cholera or an enterotoxigenic E. coli. The diagnosis is secretory, noninflammatory diarrhea. The trap is ordering an invasive-pathogen panel as the first idea when the stool is watery and afebrile. The later books still culture when the stem says blood or fever.

**Vignette.** Bloody diarrhea, fever, and fecal leukocytes after eggs that were undercooked.
**Reasoning.** The mucosa was invaded. Salmonella, Shigella, or Campylobacter. The diagnosis is inflammatory diarrhea. The trap is a secretory toxin, which does not put white cells or blood in the stool.

**Vignette.** Epigastric pain boring to the back, a high lipase, and hypocalcemia. The person drinks heavily.
**Reasoning.** The pancreas digested itself, including the fat around it, and calcium saponified. The diagnosis is acute pancreatitis. The trap is a perforated ulcer, which can also cause a rigid abdomen. Lipase and the calcium are the pancreas.

**Vignette.** A child with cystic fibrosis has greasy stools and a normal intestinal biopsy.
**Reasoning.** The mucosa is fine. The lipase never arrived. The diagnosis is pancreatic insufficiency. The trap is celiac disease, which flattens the biopsy.

**Vignette.** Right-upper pain after fatty meals, then a fever and jaundice.
**Reasoning.** A stone left the gallbladder, blocked the duct, and the duct is now infected. The diagnosis is cholangitis on top of choledocholithiasis. The mechanism is obstruction plus bacteria. Drainage is the later book. The trap is simple biliary colic, which has no fever and no jaundice that persists.

**Vignette.** An older adult has severe abdominal pain and a soft abdomen. Lactate is high. Atrial fibrillation is in the history.
**Reasoning.** Pain out of proportion is bowel that is ischemic before the peritoneum is irritated. An embolus from the heart is the classic passenger. The diagnosis is acute mesenteric ischemia. The trap is gastroenteritis because the abdomen is soft.

**Vignette.** A newborn has not passed meconium. The rectum is empty and narrow. The dilated bowel is upstream. A biopsy of the narrow part shows no ganglion cells.
**Reasoning.** Neural crest cells did not arrive. The diagnosis is Hirschsprung disease. The trap is calling the dilated part the diseased part. The disease is the narrow, aganglionic segment.

## Stem craft

Bilirubin, hormones, and the segment that was lost.

The vignette withholds the name. The reasoning earns it.

**Vignette 1.** Jaundice, no bilirubin in the urine, normal enzymes, a low haptoglobin.
**Reasoning.** Unconjugated bilirubin from hemolysis. It cannot enter urine. A duct stone would darken the urine and raise ALP. The trap is obstruction for every yellow patient.

**Vignette 2.** Mild unconjugated jaundice during a fast. Enzymes and the blood count are normal. A parent has the same pattern.
**Reasoning.** Gilbert. A lazy conjugating enzyme. Hemolysis would consume haptoglobin. Hepatitis would raise the enzymes.

**Vignette 3.** Greasy stools, a normal biopsy, and a history of recurrent pancreatitis.
**Reasoning.** Lipase never arrived. The mucosa can still absorb a sugar like xylose. Celiac would flatten the biopsy. The diagnosis is pancreatic insufficiency.

**Vignette 4.** Bloody diarrhea and fecal leukocytes a day after undercooked eggs.
**Reasoning.** Invasion. A preformed toxin vomits in hours and does not put white cells in the stool. The trap is staphylococcal food poisoning for a febrile bloody illness.

**Vignette 5.** Painless jaundice, a palpable nontender gallbladder, weight loss, a dilated duct.
**Reasoning.** The block is below the cystic duct join, classically a pancreatic head mass. A stone hurts. Courvoisier’s idea is the teaching point, not a promise that every such gallbladder is cancer, but the stem is built that way.

## Another pass on the stems

More bilirubin and segment stems.

The vignette withholds the name. The reasoning earns it.

**Vignette 1.** Dark urine, pale stool, a very high alkaline phosphatase, and a duct that is dilated on ultrasound. Pain woke the patient at night.
**Reasoning.** Conjugated bilirubin cannot leave, and the pain means a stone is still the leading block. A painless version of the same labs is the pancreatic-head story. The trap is hemolysis, which does not dilate a duct or pale the stool.

**Vignette 2.** After an ileal resection, the patient has greasy stools and cannot absorb a vitamin that needs bile acids, and also has numb feet.
**Reasoning.** Bile acids and B12 both needed the terminal ileum. Fat-soluble vitamins fail because the bile-acid pool is lost. B12 fails because intrinsic factor has nowhere to bind. The trap is a single vitamin.

**Vignette 3.** A child with currant-jelly stool and intermittent pain has a mass in the right abdomen.
**Reasoning.** Intussusception. The lead point in a child is often lymphoid tissue. A Meckel diverticulum bleeds without the intermittent mass in the classic contrast. The trap is infectious colitis for a currant-jelly stool that comes and goes with a mass.

**Vignette 4.** AST is twice ALT, both under a few hundred, in a person who drinks daily. The MCV is high.
**Reasoning.** The ratio and the dose of alcohol are the clue. Viral hepatitis usually drives both enzymes much higher and ALT often leads. The ratio is not a diagnosis by itself. The history is.

## Checklist

- [ ] Gastrin, secretin, and CCK each have one trigger.
- [ ] Ileum is B12 and bile acids. Duodenum is iron and folate.
- [ ] Unconjugated bilirubin does not darken the urine.
- [ ] Zone 3 is the hypoxic center of the lobule.
- [ ] Portal pressure explains varices, spleen, and ascites together.

## Common traps

- Calling every jaundice “obstruction.” Urine bilirubin and the enzymes separate hemolysis, hepatocyte injury, and a block.
- Forgetting that a gastric bypass or ileal resection creates a B12 problem even when the diet is fine.
- Treating secretin and CCK as the same duodenal hormone. Acid versus fat.
- Quoting a MELD cutoff or a transfusion threshold. Those are clinical rules for later Steps.

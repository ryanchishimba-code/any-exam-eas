# Endocrine mechanisms

> **Study aid only.** Hormone, receptor, and the axis that broke. Starting insulin or a statin is a Step 2 and Step 3 decision and follows the current ADA or ACC/AHA document.

## What this chapter covers

The axes, the second messengers, and the failure patterns for pituitary, thyroid, adrenal, parathyroid, and the pancreatic islet. Reproductive and endocrine together are the heaviest Step 1 system range, about 12–16%. This chapter is the hormone half. Reproduction is next.

## The framework

A hormone item is three questions. Where is it made? What receptor class does it use? What turns it off? Steroids and thyroid hormone use intracellular receptors and change transcription. Peptides and catecholamines use surface receptors and are faster.

| Hormone | Messenger | The fact that answers items |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Insulin | Receptor tyrosine kinase | Stores fuel. Drives potassium into cells. |
| Glucagon, epinephrine at beta receptors | Gs, cAMP | Releases fuel. |
| Cortisol | Intracellular | Permissive for catecholamines. Raises glucose. Breaks protein and bone over time. |
| Thyroid hormone | Intracellular | Raises basal metabolic rate and beta-receptor sensitivity. |
| PTH | Gs at the kidney and bone | Raises calcium, lowers phosphate, activates vitamin D. |
| ADH (V2) | Gs at the collecting duct | Water reabsorption. V1 is Gq and vasoconstriction. |
| Growth hormone | JAK-STAT, via IGF-1 | Anabolic. Excess after epiphyses close is acromegaly, not height. |
| Prolactin | Tonically inhibited by dopamine | A pituitary stalk lesion raises prolactin and drops the other pituitary hormones. |

Negative feedback is the default. Primary gland failure: the gland hormone is low and the pituitary signal is high. Secondary failure: both are low, or the pituitary signal is inappropriately normal. Tertiary is the hypothalamus. Always name the level before the disease.

## Pairs that are pure logic

- Cushing from an adrenal tumor suppresses ACTH. Cushing from pituitary adenoma (Cushing disease) has a high or inappropriately normal ACTH. Ectopic ACTH is very high and does not suppress the way a pituitary source often does. The dexamethasone logic is about level, not about memorizing a dose.
- Primary aldosteronism: hypertension, low potassium, low renin, high aldosterone. Secondary hyperaldosteronism has high renin (the kidney thinks volume is low).
- Primary adrenal failure (Addison): low cortisol and low aldosterone, high ACTH, high potassium, low sodium. Secondary adrenal failure spares aldosterone, because aldosterone is mostly angiotensin-driven, so potassium is nearer normal and hyperpigmentation is absent.
- Hyperthyroidism with high uptake is the gland making hormone. Low uptake with high hormone is leak (thyroiditis) or exogenous hormone. That split decides whether an antithyroid drug makes any sense. The choice itself is a later Step.
- Diabetes insipidus is water loss with dilute urine. Nephrogenic does not respond to desmopressin. Central does. SIADH is the opposite problem: too much ADH, concentrated urine, low serum osmolality, and clinically euvolemic hyponatremia.
- MEN patterns: parathyroid plus pituitary plus pancreas is one cluster. Medullary thyroid, pheo, and parathyroid is another. Medullary thyroid, pheo, and mucosal neuromas is the third. The point is which tumors travel together, not a numbering trivia contest. Confirm the cluster if an item hinges on a rare third tumor.

## Calcium

PTH raises calcium. Vitamin D raises calcium and phosphate absorption. FGF23 lowers phosphate. Hypercalcemia symptoms are “stones, bones, groans, psychiatric overtones,” which is a memory hook for stones, bone pain, abdominal pain, and altered mood. The mechanism hook is better: high calcium slows nerve firing, so reflexes fall and the QT shortens. Low calcium does the opposite, including tetany.

## High-yield atlas

An endocrine stem is three levels and one receptor class. Draw the hypothalamus, the pituitary, and the gland before you read the choices. Primary disease is the gland. Secondary is the pituitary. Tertiary is the hypothalamus. The gland hormone and the pituitary signal move in opposite directions in primary disease and in the same direction in secondary disease.

### Signaling, so the drug and the tumor make sense

| Hormone | Receptor class | What a stem uses |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Insulin | Tyrosine kinase | Stores fuel. Drives potassium into cells. A receptor that does not answer is insulin resistance. A missing beta cell is type 1 |
| Glucagon, epinephrine at beta receptors | Gs, cAMP | Releases fuel |
| Cortisol, aldosterone, sex steroids, thyroid hormone, vitamin D | Intracellular | Slow, transcriptional. A carrier protein can change the total and not the free hormone |
| PTH, ACTH, TSH, ADH at V2, glucagon | Gs | A stimulating antibody can impersonate the pituitary hormone |
| GH, prolactin | JAK-STAT | Prolactin is the one pituitary hormone under tonic inhibition |
| ANP, nitric oxide | cGMP | The vascular and the natriuretic story |

**Vignette.** A woman has a high total T4, a normal free T4, and no symptoms. She is pregnant. TSH is normal.
**Reasoning.** Estrogen raised thyroid-binding globulin, so the bound hormone rose and the free hormone did not. The gland is not overactive. The diagnosis is a binding-protein change. The trap is hyperthyroidism because the total was high.

**Hook.** Free hormone is the actor. Total hormone includes the passengers on the binding protein.

### The axes, primary versus secondary

| Axis | Primary failure | Secondary failure | The extra clue |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Thyroid | Low free hormone, high TSH | Low free hormone, low or inappropriately normal TSH | Primary hypothyroidism is the common one. Secondary lacks the high TSH and may have other missing pituitary hormones |
| Adrenal cortisol | Low cortisol, high ACTH | Low cortisol, low ACTH | Primary also loses aldosterone, so potassium is high. Secondary spares aldosterone because angiotensin still drives it. Hyperpigmentation needs a high ACTH |
| Gonad | Low sex steroid, high FSH and LH | Low sex steroid, low or normal FSH and LH | Menopause is a primary ovarian failure and is physiologic. A pituitary mass is secondary |
| Growth | A child who makes GH but whose tissues do not answer IGF-1, versus a pituitary that does not make GH | Low GH and low IGF-1, and often other missing hormones | A random GH is useless. The question is the stimulation test or the IGF-1 in context |

**Vignette.** Fatigue, a low sodium, a high potassium, and darker skin in the creases. Blood pressure is low. Cortisol does not rise when ACTH is given.
**Reasoning.** The gland itself cannot make cortisol or aldosterone, and the high ACTH is pigmenting the skin. The diagnosis is primary adrenal failure. The trap is secondary adrenal failure, which is pale, not pigmented, and usually keeps the potassium nearer normal.

**Pair.** Cushing from an adrenal tumor suppresses ACTH. Cushing disease is a pituitary adenoma and has a high or inappropriately normal ACTH. Ectopic ACTH is very high and often does not suppress the way a pituitary source can. The level of ACTH is the split, not the word Cushing.

### Sugar, from mechanism to the emergency pattern

Insulin pushes glucose into muscle and fat, stores glycogen, and stops ketogenesis. Without insulin, glucagon wins: glucose rises, fat breaks down, ketones appear. Type 1 is an autoimmune loss of beta cells. Type 2 is resistance plus a beta cell that eventually cannot keep up. The diagnostic numbers are the ADA Standards and live in the Step 2 book. The mechanism is this page.

| State | Insulin | Ketones | The osmolar picture |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Diabetic ketoacidosis | Absent or not working | Present. A gap acidosis | Glucose is high, but the illness is the ketoacidosis and the potassium shift |
| Hyperosmolar state | Some insulin remains, enough to suppress ketones | Little | Profound dehydration and a very high osmolality |
| Hypoglycemia in a person on insulin | High, from the injection | — | Sweat, tremor, confusion. A beta blocker can hide the tremor and the tachycardia |
| Insulinoma | High, inappropriate | — | Whipple’s triad: symptoms, a low glucose, and relief when glucose is given. C-peptide is high because the insulin is endogenous |
| Factitious insulin | High insulin, low C-peptide | — | The bottle does not come with C-peptide |
| Sulfonylurea abuse | High insulin, high C-peptide | — | Looks like an insulinoma. A drug screen is the split |

Potassium in ketoacidosis is often high in the blood and low in the body. Insulin will drive it into cells. The mechanism is the shift. The replacement plan is the later book.

**Vignette.** A thin adolescent is breathing deeply, smells of ketones, and has a glucose of 420. The potassium on the panel is 5.4. The abdomen hurts.
**Reasoning.** No insulin, lipolysis, ketones, a gap acidosis, and a compensatory hyperventilation. The potassium is high only because it left the cells. The diagnosis is diabetic ketoacidosis. The trap is “the potassium is fine, do not replace it.” The body stores are low. Abdominal pain is the ketosis, not automatically a surgical abdomen.

**Pair.** Ketoacidosis has ketones and a gap. The hyperosmolar state has a higher glucose, more dehydration, and little ketosis, usually in an older person with some remaining insulin.

### Thyroid

| State | TSH | Free hormone | Uptake or scan | What it is |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Graves | Low | High | High, diffuse | A stimulating antibody on the TSH receptor. Type II hypersensitivity |
| Toxic nodule or a toxic multinodular gland | Low | High | High in the nodule, the rest is suppressed | The nodule escaped feedback |
| Thyroiditis, the leak phase | Low | High | Low | Preformed hormone is spilling. An antithyroid drug does not stop a leak |
| Exogenous hormone | Low | High | Low | The gland is asleep. Thyroglobulin is low |
| Primary hypothyroidism, including Hashimoto | High | Low | — | The gland failed. Antibodies may be present. Hurthle cells and lymphoid tissue are the histology |
| Secondary hypothyroidism | Low or normal | Low | — | Look at the rest of the pituitary |

**Vignette.** A woman has a tremor, a stare, weight loss, and a tender gland is not the story. TSH is suppressed. The scan shows diffuse high uptake. The eyes are irritated.
**Reasoning.** The gland is being driven, not leaking. An antibody is pretending to be TSH. The diagnosis is Graves disease. The trap is thyroiditis, which has a low uptake. Eye disease is the fibroblast story of Graves and is not required for the diagnosis, but it supports it.

**Vignette.** Two weeks after a viral illness, the gland is tender. TSH is low, free hormone is high, and uptake is low.
**Reasoning.** Hormone is leaking from a damaged gland. The diagnosis is subacute thyroiditis. The trap is starting a synthesis-blocking drug as if the gland were overproducing. Beta blockade for symptoms is the mechanism-level comfort. The later books name the drug. Here the point is the low uptake.

**Hook.** High hormone plus high uptake is production. High hormone plus low uptake is a leak or a pill.

### Calcium, phosphate, and the three hormones

PTH raises calcium, lowers phosphate, and turns on vitamin D activation. Vitamin D raises absorption of both calcium and phosphate. FGF23 lowers phosphate. Calcitonin exists and is rarely the answer.

| Disease | Calcium | Phosphate | PTH | The one-line mechanism |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Primary hyperparathyroidism | High | Low | High or inappropriately normal | A gland that will not turn off. Stones, bones, groans |
| Humoral hypercalcemia of malignancy | High | Low | Low | PTH-related peptide. The gland is suppressed |
| Bone metastases | High | Varies | Low | Local osteolysis. Not a peptide |
| Vitamin D excess | High | High | Low | Gut absorption of both minerals |
| Hypoparathyroidism | Low | High | Low | Often after neck surgery |
| Pseudohypoparathyroidism | Low | High | High | The kidney does not answer PTH. The Albright phenotype is the classic stem |
| Secondary hyperparathyroidism of kidney failure | Low or normal | High | High | Low vitamin D and high phosphate. See the renal chapter |
| Familial hypocalciuric hypercalcemia | High, mild | — | Normal or slightly high | The calcium sensor is numb. Urine calcium is low. Do not take the glands out |

High calcium slows nerve firing: reflexes fall, the QT shortens, and the person is tired or confused. Low calcium does the opposite, including tetany and a prolonged QT. Chvostek and Trousseau are the irritably firing nerve.

**Vignette.** A smoker has lost weight. Calcium is high, phosphate is low, and PTH is suppressed. There is no adenoma on a neck exam.
**Reasoning.** The parathyroid is asleep, so the calcium is coming from something that mimics PTH. A lung squamous-cell cancer is the classic source of PTH-related peptide. The diagnosis is humoral hypercalcemia of malignancy. The trap is primary hyperparathyroidism, which would not suppress PTH.

### Pituitary, the stalk, and the adenoma that secretes

The stalk carries dopamine down to inhibit prolactin, and it carries releasing hormones down to stimulate everything else. A stalk lesion therefore raises prolactin and drops the other pituitary hormones. A prolactinoma raises prolactin by secretion and can also be large enough to press the chiasm. A very high prolactin is more the tumor. A mild rise is more likely the stalk, a drug (antipsychotics block dopamine), or hypothyroidism (TRH can tickle the lactotroph).

Growth hormone excess before the growth plates close is gigantism. After they close it is acromegaly: the jaw, the hands, the heart, the glucose, and a high IGF-1. A random GH is not the test. Glucose should suppress GH and does not, in the classic confirmatory idea.

Bitemporal hemianopsia means the chiasm, and a pituitary mass is the usual lump sitting under it.

**Vignette.** A man on an antipsychotic has a prolactin that is mildly high, no headache, and a normal visual field. TSH is normal.
**Reasoning.** Dopamine was blocked, so the lactotrophs were released from inhibition. The diagnosis is drug-induced hyperprolactinemia. The trap is sending him for a transsphenoidal surgery. Stop or change the drug, with the psychiatrist, before you operate on a mild number.

**Pair.** A prolactin of several hundred with a field cut is a tumor until imaged. A prolactin of thirty on risperidone is the drug until proven otherwise.

### Adrenal medulla and cortex, and the MEN clusters

The cortex makes salt, sugar, and sex steroids, from the outside in: glomerulosa (aldosterone), fasciculata (cortisol), reticularis (androgens). The medulla makes catecholamines and is a neural-crest organ. A pheochromocytoma is a medulla tumor. Headache, palpitations, and pressure spells. The metabolites are metanephrines. You block alpha before beta if you are going to block at all, because beta blockade alone leaves alpha constriction unopposed. That order is the mechanism.

| Cluster, described without relying on a number you might swap | What travels together |
| --- | --- |
| Parathyroid, pituitary, and pancreas | The MEN that is mostly benign endocrine tumors. Gastrin or insulin can be the pancreatic hormone |
| Medullary thyroid, pheochromocytoma, and parathyroid | The MEN that includes a calcitonin-producing thyroid cancer. RET is the gene in the familial form |
| Medullary thyroid, pheochromocytoma, and mucosal neuromas, with a marfanoid look | The MEN that does not feature parathyroid disease. Also RET |

Confirm a rare third tumor rather than stacking eponyms. The testable point is which tumors travel, and that medullary thyroid is calcitonin, not thyroid hormone.

**Vignette.** A patient has episodic headache and palpitations. A thyroid nodule is calcitonin-positive. Calcium is normal.
**Reasoning.** A catecholamine spell plus a calcitonin tumor is the familial cluster that includes pheochromocytoma and medullary thyroid. The diagnosis is that cluster. The trap is Graves disease because of the palpitations. Graves does not make calcitonin.

### Congenital adrenal enzyme blocks

The pathway is cholesterol to mineralocorticoid, glucocorticoid, and androgen. A block backs substrate up into the open lanes.

| Block | Blood pressure | Androgens | The salt |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 21-hydroxylase, the common one | Low if salt-wasting | High. A virilized XX newborn | Low sodium, high potassium, because aldosterone and cortisol both need that enzyme |
| 11-hydroxylase | High | High | A mineralocorticoid precursor builds up and holds salt, even though cortisol is low |
| 17-hydroxylase | High | Low. An under-virilized XY newborn | Mineralocorticoids pile up. Sex steroids cannot be made |

**Vignette.** A newborn XX infant has ambiguous genitalia, vomiting, and a high potassium in the second week of life.
**Reasoning.** Androgens were overproduced and cortisol and aldosterone were not. The diagnosis is 21-hydroxylase deficiency. The trap is 11-hydroxylase, which virilizes but raises blood pressure. This baby is losing salt.

**NBME trap.** They ask which steroid is high and which is low. Draw the three lanes. Do not memorize a single “CAH” picture.

### More worked vignettes

**Vignette.** A man has a large tongue, a headache, sweating, and a glucose that is newly high. IGF-1 is high. The visual fields are closing in from the sides.
**Reasoning.** Growth hormone after the plates closed, plus a mass at the chiasm. The diagnosis is acromegaly from a pituitary adenoma. The trap is “just type 2 diabetes” because of the glucose. The face and the fields are the hormone.

**Vignette.** After a prolonged labor with bleeding, a woman cannot lactate and is fatigued. TSH and cortisol are low. The pituitary fossa may be empty on imaging later.
**Reasoning.** The enlarged pituitary of pregnancy was ischemic. Several hormones fell at once. The diagnosis is Sheehan syndrome, a postpartum pituitary infarction. The trap is a single postpartum thyroiditis. Thyroiditis would not take the cortisol and the milk.

**Vignette.** A child has a low calcium, a high phosphate, a high PTH, and short fourth metacarpals.
**Reasoning.** PTH is high and the kidney is not answering it. The diagnosis is pseudohypoparathyroidism. The trap is hypoparathyroidism, which has a low PTH.

**Vignette.** Fasting hypoglycemia, a high insulin, and a high C-peptide. A sulfonylurea screen is negative. Imaging is aimed at the pancreas.
**Reasoning.** The beta cell is secreting on its own. The diagnosis is an insulinoma. The trap is injected insulin, which would suppress C-peptide.

## Stem craft

Axes and calcium, with the level named last.

The vignette withholds the name. The reasoning earns it.

**Vignette 1.** Low free T4 and a high TSH. The gland is small and firm. Antibodies are present.
**Reasoning.** Primary hypothyroidism, Hashimoto in the classic autoimmune stem. Secondary disease would not raise TSH. The trap is a low TSH with a low hormone, which is the pituitary.

**Vignette 2.** ACTH is very high and does not suppress. Cortisol is high. A lung mass is present. Potassium is low.
**Reasoning.** Ectopic ACTH. A pituitary source often suppresses on the high-dose test in the classic teaching. An adrenal tumor would suppress ACTH. The trap is one word, Cushing.

**Vignette 3.** Fasting hypoglycemia, high insulin, low C-peptide.
**Reasoning.** Injected insulin. An insulinoma and a sulfonylurea both raise C-peptide. The trap is going to the pancreas first.

**Vignette 4.** A high calcium that is mild, a low urine calcium, and a family pattern. PTH is not suppressed.
**Reasoning.** The calcium sensor is numb. Familial hypocalciuric hypercalcemia. Do not take the glands out. Primary hyperparathyroidism wastes calcium in the urine.

**Vignette 5.** Episodic headache and palpitations. A thyroid nodule stains for calcitonin.
**Reasoning.** Pheochromocytoma plus medullary thyroid carcinoma. Block alpha before beta if you block at all. The trap is Graves disease for the palpitations.

## Filing the stem

**Vignette.** A woman on estrogen has a high total T4, a normal free T4, and a normal TSH. She feels well.
**Reasoning.** The binding protein rose. The free hormone did not. This is not hyperthyroidism. The trap is treating a total.

**Vignette.** Cortisol is low, ACTH is low, potassium is normal, and the skin is not dark. A pituitary mass is on the film.
**Reasoning.** Secondary adrenal failure. Aldosterone is mostly angiotensin-driven, so potassium stays nearer normal and there is no ACTH to pigment the skin. Primary failure is the dark, hyperkalemic one.

**Vignette.** A glucose of 40, a high insulin, and a high C-peptide. The sulfonylurea screen is negative.
**Reasoning.** The beta cell is secreting. Injected insulin would suppress C-peptide. The diagnosis is an insulinoma. The trap is a factitious story you did not earn.

**Vignette.** After a thyroidectomy the calcium is low, the phosphate is high, and the PTH is low.
**Reasoning.** The parathyroids went with the thyroid or their blood supply did. Pseudohypoparathyroidism has a high PTH. The trap is vitamin D deficiency, which raises PTH.

## Checklist

- [ ] Primary versus secondary before the disease name.
- [ ] Stalk compression raises prolactin and drops the rest.
- [ ] Aldosterone follows angiotensin. Cortisol follows ACTH.
- [ ] High hormone plus low uptake is leak or exogenous, not overproduction.
- [ ] Insulin moves potassium in. That is why it shows up in hyperkalemia pathways later.

## Common traps

- Calling every low TSH hyperthyroidism. Low TSH with low free hormone is central hypothyroidism.
- Treating SIADH with the diabetes-insipidus logic. The urine is concentrated in SIADH and dilute in untreated diabetes insipidus.
- Memorizing steroid dose equivalents. Not this exam’s job.
- Quoting an A1C goal. Diagnostic thresholds and goals are ADA Standards of Care and live in the Step 2 and Step 3 books.

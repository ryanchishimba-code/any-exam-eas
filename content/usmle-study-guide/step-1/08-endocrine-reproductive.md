# Endocrine and reproductive mechanisms

## Framework

This is the heaviest Step 1 system range in the recorded outline. Learn axes, receptor class, and the one enzyme that commits a pathway.

**Peptide versus steroid**

| Hormone class | Receptor | What that means |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Peptide, catecholamine | Membrane. Second messengers. | Fast. Stored in vesicles. |
| Steroid, thyroid hormone | Intracellular. Transcription. | Slower. Carried on proteins. Free hormone is the active fraction. |

**Axes**

| Axis | The loss that shows |
| --- | --- |
| CRH–ACTH–cortisol | Primary adrenal failure: low cortisol, high ACTH, high potassium, low sodium, dark skin. Secondary: low ACTH, no dark skin, potassium often normal because aldosterone is mostly renin. |
| TRH–TSH–T4 | Primary hypothyroidism: high TSH, low free T4. |
| GnRH–LH/FSH–sex steroid | Pulsatile GnRH is required. Continuous GnRH shuts the axis down. That is the mechanism of the long-acting analogs. |

Insulin puts GLUT4 in muscle and fat. The liver does not need insulin to take up glucose, but insulin turns on glycogen synthesis and turns off gluconeogenesis. Glucagon does the opposite in the liver. Type 1 is no insulin, so ketoacidosis can happen. Type 2 is resistance first. The diagnosis numbers and the drug choice by comorbidity are Step 2 work. Here, know why ketoacids appear: no insulin means hormone-sensitive lipase floods the liver with fatty acids.

**Cycle, only the turns**

Follicular phase builds estrogen. The LH surge follows sustained estrogen and ovulates. The corpus luteum makes progesterone, which warms the body and stabilizes endometrium. No fertilization, the luteum dies, progesterone falls, menses starts. Progesterone withdraws. It does not "build up until it bleeds" as a toxin.

Male: Leydig cells make testosterone under LH. Sertoli cells support sperm under FSH and make inhibin. DHT, via 5α-reductase, builds the external genitalia and the prostate. A 5α-reductase deficiency leaves internal male ducts (testosterone) and feminized external genitalia until puberty.

## Checklist

- [ ] Primary versus secondary by the pituitary hormone.
- [ ] Continuous GnRH suppresses. Pulsatile GnRH stimulates.
- [ ] No insulin, hormone-sensitive lipase, ketones.
- [ ] Progesterone falls, then menses.
- [ ] DHT is external genitalia and prostate. Testosterone is internal male ducts.

## Common traps

- Calling secondary adrenal failure "hyperpigmented." Pigment needs high ACTH.
- Treating thyroid hormone as a peptide. It acts like a steroid on transcription.
- Using a 5α-reductase story for androgen-receptor failure. Receptor failure cannot respond to testosterone or DHT.

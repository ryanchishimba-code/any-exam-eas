# Biostatistics and ethics

## Framework

This is a smaller Step 1 range than on Step 3, and it is still a cluster of items. The math is a 2-by-2 table. The ethics is what you may do when someone cannot decide.

**The square**

|  | Disease | No disease |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Test positive | True positive | False positive |
| Test negative | False negative | True negative |

Sensitivity is true positives over everyone with disease. A sensitive test, when negative, helps rule a disease out. Specificity is true negatives over everyone without disease. A specific test, when positive, helps rule a disease in. Those mnemonics fail when the prevalence is extreme. Positive predictive value falls when the disease is rare, even if the test is unchanged.

| Idea | The question it answers |
| --- | --- |
| Relative risk | Cohort. How many times more often the outcome happens in the exposed. |
| Odds ratio | Case-control. You did not follow people. You cannot quote a risk directly. |
| Number needed to treat | 1 divided by the absolute risk reduction. Not by the relative risk. |
| Lead-time bias | A screen finds the disease earlier, so survival looks longer without a later death. |
| Confounding | A third factor is tied to both the exposure and the outcome. |

Intention-to-treat keeps people in the group they were assigned to, even if they quit the drug. That protects the randomization. Per-protocol answers a different question and can cheat the balance.

**Ethics that Step 1 actually asks**

A capable adult decides. Capacity is a clinical judgment for this decision: understand, appreciate, reason, and express a choice. It is not the same word as competence, which is a legal finding. A surrogate uses the patient's known wishes, then the patient's values, then best interest. You do not lie because the family asked you to. You do not treat a refusing capable adult because the family is scared. Confidentiality bends for a serious danger to an identifiable person, and for reportable diseases. Confirm the local duty. The exam wants the principle.

## Checklist

- [ ] Sensitivity uses the disease column. Specificity uses the no-disease column.
- [ ] Predictive value moves when prevalence moves.
- [ ] Odds ratio is the case-control measure.
- [ ] Capacity is decision-specific.
- [ ] A capable refusal stands.

## Common traps

- Quoting relative risk from a case-control study.
- Treating a longer survival after screening as proof the screen works, without asking about lead time.
- Letting a family override a capable patient.

# How Step 1 works

## The outline

Step 1 asks you to apply foundational science to a patient. USMLE reports the result as pass or fail. Confirm that reporting, and the current timing, on the bulletin before you sit. This book does not state a passing standard, a pass rate, or an item count.

The physician-task ranges below are the ones recorded from the USMLE content outline on 2026-09-06. They are ranges, not a promise of what your form will contain. The live outline wins if it has changed.

| Task | Recorded range |
| --- | --- |
| Applying foundational science | 60–70% |
| Diagnosis | 20–25% |
| Communication and systems-based practice | 6–9% |
| Practice-based learning | 4–6% |

Discipline ranges overlap, so they do not add to 100%. That is how the outline is built. Pathology is the largest share (about 45–55% in the recorded table). Physiology is about 30–40%. Pharmacology, microbiology, and anatomy are smaller and still enough to miss a form if you skip them.

**How a question is built**

1. The vignette gives a mechanism in disguise: a lab, a drug effect, or a finding.
2. Name the process, not the eponym, first.
3. The answer is the step that must be true if that process is true.
4. If two answers are true, pick the one the stem asked (cause, consequence, or next molecular step).

## Common traps

- Memorizing a percent as if your form is the midpoint.
- Answering with a diagnosis when the stem asked for the mechanism.
- Ignoring a 5% discipline because it looks small. Five percent of a form is still a cluster of items.

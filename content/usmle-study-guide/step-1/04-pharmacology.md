# Pharmacology

## Framework

Step 1 pharmacology is a receptor, a curve, and a toxicity that follows from the mechanism. Doses are not the point.

**Autonomic map**

| Receptor | When you turn it on | When you block it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| α1 | Constrict vessels, dilate the pupil | Drop blood pressure, block urinary smooth muscle |
| β1 | Faster and stronger heart, renin | Slow the heart. Careful in a failing heart if you block too fast. |
| β2 | Open bronchi, dilate some vessels | Wheeze if a nonselective blocker hits a patient who needs the airway |
| Muscarinic | Rest-and-digest, including SLUDGE | Dry mouth, urinary retention, hot skin, delirium |

A shift of the dose-response curve to the right with the same maximum is competitive. A lower maximum is noncompetitive or irreversible. A partial agonist can antagonize a full agonist by occupying the receptor.

**Kinetics that change an answer**

| Idea | What it means on a question |
| --- | --- |
| Volume of distribution | A huge Vd means the drug left the plasma. Dialysis will not catch it. |
| Clearance and half-life | Half-life falls when clearance rises or Vd falls. Steady state is about four half-lives. |
| Zero order | A fixed amount per hour. Phenytoin, aspirin at high dose, alcohol. A small dose change is a big level change. |
| First pass | Oral dose is chewed up by the liver before it circulates. |

Inducers (rifampin, phenytoin, carbamazepine, chronic alcohol) lower levels of drugs that use those enzymes. Inhibitors (azoles, macrolides, ritonavir, acute alcohol) raise them. Name the direction before you name the drug.

**Toxicity that is the mechanism**

| Drug | The harm that follows |
| --- | --- |
| Acetaminophen | NAPQI when glutathione is gone. N-acetylcysteine refills it. |
| Aspirin | Early respiratory alkalosis, then anion-gap acidosis. Tinnitus. |
| Aminoglycosides | Kidney and ear. The drug sits in those cells. |
| Bleomycin | Lung. |
| Doxorubicin | Heart. |
| Cyclophosphamide | Hemorrhagic cystitis. Mesna is for the bladder metabolite. |
| ACE inhibitor | Cough, high potassium, fetal harm, angioedema. |

## Checklist

- [ ] Competitive versus a lower maximum.
- [ ] Four half-lives to steady state, if the dose interval is regular.
- [ ] Zero-order drugs do not forgive a dose increase.
- [ ] Inducer lowers the victim drug. Inhibitor raises it.
- [ ] Match the famous toxicity to the organ the mechanism hits.

## Common traps

- Stopping an ACE inhibitor cough by switching to another ACE inhibitor. The class does this. An ARB is the usual next conversation, if a blocker of that axis is still wanted.
- Dialyzing a drug with a huge volume of distribution.
- Calling every antibiotic failure "resistance" when the problem is an inducer that dropped the level.

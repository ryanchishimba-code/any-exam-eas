# A Step 1 study plan

> **Study aid only.** A plan is a way to spend hours. It is not a prediction of a result.

## What this chapter covers

How to spend a finite number of weeks on the outline you just read, and how this book sits next to the question bank and the study feed. It does not prescribe a commercial schedule, a daily item count, or a score target.

## The framework

Three passes, always in this order:

1. **Mechanism.** One chapter here. Leave with the tables, not with a reread.
2. **Questions.** A block on that system in the question bank the same day. Misses teach more than a second read.
3. **One association.** A few study-feed cards for the facts you could not retrieve. The card is the hook. The chapter is the reason the hook is true.

Do not invert that order. Cards without a mechanism become trivia. A chapter without questions becomes a false sense of fluency.

Weight the weeks by the published ranges, not by nostalgia. Reproductive plus endocrine, respiratory plus renal, and nervous system plus behavior are the heavy groups. Cardiovascular, blood and immune, musculoskeletal and skin, and multisystem processes are close behind. Biostatistics is smaller and unusually cheap to learn, so it does not wait until the last weekend.

## A simple week

| Day | Work |
| --- | --- |
| New system | One chapter. Write the framework in your own words before any cards. |
| Same day | Question-bank items on that system. Review only the misses and the lucky guesses. |
| Next day | The feed cards that match the misses. Then a short mixed set so yesterday’s system is not cued by the chapter title. |
| Later in the week | A second system. Keep one mixed block so the first system does not fade. |
| End of week | Re-do the misses. If the mechanism is still fuzzy, return to the table, not to a new resource. |

If your test date is soon, cut new reading before you cut questions. An unread chapter can be a table. An unworked miss cannot.

## What to put on one page

For each disease you keep missing, four lines only:

- Normal process
- The broken step
- The finding that proves it
- The nearest wrong answer, and why it is the neighbor rather than the answer

That page replaces a second textbook.

## Checklist

- [ ] The week’s systems match the outline ranges, not the order of a syllabus.
- [ ] Every new chapter has questions the same day.
- [ ] Feed cards are tied to misses, not scrolled as a separate hobby.
- [ ] Biostatistics and ethics have a place before the last week.
- [ ] You can explain a miss without looking at the option list.

## Common traps

- Adding another resource when the miss was a reasoning error. A new book does not fix a skipped normal-process step.
- Studying in organ-system order from school and never mixing. The form is mixed.
- Equating hours with readiness. Hours spent rereading a chapter you already understand do not move a weak system.
- Saving all of pharmacology for the end. Mechanisms of drugs belong inside the organ chapter where the receptor lives.

## Practice this in the Qbank

Use the bank as the second pass, not as a score ritual. After a block, sort misses into “did not know the normal process” and “knew it and still picked the neighbor.” Only the first pile sends you back to a chapter. The second pile is a reread of the stem. The feed is the third pass for the first pile.

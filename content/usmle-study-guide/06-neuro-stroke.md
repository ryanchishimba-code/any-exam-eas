# Chapter 6 — Neurology & Stroke

> **Study aid only.** Time-is-brain sequencing. Confirm current lytic windows and BP targets before clinical use.

## Why this chapter matters

Neuro emergencies are sequencing items: last-known-well, non-contrast CT, then a **BP target that flips** the moment you decide to lyse. Small order errors are easy to write and easy to miss.

## Core concepts

- **Ischemic stroke:** Non-contrast CT first to exclude hemorrhage → IV thrombolysis within the eligible window (classically 4.5 h for alteplase/tenecteplase in selected patients).
- **Large-vessel occlusion:** Thrombectomy up to 24 h when imaging shows salvageable penumbra.
- **BP:** Permissive up to 220/120 if *not* lysing. Lower to <185/110 *before* thrombolytics.
- **ICH:** Reverse warfarin with 4-factor PCC + vitamin K; SBP target often ~140; neurosurgery for cerebellar bleed >3 cm or herniation.
- **SAH:** Thunderclap → CT (most sensitive early) → LP for xanthochromia if CT is negative. Nimodipine to prevent vasospasm.
- **Status epilepticus:** IV lorazepam (IM midazolam if no IV) → fosphenytoin / levetiracetam / valproate → anesthetic drip.
- **Glucose** on every acute neuro change. Hypoglycemia mimics stroke.
- **Wernicke:** Confusion, ataxia, ophthalmoplegia — IV thiamine before glucose in at-risk patients.

## Clinical applications

- AF, aphasia + arm weakness at 2 hours, CT clean, not anticoagulated — lytic checklist, not “CTA first, aspirin later.”
- BP 190/115 and otherwise eligible — treat BP, then lyse.
- Worst headache of life, CT negative — LP. Do not discharge.
- Still seizing at 5 minutes — second-line AED; do not watch and wait for “it to stop.”
- New deficit after a seizure may be Todd’s — still exclude stroke.

## Comparison — stroke BP

| Situation | Target idea | Trap |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ischemic, no lytic | Permissive (up to ~220/120) | Dropping to “normal” and losing penumbra |
| Pre-thrombolysis | <185/110 | Giving tPA at 200/120 |
| ICH | Often SBP ~140 | Ignoring coagulopathy reversal |

## Misconceptions

- Aspirin before the CT.
- Discharging thunderclap after a normal CT.
- Calling every post-ictal weakness “Todd’s” without imaging.

## Pearls

- CT is to *exclude* blood, not to prove ischemia.
- Thiamine is cheap; Wernicke is not.
- Status is a clock, not a vibe.

# Clinical review for the USMLE study guides

A physician should check every claim below against the current source before these books are shown to students. The chapters were written to the USMLE specifications and Bulletin available in November 2026, and to the guideline pages cited in each chapter. They are study aids. They are not orders.

Ranges, block counts, and passing standards are quoted from USMLE and can change. Where this file says “confirm,” the chapter’s wording should be edited if the source disagrees. Do not leave a stale number in the book because it was once correct.

## How to read this list

- **Source of truth:** the page that should win.
- **What to check:** the claim a wrong digit would hurt.

## Step 1 mechanisms

These are consensus physiology and pathology. A physician should still spot-check:

- Shock table: obstructive shock grouped with low cardiac output and high resistance. Tamponade versus constriction (y descent).
- Maneuvers: hypertrophic obstruction and mitral valve prolapse soften when preload rises.
- Carbon monoxide and standard two-wavelength pulse oximetry.
- Nephron: NKCC2 loops, NCC thiazides, calcium direction of those diuretics, RTA potassium patterns.
- Winter’s formula is named and not written out. If someone adds the equation, check it.
- Sexual differentiation fork (SRY, anti-Müllerian hormone, testosterone, DHT).
- Hemolysis versus obstruction and which bilirubin reaches the urine.
- B12 deficiency affects the cord; folate repletion does not fix it.
- Zone 3 of the liver lobule is the hypoxic center.
- Cord hemisection tract crossings.
- Pemphigus versus bullous pemphigoid level of the split.
- Osteomyelitis: Staphylococcus is the common organism; Salmonella is the sickle-cell classic and must not replace the common one.
- Refeeding hypophosphatemia. Thiamine before glucose in a depleted person.
- Warfarin’s early drop in protein C. Confirm this stays in the mechanism chapter and does not grow a dose.

No drug doses are intended as orders on Step 1. If a dose appears, remove it or move it to a Step 2 or Step 3 chapter with a source.

## Ethics and safety

Capacity versus competence, professional interpreters, disclosure of harmful error, and time-outs are principles, not statutes. A physician-reviewer who also knows the local jurisdiction should confirm nothing in the ethics chapters reads like a fake law. No state’s involuntary-hold statute is stated.

## What these books deliberately omit

- A minimum passing score, a pass rate, or a CCS point percent.
- National antibiotic menus that ignore local resistance.
- Chemotherapy cycle lengths and most doses.
- Parkland formula milliliters.
- A frozen GOLD letter scheme.

If a later editor adds any of those, the addition needs its own source line and a new row in this file.

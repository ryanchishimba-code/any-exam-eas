# Chapter 3 — Pulmonary

> **Study aid only.** COPD, PE, pneumonia, and pleural disease as next-best-step items.

## Why this chapter matters

Pulmonary stems cluster around COPD/asthma exacerbations, PE workup, pneumonia severity, and when to drain a pleural effusion. The board asks whether you choose **BiPAP before the tube**, **CT-PA when Wells is intermediate-high**, and **Light criteria before a reflex chest tube**.

## Core concepts

- **COPD exacerbation:** SABA/SAMA + systemic steroids (short course) + antibiotics when sputum is purulent or the patient is severe.
- **BiPAP:** pH roughly 7.25–7.35 with hypercapnia in COPD — reduces intubation and mortality. Do not jump to intubation if NIV is appropriate and the airway is protected.
- **Asthma:** Step up controller therapy; add ICS-formoterol as MART where guidelines support it. Status asthmaticus: continuous nebs, steroids, magnesium; intubation is last and dangerous.
- **PE:** Wells / gestalt first. PERC only to *exclude* when probability is already low. Intermediate-high → CT-PA (or V/Q if contrast is unsafe). Unstable massive PE → thrombolysis / embolectomy pathway, not a leisurely d-dimer.
- **CAP:** Outpatient healthy: amoxicillin, doxycycline, or a macrolide if local resistance is low. Inpatient non-ICU: beta-lactam + atypical coverage (or a respiratory FQ). ICU / MRSA risk: add vancomycin or linezolid — **never daptomycin for pneumonia**.
- **Pleural effusion:** Light criteria before assuming “just CHF fluid.” Complicated parapneumonic / empyema needs drainage.

## Clinical applications

- COPD, pH 7.28, PCO₂ 68, protecting airway — BiPAP + steroids + bronchodilators, not immediate RSI.
- Post-op dyspnea, Wells high, d-dimer not helpful — CT-PA.
- Hypotensive PE with RV strain — reperfusion, not heparin-and-hope alone.
- Community pneumonia plus ICU shock — cover MRSA/Pseudomonas based on risk; get cultures, do not delay the first hour of antibiotics in sepsis.

## Comparison — PE testing

| Pretest | Next test | Trap |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Low + PERC negative | Stop | Ordering a d-dimer “just in case” after PERC excludes |
| Low + PERC positive | D-dimer | Skipping to CT in a truly low-probability patient |
| Intermediate / high | CT-PA (or V/Q) | D-dimer to “rule out” high probability |
| Massive / unstable | Treat + urgent reperfusion | Sending an unstable patient through the scanner first |

## Misconceptions

- Daptomycin for MRSA pneumonia (surfactant inactivates it).
- D-dimer to clear a high-Wells patient.
- Intubating every COPD exacerbation with a pH of 7.30.

## Pearls

- NIV is a therapy, not a delay, when indicated.
- Light criteria exist so you do not drain every effusion — and so you do not leave empyema in the chest.

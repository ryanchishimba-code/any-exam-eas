# Chapter 4 — Renal & Electrolytes

> **Study aid only.** AKI category, AEIOU dialysis, and the potassium / sodium clocks.

## Why this chapter matters

Renal items hinge on a few discriminating numbers: FeNa, BUN:Cr, the potassium ECG, and the sodium correction rate. Get the **category** right and the next step writes itself.

## Core concepts

- **AKI:** Creatinine rise ≥0.3 mg/dL in 48 h, ≥1.5× baseline in 7 days, or oliguria.
- **Prerenal:** BUN:Cr >20, FeNa <1%, bland sediment, volume responsive.
- **ATN:** FeNa >2%, muddy-brown casts; ischemic or toxic.
- **Postrenal:** Hydronephrosis — relieve obstruction (Foley, stent, nephrostomy).
- **AEIOU dialysis:** Acidosis, Electrolytes (refractory K⁺), Ingestions (toxic alcohols, lithium, salicylate), Overload, Uremia (pericarditis, encephalopathy).
- **Hyperkalemia ladder:** Calcium if ECG changes → insulin+dextrose / albuterol shift → remove (diuretic, binder, dialysis).
- **Hyponatremia:** Volume status first. SIADH is euvolemic, urine osm >100, urine Na typically >40.
- **Chronic hyponatremia correction:** ≤8 mEq/L/24 h to avoid osmotic demyelination. Seizing / severely symptomatic: small 3% saline aliquots, then stop.

## Clinical applications

- Post-op oliguria, BUN:Cr 33, FeNa 0.4% — fluid challenge, not a diuretic.
- On diuretics, FeNa lies — use FEUrea (<35% suggests prerenal).
- Peaked T waves or widening QRS — IV calcium *first*.
- Refractory hypokalemia — replete magnesium.
- Thunderclap is not this chapter; seizing hyponatremia is 3% saline, not a 2-liter normal-saline bolus.

## Comparison — AKI

| Category | BUN:Cr | FeNa | Clue |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Prerenal | >20 | <1% | Volume responsive; bland sediment |
| Intrinsic (ATN) | ~10–15 | >2% | Muddy-brown casts |
| Postrenal | Variable | Variable | Hydronephrosis; relieve obstruction |

## Misconceptions

- Kayexalate as the first move in a wide-complex hyperkalemia.
- Rapidly “normalizing” chronic sodium because the number looks scary.
- Calling all rising creatinine “ATN” without a post-void residual.

## Pearls

- Calcium buys electrical stability; it does not lower potassium.
- AEIOU is a memory hook, not a suggestion to dialyze every elevated creatinine.

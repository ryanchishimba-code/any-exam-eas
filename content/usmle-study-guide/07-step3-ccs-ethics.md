# Chapter 7 — Step 3 CCS, Ethics & Biostats

> **Study aid only.** CCS sequencing, consent/capacity, and abstract/ad appraisal. Confirm current Step 3 format in the official bulletin.

## Why this chapter matters

Step 3 adds **order-entry thinking** and **critical appraisal**. A correct diagnosis with the wrong sequence still fails the case. Drug-ad and abstract items punish relative-risk theater.

## CCS loop

1. **Stabilize** — ABCs, IV access, monitors, glucose, pregnancy test when relevant, NPO if a procedure is coming.
2. **Diagnose** — targeted labs and imaging. Do not order the whole hospital.
3. **Treat** — the disease *and* the complication (DVT prophylaxis, GI prophylaxis, counsel, vaccines).
4. **Monitor** — advance the clock. Recheck the vital that made you nervous. Interval follow-up in clinic cases.

If the patient is crashing, treat first. Then confirm.

## Next-best-step examples

- Chest pain + ST elevation — aspirin, anticoagulation, cath activation. Do not start with a stress test.
- Febrile neutropenia — cultures, then anti-pseudomonal beta-lactam inside an hour.
- Suspected SAH, CT negative — LP.
- New AF, hypotensive — cardiovert; do not “try metoprolol.”

## Ethics & legal (high-yield)

- **Capacity** is decision-specific: understand, appreciate, reason, express a choice.
- A capacitated adult may refuse even life-saving care. Document and offer alternatives.
- No capacity → surrogate (health-care proxy, then next of kin per jurisdiction). Advance directives beat family preference when valid.
- **Confidentiality** exceptions: harm to identifiable others, abuse reporting, some communicable diseases — know the theme, verify local law.
- Minors: emergencies first; contraception / STI / pregnancy rules vary — the exam usually flags the emancipated or emergency path.
- Do not lie to a patient because a family member asked you to.

## Biostats & ads

- **NNT = 1 / ARR.** Relative risk reduction without event rates is marketing.
- Surrogate endpoints (LDL, tumor shrinkage) are not hard outcomes (death, MI, stroke).
- CI that crosses the null is not “almost significant.”
- ITT preserves randomization; per-protocol can inflate efficacy.
- Lead-time and length-time bias inflate screening “survival.”
- Post-hoc subgroups are hypotheses, not labeling claims.

## Appraisal table

| Question | Look for | Red flag |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Design? | RCT | Observational ad as proof |
| Endpoint? | Hard outcome | Surrogate only |
| Absolute benefit? | Both event rates | RRR alone |
| Population? | Looks like your patient | Elderly / CKD excluded |
| Harm? | NNH | Buried adverse events |

## Pearls

- CCS is a *sequence*, not a shopping list.
- Capacity ≠ Glasgow Coma Scale.
- If they hide the ARR, calculate it or walk away from the claim.

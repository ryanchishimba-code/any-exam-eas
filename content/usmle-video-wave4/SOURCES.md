## Step 2 CK

### 31. A Fever Too Young to Send Home
Pantell RH, et al. Evaluation and Management of Well-Appearing Febrile Infants 8 to 60 Days Old. Pediatrics. 2021;148(2):e2021052228.
Spoken: 38.0°C (100.4°F); ages 8–21, 22–28, and 29–60 days; not under 8 days; born at 37 weeks to under 42 weeks; urine by catheter or suprapubic aspiration; cultures negative 24–36 hours; temperature above 38.5°C; procalcitonin above 0.5 ng/mL; CRP above 20 mg/L; absolute neutrophil count above 4,000 to 5,200 per mm³.
Flag: the full abnormal-marker branch for days 29–60 was not extracted line by line, so the video says abnormal markers change the plan and does not add a further numeric action.

### 32. The Next Psych Medicine, and Who Cannot Wait
VA/DoD Clinical Practice Guideline for the Management of Major Depressive Disorder, 2022.
ASAM Clinical Practice Guideline on Alcohol Withdrawal Management, 2020.
Spoken: psychotherapy or one medicine by preference; SSRI, SNRI, bupropion, mirtazapine, trazodone, vilazodone, or vortioxetine; against initial tricyclic, MAOI, nefazodone, ketamine, or esketamine; four to six weeks after a maximized dose; psychotherapy first for mild or moderate depression in pregnancy or while breastfeeding; CIWA-Ar under 10, 10–18, and 19 or higher; benzodiazepine first line for moderate or severe withdrawal; phenobarbital only for clinicians who use it often.

### 33. Four Pillars for a Weak Pump
2022 AHA/ACC/HFSA Guideline for the Management of Heart Failure.
Spoken: ejection fraction 40% or less, 41–49%, and 50% or more with high filling pressure; four classes including an SGLT2 inhibitor for a reduced pump; class 2a for an SGLT2 inhibitor when ejection fraction is preserved; class 2b for a mineralocorticoid antagonist and an ARNI in that setting; class 3 no benefit for routine nitrates or a PDE-5 inhibitor for activity or quality of life; class 1 blood-pressure treatment. The European class 1 is not used as the U.S. recommendation.

### 34. A Bleed After the Baby
ACOG Practice Bulletin No. 183 (2017), reVITALize definition.
Spoken: at least 1000 mL, or signs of hypovolemia, within 24 hours, any route; 500–999 mL increases supervision; more than 500 mL after a vaginal birth is abnormal; atony about 70–80%; tone, trauma, tissue, thrombin; tamponade such as an intrauterine balloon when medicine fails; about 1500 mL or abnormal vital signs means prepare transfusion.
Flag: a 2025 ACOG clinical practice update is listed on the bulletin page. This episode does not quote that update, because its full text was not read. The balloon statement is from Practice Bulletin 183.

### 35. Potassium You Treat Now, and When You Dialyze
2025 AHA ECC guidelines, hyperkalemia section (cardiac arrest). ILCOR 2025 non-arrest pharmacology table. KDIGO AKI 2012, recommendations 5.1.1 and 5.1.2.
Spoken: adult hyperkalemia above 5.0; severe hyperkalemia in hospitalized adults described as above 6.5; calcium is not a proven survival benefit in arrest and does not lower potassium; insulin with glucose, pooled change about 0.7 per liter; start renal replacement for life-threatening fluid, electrolyte, or acid-base changes, not from one creatinine.

### 36. Calories That Change the Plan
AGA 2022 pharmacological obesity guideline. ASPEN refeeding consensus, Nutrition in Clinical Practice, 2020.
Spoken: BMI 30 or higher, or 27 or higher with a weight-related condition, after lifestyle has not been enough, add a medicine (strong); semaglutide 2.4 mg weekly and liraglutide 3 mg daily are suggestions; suggest against orlistat; thiamine 100 mg before feeding or dextrose fluid; 100 mg daily for 5–7 days when starvation, alcohol, or deficiency signs are present; first day 100–150 g dextrose or 10–20 kcal/kg; advance about one third of the goal every 1–2 days; delay if phosphorus, potassium, or magnesium are severely low; drops within 5 days of 10–20% mild, 20–30% moderate, above 30% or organ dysfunction severe.
Flag: no surgery threshold is spoken. AGA 2022 is a drug guideline.

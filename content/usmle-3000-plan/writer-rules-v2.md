# USMLE writer rules, version 2

Batches 9 and 10 were mostly scored thin. The notes repeat one cause: the stem already named the contrast. "Battery plant, wrist drop, and stippling" is lead. "Neutrophils in the first hour" is preformed antibody. "The drug closes a potassium channel" is the answer written into the vignette. A second patient who has the opposite textbook picture does not add a step if each picture is already labeled.

Items that scored sound did not name the mechanism. They gave numbers, and the keyed option was an inference those numbers do not state: a bicarbonate of 12 predicts a carbon dioxide near 26, so 40 is a second disorder; a normal pH with an anion gap of 28 hides two disorders; heparin that does not prolong the time, plus heavy protein loss, means the cofactor is gone.

## What a stem may contain

Raw findings only.

- Vital signs, lab numbers, timed intervals, doses, and imaging measurements.
- Exam findings described as what is seen or felt, not as the name of a sign or syndrome.
- A classmate's plan or reading, stated as an action, so the question can ask why it fails.
- One number that makes the classmate's plan look reasonable.

## What a stem must not contain

- The diagnosis, the syndrome name, or the mechanism words that appear in the keyed option.
- The drug's action ("closes a channel", "blocks reuptake", "lyses the wall") when that action is the answer.
- A classic triad written in the words a textbook uses for it.
- An interpretive label that is the distinguishing feature ("euvolemic", "selective albuminuria", "fusion beat", "delta wave", "frameshift").
- A second case whose only job is to display the opposite named disease.

## The two steps

1. The student interprets the raw numbers into a state the stem never names (a predicted value, a hidden gap, a time course, a missing cofactor).
2. That state kills the classmate's plan. The killing sentence is the keyed option, and it is not paraphrased in the stem.

If a reader who only pattern-matches the stem can pick the key without calculating or recalling an unstated step, the item is thin. Rewrite it.

## Options

Five options. Exactly one is medically best. Each distractor is what a student would pick after misreading one number or stopping after the first step. Do not use a distractor the raw findings have already made impossible in a single glance, and do not use a second option that is also medically right.

The question must not use these words: next step, most appropriate, administer, start, give, treat, management, priority. If the decision is a treatment choice, the explanation or citation must include a year or one of AHA, ASA, ACOG, CDC, IDSA, KDIGO, GINA, ADA, USPSTF, ACG, ASH.

Every option string appears in the explanation. The exam takeaway is at least 40 characters and appears in the explanation. The scenario contains "year-old patient" or "month-old patient". The blueprint tag is the official 2026 slug for what the keyed answer tests, valid for that Step. Step 1 does not use a Step 2-only slug.

## Numbers must agree

If the stem prints pH, bicarbonate, and carbon dioxide together, they must fit pH = 6.1 + log10(HCO3 / (0.03 × PCO2)) within 0.02. Do not print the pH that would exist only after a compensation the item says has not happened. Other formulas (anion gap, osmolality, alveolar gas, half-life, predictive value) must use the numbers in the stem and land on the value the keyed option relies on.

## Check before keeping an item

Read the stem with the key covered. If the diagnosis or the mechanism is already named, discard the item. If the key is just the stem's contrast restated, discard it.

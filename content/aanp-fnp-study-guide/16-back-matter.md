## FAQ

### How many questions are on the AANP FNP exam (AANPCB FNP-C)?

**150** total — **135 scored** + **15 pretest**. You will not know which items are pretest, so treat every question seriously. Confirm current details in the official AANPCB Candidate Handbook.

### What are the official Domain I weights?

| Process | Items | Weight |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Assess | 43 | **32%** |
| Diagnose | 36 | **26.5%** |
| Plan | 36 | **26.5%** |
| Evaluate | 20 | **15%** |

Coach tip: if you habitually jump to a drug name, you are under-practicing Assess and Diagnose — the majority of Domain I.

### Is this book for ANCC FNP-BC too?

It is **AANP/AANPCB-first**. ANCC uses a separate content outline and scoring approach. If you sit ANCC, use ANCC’s official outline for weights and domains. Clinical ADPE chapters still strengthen judgment across either exam, but do **not** memorize AANPCB percents for an ANCC attempt (or the reverse).

### How long should I study?

Depends on baseline, work hours, and how recently you finished clinical rotations. Many candidates use structured **4–8 week** plans; shorter intensives can work when content is fresh and question volume is high. Consistency + daily questions + error-log review beat weekend cramming. Protect sleep in the final week.

### Is the book enough without a question bank?

**No — and that’s intentional.** This guide builds frameworks, can’t-miss filters, and lifespan twists. **Exam judgment is built by practicing stems**, reviewing rationales, and tagging misses by ADPE stage and age band. Pair this book with a strong Qbank and the official handbook.

### How do I use this book with a Qbank (step-by-step)?

1. Read one clinical chapter (or one topic block).  
2. Immediately do a focused Qbank block on that system (mixed age bands).  
3. Review **every** miss and every lucky guess — read full rationales.  
4. Tag the error log: **A/D/P/E** + age band (+ PREN / DISP / STEW when relevant).  
5. Skim the chapter’s MUST KNOW / TRAP boxes again.  
6. Weekly: one mixed timed set; rewrite your top 10 recurring traps.  
7. Final 7 days: follow Chapter 15’s sprint — light on −1.

If your Qbank shows a weak Domain I process (e.g., Evaluate), deliberately hunt items that ask about monitoring, follow-up, or revising the plan — don’t only drill diagnosis facts.

### What if I fail or feel far from ready?

Use your score report (when available) to map weak domains/age bands, then rebuild a shorter plan with higher question volume on those gaps. Failing once is data, not identity. Reconfirm eligibility, wait times, and retake rules in the official handbook — don’t rely on social-media retellings.

### Should I memorize drug doses?

Prefer **class + indication + safety filters + monitoring**. When a stem needs a dose, verify against current labeling/guidelines in real practice. This study aid uses teaching themes, not standing orders. Inventing precise mg values that aren’t in official materials is a waste of scarce memory.

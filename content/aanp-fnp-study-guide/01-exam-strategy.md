## Why this chapter matters

The AANP FNP exam is not a trivia bee. It asks whether you can practice as an **entry-level family nurse practitioner** safely across the lifespan. Domain I grades *how you think* (Assess → Diagnose → Plan → Evaluate). Domain II grades *who is in front of you* — and the weights are uneven on purpose: **Older Adult 30%**, **Middle Adult 26%**, **Young Adult\* 22%** (prenatal included) dwarf the pediatric bands. If your study plan is “adult cardiology and call it a day,” you will feel Domain II on test day.

This chapter locks the official numbers, teaches the ADPE habit, gives study plans that respect those weights, and names the traps that quietly sink strong clinicians.

## How the AANP FNP exam works (plain English)

### The big picture

| Fact | Detail (verify in current AANPCB Candidate Handbook) |
| --- | --- |
| Credential path | AANPCB **FNP-C** (this book’s primary focus) |
| Delivery | Computer-based testing (commonly Pearson VUE — confirm handbook) |
| Length | **150** questions total — **135 scored** + **15 pretest** |
| What you can’t do | Spot pretest items — treat every question seriously |
| Scored organization | **Domain I Practice** processes × **Domain II Patient Age** |
| Knowledge areas woven through items | Health Assessment, Pathophysiology, Therapeutics, Evidence-informed Practice (diagnostic reasoning, health promotion, SDoH, legal/ethical, procedures, transitions of care, etc.) |
| Result | Certification decision per AANPCB rules; attempt/retake policies — confirm handbook |

### Domain I — Practice (official — do not invent other percents)

| Process | Approx. scored items | Weight | Stem “feel” |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| **Assess** | 43 | **32%** | What else do you need? History, exam, risk, SDoH, stability |
| **Diagnose** | 36 | **26.5%** | Most likely vs must-not-miss; best next test |
| **Plan** | 36 | **26.5%** | Pharm/nonpharm, education, referral, shared decisions |
| **Evaluate** | 20 | **15%** | Did it work? Monitor what? Revise when? |

**Study implication:** Assess + Diagnose + Plan ≈ **85%** of Domain I. Evaluate is smaller but high-yield for monitoring, follow-up timing, adherence, and “therapy failed — rethink diagnosis.”

### Domain II — Patient Age (official — do not invent other percents)

| Age category | Weight | What “ignore me” costs you |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Newborn | **2%** | Tiny %, brutal misses (fever, jaundice, feeding, congenital screens) |
| Infant | **3%** | Well child, fever, bronchiolitis themes |
| Toddler | **4%** | Development, AOM, safety, foreign body themes |
| Child | **4%** | School-age asthma/ADHD/well care |
| Adolescent\* | **9%** | Risk behavior, confidential care, sports, mental health |
| Young Adult\* | **22%** | Reproductive health; **prenatal included** |
| Middle Adult | **26%** | Prevention + chronic disease peak |
| Older Adult | **30%** | Largest band — multimorbidity, Beers themes, falls, cognition, atypical presentations |

\*Includes prenatal. Age parameters are not fixed by AANPCB.

### Knowledge areas (how content sneaks into ADPE)

You will not see a separate “SDoH section score,” but SDoH appears inside Assess/Plan/Evaluate. Pathophysiology shows up when you explain *why* a test or drug fits. Therapeutics is Plan — with safety filters. Evidence-informed practice shows up in screening choices and shared decisions.

### If you’re taking ANCC (FNP-BC) — short callout only

ANCC’s **FNP-BC** uses a **different** test content outline. Clinical judgment overlaps; **weights and emphasis do not magically match AANPCB**. If ANCC is your exam, download ANCC’s official outline and use it as your weight map. This book remains useful for ADPE primary-care depth — recalibrate time allocation, don’t assume identical blueprints.

## Core framework: ADPE (maps to Domain I)

### 1) Assess (32%) — the biggest slice

Habits:
- Name stability first (ABCs, severe pain, SI, hypoxia).  
- Build HPI with a structure (OPQRST / OLD CARTS).  
- Targeted ROS/exam — depth matches danger.  
- Meds, allergies, substances, OTCs, contraceptives.  
- Social history + **SDoH** (food, housing, transport, safety, literacy, pharmacy access).  
- Age-band modifiers (developmental stage, pregnancy potential, frailty).  
- Ask: what information is missing before I act?

### 2) Diagnose (26.5%)

Habits:
- Hold three lanes: most likely · most dangerous · common mimics.  
- Choose tests that **change management**.  
- Know when clinical diagnosis is enough (many viral URIs, uncomplicated MSK).  
- Separate screening from diagnostic testing for symptomatic patients.

### 3) Plan (26.5%)

Habits:
- Nonpharm + pharm themes; verify current guidelines/doses before clinical use.  
- Patient education with teach-back and return precautions.  
- Referral timing (emergent / urgent / routine).  
- Opportunistic prevention in the same visit when safe.  
- Shared decisions when evidence is preference-sensitive (PSA themes, some mammography ages — verify USPSTF).

### 4) Evaluate (15%)

Habits:
- Define success metrics (BP log, A1c timing, PHQ-9, weight, peak flows).  
- Adverse effects and adherence barriers (including cost).  
- Know when to escalate, de-escalate, or **rethink the diagnosis**.  
- Transitions of care after ED/hospital.

> **HIGHLIGHT** — A gorgeous prescription fails Domain I if you skipped assessment cues or ordered the wrong first test.

## Supporting frameworks (use with ADPE)

### Red-flag filter

Before “routine primary care,” classify **emergent / urgent / routine**. Missing ectopic, ACS, PE, cauda equina, septic joint, meningitis themes, or suicidal crisis is how strong students fail soft stems.

### Patient-factor filter

Allergies · pregnancy/lactation · renal/hepatic function · age extremes · interacting drugs · adherence/cost · trauma-informed cues · cultural/language needs.

### Lifespan lens (Domain II)

After “what disease?,” ask “what changes because of age/stage?” Vaccines, milestones, Beers criteria themes, prenatal labs, adolescent confidentiality, atypical ACS in elders.

### Evidence-informed practice

Screening grades logic (offer / selective / discourage), overdiagnosis awareness, translating guidelines without cookbook rigidity, knowing when *not* to image.

### Elimination / keyword strategy

1. Find the real ask (which ADPE verb?).  
2. Predict answer category.  
3. Kill unsafe, age-blind, out-of-scope options.  
4. Choose the most patient-specific immediate action.

| Keyword vibe | Often means |
| --- | --- |
| Most appropriate next step | Timing + safety + ADPE stage |
| Initial / first | Stabilize or gather critical data |
| Most likely diagnosis | Pretest probability + pattern |
| Best initial test | Changes management — not fanciest |
| Educate / counsel | Teach-back + red-flag return precautions |
| Follow-up / monitor | Evaluate process |

## Common traps

| Trap | Fix |
| --- | --- |
| Studying only adult chronic disease | Force Domain II mix — Older Adult 30%, prenatal in YA\* |
| Memorizing drugs without ADPE | Name Assess/Diagnose before Plan every stem |
| Ignoring Evaluate | Weekly monitoring/follow-up drills |
| Treating ANCC = AANP | Lock your board’s official outline |
| Skipping peds because “small %” | Spaced pediatrics — high regret misses |
| Resource overload | One primary bank + handbook + this book |
| No timed practice | Build endurance |
| Inventing exact doses under stress | Know class/safety; verify teaching doses |
| Changing answers from anxiety | Change only for misread stems |
| Studying only strengths | Error log drives tomorrow’s set |

**Mindset:** Safe primary-care judgment across the lifespan — not trivia olympics.

## Practice strategy that actually improves scores

### Volume by phase

| Phase | Practice/day | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Foundation | 40–60 | Accuracy + ADPE labeling |
| Build | 60–100 | Mixed ages; hard presentations |
| Peak | Timed blocks + full sims | Endurance + cleanup |

**Quality rule:** Review rationales for wrong *and* lucky-right answers.

### 5-box error log (FNP-adapted)

1. Topic  
2. Age band (Domain II)  
3. Miss type (cue / priority / content / misread / ADPE stage / strategy)  
4. Correct rule (one sentence)  
5. Next drill  

### Practice % interpretation

Practice percentage is **not** an official exam score. Trends matter: better rationale understanding, fewer guesses, stronger Assess/Diagnose/Plan, steadier pacing across age bands.

## Blueprint-aligned weekly mix (use in any plan length)

When you build a mixed practice block, approximate Domain II gravity without obsessing over exact counts:

| Rough share of mixed adult-heavy weeks | Age focus |
| --- | --- |
| ~1/3 | Older Adult multimorbidity, Beers, falls, atypical ACS/infection |
| ~1/4 | Middle Adult prevention + chronic disease titration |
| ~1/5 | Young Adult\* reproductive + prenatal emergencies/basics |
| Remainder | Adolescent risk + pediatrics spaced (fever-by-age, AOM, asthma, ADHD) |

Inside each item, silently label Assess/Diagnose/Plan/Evaluate. If your misses cluster in Assess, you are jumping to drugs. If they cluster in Diagnose, you are under-building differentials. If Plan misses dominate, safety filters (pregnancy, kidneys, interactions) need drills. If Evaluate misses dominate, you are stopping at the prescription.

### Legal/ethical & procedures awareness (knowledge area — light but real)

Expect occasional stems on consent/assent themes, confidentiality (especially adolescent\*), mandatory reporting cues, scope/collaborative agreement awareness (state-specific — don’t invent law), and “which procedure is appropriate in primary care vs refer.” Stay high-level: protect safety, stay in scope, document, escalate.

### Transitions of care as Evaluate gold

Hospital discharge with new anticoagulant, insulin, or HF meds → early follow-up, med reconciliation, teach-back, red-flag return precautions. Missing this is a classic Evaluate miss pattern.

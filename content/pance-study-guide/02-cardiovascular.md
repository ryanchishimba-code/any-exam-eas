## System checklists

- **"Classic clue"** columns = standard textbook teaching points. They help you *recognize* a disease.

# Cardiovascular (11%): your biggest block

**Top 5 high-yield**

1. Blood pressure stages and when to start drugs
2. STEMI timing: cath lab vs clot-buster
3. Heart failure "four pillars"
4. AF: when to start a blood thinner
5. Statin groups

**Abbreviations:** BP = blood pressure. CVD = cardiovascular disease. CKD = chronic kidney disease. ACS = acute coronary syndrome. STEMI = ST-elevation heart attack. PCI = cath-lab stenting. HFrEF = heart failure with reduced ejection fraction. AF = atrial fibrillation. DOAC = direct oral anticoagulant. LDL = "bad" cholesterol. ECG = electrocardiogram. ARNI = angiotensin receptor–neprilysin inhibitor. MRA = mineralocorticoid receptor antagonist (e.g., spironolactone). SGLT2 inhibitor = a diabetes-drug class with heart and kidney benefit. CHA₂DS₂-VASc = AF stroke-risk score.

**Blood pressure stages [C1]**

| Stage | Top number | Bottom number |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Normal | <120 | and <80 |
| Elevated | 120–129 | and <80 |
| Stage 1 | 130–139 | or 80–89 |
| Stage 2 | ≥140 | or ≥90 |


![Flowchart: confirmed BP of 130/80 or higher. If clinical CVD, diabetes, CKD or PREVENT risk of 7.5% or more, start lifestyle plus medicine. Otherwise lifestyle for 3 to 6 months, then medicine if still 130/80 or higher.](visuals/05-htn-2025.svg)

*Figure: When to start BP medicine. 2025 AHA/ACC.*

**Checklist**

- [ ] **General BP goal: <130/80.** [C2]
- [ ] **Start BP drugs at ≥130/80** if the patient has heart disease, diabetes, CKD, or a 10-year risk ≥7.5% (PREVENT calculator). [C3]
- [ ] **Lower risk?** Try lifestyle for 3–6 months. Start drugs if BP stays ≥130/80. [C3]
- [ ] **First-line BP drug classes:** thiazide-type diuretic, long-acting calcium channel blocker (dihydropyridine), ACE inhibitor, or ARB. [C4]
- [ ] **STEMI:** cath lab (PCI) within **90 min** of first medical contact. Within **120 min** if transferring. [C5]
- [ ] **Can't get PCI within 120 min?** Give clot-busting drugs (fibrinolytics) within **30 min** of arrival, if no contraindication. [C5]
- [ ] **HFrEF four pillars:** ARNI (or ACE inhibitor/ARB), a proven beta-blocker, an MRA, and an SGLT2 inhibitor. [C6]
- [ ] **AF:** start a blood thinner at **CHA₂DS₂-VASc ≥2 (men) or ≥3 (women)**. [C7]
- [ ] **DOAC over warfarin** in AF, *except* mechanical heart valve or moderate–severe rheumatic mitral stenosis. [C7]
- [ ] **Statins:** known heart/vessel disease (ASCVD) → high-intensity. LDL ≥190 → high-intensity. Diabetes, age 40–75 → at least moderate-intensity. [C8]
- [ ] **ASCVD LDL goal:** <70 mg/dL (<55 if very high risk). [C8]
- [ ] **Kawasaki disease (child):** IV immune globulin (IVIG) 2 g/kg + aspirin, ideally within 10 days of illness onset. [C9]

**Look-alike chest pain**

| Disease | Classic clue (teaching point) | First test |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Heart attack (ACS) | Pressure-like pain, risk factors | ECG |
| Pericarditis | Sharp pain, better leaning forward; diffuse ST elevation | ECG |
| Aortic dissection | Tearing pain to the back; arm BP difference | Imaging of aorta |
| Pulmonary embolism | Sudden shortness of breath, fast heart rate | See PE algorithm |

**Algorithm: STEMI [C5]**

![Flowchart: ST elevation on ECG; if PCI possible within 90 minutes (120 with transfer) go to cath lab; otherwise, if symptoms under 12 hours, give a fibrinolytic within 30 minutes, then transfer.](visuals/10-stemi.svg)

*Figure: STEMI reperfusion. 2025 ACS guideline.*

*Text version:* PCI in time → cath lab. PCI not in time and symptoms <12 h → fibrinolytic within 30 min, then transfer.

> **Important to remember**
> - **ECG first** for chest pain.
> - **Trap:** choosing a clot-buster when the stem says PCI is available in time.
> - **The 2025 BP guideline** uses the PREVENT risk tool and a 7.5% cut-off.

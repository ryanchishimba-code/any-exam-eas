## Cardiovascular in depth

Cardiovascular disease is 11% of the PANCE, the largest organ system. The 2025 ACC/AHA acute coronary syndrome guideline and the 2025 AHA/ACC blood pressure guideline are the two documents that change the most answers.

**Blood pressure, beyond the stage table**

Confirm the reading with the correct cuff and repeated measurements. Start medication at an average of 130/80 mm Hg or higher when the patient has clinical cardiovascular disease, diabetes, chronic kidney disease, or a PREVENT 10-year risk of 7.5% or higher. If PREVENT risk is under 7.5% and those conditions are absent, use lifestyle treatment for 3 to 6 months and add a drug if the blood pressure remains 130/80 mm Hg or higher. The treatment goal in the guideline is under 130/80 mm Hg. First-line classes are a thiazide-type diuretic, a long-acting dihydropyridine calcium-channel blocker, an ACE inhibitor, or an ARB. Two drugs are reasonable when the pressure is well above goal. An ACE inhibitor and an ARB are not combined.

A blood pressure above 180/120 mm Hg without acute organ damage is hypertensive urgency: oral treatment and follow-up, not an intravenous drip that normalizes the pressure in an hour. The same number with encephalopathy, acute coronary syndrome, acute heart failure, dissection, eclampsia, or thrombotic microangiopathy is an emergency. Lower mean arterial pressure by about 25% in the first hour unless a specific condition sets a different target. Aortic dissection is lowered faster, toward a systolic pressure of about 120 mm Hg, usually with a beta-blocker before a vasodilator so the heart rate does not rise.

**Lipids**

The 2026 ACC/AHA dyslipidemia guideline uses the PREVENT equation, not the older pooled cohort equations. For adults 30 to 79 with LDL cholesterol 70 to 189 mg/dL, PREVENT 10-year risk is low under 3%, borderline from 3% to under 5%, intermediate from 5% to under 10%, and high at 10% or more. A statin is reasonable after a discussion at borderline risk, at least moderate intensity at intermediate risk, and high intensity at high risk. LDL cholesterol of 190 mg/dL or higher gets a maximally tolerated statin. Ages 40 to 75 with diabetes, CKD stage 3 or 4, or HIV are treated with LDL-lowering therapy. Very-high-risk atherosclerotic disease has an LDL goal under 55 mg/dL and a non-HDL goal under 85 mg/dL. Add ezetimibe, then a PCSK9 inhibitor or bempedoic acid, if the goal is not met. Measure lipoprotein(a) at least once.

**Coronary disease and heart failure**

Chest pain gets an ECG within 10 minutes of first medical contact. ST elevation is a STEMI. Primary PCI is preferred, with a first-medical-contact-to-device time of 90 minutes or less, or 120 minutes or less if transfer is required. If PCI cannot be reached within 120 minutes and symptoms began under 12 hours ago, give a fibrinolytic when there is no contraindication, then transfer. ST depression in V1 through V3 can be a posterior STEMI. Record V7 through V9. Do not choose a fibrinolytic when the stem says the cath lab is available in time.

NSTE-ACS starts with aspirin, an anticoagulant, and a P2Y12 inhibitor, plus an early invasive strategy when risk is high. Chronic coronary disease with angina is treated first with a beta-blocker or a calcium-channel blocker for symptoms, a short-acting nitrate for attacks, and a statin for risk. A beta-blocker is required long term when there has been a myocardial infarction within a year, when the ejection fraction is 50% or lower, or when another indication exists. It is not mandatory for every patient with stable angina.

HFrEF rests on four drug classes from the 2022 AHA/ACC/HFSA heart failure guideline, which remains the current ACC/AHA heart failure guideline: an ARNI (an ACE inhibitor or ARB if an ARNI is not possible), an evidence-based beta-blocker (carvedilol, metoprolol succinate, or bisoprolol), a mineralocorticoid receptor antagonist when the eGFR is above 30 and the potassium is under 5.0, and an SGLT2 inhibitor with or without diabetes. Do not give an ARNI with an ACE inhibitor or within 36 hours of the last ACE inhibitor dose. Acute decompensated heart failure with congestion is treated with an intravenous loop diuretic. Diltiazem, verapamil, and pioglitazone worsen HFrEF.

**Rhythm, valves, and vessels**

The 2023 ACC/AHA atrial fibrillation guideline recommends anticoagulation when the annual thromboembolic risk is about 2% or higher, which corresponds to a CHA2DS2-VASc score of 2 or more in men and 3 or more in women. A DOAC is preferred to warfarin except for a mechanical valve or moderate to severe mitral stenosis. Unstable atrial fibrillation is electrically cardioverted. Rate control with a beta-blocker or a non-dihydropyridine calcium-channel blocker is used when the ejection fraction is above 40%. If atrial fibrillation has lasted 48 hours or longer, anticoagulate for at least 3 weeks or exclude thrombus before cardioversion, and continue anticoagulation for at least 4 weeks after.

Severe aortic stenosis is the triad of exertional syncope, angina, and heart failure, with a harsh systolic murmur at the right upper sternal border that radiates to the carotids. Symptomatic severe stenosis is a valve referral. Obstructive hypertrophic cardiomyopathy is treated with a beta-blocker. Avoid a pure vasodilator and dehydration. A tearing pain to the back with a pulse or blood-pressure difference between the arms is aortic dissection until imaging excludes it. Calf pain with walking that resolves with rest, and a diminished pulse, is peripheral artery disease. An ankle-brachial index confirms it.

> **Important to remember**
> - PREVENT, not the pooled cohort equations, is the 2026 lipid risk tool. Very-high-risk disease: LDL cholesterol under 55 mg/dL.
> - Four heart-failure drugs. Never an ACE inhibitor and an ARNI together.
> - ECG first. Posterior leads when V1 through V3 show ST depression.

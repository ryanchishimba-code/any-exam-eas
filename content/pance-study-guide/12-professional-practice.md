## Professional practice in depth

Professional practice is 6% of the exam, and it is the one task area that is not also coded as medical content. The questions are rules, not drug doses.

**Consent, capacity, and confidentiality**

Informed consent covers the nature of the procedure, the risks, the benefits, and the alternatives, including no treatment. The person who performs the procedure is responsible for the consent. A signature on a form is not a substitute for the conversation. Decision-making capacity is the ability to understand, appreciate, reason, and state a choice. It is decision-specific and can change. Competence is a legal determination made by a court. A patient with capacity may refuse treatment. Document the refusal and the information you gave. Do not coerce, and do not abandon.

Share information with a consulting clinician who is treating the patient. HIPAA allows that disclosure for treatment without a separate written authorization. It does not allow a conversation in a hallway that identifies the patient, and it does not allow a family member to receive details when the patient has capacity and has said no. A patient with limited English proficiency gets a qualified interpreter. A minor child is not the interpreter for a consent conversation.

**Errors, abuse, and impairment**

Disclose a harmful error to the patient. An error that changes nothing visible is still disclosed when it caused harm or required a change. Report it through the institution. The disclosure is not a guess about blame. Suspected child abuse is reported. Certainty is not required. Older-adult abuse and neglect are reported under the state process. A colleague who appears impaired at work is a patient-safety duty. Tell the supervisor or the physician health program. Do not cover the shift in silence. A romantic or sexual relationship with a current patient is not permitted.

**Directives and the team**

An advance directive speaks for the patient who has lost capacity. A family request does not override a clear directive. When there is no directive, the surrogate decides as the patient would have. Optimal Team Practice, as the AAPA describes it, is team practice without a requirement for a specific supervisory agreement in law. Billing rules are separate. A physician assistant bills Medicare under the rules in effect for that setting. Do not invent a supervision percentage.

**Numbers the professional-practice items use**

Sensitivity is the share of people with disease who test positive. A sensitive test, when negative, helps rule disease out. Specificity is the share of people without disease who test negative. A specific test, when positive, helps rule disease in. Number needed to treat is 1 divided by the absolute risk reduction. These are foundational calculations that also appear as professional-practice items. State the definition the stem asks for. Do not invent a pass rate or a class score.

> **Important to remember**
> - Capacity is clinical. Competence is legal.
> - Report suspected child abuse. You do not need proof.
> - A negative result on a highly sensitive test makes the disease unlikely.

## Prescribing, leaving, and the words you use

**Controlled substances.** A prescription for a controlled substance needs a documented indication. It follows the DEA and the law of the state where the patient is treated. Do not post-date a prescription to get around a limit, and do not sign a blank form. When the state requires a prescription-drug monitoring program query, do the query before you write. The AAPA Guidelines for Ethical Conduct (2013) prohibit a sexual or romantic relationship with a current patient and require honesty when care causes harm.

**A patient who wants to leave.** An adult with decision-specific capacity may refuse admission or leave. Explain the risks in plain language, document that explanation, and invite the patient to return. Do not restrain a capacitated adult because you disagree. If capacity is absent, the refusal is not valid and you treat under the emergency or surrogate standard that applies.

**The interpreter.** A qualified interpreter is part of informed consent and of any serious diagnosis. HHS Section 1557 (2024) requires meaningful language access. A minor child is not the interpreter for that conversation. A bilingual staff member who is not qualified is not a substitute when the decision is high stakes.

**What you write.** The note records the facts, the decision, and the information the patient received. It is not a place to speculate about motive or to argue with a colleague. Do not put an identifiable case on social media. A hallway conversation that names the patient is a disclosure. HIPAA permits sharing with another clinician who is treating the patient. It does not permit sharing with a curious family member when the patient has capacity and has said no.

**Numbers without fiction.** Number needed to treat is 1 divided by the absolute risk reduction. Say that definition when the stem asks for it. Do not invent a pass rate, a class average, or a supervision percentage the stem did not give you.

## Dermatologic in depth

A pigmented lesion that is asymmetric, has an irregular border, has more than one color, is wider than 6 mm, or is evolving needs a biopsy. The ugly-duckling lesion, the one that does not match the patient's other moles, is included even when it is small. The biopsy for a lesion you think might be melanoma is a complete excision with a narrow (1 to 3 mm) margin of normal skin, deep enough to measure Breslow thickness. A superficial shave that cuts through the base ruins the depth measurement. Breslow thickness drives the wide-excision margin and whether a sentinel node is discussed. Mohs surgery is for many basal cell and squamous cell cancers on the face. It is not the first step for a suspected melanoma.

Basal cell carcinoma is a pearly papule with telangiectasias and a rolled border, often on the nose or another sun-exposed site. It almost never metastasizes, and it still destroys local tissue. Squamous cell carcinoma is a scaly, ulcerated, or keratinized nodule. It can metastasize, especially from the lip, ear, or a scar, and in a patient who is immunosuppressed. Actinic keratoses are the rough sand-paper precursors of squamous cell carcinoma. Cryotherapy or a field treatment such as 5-fluorouracil is used. A cutaneous horn or a lesion that ulcerates is biopsied, not frozen and forgotten.

**Rashes that look alike**

| Rash | Clue | First move |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Psoriasis | Extensor silvery plaques, nail pits, Auspitz pinpoint bleeding | Topical steroid plus a vitamin D analog. The AAD-NPF guidelines support systemic therapy or a biologic when body surface area is large or joints are involved |
| Atopic dermatitis | Flexural itch, personal or family atopy | Emollients and a topical corticosteroid. The AAD 2023–2024 atopic dermatitis guidelines support dupilumab or another advanced therapy for moderate to severe disease. Daily oral steroids are not a plan |
| Tinea corporis | Annular plaque, central clearing, scale at the edge | Topical terbinafine. Confirm with a KOH prep when the diagnosis is uncertain. Do not put a steroid on an undiagnosed annular rash |
| Candidiasis | Satellite pustules in a moist fold | Topical azole. Dry the skin. Look for diabetes |
| Pityriasis rosea | Herald patch, then a Christmas-tree pattern on the trunk | Reassurance in a well adolescent. It lasts weeks |
| Scabies | Night itch, burrows in finger webs, household contacts | Permethrin from neck (or scalp in infants) to toes, repeated in 1 week. Treat close contacts. Wash bedding |
| Seborrheic dermatitis | Scale in the brows, nasolabial folds, and scalp | Antifungal shampoo plus a low-potency steroid for flares |

**Infection of the skin**

The IDSA 2014 skin and soft-tissue guideline is still the IDSA guideline. Purulent infection (a boil, a carbuncle, an abscess) is incised and drained. Add an antibiotic that covers MRSA (trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole, doxycycline, or clindamycin) when there is extensive disease, systemic signs, immunosuppression, or a failed drainage. Nonpurulent cellulitis is usually streptococcal. Cephalexin or another beta-lactam is appropriate. MRSA coverage is added when the patient is severely ill, has a penetrating trauma, or has MRSA risk and is not improving. Mark the border. Recheck. A hot, swollen joint is septic arthritis until a tap says otherwise. Do not call it cellulitis and send the patient out. Necrotizing infection is pain out of proportion, bullae, crepitus, or rapid spread in a sick patient. That is surgical exploration, not another day of oral antibiotics. Group A strep and mixed organisms both do this.

Herpes zoster is a painful vesicular rash in one or two dermatomes. Antiviral treatment (acyclovir, valacyclovir, or famciclovir) is most useful within 72 hours, and it is still given later if new lesions are appearing or the patient is immunocompromised. The eye branch of the trigeminal nerve (tip of the nose, Hutchinson sign) needs ophthalmology. ACIP recommends recombinant zoster vaccine for adults 50 and older, and for adults 19 and older who are immunocompromised.

**Drug rashes**

A morbilliform rash a week into a penicillin course, in a well patient, is a simple exanthem. Stop the drug if you can. Stevens-Johnson syndrome and toxic epidermal necrolysis start with flu-like symptoms, then painful skin and mucosal erosions (eyes, mouth, genitals). Skin sloughs. Nikolsky sign may be present. Common triggers are allopurinol, sulfonamides, lamotrigine, carbamazepine, phenytoin, and some NSAIDs and antiretrovirals. Stop the drug. Treat the patient like a burn patient. A rash that involves mucosa is a reason to stop the drug and treat the patient as a burn injury. DRESS is a drug rash with eosinophilia and systemic symptoms (liver, kidney, lymph nodes), often weeks after the drug starts. Allopurinol, anticonvulsants, and sulfonamides are typical causes. Acute generalized exanthematous pustulosis is sheets of sterile pustules, often from a beta-lactam.

Acne is not "dirty skin." The AAD 2024 acne guideline starts inflammatory acne with a topical retinoid plus benzoyl peroxide. Add oral doxycycline when the trunk or the face is moderately inflamed, and limit the antibiotic course. Isotretinoin is for nodulocystic or scarring acne. It is teratogenic. Do not combine it with a tetracycline. Both raise intracranial pressure.

**Burns and wounds**

The American Burn Association uses the rule of nines in adults: each leg 18%, each arm 9%, anterior trunk 18%, posterior trunk 18%, head 9%, perineum 1%. A child's head is larger and the legs are smaller, so do not apply adult nines to a toddler. Partial-thickness burns blister and hurt. Full-thickness burns are leathery and may not hurt in the center. Parkland resuscitation for adults with partial-thickness burns over about 20% of the body, and for most burns over 10% in children or older adults, is 4 mL times body weight in kilograms times percent body surface of lactated Ringer, with half in the first 8 hours from the time of the burn, not from the time of arrival. Adjust to urine output. Inhalation injury (a fire in a closed space, soot, hoarseness) is an airway problem first.

> **Important to remember**
> - Excise a suspected melanoma. Do not shave through the base.
> - Drain the abscess. Cellulitis without pus is usually streptococcal.
> - Mucosal pain plus a new drug is Stevens-Johnson until you have proved it is not.

## Psychiatry in depth

The USPSTF 2023 depression recommendation is a grade B to screen adults, including people who are pregnant or postpartum. Adolescents 12 to 18 are covered by the USPSTF 2022 adolescent recommendation, also grade B. A positive PHQ-2 needs a PHQ-9 or a diagnostic interview. It is not a prescription by itself. The USPSTF 2023 statement on suicide-risk screening in people who do not already have a recognized risk is an I statement (evidence is insufficient for a universal screen). A patient who describes a plan, intent, or recent attempt is not a screening question. That patient needs a safety evaluation now. Asking about suicide does not plant the idea.

The USPSTF 2023 anxiety recommendation is a grade B to screen adults 64 and younger, including pregnant and postpartum patients. The GAD-7 is the usual tool. Adults 65 and older are an I statement for anxiety screening. Children and adolescents 8 to 18 are covered by the separate USPSTF 2022 anxiety recommendation.

**Depression treatment**

The VA/DoD clinical practice guideline for major depressive disorder (2022) supports psychotherapy, an antidepressant, or both as initial treatment for mild to moderate depression. SSRIs and SNRIs are the usual first drugs. Tell the patient that energy and sleep may change before mood does, often over 2 to 4 weeks, and that a fair trial is about 6 to 8 weeks at a therapeutic dose. Sertraline and escitalopram have fewer drug interactions than fluoxetine, paroxetine, or fluvoxamine. Paroxetine is a poor choice in pregnancy and in older adults (anticholinergic, Beers 2023). Do not stop an SSRI abruptly. Discontinuation symptoms are dizziness, electric-shock sensations, and irritability, worst with paroxetine and venlafaxine.

If the first antidepressant fails, the VA/DoD and common practice support switching or augmenting. Check adherence, alcohol, thyroid, and bipolar history before you stack a second drug. Augmentation options that show up on exams include bupropion, buspirone, an atypical antipsychotic such as aripiprazole, or lithium. Mirtazapine helps when insomnia and poor appetite dominate, and it causes weight gain. Bupropion is useful when fatigue and low motivation dominate, and when you want to avoid sexual side effects. Bupropion lowers the seizure threshold and is a poor choice in bulimia or in a patient who is abruptly stopping alcohol or benzodiazepines.

**Bipolar disorder is the trap inside a depression stem**

Decreased need for sleep, racing thoughts, spending, or irritability means you do not start an antidepressant alone. Antidepressant monotherapy can precipitate mania. Acute mania is treated with lithium, valproate, or an antipsychotic (quetiapine, aripiprazole, risperidone, olanzapine, and others have evidence). The CANMAT and ISBD 2018 guidelines remain the widely used bipolar framework: lithium or valproate or an antipsychotic for mania, and quetiapine, lurasidone, or lamotrigine pathways for bipolar depression. Lithium is the classic maintenance drug and lowers suicide risk in the trials that studied it. Check a level, TSH, and creatinine. Toxicity is worsened by dehydration, NSAIDs, ACE inhibitors, and thiazides. Valproate is teratogenic (neural tube defects). Do not choose it for a patient who could become pregnant unless no safer option fits. Lamotrigine is titrated slowly because a fast titration raises the risk of Stevens-Johnson syndrome. Valproate doubles lamotrigine levels, so the titration is even slower when they are combined.

**Psychosis and the movement exam**

The APA schizophrenia guideline (2020) recommends an antipsychotic and a coordinated plan. Clozapine is for illness that has not responded to two adequate antipsychotic trials, and for persistent suicidal ideation in schizophrenia. Clozapine requires absolute neutrophil count monitoring because of agranulocytosis. It also causes myocarditis, seizures, constipation that can become ileus, and sialorrhea.

Dopamine blockade explains the side effects. Nigrostriatal blockade causes dystonia, parkinsonism, and akathisia. Tuberoinfundibular blockade raises prolactin (galactorrhea, sexual dysfunction). Acute dystonia (a twisted neck, an oculogyric crisis) is treated with benztropine or diphenhydramine now. Akathisia is restlessness, not anxiety to be treated with more of the same drug. Lower the dose or use a beta-blocker. Tardive dyskinesia is late and choreiform. Valbenazine and deutetrabenazine are the treatments used for tardive dyskinesia. Neuroleptic malignant syndrome is lead-pipe rigidity, very high temperature, autonomic instability, and a high CK, usually over days. Stop the drug. Cool the patient. A dopamine agonist such as bromocriptine and dantrolene are used in severe cases. Do not confuse it with serotonin syndrome, which is clonus, hyperreflexia, and a faster onset after a serotonergic combination (an SSRI plus an MAOI, tramadol, linezolid, or a triptan).

**Substances**

The ASAM alcohol withdrawal guideline (2020) uses symptom-triggered benzodiazepines (lorazepam or diazepam) guided by a CIWA-type score. Thiamine before glucose in a malnourished patient prevents Wernicke encephalopathy (confusion, ataxia, ophthalmoplegia). Naltrexone or acamprosate is relapse prevention after withdrawal, not the treatment of seizures. Naltrexone is avoided in acute hepatitis and when the patient needs opioids. The ASAM national practice guideline on opioid use disorder supports buprenorphine, methadone, or extended-release naltrexone. Start buprenorphine when the patient is in withdrawal, or use a low-dose overlap method, so you do not precipitate withdrawal. Offer naloxone to every patient with opioid use disorder. The old waiver requirement to prescribe buprenorphine has been removed. Methadone for opioid use disorder is dispensed through an opioid treatment program.

**Anxiety, trauma, obsession, and attention**

Panic disorder is abrupt attacks with a surge of fear and somatic symptoms, followed by worry about the next attack. An SSRI or SNRI plus cognitive behavioral therapy is first-line. A benzodiazepine can cover the first days. It is not the long-term plan. Generalized anxiety is excessive worry on more days than not for at least 6 months. The same drug classes, buspirone, and psychotherapy are the options. PTSD follows exposure to actual or threatened death, serious injury, or sexual violence, with intrusion, avoidance, negative mood, and hyperarousal for more than a month. Trauma-focused psychotherapy is first-line. Sertraline and paroxetine and venlafaxine have evidence. Prazosin is sometimes used for nightmares. It is not the treatment of the whole disorder. OCD is obsessions or compulsions that take time or cause distress. High-dose SSRI or clomipramine plus exposure and response prevention. Do not call every worrier OCD.

The AAP 2019 ADHD guideline: ages 4 to 5, parent behavior therapy is first-line. Age 6 and older, a stimulant plus behavioral treatment is first-line. Methylphenidate and amphetamine salts are the stimulants. Appetite, sleep, and growth are the monitoring points. Atomoxetine, guanfacine, and clonidine are the nonstimulants when a stimulant is a poor fit (substance risk, a tic that worsens, or the family declines a controlled substance).

**Eating disorders and personality patterns the stem uses**

An underweight adolescent with bradycardia, lanugo, or syncope needs medical stabilization, not a lecture and a follow-up in a month. Refeeding can drop phosphate. Binge-purge behavior at a normal weight is still dangerous (electrolytes, dental erosion, Mallory-Weiss tears). Fluoxetine has an FDA indication in bulimia. It does not replace a structured program. Borderline pattern in a stem is frantic efforts to avoid abandonment, unstable relationships, and self-harm. Dialectical behavior therapy is the psychotherapy with the clearest evidence. An antipsychotic is not the first answer to a personality description.

**Older adults**

The AGS Beers Criteria (2023) say to avoid benzodiazepines, Z-drugs, strongly anticholinergic antidepressants (amitriptyline, paroxetine), and to use antipsychotics for dementia-related behavior only when the patient is a threat to self or others and non-drug steps have failed. Delirium fluctuates and starts over hours to days. Dementia is slow. Do not start a cholinesterase inhibitor for delirium. Look for infection, drugs, urine retention, and pain.

> **Important to remember**
> - Screen depression in adults (USPSTF 2023) and anxiety in adults through age 64 (USPSTF 2023).
> - Antidepressant alone is the wrong first drug when the history is mania.
> - Serotonin syndrome is clonus. Neuroleptic malignant syndrome is rigidity.

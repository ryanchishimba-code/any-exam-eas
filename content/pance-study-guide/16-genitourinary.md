# Genitourinary (4%)

**Top 5 high-yield**

1. Simple bladder infection (cystitis)
2. BPH first-line drugs
3. Prostate cancer screening
4. Testicular torsion vs epididymitis
5. Kidney/bladder stones

**Abbreviations:** UTI = urinary tract infection. TMP-SMX = trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole. BPH = enlarged prostate. LUTS = urinary symptoms. PSA = prostate blood test.

**Checklist**

- [ ] **PSA screening:** for men **55–69**, screening is an individual decision after a discussion of benefits and harms. Do not screen men **70 or older**. ([USPSTF](https://www.uspreventiveservicestaskforce.org/uspstf/recommendation/prostate-cancer-screening))

**Look-alike: scrotal pain (classic teaching points)**

| Disease | Classic clue | Cremasteric reflex |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Testicular torsion | Sudden severe pain, teen, high-riding testis | **Absent** |
| Epididymitis | Gradual pain, fever, urinary symptoms | Present |

> **Important to remember:** **Sudden scrotal pain in a teen = torsion** until proven otherwise.

## Genitourinary in depth

Genitourinary disease is 4% of the exam. Torsion, hematuria, and the prostate questions are the usual set. Stones are shared with the renal chapter.

**Infection and the prostate**

Uncomplicated cystitis in a healthy nonpregnant woman is nitrofurantoin for 5 days, trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole for 3 days where resistance is acceptable, or a single dose of fosfomycin (IDSA). Fever and flank pain are pyelonephritis. That patient needs a longer course and an antibiotic that reaches the kidney. Nitrofurantoin does not. Fever, dysuria, and a tender boggy prostate are acute prostatitis. Do not massage the prostate. Treat for several weeks. Chronic pelvic pain without bacteria is not an antibiotic refill.

**Scrotum and testis**

Sudden severe scrotal pain in an adolescent, a high-riding testis, and an absent cremasteric reflex are testicular torsion. Surgical exploration is the next step. Imaging must not delay the operating room when the story is classic. Epididymitis is more gradual, often with urinary symptoms and a present cremasteric reflex. Prehn sign is not reliable enough to send a patient home. A painless firm testicular mass in a young man, especially after cryptorchidism, is a tumor until an ultrasound says otherwise. The first imaging test is a scrotal ultrasound. Tumor markers follow. Do not biopsy through the scrotum.

**Prostate, incontinence, and erection**

The USPSTF prostate cancer recommendation: for men 55 to 69, PSA screening is an individual decision after a discussion of benefit and harm. Do not screen men 70 or older. An abnormal digital rectal exam is evaluated even when a screening PSA was not the plan. Lower urinary tract symptoms with a smooth enlarged prostate are treated first with an alpha-blocker such as tamsulosin. A 5-alpha-reductase inhibitor is added when the prostate is large. A post-void residual is checked when retention is a concern.

Stress incontinence leaks with cough or a sneeze and is treated with pelvic-floor training, then surgery if that fails. Urgency incontinence is bladder training and, when a drug is needed, an antimuscarinic or a beta-3 agonist. Overflow incontinence is a distended bladder and constant dribbling. Catheterize. Do not give an anticholinergic to a patient in retention. Erectile dysfunction is treated first with a PDE-5 inhibitor unless a nitrate is on the list. The combination raises cGMP and can drop the blood pressure. Also ask about depression, testosterone symptoms, and cardiovascular risk. Erectile dysfunction can be the first sign of vascular disease.

Painless gross hematuria in an older adult who smokes is urothelial cancer until a cystoscopy and imaging of the upper tracts have been done. A culture treats infection. It does not replace the cancer evaluation when the hematuria is painless and gross.

## Stones, infection, and the priapism clock

A ureteral stone is flank pain that radiates to the groin, often with blood on the dipstick. Noncontrast CT is the most sensitive test in an adult who is not pregnant. Ultrasound is the first image in pregnancy. A stone of 5 mm or smaller often passes. Pain control is an NSAID when the kidney function allows it. Tamsulosin is discussed for distal stones. Infected obstructed kidney, a stone with fever, is a urologic emergency: antibiotics and decompression, not a plan to pass the stone at home (AUA medical management of kidney stones, 2014, and standard emergency practice).

Epididymitis in a sexually active adult under 35 is treated for gonorrhea and chlamydia (CDC STI treatment guidelines, 2021). After 35, enteric organisms are more likely and a fluoroquinolone is the usual choice when the local resistance allows it. The cremasteric reflex is present. Torsion is not.

Ischemic priapism is a painful erection lasting about 4 hours. It is a compartment syndrome of the corpora. Aspirate and irrigate. Do not send the patient home to wait. Nonischemic priapism is less painful and is related to trauma. The distinction is the pain and the blood gas, not a casual guess.

A hydrocele transilluminates and is a fluid collection around the testis. A varicocele is a bag of worms, more often on the left, and is evaluated with ultrasound if it is right-sided, sudden, or does not decompress when the patient lies down, because those features raise concern for a retroperitoneal mass.

> **Important to remember**
> - Torsion goes to the operating room. Ultrasound is for the uncertain exam, not for a classic one.
> - PSA screening at 55 to 69 is a discussion. It is not a reflex, and it stops at 70 (USPSTF).
> - Nitrofurantoin treats the bladder. It does not treat pyelonephritis.

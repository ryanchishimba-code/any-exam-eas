## GI and nutrition in depth

GI and nutrition is 8% of the PANCE, the same weight as musculoskeletal disease and just behind pulmonary disease. The questions are "what is it" and "what is the next step," not a request for a rare eponym.

**Esophagus and stomach**

The ACG clinical guideline for GERD (2022) supports an 8-week trial of a once-daily proton pump inhibitor for typical heartburn and regurgitation when there are no alarm symptoms. Take the PPI 30 to 60 minutes before a meal. Alarm symptoms are dysphagia, odynophagia, weight loss, gastrointestinal bleeding, anemia, and recurrent vomiting. Those patients get endoscopy, not a longer empiric trial. The ACG and CAG dyspepsia guideline (2017, still the ACG dyspepsia guideline) sends patients 60 and older with dyspepsia to endoscopy. Under 60, test for Helicobacter pylori and treat it if it is present. Do not scope every young patient with burning pain.

The ACG Helicobacter pylori guideline (2024) changed the first regimen. When clarithromycin susceptibility is unknown, do not use clarithromycin triple therapy. Use optimized bismuth quadruple therapy for 14 days: a PPI, bismuth, tetracycline, and metronidazole. Confirm cure with a urea breath test or a stool antigen at least 4 weeks after antibiotics and after the PPI has been held for 1 to 2 weeks. A serology stays positive after cure and cannot be the test of cure.

Upper gastrointestinal bleeding: resuscitate first. The restrictive red-cell threshold is a hemoglobin of 7 g/dL, or 8 g/dL when acute coronary disease is present (AABB transfusion guidance). A proton pump inhibitor infusion is started. Endoscopy is performed within 24 hours after resuscitation for acute upper bleeding (ACG). Octreotide and a cephalosporin are added when cirrhosis and varices are likely. Do not place a nasogastric tube to "rule out" a bleed and then skip endoscopy in a patient with melena.

**Biliary disease and pancreatitis**

Biliary colic is episodic, follows a fatty meal, and the patient is not febrile. Acute cholecystitis persists, and the Murphy sign is inspiratory arrest when the right upper quadrant is pressed. Ultrasound shows a thickened wall, pericholecystolic fluid, or a stone. HIDA is the next test when the ultrasound is not diagnostic and the story is strong. Cholangitis is infected bile under pressure. The Tokyo Guidelines 2018 (still the cited severity and diagnosis framework) use fever or chills, jaundice, and right-upper-quadrant pain, with laboratory and imaging support. Reynolds pentad adds hypotension and altered mentation. The treatment is antibiotics and biliary drainage, usually ERCP. Antibiotics alone do not decompress the duct.

Acute pancreatitis is diagnosed when two of three are present: characteristic pain, lipase or amylase at least three times the upper limit of normal, and imaging that shows pancreatitis. The revised Atlanta classification separates mild, moderately severe, and severe disease by organ failure and local complications. Early oral feeding is preferred when the patient can eat. Lactated Ringer is the usual fluid. Routine antibiotics and routine nasogastric suction are not indicated for mild disease. Gallstone pancreatitis that is mild should have the gallbladder removed during the same admission, after the inflammation settles, so it does not happen again. Alcohol is the other common cause. Triglycerides above about 1,000 mg/dL are the metabolic cause. Chronic pancreatitis is pain, fat malabsorption, and, later, diabetes. Enzymes are replaced with meals.

**Liver**

| Pattern | HBsAg | Anti-HBc | Anti-HBs | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Susceptible | Negative | Negative | Negative | Vaccinate |
| Immune from vaccine | Negative | Negative | Positive | No core antibody |
| Immune from infection | Negative | Positive | Positive | Resolved |
| Acute or chronic infection | Positive | Positive | Negative | IgM core means acute. IgG core with HBsAg for more than 6 months means chronic |
| Isolated core | Negative | Positive | Negative | Window, false positive, or occult infection. Check DNA if it matters |

USPSTF hepatitis B screening (2020 recommendation, expanded since) and the CDC now support screening adults for hepatitis B. Hepatitis C screening is once for adults 18 to 79 (USPSTF 2020). Antibody with reflex RNA. RNA means active infection and a path to direct-acting antivirals. Antibody alone can be a cured infection.

Cirrhosis with ascites, fever, and a tender abdomen is spontaneous bacterial peritonitis until a paracentesis says otherwise. A neutrophil count in the fluid of at least 250 cells/mm³ is the diagnostic threshold. Treat with a third-generation cephalosporin. Albumin is given in higher-risk patients. Secondary prophylaxis follows a first episode. Hepatic encephalopathy is lactulose, titrated to two or three stools a day, with rifaximin added when episodes recur. A nonselective beta-blocker or a band is used for varices that have bled or that meet the size criteria. Do not fluid-restrict every patient with ascites. Salt restriction and diuretics (spironolactone, often with furosemide) are the base. A large-volume tap is for tense ascites.

**Bowel**

Appendicitis is periumbilical pain that moves to the right lower quadrant, with anorexia. CT is the usual adult image. Ultrasound is preferred first in children and in pregnancy. Diverticulitis is left-lower-quadrant pain and fever in an older adult. CT confirms it. Uncomplicated disease in an otherwise well, immunocompetent patient may be watched without antibiotics (ACP and AGA guidance). Antibiotics are used when the patient is immunocompromised, septic, or abscessed. A colonoscopy is done after healing, if one has not been done recently, to exclude cancer. Do not scope during the acute infection.

Ulcerative colitis is continuous from the rectum. Crohn disease skips and can fistulize and affect any part of the gut. Mesalamine treats mild ulcerative colitis and does not treat Crohn disease of the small bowel. Steroids induce remission. They are not maintenance. Thiopurines and biologics maintain remission. Toxic megacolon is dilation plus systemic toxicity. That patient does not get an antidiarrheal.

Celiac disease is iron deficiency, diarrhea, and dermatitis herpetiformis (an itchy vesicular rash). Test IgA tissue transglutaminase and total IgA while the patient is eating gluten. A biopsy confirms it before a lifelong gluten-free diet, except when the serology and the story are overwhelming and a specialist agrees. Irritable bowel syndrome is pain related to defecation, with a change in stool form, and no alarm feature (weight loss, bleeding, anemia, nocturnal symptoms, onset after age 50). It is a positive diagnosis after a limited evaluation, not "everything else."

Colorectal cancer screening, USPSTF 2021: start at 45 and continue through 75 for average risk. Colonoscopy every 10 years, annual FIT, stool DNA-FIT every 1 to 3 years, CT colonography every 5 years, or flexible sigmoidoscopy every 5 years (or every 10 with annual FIT). A positive stool test leads to colonoscopy, not to a repeat stool test. One first-degree relative with colorectal cancer or an advanced polyp should start earlier, often at 40, or 10 years before the relative's diagnosis. Stop routine screening after 85. Ages 76 to 85 are an individual decision.

**Pediatric emergencies in one table**

| Clue | Diagnosis | Next step |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Projectile nonbilious vomiting at 3 to 6 weeks, an olive mass | Pyloric stenosis | Fluid and electrolyte repair, then pyloromyotomy. Ultrasound |
| Currant-jelly stool, intermittent colic, a sausage mass | Intussusception | Air or contrast enema. Surgery if it fails or the patient is unstable |
| Bilious vomiting in the first month | Malrotation with volvulus until proven otherwise | Upper GI series. Do not observe bilious vomiting in an infant |
| Failure to pass meconium, then a squirt on rectal exam | Hirschsprung disease | Contrast enema and a rectal biopsy |

**Nutrition deficiencies that are mechanism questions**

Thiamine deficiency is Wernicke encephalopathy and wet or dry beriberi. Give thiamine before glucose in a patient with alcohol use disorder. Niacin deficiency is pellagra: dermatitis, diarrhea, dementia. Vitamin C deficiency is scurvy: bleeding gums, corkscrew hairs, poor wound healing. Vitamin D deficiency is rickets or osteomalacia. B12 deficiency is megaloblastic anemia plus posterior-column findings (loss of vibration and position). Folate deficiency is megaloblastic anemia without those neurologic findings. Vitamin A deficiency is night blindness. Vitamin K deficiency, or warfarin, prolongs the prothrombin time.

> **Important to remember**
> - Bismuth quadruple therapy when clarithromycin susceptibility is unknown (ACG 2024).
> - Two of three features diagnose pancreatitis. Feed early if the patient can eat.
> - Colorectal screening starts at 45 (USPSTF 2021).

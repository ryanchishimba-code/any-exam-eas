# Common traps

| Trap | Fix |
| --- | --- |
| Answering "best test" when the stem asks "initial" | Underline the question first |
| Missing pregnancy, kidney disease, or allergy | Scan the stem before choosing |
| Studying small systems first | Work the index from the top |
| Ignoring kids (12–15%) and surgery (8–10%) | Tag those questions weekly |
| Using a rule with no source on the line | Open the linked official page before you memorize a dose |
| Fading in blocks 4–5 | Do at least 2 full mocks |

### The 4-box error log

1. **System + task** (example: Pulm / drugs)
2. **Why you missed it:** didn't know · misread · missed a trap · changed answer
3. **The correct rule** in one sentence
4. **Tomorrow's drill:** 10 similar questions

**Practice scores are not PANCE scores.** Watch trends: fewer misreads, stronger big systems, steady 1-minute pace.

## How each trap shows up in a stem

**The question word.** "Initial," "next," "most appropriate," and "best" are different questions. The initial test is the one you do now, often the safer or more available one. The best test is the one that confirms the diagnosis. If the patient is unstable, the next step is to stabilize, not to order the confirmatory scan. Underline the verb before you read the choices. This is the NCCPA task "Using Diagnostic and Laboratory Studies" and "Clinical Intervention" (blueprint effective January 2025).

**The hidden constraint.** Pregnancy, reduced kidney function, and a drug allergy change the answer even when the diagnosis is obvious. ACE inhibitors and ARBs are avoided in pregnancy (ACOG Practice Bulletin 222, 2020, for hypertensive disorders, and product labeling). Nitrofurantoin and a long list of other drugs change with the eGFR (KDIGO CKD, 2024, for the staging; the dose itself comes from the label). Scan the stem for those three words before you commit.

**The weight of the blueprint.** Cardiovascular disease is 11% and pulmonary disease is 9%. Dermatology is 4%. A plan that starts with the smallest systems leaves the largest share unpracticed. The organ-system weights are the NCCPA blueprint effective January 2025. Children are about 12 to 15% of items and surgical topics about 8 to 10%. Those ranges sit on top of the organ systems. They are not extra systems.

**A rule with no source.** If you cannot name the guideline and the year, you do not have the rule yet. Blood pressure targets in this book follow the 2025 AHA/ACC guideline. Lipid decisions follow the 2026 ACC/AHA dyslipidemia guideline. Streptococcal pharyngitis follows IDSA 2012. Acute otitis media follows the AAP 2013 guideline. Memorizing a dose you cannot place is how a trap answer gets chosen.

**The late blocks.** Fatigue shows up as rereading a stem you already understood and changing a right answer. Two full-length practice sets, with the clock, tell you whether that happens. The fix is sleep and a one-minute pace, not a new resource the night before.

**Age that changes the drug, not the diagnosis.** A diagnosis can be right and the drug still wrong because of age. High-intensity statin for secondary prevention is the 2026 ACC/AHA recommendation at 75 and younger; after 75 the same guideline says to individualize. Subclinical hypothyroidism with TSH under 10 is often watched in older adults (TRUST, NEJM 2017). Stage 2 blood pressure can start with two drugs in a younger adult (2025 AHA/ACC), and the same guideline allows a slower start when the patient is older or prone to falls. If the stem gives an age over 75, do not apply the under-75 default without reading the exception.

**A red flag that is the age itself.** New back pain after 50, and a change in bowel habit after 50, are reasons to image or to refer, not to reassure (ACR Appropriateness Criteria for low back pain, 2021; ACG irritable bowel syndrome guideline, 2021). "No red flags, so no imaging" is the rule for a younger adult whose stem has no neurologic deficit. It is the wrong rule when the stem's age is the red flag.

# Infectious Diseases (7%)

**Top 5 high-yield**

1. STI regimens (gonorrhea, chlamydia, syphilis)
2. Sepsis first hour
3. Meningitis empiric antibiotics
4. Cellulitis vs abscess
5. Lyme disease

**Abbreviations:** STI = sexually transmitted infection. IM = injection into muscle. MAP = mean arterial pressure. I&D = incision and drainage.

**STI regimens (CDC) [C28–C30]**

| Infection | First-line |
| --- | --- |
| Gonorrhea | Ceftriaxone 500 mg IM once (1 g if ≥150 kg) |
| Chlamydia | Doxycycline 100 mg twice daily × 7 days |
| Early syphilis | Benzathine penicillin G 2.4 million units IM once |
| Late / unknown syphilis | 2.4 million units IM weekly × 3 |

**Checklist**

- [ ] **HIV screening:** everyone **15–65** at least once. [C31]
- [ ] **Sepsis with low BP or high lactate:** at least **30 mL/kg** IV crystalloid within 3 hours. [C32]
- [ ] **Antibiotics right away**, ideally within 1 hour. Cultures first. [C32]
- [ ] **Septic shock:** norepinephrine first; target **MAP 65**. [C32]
- [ ] **Bacterial meningitis (empiric):** vancomycin + ceftriaxone (or cefotaxime). Add ampicillin if Listeria risk. [C34]
- [ ] **Steroid (dexamethasone):** give before or with the first antibiotic dose in adults. [C34]
- [ ] **Non-pus cellulitis:** cover strep; 5-day course. [C35]
- [ ] **Abscess:** **I&D is the main treatment.** [C35]
- [ ] **Lyme rash (erythema migrans):** doxycycline × 10 days. [C33]

> **Important to remember**
> - **Trap:** azithromycin as "preferred" for chlamydia. **Doxycycline is preferred.** [C29]
> - **2026 note:** during the Bicillin L-A shortage, CDC prioritizes penicillin for pregnancy. [C30]

# Infectious Diseases (7%)

**Top 5 high-yield**

1. STI regimens (gonorrhea, chlamydia, syphilis)
2. Sepsis first hour
3. Meningitis empiric antibiotics
4. Cellulitis vs abscess
5. Lyme disease

**Abbreviations:** STI = sexually transmitted infection. IM = injection into muscle. MAP = mean arterial pressure. I&D = incision and drainage.

**STI regimens**

Benzathine penicillin G in this table means **Bicillin L-A (penicillin G benzathine)**, given by deep intramuscular injection. Do not substitute Bicillin C-R.

| Infection | First-line |
| --- | --- |
| Gonorrhea of the cervix, urethra, or rectum | Ceftriaxone 500 mg IM once if under 150 kg; **1 g IM once if 150 kg or more** ([CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/std/treatment-guidelines/gonorrhea-adults.htm)) |
| Chlamydia, adolescents and adults | Doxycycline 100 mg by mouth twice daily for **7 days** ([CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/std/treatment-guidelines/chlamydia.htm)) |
| Primary, secondary, or early latent syphilis | Benzathine penicillin G **2.4 million units IM once** ([CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/std/treatment-guidelines/p-and-s-syphilis.htm), [early latent](https://www.cdc.gov/std/treatment-guidelines/latent-syphilis.htm)) |
| Late latent syphilis, or latent syphilis of unknown duration | Benzathine penicillin G **2.4 million units IM once a week for 3 weeks** (7.2 million units total) ([CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/std/treatment-guidelines/latent-syphilis.htm)) |

Neurosyphilis, ocular syphilis, and otosyphilis are not treated with this weekly benzathine schedule. The CDC wall chart uses aqueous crystalline penicillin G for those infections.

The 2026 FDA label for Bicillin L-A lists primary, secondary, and latent syphilis as 2.4 million units in one dose, and late syphilis (tertiary and neurosyphilis) as 2.4 million units at 7-day intervals for three doses ([FDA label, 2026](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2026/050141s243lbl.pdf)). The rows above follow the CDC STI Treatment Guidelines, which separate early latent disease (one dose) from late latent disease and unknown duration (weekly for 3 weeks).

During the Bicillin L-A shortage, CDC recommends preserving benzathine penicillin G for patients who are pregnant, because penicillin is the only recommended syphilis treatment in pregnancy ([CDC NCHHSTP, April 14, 2026](https://www.cdc.gov/nchhstp/whats-new/bicillin-updates.html)).

> **Important to remember**
> - **Trap:** azithromycin as "preferred" for chlamydia. **Doxycycline 100 mg by mouth twice daily for 7 days is the recommended regimen** for adolescents and adults. ([CDC STI Treatment Guidelines](https://www.cdc.gov/std/treatment-guidelines/chlamydia.htm))

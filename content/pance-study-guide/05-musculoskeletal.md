## Musculoskeletal in depth

Musculoskeletal disease is 8% of the exam, tied with GI. Most items are an injury rule, a joint that might be infected, or a drug that is first-line for a named arthritis.

**Who needs a film**

The Ottawa ankle rules: ankle films if there is pain in the malleolar zone and any one of bone tenderness at the posterior edge or tip of the lateral malleolus, the same finding at the medial malleolus, or inability to bear weight for four steps both immediately and in the clinic. Foot films if there is midfoot pain and navicular tenderness, base-of-fifth-metatarsal tenderness, or the same inability to bear weight. The rules are for adults who can be examined. They are not a reason to skip films in a patient who cannot walk because of another injury.

A fall on an outstretched hand with snuffbox tenderness is a scaphoid fracture until proven otherwise. The first film is often normal. Place a thumb-spica splint and repeat imaging in 10 to 14 days, or get an MRI sooner if the diagnosis must be settled. A missed scaphoid fracture necroses the proximal pole because the blood supply enters distally. A shortened, externally rotated leg after a fall is a hip fracture. Image it and plan surgery. Do not walk the patient "to see."

Knee films are not automatic for a twist on the field. The Ottawa knee rule (age 55 or older, isolated tenderness of the patella, tenderness at the head of the fibula, inability to flex to 90 degrees, or inability to bear weight) is the selective approach. A locked knee or a large effusion after a twist may be a meniscal tear or a ligament tear. The Lachman test is the sensitive test for an ACL tear. Immediate swelling is blood until proven otherwise (ACL, fracture, patellar dislocation). Swelling the next day is more often a meniscus.

**The red-flag back**

The ACP low back pain guideline (2017) advises against imaging for uncomplicated acute low back pain. NSAIDs and continued activity are preferred to bed rest. Image and act when a red flag is present:

| Flag | What you are afraid of | Next step |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Urinary retention, saddle anesthesia, bilateral leg weakness | Cauda equina | Emergency MRI and surgical consultation |
| Fever, injection drug use, a recent spinal procedure | Epidural abscess or osteomyelitis | MRI and antibiotics after cultures when the patient is stable enough |
| History of cancer, unexplained weight loss | Metastasis | Imaging, often MRI |
| Major trauma, or minor trauma in an older adult or a patient on steroids | Fracture | Plain films, then CT or MRI if the story is strong and the film is negative |
| Progressive motor deficit | Cord or root compression | Urgent MRI |

A positive straight-leg raise reproduces radicular pain below the knee. It supports a disc herniation. It is not an indication for surgery by itself. Most radiculopathy improves. Cauda equina does not wait for physical therapy.

**Osteoarthritis, rheumatoid arthritis, and crystals**

The ACR/AF 2019 osteoarthritis guideline strongly recommends exercise and weight loss when the patient has extra weight, and topical NSAIDs for knee osteoarthritis. Oral NSAIDs are used when the kidneys, stomach, and heart allow them. Acetaminophen is a weaker option. Opioids are not first-line. Glucosamine is not a strong recommendation. An intra-articular steroid can help a flare for a short time.

Rheumatoid arthritis is symmetric synovitis of the MCP and PIP joints and the wrists, with morning stiffness lasting more than an hour. Anti-CCP antibodies are specific. Rheumatoid factor is not. The 2021 ACR rheumatoid arthritis guideline starts a DMARD, and methotrexate is the usual first drug, with folic acid. A short steroid bridge is reasonable. Do not use two biologic agents together. Screen for tuberculosis and hepatitis B before a biologic. Hydroxychloroquine needs an eye exam on the schedule ophthalmology sets, because of retinal toxicity.

Gout, ACR 2020: diagnose a flare with a tap when you can. Needle-shaped, negatively birefringent crystals are gout. Positively birefringent rhomboids are calcium pyrophosphate (pseudogout), often in the knee or wrist of an older adult, sometimes with chondrocalcinosis. Treat the flare with an NSAID, colchicine (if it is early and the kidneys allow), or a corticosteroid. Do not start allopurinol as the only treatment of a hot joint. You may start or continue urate-lowering therapy during a flare if anti-inflammatory coverage is in place. Allopurinol is first-line urate-lowering therapy, including in CKD, started low and titrated. The serum urate target is under 6 mg/dL. Febuxostat is an alternative when allopurinol cannot be used. The CARES trial raised a cardiovascular concern. Do not treat asymptomatic hyperuricemia. A hot joint with fever is septic arthritis until the tap proves it is not. Gram stain and culture come back after you have already started antibiotics when the patient is ill. Staphylococcus aureus is the most common organism. Gonococcus is the disseminated cause in a younger, sexually active patient, often with tenosynovitis and skin lesions, and the joint culture may be negative.

**Systemic disease that lands in this chapter**

SLE uses the EULAR/ACR 2019 classification criteria. A malar rash, photosensitivity, painless oral ulcers, serositis, and a positive ANA start the story. Anti-dsDNA and anti-Smith antibodies are specific. Drug-induced lupus (hydralazine, procainamide, isoniazid, minocycline) is anti-histone and usually spares the kidney. Every patient with SLE should be offered hydroxychloroquine unless there is a reason not to. Nephritis is a referral, not a higher NSAID dose.

Polymyalgia rheumatica is an older adult with bilateral shoulder and hip stiffness and a high ESR or CRP. Low-dose prednisone produces a dramatic response. Ask about headache, scalp tenderness, and jaw claudication. Those are giant cell arteritis. Start high-dose steroids before the temporal-artery biopsy when vision is threatened. The biopsy should not delay the first dose.

Fibromyalgia is widespread pain with fatigue and a normal inflammatory workup. It is not a diagnosis you make by ordering a large antibody panel. Exercise and sleep are the base. Duloxetine, milnacipran, amitriptyline, and pregabalin have evidence. A night bone pain that is relieved by NSAIDs in a teenager is an osteoid osteoma until imaging says otherwise. That is a different disease.

**Osteoporosis and falls**

The USPSTF osteoporosis screening recommendation (updated 2025) is a grade B to screen women 65 and older, and postmenopausal women younger than 65 who are at increased risk, with bone measurement testing. A T-score of −2.5 or lower is osteoporosis. Osteopenia is −1.0 to −2.5. First-line treatment for most patients is an oral bisphosphonate (alendronate or risedronate) taken with water, remaining upright, on an empty stomach. A drug holiday is a planned pause after several years in a patient at lower risk. It is not how you stop denosumab. Stopping denosumab without a follow-on agent causes rebound vertebral fractures. The USPSTF 2024 falls recommendation supports exercise for community-dwelling adults 65 and older at increased risk of falling. Vitamin D to prevent falls in community-dwelling adults is not recommended.

**Compartment syndrome and other can't-miss injuries**

Pain with passive stretch is the earliest sign of compartment syndrome. Pallor, pulselessness, and paralysis are late. A compartment pressure within about 30 mm Hg of the diastolic pressure supports the diagnosis. The treatment is fasciotomy. Do not elevate the limb above the heart in a way that drops perfusion, and do not put ice on a compartment and wait. An open fracture gets antibiotics and irrigation. Farm injuries and puncture wounds through a shoe (Pseudomonas) change the antibiotic. Tetanus status is part of every wound.

Pediatric hips: an obese adolescent with knee pain and a limp may have a slipped capital femoral epiphysis. The knee film can be normal. Get an AP and frog-leg hip. A child 4 to 8 with a limp after a viral illness may have transient synovitis, but Perthes disease (avascular necrosis of the femoral head) and septic arthritis must be considered. Kocher criteria (non-weight-bearing, ESR over 40, fever, WBC over 12) help separate septic arthritis from transient synovitis. A newborn with a clunk on Ortolani or Barlow has developmental dysplasia. Ultrasound if the exam is abnormal, or if risk is high, after about 6 weeks when the ossification allows it. Pavlik harness, not observation of a dislocated hip.

> **Important to remember**
> - Snuffbox tenderness gets a thumb spica even when the film is normal.
> - Tap a hot joint before you commit to gout.
> - Methotrexate first for rheumatoid arthritis (ACR 2021). Allopurinol titrated to a urate under 6 for gout (ACR 2020).

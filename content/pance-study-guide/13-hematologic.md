## Hematologic in depth

Hematologic disease is 5% of the exam. The smear and the clock (fever in sickle cell disease, a falling platelet on heparin) decide most items.

**Anemia**

Microcytic anemia in an older man is colon cancer until the colon has been evaluated. Iron deficiency has a low ferritin. Thalassemia trait has a normal ferritin and a very low mean corpuscular volume for the degree of anemia. Anemia of chronic disease has a low serum iron and a normal or high ferritin. Macrocytic anemia with paresthesias, ataxia, and hypersegmented neutrophils is vitamin B12 deficiency. Folate deficiency does not cause the posterior-column findings. Replace B12 when it is low. Folate alone can worsen the neurologic injury if B12 is also low.

**Hemolysis, platelets, and transfusion**

Fever, hemolysis with schistocytes, thrombocytopenia, and kidney or neurologic injury is thrombotic thrombocytopenic purpura. Plasma exchange is the treatment. Do not transfuse platelets as the treatment. Immune thrombocytopenia is isolated low platelets. Steroids are first-line when treatment is needed. A platelet count that falls by about half, 5 to 10 days after heparin starts, with or without a new clot, is heparin-induced thrombocytopenia. Stop all heparin, including flushes, and start a non-heparin anticoagulant such as argatroban. Do not start warfarin alone in the acute phase.

Fever, flank pain, and red urine during a transfusion is acute hemolytic reaction until proven otherwise. Stop the transfusion. Check the identity of the unit. Support blood pressure and urine output. TRALI is acute hypoxia and noncardiogenic pulmonary edema during or soon after a transfusion. Stop the unit and support breathing.

**Sickle cell disease, reversal, and marrow disease**

Fever of 38.5 °C or higher in sickle cell disease is an emergency because of functional asplenia. Draw cultures and give a parenteral antibiotic that covers pneumococcus. Do not send the child home on an oral antibiotic from the waiting room. Chest pain or hypoxia is acute chest syndrome until a radiograph excludes it. Hydroxyurea is offered from 9 months of age to reduce crises. Patients with asplenia also need pneumococcal, meningococcal, Hib, and influenza vaccines (ACIP).

Life-threatening bleeding on warfarin is treated with vitamin K and four-factor prothrombin complex concentrate. Dabigatran is reversed with idarucizumab. Apixaban or rivaroxaban is reversed with andexanet alfa, or with prothrombin complex concentrate when andexanet is not available. Vitamin K does not reverse a direct oral anticoagulant. Protamine reverses heparin.

Auer rods are acute myeloid leukemia. Smudge cells suggest chronic lymphocytic leukemia. Reed-Sternberg cells suggest Hodgkin lymphoma. The Philadelphia chromosome is chronic myeloid leukemia and is treated with a tyrosine kinase inhibitor. An older adult with back pain, anemia, kidney injury, and a calcium of 12 mg/dL has multiple myeloma until the protein studies say otherwise.

## Iron studies, transfusion, and the smear

Iron deficiency is a low ferritin. Ferritin is an acute-phase reactant, so inflammation can hide a low store; a ferritin under 30 ng/mL still means iron deficiency, and a ferritin under 100 ng/mL with inflammation is treated as deficiency until proven otherwise (AGA iron-deficiency guideline, 2020, and common hematology practice). Total iron-binding capacity is high in iron deficiency and low in anemia of inflammation. Thalassemia trait is a very low mean corpuscular volume with a normal or high red-cell count and a normal ferritin. Do not give iron for months to a patient whose ferritin is already high.

The AABB transfusion guideline uses a restrictive threshold: transfuse red cells at a hemoglobin under 7 g/dL in stable hospitalized adults, and under 8 g/dL when there is symptomatic cardiovascular disease or the stem says the patient is unstable. Do not transfuse to a hemoglobin of 10 because the number looks low. Platelets are not the treatment of thrombotic thrombocytopenic purpura. They can be given for life-threatening bleeding, not as the plan that replaces plasma exchange.

A smear with schistocytes and few platelets is a microangiopathy. Draw the ADAMTS13 sample before plasma, then start plasma exchange. Waiting for the activity result is the trap. Isolated thrombocytopenia with a normal smear is immune thrombocytopenia. Steroids are first-line when a drug is needed. A new clot and a falling platelet count on heparin is heparin-induced thrombocytopenia. Stop every heparin exposure.

> **Important to remember**
> - Fever in sickle cell disease is parenteral antibiotics, not reassurance.
> - A platelet fall on heparin is heparin-induced thrombocytopenia until you have excluded it. Stop the heparin.
> - Match the reversal agent to the anticoagulant.

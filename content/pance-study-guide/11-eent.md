# EENT (6%)

**Top 5 high-yield**

1. Strep throat: test, then penicillin/amoxicillin
2. Ear infection: high-dose amoxicillin
3. Red-eye emergencies
4. Vision loss emergencies
5. Nosebleeds

**Abbreviations:** GAS = group A strep. RADT = rapid strep test. AOM = acute otitis media.

**Look-alike: red eye (classic teaching points)**

| Disease | Classic clue | Urgent? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Conjunctivitis | Discharge, normal vision | No |
| Acute angle-closure glaucoma | Pain, halos, mid-dilated pupil | **Yes** |
| Corneal abrasion | Foreign-body feeling, stain uptake | Usually no |

> **Important to remember:** **Painful red eye + vision change = emergency** until proven otherwise.

## Eyes, ears, nose, and throat in depth

Eyes, ears, nose, and throat are 6% of the exam. The emergencies are angle closure, arterial occlusion, peritonsillar abscess, and epiglottitis. The common visits are otitis, pharyngitis, and epistaxis.

**Throat and ear**

The IDSA 2012 streptococcal pharyngitis guideline remains the IDSA document. Centor features are fever, tonsillar exudate, tender anterior cervical nodes, and absence of cough. Test when the score is intermediate. Treat group A streptococcus with penicillin or amoxicillin. Do not treat a viral sore throat with an antibiotic because the tonsils are red. A peritonsillar abscess is trismus, a muffled voice, and deviation of the uvula. Drain it and give antibiotics. Epiglottitis is drooling and a forward posture. Do not examine the throat with a tongue blade in a child who looks toxic. Secure the airway.

Acute otitis media is a bulging tympanic membrane, or new otorrhea, with acute symptoms (AAP). First-line treatment is high-dose amoxicillin (80 to 90 mg/kg/day) when antibiotics are indicated. Observation is appropriate for selected older children with mild, unilateral disease. Otitis externa is tragal tenderness and a swollen canal. Topical antibiotic drops, not oral amoxicillin. Sudden sensorineural hearing loss over hours to days, with the Weber test lateralizing to the good ear, is an otology urgency. Oral corticosteroids are started promptly. It is not cerumen, and irrigation is the wrong next step.

**Eye**

Acute angle-closure glaucoma is a red eye, halos, a mid-dilated pupil, nausea, and a hard globe. Lower the pressure and arrange a laser iridotomy. Do not dilate the pupil further. A painless sudden monocular loss of vision with a cherry-red spot is central retinal artery occlusion. That is a stroke equivalent. A curtain of floaters and flashes is a retinal detachment until the retina is examined. Viral conjunctivitis is watery and follows a cold. It is supportive care. Do not prescribe a topical corticosteroid for an undiagnosed red eye. Herpes keratitis is a dendritic stain. A steroid without an antiviral harms it. A penetrating injury with a peaked pupil is an open globe. Shield the eye. Do not press on it and do not check the pressure. Diabetic retinopathy screening begins at the diagnosis of type 2 diabetes and within 5 years of type 1, then on the schedule ophthalmology sets. Center-involved macular edema is treated in ophthalmology, often with an intravitreal injection.

Open-angle glaucoma is impaired outflow through the trabecular meshwork. The optic disc is cupped and the pressure is often high. A prostaglandin analog at night is first-line medical therapy (AAO Preferred Practice Pattern). A relative afferent pupillary defect means optic-nerve or severe retinal disease. A cataract does not cause one.

**Nose and hearing in infants**

Active anterior epistaxis: the patient sits forward, and the soft part of the nose is pinched for a full 10 to 15 minutes. Packing is for bleeding that continues. Posterior bleeding in an older patient on an anticoagulant can be occult and serious. The Joint Committee on Infant Hearing (2019) standard is screen by 1 month, diagnose by 3 months, and intervene by 6 months. A passed newborn screen does not exclude a later progressive loss.

> **Important to remember**
> - Pain, halos, and a mid-dilated pupil: angle closure. Do not send the patient home with antibiotic drops.
> - High-dose amoxicillin for acute otitis media when an antibiotic is indicated (AAP).
> - Pinch the nose for a full 15 minutes before you pack it.

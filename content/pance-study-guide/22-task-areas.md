# Task areas

The NCCPA blueprint effective January 2025 scores every medical item on an organ system and on a task. Organ-system weights are in the exam-strategy chapter. The task weights are the second axis. A cardiology item can be a history question, a drug question, or a basic-science question. Studying only "the disease and the drug" under-prepares the 16% history exam and the 8% science exam.

| Task | Share |
| --- | --- |
| Formulating most likely diagnosis | 18% |
| History taking and performing physical examination | 16% |
| Clinical intervention | 16% |
| Pharmaceutical therapeutics | 15% |
| Health maintenance, patient education, and preventive measures | 11% |
| Using diagnostic and laboratory studies | 10% |
| Applying foundational scientific concepts | 8% |
| Professional practice | 6% |

Medical content is 94% of the exam. Professional practice is the other 6% and is not double-coded to an organ system. About 8 to 10% of items are surgical. About 12 to 15% concern children. Those ranges sit on top of the organ-system weights. They are not a nineteenth system.

## History taking and performing physical examination (16%)

The stem already contains the diagnosis if you read the examination. Learn the finding that separates two diseases, not a list of every possible sign.

| Finding | What it supports |
| --- | --- |
| Murmur at the right upper sternal border, radiating to the carotids, with exertional syncope | Severe aortic stenosis |
| Systolic murmur that increases with Valsalva | Obstructive hypertrophic cardiomyopathy |
| Pulsus paradoxus, distant hearts sounds, hypotension | Pericardial tamponade |
| Absent cremasteric reflex, high-riding testis | Testicular torsion |
| Murphy sign | Acute cholecystitis |
| Forehead involved in facial weakness | Peripheral facial nerve, not a hemisphere |
| Skew deviation or a normal head-impulse test in acute vertigo | Central cause until a stroke is excluded |
| Lost reflexes, ascending weakness | Guillain-Barré syndrome |
| Fatigable ptosis | Myasthenia gravis |

Risk factors are part of the history task. A 35-year-old who smokes a pack a day is a different contraception question than a nonsmoker. Ask the question the stem is really asking: which finding, if present, changes the next step?

## Using diagnostic and laboratory studies (10%)

Order the test that changes management. Do not order the most expensive test because it is available.

| Question | The test that answers it |
| --- | --- |
| Is this a STEMI or a posterior infarct? | ECG within 10 minutes. Add V7 to V9 when V1 to V3 are depressed |
| Stone versus appendicitis versus torsion | The imaging that the age and the sex require. Ultrasound before CT in pregnancy |
| Exudate or transudate? | Light criteria on a paired fluid and serum sample |
| Iron deficiency or thalassemia trait? | Ferritin. Then the colon in an older adult with iron deficiency |
| Active hepatitis C or a cleared infection? | HCV RNA, not the antibody alone |
| Central or nephrogenic diabetes insipidus? | Desmopressin response when the patient is already hypernatremic. Do not water-deprive that patient |
| Scaphoid fracture with a normal film? | Immobilize and repeat imaging or obtain MRI. A normal film does not clear the snuffbox |

Screening tests belong to health maintenance. A diagnostic test is for a symptom. A mammogram for a palpable mass is diagnostic imaging, not the screening interval.

## Formulating the most likely diagnosis (18%)

This is the largest task. Build three names for every stem: the most likely disease, the one you cannot miss, and the mimic that the wrong answer will offer. The classic pairs in this book (previa and abruption, ulcerative colitis and Crohn disease, nephrotic and nephritic, osteoarthritis and rheumatoid arthritis and gout) are diagnosis items. Name the disease before you name the drug. A stem that gives you the disease and asks for treatment is not this task.

## Health maintenance, patient education, and preventive measures (11%)

Use the posted recommendation and the year.

| Prevention | Who | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Breast cancer, mammogram every 2 years | Ages 40 through 74 | USPSTF 2024 |
| Colorectal cancer | Ages 45 through 75, average risk | USPSTF 2021 |
| Lung cancer, annual low-dose CT | Ages 50 to 80, 20 pack-years, current smoker or quit within 15 years | USPSTF 2021 |
| Cervical cancer | Cytology every 3 years at 21 to 29. At 30 to 65, cytology every 3 years, HPV every 5, or both every 5 | USPSTF 2018, still the posted recommendation |
| Hepatitis C, once | Adults 18 to 79 | USPSTF 2020 |
| Depression | Adults, including pregnant and postpartum patients. Adolescents 12 to 18 | USPSTF 2023 and 2022 |
| Anxiety | Adults through age 64, including pregnant patients | USPSTF 2023 |
| Osteoporosis, DXA | Women 65 and older, and younger postmenopausal women at increased risk | USPSTF 2025 |
| Falls | Exercise for community-dwelling adults 65 and older at increased risk | USPSTF 2024 |
| Unhealthy alcohol use | Screen adults 18 and older and offer a brief intervention | USPSTF 2018, still the posted recommendation |
| Tobacco | Ask, advise, and offer varenicline, nicotine replacement, or bupropion plus support | USPSTF 2021 |
| Pneumococcal, zoster, RSV, Tdap, influenza | Follow the current ACIP schedule for age and risk | ACIP |
| Infant hearing | Screen by 1 month, diagnose by 3, intervene by 6 | Joint Committee on Infant Hearing, 2019 |

A grade I statement means the evidence is insufficient for a universal recommendation. It does not mean the service is forbidden for a patient with a symptom.

## Clinical intervention (16%)

This task is the next action. Airway, reperfusion, drainage, and delivery show up here. Examples that are easy to miss: fever in sickle cell disease is parenteral antibiotics now. An obstructing kidney stone with fever is decompression. A boggy uterus after delivery is massage and oxytocin. An open globe is a shield, not a pressure check. Status epilepticus is a benzodiazepine, then a second drug. The intervention chapter of each organ system is this task.

## Pharmaceutical therapeutics (15%)

Match the drug to the comorbidity and the contraindication.

| Condition | First drug principle | Do not choose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| HFrEF | ARNI, evidence-based beta-blocker, MRA, SGLT2 inhibitor | ACE inhibitor together with an ARNI. Non-dihydropyridine calcium-channel blocker |
| Very-high-risk ASCVD | High-intensity statin, then ezetimibe, then a PCSK9 inhibitor, LDL under 55 mg/dL | Stopping at a moderate statin when the LDL is still 90 |
| Atrial fibrillation, high stroke risk | A DOAC | Warfarin, unless there is a mechanical valve or moderate to severe mitral stenosis |
| GINA asthma, age 12 and older | Inhaled corticosteroid-formoterol | Albuterol as the only drug |
| Rheumatoid arthritis | Methotrexate, with folic acid | Two biologic agents together |
| Gout, long term | Allopurinol, titrated to a urate under 6 mg/dL | Allopurinol as the only treatment of a hot joint |
| Acne | Topical retinoid plus benzoyl peroxide (AAD 2024) | Tetracycline plus isotretinoin |
| Syphilis in pregnancy | Penicillin | Doxycycline |

## Applying foundational scientific concepts (8%)

These items ask why the drug or the laboratory result works.

- An NSAID constricts the afferent arteriole. An ACE inhibitor dilates the efferent arteriole. Together, in a dry patient, the GFR falls.
- Extra oxygen in COPD releases hypoxic vasoconstriction and worsens ventilation-perfusion matching.
- Nigrostriatal dopamine blockade causes dystonia. Tuberoinfundibular blockade raises prolactin.
- A nitrate and a PDE-5 inhibitor both raise cGMP. The blood pressure falls.
- Primary hypothyroidism is a failed thyroid. TSH rises because the feedback signal is gone.
- Aqueous humor leaves through the trabecular meshwork. That is the lesion of open-angle glaucoma.
- Sensitivity rules disease out when the test is negative. Specificity rules disease in when the test is positive. Number needed to treat is 1 divided by the absolute risk reduction.
- Insulin shifts potassium into cells. Intravenous calcium stabilizes the membrane and does not lower the potassium.
- Thiamine before glucose in a malnourished patient prevents Wernicke encephalopathy. Niacin deficiency is pellagra. Vitamin C deficiency is scurvy. B12 deficiency affects the posterior columns. Folate deficiency does not.

## Professional practice (6%)

Capacity, consent, confidentiality, disclosure of error, and mandated reporting are the chapter on professional practice. Read that chapter as its own 6%, not as a footnote to cardiology.

> **Important to remember**
> - Diagnosis plus history and examination are about a third of the scored tasks. Drugs, procedures, and prevention are the rest.
> - Name the guideline and the year when the task is prevention or a drug.
> - A science item is the mechanism that makes the clinical rule true.

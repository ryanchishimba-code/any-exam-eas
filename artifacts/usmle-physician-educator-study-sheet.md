## 1. nephrology (step2) — batch-01

A 68-year-old man with type 2 diabetes and hypertension is brought from a skilled nursing facility because he has been "sleepier than usual" for two days. His nurse noticed he missed two dialysis sessions last week while recovering from a urinary tract infection treated with trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole. He takes lisinopril, amlodipine, and insulin glargine. On arrival he is oriented only to person. Blood pressure 102/58 mm Hg, pulse 58/min, respirations 22/min. Lungs are clear. There is 2+ pitting edema to the mid-shin. Laboratory studies show:
Na⁺ 134 mEq/L · K⁺ 6.8 mEq/L · Cl⁻ 98 mEq/L · HCO₃⁻ 16 mEq/L · BUN 89 mg/dL · creatinine 4.1 mg/dL (baseline 1.9) · glucose 142 mg/dL
ECG: peaked T waves, widened QRS

**Which of the following is the most appropriate immediate management?**

A. Intravenous calcium gluconate ✓
B. Oral sodium polystyrene sulfonate
C. Intravenous furosemide alone
D. Emergent hemodialysis without stabilization
E. Subcutaneous insulin and dextrose only

**Rationale:** This patient has life-threatening hyperkalemia (K⁺ 6.8, ECG changes) on top of AKI in a dialysis-dependent context. Membrane stabilization with IV calcium is the first step before shifting K⁺ intracellularly (insulin/dextrose, β-agonist) or removing K⁺ (diuretics if urine output exists, patiromer/PSP if appropriate, dialysis). Furosemide alone will not act quickly enough with probable oliguria. Dialysis is likely needed but not before stabilizing the myocardium.

## 2. pharmacology (step1) — batch-01

A 34-year-old woman with rheumatoid arthritis comes to clinic for a medication check. She was started on methotrexate 15 mg orally once weekly and folic acid 1 mg daily six weeks ago. She reports painful mouth sores and increased fatigue but denies fever, cough, or dysuria. Exam shows several shallow oral ulcers and mild splenomegaly. Labs:
WBC 3.2 × 10³/µL · Hgb 10.8 g/dL · MCV 98 fL · platelets 118 × 10³/µL · AST 62 U/L · ALT 58 U/L · creatinine 0.9 mg/dL
Chest radiograph: clear

**Which of the following is the most appropriate next step in management?**

A. Increase folic acid to 5 mg daily
B. Add trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole prophylaxis
C. Hold methotrexate and obtain hepatitis serologies ✓
D. Switch to leflunomide immediately
E. Administer folinic acid rescue and continue methotrexate unchanged

**Rationale:** Methotrexate commonly causes stomatitis and cytopenias; holding the drug while evaluating for toxicity vs intercurrent infection is appropriate. Folic acid reduces but does not eliminate MTX mucosal toxicity—dose adjustment/hold comes first. TMP-SMX plus MTX increases pancytopenia risk and is not indicated without PCP prophylaxis indication. Folinic acid rescue is for high-dose MTX regimens, not low-dose RA therapy. Hepatitis serologies are part of baseline/monitoring when LFTs rise.

## 3. pediatrics (step2) — batch-01

A 3-year-old boy is brought to the office in December with three days of runny nose and cough. His mother says he "sounds wheezy at night." He attends daycare and several classmates are sick. He has no fever today, is drinking well, and is playful in the exam room. Temperature 37.4°C (99.3°F), RR 28/min, O₂ sat 98% on room air. TMs mildly dull without bulging. Throat mildly erythematous. Scattered expiratory wheezes; no retractions. Rapid influenza A/B: negative. Rapid strep: negative.

**Which of the following is the most appropriate management?**

A. Azithromycin for 5 days
B. Amoxicillin-clavulanate for 10 days
C. Albuterol inhaler with spacer as needed ✓
D. Oral prednisolone for 5 days
E. Chest radiograph and blood cultures

**Rationale:** Well-appearing child with viral upper respiratory symptoms and mild reactive airways (expiratory wheeze, no hypoxia/distress) — supportive care with bronchodilator PRN is standard. Antibiotics are not indicated without bacterial focus (otitis with bulging TM, pneumonia, strep pharyngitis). Prednisone is reserved for moderate-severe wheeze or asthma exacerbation, not mild viral wheeze in a comfortable toddler.

## 4. hematology (step1) — batch-01

A 22-year-old college student develops dark urine and fatigue two days after eating at a campus buffet while visiting friends in Nigeria. He denies recent medications except ibuprofen for a headache. Exam: scleral icterus, no hepatomegaly. Labs:
Hgb 9.4 g/dL · reticulocytes 4.2% · LDH 420 U/L · haptoglobin undetectable · indirect bilirubin 3.8 mg/dL · AST 32 · ALT 28
Peripheral smear: bite cells and Heinz bodies

**Which of the following is the most likely underlying defect?**

A. Spectrin deficiency
B. Glucose-6-phosphate dehydrogenase deficiency ✓
C. Factor V Leiden mutation
D. ADAMTS13 deficiency
E. Pyruvate kinase deficiency

**Rationale:** Oxidant stress (fava beans/classically, infection, sulfa drugs, antimalarials) triggering intravascular hemolysis with bite cells/Heinz bodies is classic G6PD. Spectrin/hereditary spherocytosis shows spherocytes without Heinz bodies. ADAMTS13 causes TTP (schistocytes, neuro symptoms). Factor V Leiden is thrombophilia, not hemolysis.

## 5. obgyn (step2) — batch-01

A 26-year-old woman, G2P1, at 7 weeks gestation by last menstrual period presents with sharp left lower quadrant pain and light vaginal spotting for six hours. She has no medical problems. Last pregnancy was an uncomplicated vaginal delivery. BP 118/72, pulse 88/min. Abdomen soft with left adnexal tenderness; no guarding. Transvaginal ultrasound: empty uterus; left adnexal mass 2.1 cm with surrounding free fluid; no intrauterine gestational sac. β-hCG 2,400 mIU/mL (discriminatory zone)

**Which of the following is the most appropriate next step in management?**

A. Repeat β-hCG in 48 hours and reassess
B. Methotrexate intramuscular injection ✓
C. Diagnostic laparoscopy
D. Expectant management with bed rest
E. Dilation and curettage

**Rationale:** Stable patient with unruptured ectopic pregnancy, β-hCG below common protocol thresholds, mass <3.5–4 cm, no fetal cardiac activity — criteria often met for systemic methotrexate. Laparoscopy is for rupture, hemodynamic instability, or MTX failure/contraindication. Repeat β-hCG alone delays treatment when ectopic is already suspected with empty uterus and adnexal mass. D&C does not treat ectopic.

A. Discharge with ciprofloxacin and metronidazole

## 7. psychiatry (step2) — batch-01

An 81-year-old man is hospitalized after hip repair. On postoperative day 2 the nurse calls because he pulled at his IV lines overnight and tried to climb out of bed. He alternates between drowsiness and agitation, does not know the month, and believes the nurse is his daughter. Baseline: mild cognitive impairment at home, independent in ADLs. Vitals stable. No focal neurologic deficits. Na⁺ 128 mEq/L (132 on admission), urinalysis positive for nitrites.

**Which of the following is the most likely diagnosis?**

A. Alzheimer disease acute exacerbation
B. Delirium ✓
C. Major depressive disorder with psychosis
D. Schizophrenia
E. Normal pressure hydrocephalus

**Rationale:** Acute fluctuating attention and awareness with inattention, new perceptual disturbance, precipitant (surgery, possible UTI, hyponatremia) — delirium until proven otherwise in hospitalized elders. ## 8. microbiology (step1) — batch-01

A 7-year-old girl develops fever and a sore throat. Temperature 38.4°C (101.1°F). Exam shows tonsillar exudates and tender anterior cervical lymphadenopathy. Rapid strep antigen: positive. Mother asks whether her 4-year-old brother, who feels fine, needs treatment.

B. Antibiotics prevent post-strep glomerulonephritis but not rheumatic fever

## 9. ethics (step2) — batch-01

A 16-year-old girl requests contraceptive counseling. She is sexually active with one partner and asks that her parents not be informed. She demonstrates understanding of risks and benefits. State law permits confidential reproductive services for minors. Her mother, who accompanied her to the waiting room, demands to know what was discussed.

**Which of the following is the most appropriate response?**

A. Disclose the visit details to the mother because the patient is a minor
B. Refuse contraception until parental consent is obtained
C. Respect confidentiality and discuss contraception with the patient alone ✓
D. Report the patient to child protective services for sexual activity
E. Schedule a family meeting and require the patient to disclose first

**Rationale:** Mature minor/emancipated reproductive health statutes in many jurisdictions allow confidential contraceptive care when the adolescent demonstrates capacity. Breaching confidentiality without consent violates trust and may deter care. Parental notification is not legally required here. CPS reporting is for abuse, not consensual peer activity. Refusing care pending parental consent is inappropriate when law permits confidential services.

## 10. pathology (step1) — batch-01

A 29-year-old man who emigrated from India five years ago has two months of low-grade fever, night sweats, and a 6-kg (13-lb) weight loss. Temperature is 38.1°C (100.6°F), pulse 92/min, and blood pressure 118/74 mm Hg. Chest radiograph shows a right upper lobe cavitary lesion. Sputum acid-fast stain is positive. Biopsy of a cervical lymph node shows granulomas with central caseation.

**Which of the following is the most likely mechanism of cell death in the center of these granulomas?**

A. Apoptosis regulated by caspases
B. Coagulative necrosis from ischemia
C. Liquefactive necrosis from proteases
D. Enzymatic fat necrosis
E. Macrophage-mediated tissue destruction with persistent mycobacterial antigen ✓

**Rationale:** Caseating granulomas in TB reflect type IV HSR: persistent antigen (M. tuberculosis) drives macrophage aggregation and central necrosis — a distinctive pattern, not simple ischemic coagulative necrosis. Liquefactive necrosis is typical of abscess/bacterial brain. Fat necrosis involves pancreatic lipases. Pure apoptosis does not produce caseous centers.

## 11. cardiology (step2) — batch-01

A 72-year-old man with HFrEF (EF 30%) on lisinopril, carvedilol, spironolactone, and furosemide is seen for routine follow-up. He has no chest pain or orthopnea. A. Stop carvedilol
B. Stop lisinopril
C. Stop spironolactone ✓

E. Add amlodipine

## 12. neurology (step2) — batch-01

A 35-year-old man comes to the clinic reporting six weeks of recurrent severe headaches. Attacks wake him from sleep at 1–2 AM, last about 45 minutes, and occur almost nightly for two weeks then remit. During attacks he paces around the room and occasionally hits his head against the wall. Between episodes he is headache-free. Between attacks, blood pressure is 124/76 mm Hg, pulse 62/min, and neurologic examination is normal. MRI brain: normal.

**Which of the following is the most appropriate prophylactic treatment?**

A. Sumatriptan at headache onset only
B. Topiramate
C. Verapamil ✓
D. Indomethacin
E. High-flow oxygen during attacks only

**Rationale:** Cluster headache — circadian/seasonal pattern, autonomic features, restlessness (algophobia), male predominance. Acute: high-flow O₂ or triptans. Sumatriptan/O₂ treat acute attacks but prophylaxis is needed for frequent cycles.

## 13. pulmonology (step2) — batch-02

A 62-year-old woman with recently diagnosed breast cancer is sent to the emergency department from oncology clinic because she became light-headed while waiting for labs. She reports two days of mild right calf aching but denies chest pain. She received her first dose of chemotherapy one week ago and has been less mobile. She does not smoke. Temperature 37.2°C (99.0°F), blood pressure 94/60 mm Hg, pulse 118/min, respirations 22/min, oxygen saturation 89% on room air. Lungs are clear. Heart sounds are tachycardic without murmurs. The right calf is swollen and tender. Laboratory studies show:
Hemoglobin 11.4 g/dL · WBC 6,800/mm³ · platelets 210,000/mm³ · creatinine 1.0 mg/dL · lactate 2.1 mmol/L · troponin I 0.04 ng/mL (reference <0.04)

**Which of the following is the most appropriate next step in management?**

A. Ventilation-perfusion scan
B. CT pulmonary angiography ✓
C. Lower extremity Doppler ultrasonography only
D. Intravenous heparin infusion after confirming diagnosis with imaging
E. Two sets of blood cultures and empiric antibiotics

**Rationale:** Active cancer, immobilization, unilateral leg swelling, tachycardia, hypoxemia, and hypotension make pulmonary embolism likely despite clear lungs. CTPA confirms PE and guides anticoagulation when contrast is feasible. V/Q scan is second-line if contrast is contraindicated. Leg Doppler alone does not evaluate the lungs. Anticoagulation follows diagnosis unless instability mandates immediate treatment while imaging is arranged. Blood cultures target sepsis, which is less likely here.

## 14. hepatology (step1) — batch-02

He drinks alcohol only on weekends and denies illicit drugs. Exam shows a fine resting tremor, mild slurred speech, and subtle sunburst pigmentation at the limbus. ## 17. pharmacology (step1) — batch-02

A 58-year-old man with hyperlipidemia and community-acquired pneumonia is prescribed azithromycin by his primary care physician. His other medications include atorvastatin 40 mg nightly, amlodipine, and omeprazole. Three days later he develops diffuse myalgias, dark urine, and weakness. ## 20. biostatistics (step1) — batch-02

A randomized trial of Drug X versus placebo for preventing stroke in 4,000 patients reports 40 strokes in the treatment group and 80 strokes in the placebo group over two years (2,000 patients per arm).

**Which of the following is the most appropriate estimate of the number needed to treat (NNT) over two years to prevent one stroke?**

A. 25
B. 50 ✓
C. 100
D. 200
E. 400

**Rationale:** Treatment event rate 40/2000 = 2%; placebo 80/2000 = 4%. Absolute risk reduction = 2%. NNT = 1/0.02 = 50. Answer 25 implies 4% ARR; 100/200/400 overestimate NNT.

## 25. emergency-medicine (step2) — batch-03

A 19-year-old college student is brought to the emergency department because of fever, headache, and neck stiffness that began 12 hours ago. His roommate reports he was confused this morning. Temperature 39.4°C (102.9°F), blood pressure 102/64 mm Hg, pulse 124/min, respirations 22/min. He is lethargic but arousable. Nuchal rigidity is present; Kernig sign is positive. No focal neurologic deficits. Skin is warm without rash. Laboratory studies:
WBC 19,200/mm³ · glucose 98 mg/dL · sodium 136 mEq/L
Lumbar puncture: opening pressure 28 cm H₂O; CSF WBC 1,850/mm³ (88% neutrophils); glucose 32 mg/dL (simultaneous serum glucose 98 mg/dL); protein 198 mg/dL; Gram stain shows gram-negative diplococci

**Which of the following is the most likely diagnosis?**

A. Acute bacterial meningitis ✓
B. Viral meningitis
C. Subarachnoid hemorrhage
D. Encephalitis
E. Brain abscess

**Rationale:** Fever, meningismus, altered mental status, neutrophilic pleocytosis, low CSF glucose, elevated protein, and gram-negative diplococci indicate acute bacterial meningitis (likely Neisseria meningitidis in a young adult). Viral meningitis typically has lymphocytic predominance and normal glucose. SAH presents with thunderclap headache and xanthochromia, not this CSF profile. Encephalitis emphasizes altered cognition/seizures with less prominent meningismus. Brain abscess shows focal deficits and ring-enhancing lesions.

## PE. emergency-medicine (step2) — batch-03

A 19-year-old college student is brought to the emergency department because of fever, headache, and neck stiffness that began 12 hours ago. His roommate reports he was confused this morning. Temperature 39.4°C (102.9°F), blood pressure 102/64 mm Hg, pulse 124/min, respirations 22/min. He is lethargic but arousable. Nuchal rigidity is present; Kernig sign is positive. No focal neurologic deficits. Skin is warm without rash. Laboratory studies:
WBC 19,200/mm³ · glucose 98 mg/dL · sodium 136 mEq/L
Lumbar puncture: opening pressure 28 cm H₂O; CSF WBC 1,850/mm³ (88% neutrophils); glucose 32 mg/dL (simultaneous serum glucose 98 mg/dL); protein 198 mg/dL; Gram stain shows gram-negative diplococci

**Which of the following is the most likely diagnosis?**

A. Acute bacterial meningitis ✓
B. Viral meningitis
C. Subarachnoid hemorrhage
D. Encephalitis
E. Brain abscess

**Rationale:** Fever, meningismus, altered mental status, neutrophilic pleocytosis, low CSF glucose, elevated protein, and gram-negative diplococci indicate acute bacterial meningitis (likely Neisseria meningitidis in a young adult). Encephalitis emphasizes altered cognition/seizures with less prominent meningismus. Brain abscess shows focal deficits and ring-enhancing lesions.
